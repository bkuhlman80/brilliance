# Centrality and Peripherality

\# Centrality vs Peripherality of Personality Facets Across Four Instruments

\#\# Question

Within each instrument's facet/aspect space, which constructs are more \*\*central\*\* (high cross-domain loadings, connecting multiple domains) and which are more \*\*peripheral\*\* (loading almost exclusively on their own domain)?

\#\# Method

\*\*Cross-domain loading ratio\*\* \= sum(|cross-domain loadings|) / |primary loading|

\- Ratio near 0 \= peripheral (domain-defining anchor)  
\- Ratio near 1 \= moderate (cross-loadings collectively equal primary)  
\- Ratio \> 1 \= central (more cross-domain than within-domain variance)

\#\# 4\. NEO-PI-R / NEO-PI-3 (30 facets × 5 factors)

\#\#\# Sources  
\- LeCorff & Bhérer (2016) Table 2, French-Canadian NEO-PI-3 (N=1,313), \*\*oblimin rotation\*\* (oblique)  
\- Pilia et al. (2006) Table S3, Sardinian NEO-PI-R (N=6,148), \*\*genetic\*\* factor loadings (Procrustes varimax)

\#\#\# Methodological notes

These two sources differ in important ways:

1\. \*\*LeCorff\*\* uses \*\*oblimin rotation\*\* (oblique), producing \*\*pattern loadings\*\* — the unique contribution of each factor after partialling out correlated factors. This systematically inflates cross-loading ratios, especially for facets whose domains share substantial variance with other domains (e.g., E facets show low unique E loadings because E shares variance with A, N, and C).

2\. \*\*Pilia\*\* uses \*\*genetic factor loadings\*\* — the heritable architecture stripped of environmental influences. Genetic loadings are not constrained by varimax orthogonality and can show large cross-domain loadings reflecting genuine pleiotropy.

Both sources yield higher ratios than varimax PCA (used for BFI-2, BFAS, HEXACO). The convergent patterns across these two very different methods are therefore especially informative.

\#\# Summary: The periphery-center map

\#\#\# Tier 1 — Peripheral (domain-defining anchors)

These constructs define what each domain IS. They have the lowest cross-loadings and the highest factor purity.

| Domain | Anchor construct | Mean ratio across instruments |  
|---|---|---|  
| C | Organization / Orderliness | \~.35 |  
| N | Anxiety / Worry | \~.30 |  
| E/X | Sociability / Social Boldness | \~.50 |  
| O | Ideas / Aesthetics / Unconventionality | \~.45 |  
| A | Compliance / Flexibility / Politeness | \~.55 |

\#\#\# Tier 2 — Intermediate

These constructs carry domain-specific information but also connect outward.

| Example constructs | Typical ratio range |  
|---|---|  
| Trust, Productiveness, Creative Imagination | .55–.80 |  
| Emotional Volatility, Aesthetic Sensitivity | .45–.70 |  
| Perfectionism, Sentimentality, Gentleness | .65–.90 |

\#\#\# Tier 3 — Central (cross-domain connectors)

These constructs sit at the intersections of multiple domains. They reveal how domains relate to each other.

| Construct | Typical ratio range | Domains bridged |  
|---|---|---|  
| \*\*Assertiveness / Agency\*\* | 1.10–2.89 | E↔O↔C↔A↔N |  
| \*\*Energy / Activity / Diligence\*\* | .88–2.53 | E↔C |  
| \*\*Depression / Withdrawal\*\* | .80–1.15 | N↔E |  
| \*\*Warmth / Compassion / Altruism\*\* | .72–2.40 | E↔A (↔H in HEXACO) |  
| \*\*Trust\*\* | .61–4.33 | A↔N↔E |  
| \*\*Fairness / Responsibility\*\* | .73–1.43 | C↔A (↔H in HEXACO) |  
| \*\*Deliberation / Prudence\*\* | .73–1.73 | C↔A↔N |  
| \*\*Impulsiveness\*\* | 1.33–2.80 | N↔C (↔A) |

\#\#\# Design implications

1\. \*\*To measure a domain cleanly\*\*, use the peripheral anchors. Organization measures C. Anxiety measures N. Sociability measures E.

2\. \*\*To predict real-world outcomes\*\*, the central facets may be more powerful because they capture cross-domain variance driving complex behaviors. Assertiveness predicts leadership, intelligence, and well-being simultaneously because it connects E, O, C, and low N.

3\. \*\*To understand domain inter-relationships\*\*, examine the central facets. The E↔C bridge (Energy Level, Activity, Diligence) and the N↔E bridge (Depression, Withdrawal) reveal the structural joints of personality space.

\#\#\# HEXACO Key Structural Differences from Big Five

1\. HEXACO Agreeableness ≠ Big Five Agreeableness.  
   \- HEXACO A is primarily Volatility-reversed (anger management, patience)  
   \- Big Five A is primarily Compassion \+ Politeness  
   \- HEXACO explicitly moved anger/irritability from N into A domain

2\. HEXACO Emotionality ≠ Big Five Neuroticism.  
   \- HEXACO E splits off anger (→ A domain) and adds sentimentality  
   \- HEXACO E \= Withdrawal \+ some Compassion content  
   \- NEO N \= Withdrawal \+ Volatility (anger stays)

3\. Honesty-Humility has no Big Five equivalent.  
   \- Best BFAS correlate is Politeness (.51) — but captures \< half the variance  
   \- HH content: sincerity, fairness, greed avoidance, modesty

# \# Synthesis of our Early (Brilliance I) Facet Studies

## Part 1: Findings \- concise (aka “Conversation 7: The Verdict”)

\#\# What Picture Did the Evidence Form?

\---

\#\#\# 1\. Summary: The Best-Supported Model of Facet-Level Personality Structure

The evidence does not support a clean hierarchical model where five domains subdivide neatly into two aspects each, with each aspect containing three tightly coherent facets. That model — the one implicit in much Big Five thinking — is wrong at the facet level.

What the evidence \*does\* support is messier, more interesting, and structurally asymmetric across domains. Here is the picture the puzzle pieces form:

\*\*The Big Five domains are real but unequally coherent.\*\* All five domains are recoverable as genetic factors across cultures (Yamagata 2006, XL). But their internal structures range from moderately tight (Neuroticism) to barely holding together (Agreeableness). The domain boundaries are not all drawn in the right places. Three boundaries are demonstrably porous: N–A, N–C, and E–C.

\*\*The two-aspect intermediate structure is a phenotypic convenience, not a genetic reality.\*\* In no domain does the published two-aspect split cleanly survive contact with genetic evidence. The closest approach is Openness, where the German sample in Jang 2002 (L) recovers the Openness/Intellect split genetically — but even there, O4 is cross-culturally unstable and O6 is adrift. In every other domain, the genetic factor structure crosscuts the published aspects. This is the single most consistent finding across all five domains.

\*\*The real structure has three recurring features:\*\*

1\. \*\*A small genetic core\*\* — typically two facets — that define what the domain "is" biologically.  
2\. \*\*Domain markers\*\* — facets that load on the domain broadly without differentiating its internal structure. These are useful for measurement but uninformative about substructure.  
3\. \*\*Boundary facets\*\* — facets that share substantial genetic variance with other domains, sit at domain margins, and behave differently from their domain siblings on at least one evidence stream.

Here is that structure, domain by domain:

| \*\*Domain-bridging\*\* | A1 Trust | Genetically split between A and low-N (in Canada, N loading −.59 exceeds A loading .49; Yamagata, XL). 62% facet-specific. Orphaned from all genetic clusters (Pilia S2, XL). Standalone in BFI-2. |  
| \*\*Cultural artifact\*\* | A5 Modesty | Developmentally flat. No heritable specific variance. Reversed MZ/DZ correlations. Genetic loading drops to .23 in Japan (Yamagata, XL). Excluded from BFI-2. May not be a personality "trait" in the endogenous sense. |

\*\*Domain coherence:\*\* The lowest of any Big Five domain by every available metric. Lowest SNP heritability (.04–.06), lowest cross-geography rG (.65), lowest cross-instrument rG (.68), only domain without PFC gene expression enrichment (Schwaba, XL). Cross-national genetic congruence .86 — lowest of any domain (Yamagata, XL). Only two facets (A2, A4) are stable genetic A markers. \*\*Confidence: Strong that A is the least coherent domain; strong for the {A2, A4} core; moderate-strong for A1's bridging status; moderate for A5's status as a cultural artifact.\*\*

\#\#\# 2\. Confident Claims

These findings are supported by multiple evidence streams, multiple studies, and do not have unresolved contradictions.

\*\*2.1. N5 Impulsiveness does not belong in the Neuroticism domain.\*\*  
Every evidence stream converges: weak N genetic loading (.37–.51), strong C genetic cross-loading (−.33 to −.53; Yamagata XL, three countries), no loading on either within-N genetic factor (Jang 2002, L), monotonic developmental trajectory unique among N facets converging with C6 Deliberation (Terracciano L, Brandt L, Jackson M), excluded from BFI-2 (Soto & John, XL), no shared biological pathways with any other N facet (Kim, S). The direction of the evidence is uniform. This is the single best-evidenced facet misplacement in the corpus.

\*\*2.2. E4 Activity is genetically a Conscientiousness facet.\*\*  
Primary genetic loading on C (.65) exceeds E (.47; Pilia S3, XL). Clusters genetically with C4 Achievement Striving at rG \= .775 (Pilia S2, XL). Absent from E-domain genetic cluster. Replicated across three countries (genetic C cross-loading .48–.54; Yamagata, XL). This is the strongest quantified cross-domain genetic signal in the corpus.

\*\*2.3. The N–A boundary is misdrawn.\*\*  
N2 Angry Hostility shares substantial genetic variance with A facets (rG with A1: −.75 to −.47 across three samples; Jang 2001 L). N2's genetic A cross-loading is −.32 to −.60 across three countries (Yamagata, XL). In Japan, N2 is genetically almost as much an A facet as an N facet. The HEXACO model independently redrew this boundary. The reciprocal signal exists from the A side: A1 Trust is genetically more N-like than A-like in at least one sample. The Compassion aspect of A is structurally and genetically porous to the N domain.

\*\*2.4. Agreeableness is genetically anomalous among the Big Five.\*\*  
Lowest SNP heritability, lowest cross-geography and cross-instrument genetic correlations, only domain without PFC enrichment (Schwaba, XL). Most variable rank-order stability (Brandt, L). Weakest within-domain co-development (Schwaba, M). Complete etiological heterogeneity in facet change (Bleidorn, M). Only two of six facets are stable genetic domain markers. No other domain has this profile.

\*\*2.5. The published two-aspect structure fails genetically in every domain.\*\*  
In no domain does the published two-aspect model map cleanly onto within-domain genetic factors. In N, the genetic split is "Hostility (N2 alone)" vs. "everything else" — not Volatility vs. Withdrawal. In E, genetic factors shift content across cultures. In O, the German data approximates the published split, but O4 and O5 are unstable. In C, C5 is genetically on the wrong side. In A, no aspect boundary is recovered. This finding is replicated across two independent genetic studies (Jang 2002, L; Yamagata 2006, XL) covering three countries.

\*\*2.6. Facets excluded from BFI-2 cluster with genetic boundary-crossers.\*\*  
N5, E5, O4, O6 were all excluded from BFI-2 on structural grounds. All four have \>50% facet-specific genetic variance and/or cross-domain genetic loadings. The BFI-2 developers' structural intuitions converge with genetic architecture evidence. This convergence between independent structural judgment (Soto & John, XL) and behavioral genetics (Jang 2002, L; Yamagata 2006, XL) is a cross-method validation.

\---

\#\#\# 3\. Where the Evidence Is Too Thin or Too Conflicted to Decide

\*\*3.1. Whether N5 and C6 share direct genetic variance.\*\*  
No bivariate rG(N5, C6) exists in the corpus. Neither appears in any shared genetic cluster in Pilia S2, suggesting rG \< .50. Their developmental convergence (strong, Tier 1\) and N5's genetic C loading (−.41 to −.53) are consistent with shared structure, but whether the relationship is through a specific N5–C6 link or through N5's diffuse C-domain affinity is unknown. This is the single most important missing data point in the project.

\*\*3.2. The internal structure of Conscientiousness.\*\*  
No two-way partition of C achieves multi-stream convergence. The published Industriousness/Orderliness split has structural support but is contradicted genetically (C5 is on the wrong side) and developmentally (trajectories split three ways, crosscutting both aspects). The genetic alternative ({C1, C4} vs. {C2, C3, C5, C6}) has support from one sample (German; Jang 2002, L) but not others. The evidence may simply not support a clean binary partition of C.

\*\*3.3. What E4 Activity actually measures in older adults.\*\*  
Multiple authors flag that E4 items may increasingly measure physical vitality rather than personality in older adults. The health-confound interpretation and the genetic-misplacement interpretation are compatible — both can be true — but no study directly controls for objective health. This matters because E4's steep late-life decline is part of the developmental evidence for its C-domain affinity. If the decline is purely physical, the developmental signal is weaker (though the genetic signal remains).

\*\*3.4. Whether Openness and Intellect are one domain or two.\*\*  
The aspect correlation (.29; Haehner, L) is almost low enough to treat them as separate domains. The German genetic data (Jang 2002, L) shows the cleanest aspect-level genetic split of any domain. But the phenotypic factor structure consistently recovers a single O domain. The evidence supports either "two weakly related aspects of one domain" or "two narrowly related but distinct domains." It does not adjudicate between these framings.

\*\*3.5. Whether A5 Modesty is a personality trait at all.\*\*  
Reversed MZ/DZ correlations, no heritable specific variance, developmental flatness while HEXACO Honesty-Humility shows \~1.0 SD increase, genetic loading dropping to .23 in Japan — all suggest A5 is more culturally shaped than endogenously driven. But its shared A-factor genetic variance is normal (.76 loading on genetic A; Pilia S3), suggesting the \*common\* genetic component is intact while the \*specific\* component is environmentally driven. The evidence does not resolve whether this makes A5 a weakly endogenous trait or a cultural norm that correlates with personality genetically through shared environment.

\*\*3.6. The status of N4 Self-Consciousness.\*\*  
N4 is genuinely underdetermined. It loads on the "general distress" genetic factor (grouping it with N1, N3, N6) but is molecularly isolated from them (no 5-HTTLPR association; unique COMT association). Near-equal structural loadings on both aspects. Limited developmental data (Brandt could not estimate due to negative latent variance). This is a gap problem, not a conflict — there simply isn't enough evidence to characterize N4 beyond "it's in N, probably in the distress cluster."

\*\*3.7. The nature of the E3 "Agency" construct.\*\*  
E3 Assertiveness is genetically the most diffuse facet in the corpus — loading on E, low-N, low-A, and C simultaneously (Yamagata, XL). This could mean E3 taps a genuine cross-domain agency dimension, or it could mean E3 items are poorly differentiated and carry general-adjustment variance. No study directly tests whether the cross-domain genetic loadings reflect a coherent latent construct or measurement artifact.

\---

\#\#\# 4\. Biggest Surprises

\*\*4.1. The aspect model is genetically empty — across all five domains.\*\*  
This is the most consequential finding. The two-aspect intermediate structure between facets and domains is the most widely used organizational principle in Big Five personality research. It fails genetically in every domain. This does not mean the aspects are "wrong" phenotypically — factor analyses consistently recover two intermediate factors. But these intermediate factors do not correspond to separable genetic architectures. They appear to be phenotypic summaries of genetically heterogeneous facets, not reflections of distinct biological dimensions. The phenotypic aspects are organizationally useful and empirically recoverable. They are not biologically deep.

\*\*4.2. Two facets are genetically in the wrong domain — not at the margin, but decisively.\*\*  
N5 and E4 are not boundary cases. N5 shares more genetic variance with C than with N in two of three countries (Yamagata, XL). E4's primary genetic loading is on C, not E (Pilia S3, XL). These are not facets leaning toward another domain; they are facets misassigned to the wrong domain by the phenotypic rotation, and the genetic evidence says so clearly.

\*\*4.3. Agreeableness is genetically a different kind of thing from the other four domains.\*\*  
The other four domains have SNP heritabilities of .06–.19, cross-geography genetic correlations of .86–.99, cross-instrument genetic correlations of .82–.97, and PFC gene expression enrichment. Agreeableness has h²\_SNP of .04–.06, cross-geography rG of .65, cross-instrument rG of .68, and no PFC enrichment. It is not a slightly weaker version of the others. It is qualitatively different — and the within-domain evidence (two-facet genetic core, etiological heterogeneity, most variable stability patterns) is consistent with the domain-level GWAS finding.

\*\*4.4. Content concerns can be empirically tested — and sometimes they're wrong.\*\*  
O3 Feelings was widely suspected of N-domain contamination based on content (emotional reactivity overlaps). But O3 turns out to be the genetically purest O facet (.81 on genetic O; Pilia S3, XL). The content concern was a reasonable prior; the genetic evidence overturned it. This is a useful caution against content-based structural arguments when genetic data are available.

\*\*4.5. The BFI-2 developers got the structural calls right — independently of genetic data.\*\*  
The facets excluded from BFI-2 on purely structural grounds (N5, E5, O4, O6, A5) are precisely the facets with the most anomalous genetic profiles. The facets made standalone (A1 Trust, C3 Dutifulness) are the ones with the most ambiguous structural and genetic positions. Soto and John's (2017, XL) structural intuitions converge with behavioral genetics evidence they did not use. This is one of the strongest cross-method convergences in the project.

\#\#\# 5\. Structural Recommendation

If I were building a personality assessment from scratch using only this evidence base, here is how I would group the facets.

\#\#\#\# 5a. Domain-level structure: Keep five domains, but acknowledge they are unequal.

The five-factor domain structure is recoverable genetically across cultures. Replacing it with six or seven factors (as HEXACO does) would resolve some boundary problems (N2→A, A6→Emotionality) but create others. The five-factor solution is defensible as a starting point.

However, the five domains are not equivalent structures. Three (N, E, O) are genetically coherent with clear cores. One (C) is genetically coherent at the domain level but internally messy. One (A) barely holds together genetically and may be better understood as a phenotypic alliance than a genetically unified domain.

\#\#\#\# 5b. Reassign three facets.

| Facet | Current domain | Reassign to | Strength of case |  
|---|---|---|---|  
| N5 Impulsiveness | N | C (reversed) | Strong — every stream agrees |  
| E4 Activity | E | C | Strong — primary genetic loading .65 on C (XL) |  
| N2 Angry Hostility | N | Keep in N, but formally flag as N–A boundary | Strong — the evidence supports reassignment to reversed-A (HEXACO solution), but within a five-factor framework, a clean reassignment is hard to operationalize. Flagging the dual membership is more honest than pretending it's cleanly N. |

\#\#\#\# 5c. Remove three facets from domain scoring.

| Facet | Current domain | Action | Reason |  
|---|---|---|---|  
| E5 Excitement-Seeking | E | Score separately or drop | 68–70% genetically specific; excluded from BFI-2; r \= .05 with life satisfaction. Not meaningfully part of E. |  
| O6 Values | O | Score separately or drop | Near-zero heritability; social-attitudinal content; excluded from BFI-2. Different kind of construct. |  
| A5 Modesty | A | Score separately or flag | Genetic loading drops to .23 in Japan; reversed MZ/DZ; no heritable specificity; developmentally inert. May not be endogenous personality. |

\#\#\#\# 5d. Intermediate structure: Do not posit a uniform two-aspect model. Use domain-specific groupings.

The evidence does not support a single structural principle (two aspects per domain) applied uniformly. It supports domain-specific internal organization:

\*\*Neuroticism:\*\* One core group {N1, N3, N4, N6} and one semi-independent facet {N2}. Not two equal aspects. The "core distress" group is the domain. N2 is a bridging facet with a foot in low-A.

\*\*Extraversion (after reassigning E4, removing E5):\*\* One sociability cluster {E1, E2, E6} and one standalone assertiveness facet {E3}. The sociability cluster is moderately coherent (two-stream convergence). E3 is its own thing — genetically multi-domain, developmentally distinct, and structurally clean. A two-aspect model is a passable approximation here, but the "Assertiveness aspect" is really just E3.

\*\*Openness:\*\* Two groups, but unequal. The Openness core {O1, O2, O3} has multi-stream support. The Intellect side is effectively just O5, with O4 peripheral and O6 removed. Two aspects is structurally correct here but misleading about symmetry — one aspect has a three-facet core, the other is a singleton.

\*\*Conscientiousness (after absorbing N5 reversed and E4):\*\* The internal structure remains unresolved. The evidence is compatible with:  
\- A "proactive achievement" cluster {C1, C4, E4} vs. a "behavioral regulation" cluster {C2, C3, C5, C6, reversed-N5}, based on the genetic factor structure.  
\- A less committed framing with {C1, C4} as a loose genetic dyad and the rest ungrouped.

I would not commit to a specific two-way partition for C. The evidence does not support one confidently. I would report both the structural (Industriousness/Orderliness) and genetic ({C1, C4} vs. rest) alternatives and flag that they disagree about C5.

\*\*Agreeableness:\*\* A tight core {A2, A4} and then varying degrees of semi-independence. A two-aspect model is actively misleading here — the Compassion aspect has no multi-stream convergence, and the Politeness aspect loses A5. The honest description is: {A2, A4} define the domain. A1 is an N-boundary construct. A3 is a domain marker. A6 is genetically semi-independent. A5 may not be endogenous personality.

\#\#\#\# 5e. Cross-domain constructs the evidence independently identifies.

Three constructs emerge from the cross-domain analysis that do not respect domain boundaries:

\*\*Impulse control / Constraint.\*\* N5 (reversed), C5, C6. Possibly including E5 content. The developmental convergence of N5 and C6 is the strongest cross-domain Tier 1 signal in the corpus. Whether this is a single dimension or a correlated cluster is untested.

\*\*Antagonism / low Agreeableness.\*\* N2 (reversed), A1, A2, A4. The HEXACO model's Agreeableness factor captures this. The genetic evidence supports treating anger/hostility and interpersonal trust as opposite poles of a single dimension that crosscuts the Big Five N and A domains.

\*\*Energetic engagement / Vitality.\*\* E4, C4. rG \= .775 (Pilia S2, XL). Both involve goal-directed energy expenditure. Possibly related to the Roberts Social Vitality construct. Whether this is a distinct dimension or simply the intersection of E and C domain factors is undetermined.

\#\#\# 6\. Transparency: Pattern-Matching Flags

\*\*6.1.\*\* The structural recommendation in §5 overlaps substantially with what the HEXACO model does for N and A. The HEXACO rotation reassigns anger/hostility content to reversed-A and sentimentality to Emotionality. My recommendation arrives at a similar endpoint — not because I'm optimizing toward HEXACO, but because the genetic evidence (Jang 2001 L, Yamagata 2006 XL) and cross-taxonomy evidence (Ludeke 2019 L) independently support the same boundary-redrawing. Evidence independently supporting this: N2 genetic A cross-loadings replicated in three countries; A1 genetic N cross-loadings; BFAS Volatility mapping to reversed HEXACO-A at r \= −.61. Evidence against: the five-factor rotation is also statistically valid; the HEXACO solution creates its own boundary problems (Honesty-Humility is partially novel content, not a simple rearrangement).

\*\*6.2.\*\* My grouping of {O2, O3} as the Openness core could be seen as validating the published Openness aspect. It does partially, but with a key difference: the published Openness aspect includes O1 as a full member, and I'm treating O1 as extended/boundary. The evidence for O1's demotion: its developmental trajectory (declining) dissociates from O2/O3 (stable), and its intelligence rG aligns with Intellect rather than Openness.

\*\*6.3.\*\* The finding that the two-aspect model is "genetically empty" could itself be an artifact of the genetic evidence being Tier 3 (weakest stream). The genetic studies are smaller, less replicated, and more method-dependent than the developmental or structural studies. A skeptic could argue that the aspect model is correct and the genetic evidence is simply underpowered to recover it. Counter: the Yamagata (2006) study is XL quality and covers three countries. If the genetic factor structure were simply a noisy version of the phenotypic aspect structure, you'd expect the aspects to emerge in at least one country for most domains. They do not — except partially in O (German sample). The failure is systematic, not sporadic. I weight this finding accordingly, but the caveat is real.

\#\#\# 7\. What Remains Unknown

\#\#\#\# Critical gaps:

1\. \*\*rG(N5, C6).\*\* The single most important untested prediction. If it's high, the impulse-control cross-domain axis is confirmed genetically. If it's low, the developmental convergence may reflect shared content rather than shared structure.

2\. \*\*Full 30 × 30 facet genetic correlation matrix.\*\* Jang (2002) analyzed within-domain only. Yamagata (2006) reports factor loadings, not pairwise rG. A complete matrix from a large twin sample would transform the project.

3\. \*\*Facet-level GWAS.\*\* Schwaba (2025, XL) is domain-level only. Facet-level molecular data would directly test whether facets within the same domain share the same genetic variants or merely share the same latent factor.

4\. \*\*Observer-report twin data.\*\* The entire evidence base is self-report. Self-report genetic factor structures may be inflated by implicit personality theory (people's beliefs about which traits go together). Observer data would test whether the genetic findings replicate when measurement method changes.

5\. \*\*Childhood-to-adulthood developmental continuity at the facet level.\*\* The NEO-PI-R was designed for adults. Brandes (2020, L) provides childhood data with non-NEO instruments. Whether the within-domain groupings identified here hold across the lifespan is unknown.

6\. \*\*Non-Western, non-Japanese genetic data.\*\* The cross-cultural genetic evidence comes from Canada, Germany, and Japan (Yamagata, XL) plus a Sardinian sample (Pilia, XL). Whether the genetic findings generalize beyond these populations is untested.

\#\#\#\# Moderate gaps:

7\. Whether E3's cross-domain genetic loadings reflect a coherent "agency" construct or measurement artifact.  
8\. O6's structural home — it doesn't fit in O, but no alternative domain is obvious.  
9\. Whether C's internal structure supports any clean binary partition.  
10\. A5 Modesty's dramatically different developmental trajectory compared to HEXACO Honesty-Humility.  
11\. Late-life developmental trajectories for most facets (studies cap at \~60).  
12\. HEXACO-to-NEO genetic correlations (would directly test the boundary-redrawing recommendation).

\---

\#\#\# 8\. Final Assessment

The Big Five domain structure is genetically real. The facets within those domains are not equally well-placed. Three facets (N5, E4, E5) are clearly in the wrong domain or have no domain home. Three more (O4, O6, A5) are peripheral enough to question their inclusion. The N–A boundary needs redrawing; the E–C boundary needs adjusting.

The two-aspect intermediate structure is a useful phenotypic description that does not reflect genetic architecture. If forced to choose between "aspects are real biological structures" and "aspects are statistical summaries of genetically heterogeneous facets," the evidence strongly favors the latter.

The domains that hold together best are Neuroticism and Extraversion. The domain that holds together worst is Agreeableness. Openness has the cleanest internal split but the weakest cross-aspect integration. Conscientiousness has decent domain-level coherence but no internally resolvable partition.

The evidence assembled here is sufficient to identify what is wrong with the standard model. It is not sufficient to build a complete alternative. The alternative sketched in §5 is the best the evidence supports — but it has gaps (especially C's internal structure and the A6/O boundary) that cannot be resolved with existing data.

## Part 2: Findings \- unpacked

This document merges two rounds of facet-level personality synthesis. Version 1 (v1) drew on approximately 27 developmental, heritability, and structural studies (including Terracciano 2005 L, Brandt 2023 L, Schwaba 2022 M, Jang 1996 M, Jang 1998 L, Jang 2001 L, Pilia 2006 XL, DeYoung 2007, Kim 2017 S, Røysamb 2018 M, Nikolasevic 2021 S, Luciano 2006 M, Briley & Tucker-Drob 2012 M, Soto & John 2017 XL, among others). The merge follows these principles: (1) v2 is taken as primary where it supersedes v1, because it had access to stronger genetic evidence; (2) where v1 and v2 disagree on a convergence rating, clustering verdict, or grouping, the disagreement is noted explicitly rather than silently resolved; (3) v1 findings that v2 did not cover are incorporated; (4) evidence gaps that v2 studies resolved are removed, while gaps present in both versions are retained; (5) duplicate evidence points appearing in both versions are consolidated into a single entry with the richest detail.

Assessment: N2 and A1 are opposite-pole markers of the same cross-domain genetic dimension: the tendency toward hostile attributions and reactive anger (N2) vs. the tendency toward secure, trusting interpersonal expectations (A1). These are not the same facet measured in reverse — N2 is anger/hostility, A1 is interpersonal trust — but genetically, they share a dimension.

The genetic and structural evidence converge — N2's anger/antagonism content sits at the N–A boundary at both levels. The HEXACO model resolved this by reassigning anger content to reversed-A. The N–A boundary is genuinely misdrawn at the facet level.

What this means: The Big Five N–A boundary runs through the middle of what should probably be separated into (a) an Emotionality/Withdrawal factor (N1, N3, N6, plus A6 Sentimentality content) and (b) an Antagonism factor (reversed N2, reversed A1 Trust, A2, A4 Politeness content). This is essentially what the HEXACO model does, and the evidence reviewed here independently supports that rotation.

Developmental complement: N2 robustly declines across adulthood (Terracciano L, Schwaba M, Brandt L, Ringwald L). A-domain facets broadly increase. This is directionally consistent with their genetic antagonism — they move in opposite directions across the lifespan — though developmental convergence doesn't require genetic mediation.

From the A side: A6 Tender-Mindedness maps to HEXACO Emotionality (not A). A1 Trust is genetically entangled with N. The Compassion aspect of A is structurally and genetically porous to the N domain.

\#\#\# 1.4 The Warmth/Prosociality Bridge: E1 → A

E1 Warmth has a genetic A cross-loading of .45 (Pilia S3 / Yamagata 2006 XL). In Jang (2002 L), E1 loads on both within-E genetic factors. A3 Altruism shows the mirror pattern: genetic loadings across A (.32–.62), C (.39 in Canada), and E (.37 in Canada). \#\#\# 1.5 The Assertiveness/"Agency" Complex: E3 → multiple domains

E3 is genetically the most diffuse facet in the corpus. Yamagata (2006 XL) reports genetic loadings on four domains: E (.45–.55), low-N (−.19 to −.43), low-A (−.26 to −.44), C (.30–.39). Strong signal for genetic diffuseness (XL quality, three samples).

\#\#\# 1.7 The C1–Intellect Overlap

C1 Competence shares genetic variance with intelligence (Luciano 2006 M: \~2% shared variance; Nikolasevic 2021 S: rG \= \+0.16 with fluid intelligence). Replicated cross-culturally. O5 Ideas shows the same direction at larger magnitude (rG \= .38). C1's content (self-perceived competence) overlaps Intellect content. Moderate signal for IQ overlap (replicated); weak signal for structural implication (small effect).

\#\#\# 1.8 O3 Feelings Content Ambiguity

O3 has content overlapping N (emotional reactivity) and A (empathy). However, Pilia S3 (XL) shows O3's genetic loading is .81 on O — the highest O genetic loading, tied with O5. Modest cross-loadings on E (.41) and C (.19) but nothing toward N. The Jang 1996 "no genetics" finding (CE model, unique in NEO-PI-R) did not replicate (Jang 1998 L: AE model). Weak signal, now contradicted by genetic evidence — O3 belongs firmly in O genetically despite content concerns.

\#\#\# 1.9 The N6–Cognitive Decline Link

N6 Vulnerability's late-life trajectory may share variance with C-domain decline. Terracciano (2005 L): N6 shows the largest late-life increase of any N facet, explicitly linked to brain aging. C1 Competence and C5 Self-Discipline decline in late life. Weak signal — one author's interpretation, no independent confirmation.

\#\#\# 2.6 The Heritable-Specificity vs. Domain-Dependent Split

Within each domain, some facets have heritable specific variance (AE residual) while others do not (CE residual):  
\- N and E: all facets have heritable specificity.  
\- C: C4, C6, and marginally C1 lack heritable specificity.  
\- A: A3 and A5 lack heritable specificity.  
\- O: borderline case (O3 in Jang 1996 M, not replicated in Jang 1998 L).

The CE-residual facets (C4, C6, A3, A5, marginally C1) are "well-behaved" domain members phenotypically — their content is prototypically domain-relevant. Moderate signal; the split is from a single study (Jang 1998 L) with no replication.

\#\#\# 2.9 Dual-Loading Facets Are Domain Markers, Not Aspect Markers

3\. Evidence That Domain Boundaries Are Misdrawn  
────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\#\#\# 3.1 The N–A Boundary  
Strength of evidence: Strong.

The most heavily evidenced misdrawn boundary in the Big Five. The case rests on:  
\- Genetic evidence (L quality, replicated): N2 Angry Hostility shares as much genetic variance with A facets (especially A1 Trust) as with N facets. Three independent samples in Jang (2001). Yamagata (2006 XL) confirms across three countries with genetic factor loadings. A1 Trust reciprocally behaves like inverted-N genetically — in Canada, its genetic N loading exceeds its A loading.  
\- Cross-taxonomy evidence (L quality): The two N aspects map to completely different HEXACO domains. BFAS Volatility maps to reversed HEXACO-A (r \= −.61, Patience r \= −.70). BFAS Withdrawal maps to HEXACO-E (r \= .53). Ludeke 2019 (L) quantifies: the correlation between BFAS Volatility and HEXACO-E is r \= .08, essentially zero — it's entirely an A-domain construct in HEXACO space.  
\- The HEXACO precedent: HEXACO explicitly resolved this by reassigning anger/antagonism to the A domain and creating a narrower Emotionality factor. The developmental data broadly support this: HEXACO Patience/Forgivingness (reversed anger/antagonism) shows a U-shaped developmental curve distinct from Emotionality facets' linear declines (Ashton & Lee 2016 M).  
\- From the A side: A6 Tender-Mindedness maps to HEXACO Emotionality (not A). A1 Trust is genetically entangled with N. The Compassion aspect of A is structurally and genetically porous to the N domain.

Net assessment: The Big Five N–A boundary runs through the middle of what should probably be separated into (a) an Emotionality/Withdrawal factor (N1, N3, N6, plus A6 Sentimentality content) and (b) an Antagonism factor (reversed N2, reversed A1, A2, A4 Politeness content). This is essentially the HEXACO solution, and the evidence independently supports that rotation.

\#\#\# 3.2 The E–C Boundary  
Strength of evidence: Strong (XL quality, now quantified).

The case rests primarily on E4 Activity, and the Pilia (2006) supplementary tables provide decisive quantification:  
\- Pilia S3 (XL): E4 genetic factor loading \= .65 on C, .47 on E. Primary genetic loading is on a different domain than its assignment. This is the only facet in the entire NEO-PI-R whose primary genetic loading is on a different domain.  
\- Pilia S2 (XL): E4 clusters with C4 Achievement Striving at rG \= .775 — one of the highest cross-domain genetic correlations in the entire matrix. C4 is pulled out of the C cluster (Cluster 17: C1, C2, C3, C5, C6) and placed in its own cluster with E4 (Cluster 9).  
\- Pilia S2 (XL): E4 appears in NO E-domain cluster. The E genetic cluster (Cluster 18\) contains E1, E3, E6 — not E4, not E2, not E5.  
\- Yamagata (XL): E4 genetic C cross-loading .48–.54 in three countries — stronger genetically than phenotypically.  
\- Roberts (2006 XL): E4 belongs to Social Vitality, which declines — opposite from Social Dominance (E3).

The boundary redrawing is narrow (one facet) but the evidence is stronger than for the N–C boundary. E4 is genetically a C facet. The finding is not subtle or ambiguous.

Secondary signal: C4 Achievement Striving is pulled from its C-domain genetic cluster and placed with E4 (rG \= .775). C4 also has no heritable specific variance (Jang 1998 L). This suggests C4 and E4 share genetic variance through a cross-domain pathway (possibly related to energetic engagement/vigor) rather than through the C domain factor alone.

\#\#\# 3.3 The N–C Boundary (Impulsivity Zone)  
Strength of evidence: Moderate-to-strong (developmental Tier 1, now with partial genetic confirmation).

N5 Impulsiveness has stronger developmental affinity with C-domain facets than with N-domain facets. The developmental convergence with C6 Deliberation is replicated across three independent studies (Terracciano L, Brandt L, Jackson M). Pilia S3 (XL) provides partial genetic confirmation: N5's genetic loading on reversed-C (−.41) is nearly as large as its loading on N (.45). N5 also appears in zero genetic clusters (Table S2) — it has no rG \> .50 with any other facet in any domain. Yamagata (XL) extends this with three-country genetic loadings: N5 C loading −.33 to −.53.

\[v1 rated this as "developmental Tier 1, genetic evidence circumstantial"; v2 upgraded to "partial genetic confirmation" after Yamagata XL loadings became available. The upgrade is justified: genetic factor loadings from a three-country XL study are substantially stronger evidence than the within-domain hints available in v1.\]

The pairwise rG(N5, C6) remains unavailable — neither appears in any shared cluster, suggesting their pairwise rG \< .50. But N5's genetic factor loading on C (−.41) provides indirect evidence that N5 shares genetic variance with C-domain facets collectively, even without a specific C6 link. The developmental convergence with C6 specifically may reflect shared content overlap within a broader N5–C genetic relationship.

\#\#\# 3.4 The C–A Boundary  
Strength of evidence: Moderate (structural-genetic conflict).

C3 Dutifulness was aspect-orphaned in BFI-2 (XL). BFI-2 Responsibility has .26 secondary loading on A. In 2/3 Swedish samples, Responsibility's between-domain A correlation exceeded within-C (Zakrisson 2025 L). However, C3 loads firmly on genetic C (Yamagata 2006 XL; Jang 2002 L). The C–A overlap is phenotypic/environmental, not genetic.

\#\#\# 3.5 The O Internal Boundary (Intellect vs. Openness)  
Strength of evidence: Strong for the split; ambiguous for the grouping.

Lowest inter-aspect correlation (.29). Clean mapping to different external criteria and HEXACO subdomains. But developmental evidence crosscuts the structural split: {O2, O5} cluster (both stable) despite opposite aspects; {O1, O3, O4} cluster (declining) across aspects. v2 adds: genetic data (Jang 2002 L) shows clean Openness/Intellect genetic split in German sample — the best aspect-level genetic fit of any domain — but O4 cross-culturally unstable and O6 genetically adrift.

\#\#\# 3.6 O is the most structurally questionable domain.  
Lowest aspect correlation (.29). Worst CFA fit of any BFI-2 domain (Zakrisson 2025 L). Two facets excluded from BFI-2. Only metric (not scalar) cross-cultural invariance. The Openness aspect {O2, O3; O1 extended} is well-supported. The Intellect aspect {O5 primary; O4, O6 peripheral} is fragmented.

\#\#\# 3.7 A is the most genetically incoherent domain.  
Lowest cross-national genetic factor congruence (.86 Canada–Japan; Yamagata 2006 XL). Only two facets (A2, A4) are stable genetic A markers. Complete etiological heterogeneity in developmental change (Bleidorn 2009 M). Lowest BFI-2 congruence with US original in Sweden (.75; Zakrisson 2025 L). Schwaba (2025 XL): A has the lowest h²\_SNP, lowest cross-geography rG, lowest cross-instrument rG, and is the only domain without PFC gene expression enrichment.

\#\#\# What the convergence shows

The only pair with confident multi-stream convergence is N1–N3: structural co-placement (Withdrawal) and genetic near-identity (rG=.90). v1's developmental stream dissented (N1 doesn't decline; N3 probably does), but two streams of support with the dissenting stream showing mixed evidence for N3 still yields moderate-high confidence. The developmental dissent is real but can be explained by environmentally mediated differentiation of a shared genetic core (consistent with Røysamb's finding that rE between N1 and N3 is only .53, much lower than rG=.90).

{N1, N3, N4} form the best-supported core grouping. v2's genetic evidence (Jang 2002 L) extends this to {N1, N3, N4, N6} — all four load on the "general distress" genetic factor. N3's phenotypic E cross-loading (−.43) is a tension point but appears environmentally rather than genetically mediated (genetic N3 is clean across all three countries). N4's placement in the core is weakened by its 5-HTTLPR molecular isolation from the other members (Jang 2001 L) — N4 lacks serotonin transporter gene association while N1, N3, and N6 all show it. This means N4 is genetically grouped with the core on the common factor level but molecularly distinct at the candidate-gene level.

N6 is part of this core but with a genetic caveat. Structurally, N6 maps to Withdrawal. Genetically, it loads on the same within-domain factor as N1/N3/N4. But its consistent genetic C cross-loading (−.34 to −.57 across three countries) gives it a dual nature that the other core members lack. Its late-life increase and link to cognitive decline (Terracciano 2005 L) further distinguish it from the stable or declining trajectories of other core members.

N2 is structurally and genetically distinct from the core. It maps to Volatility (structural) and anchors its own within-domain genetic factor while bridging to A (genetic A cross-loading −.32 to −.60 across three countries). The cross-taxonomy evidence confirms this separation: BFAS Volatility (containing N2) maps to reversed HEXACO-A, while BFAS Withdrawal (containing N1/N3/N6) maps to HEXACO-E (Ludeke 2019 L). These are completely different HEXACO domains.

N5 is consistently rejected as a domain member. Structural exclusion (BFI-2) \+ genetic isolation (facet-specific variance, weak N loading, C cross-loading) converge across two independent evidence streams and multiple studies. The convergence is not marginal — every available evidence stream points in the same direction.

\--- Step 5: Domain Coherence \---

Neuroticism is a genetically coherent domain — with one exception.

What holds the domain together:  
\- Five of six facets (N1–N4, N6) load .56–.86 on the genetic N factor across three countries (Yamagata XL).  
\- Within the domain, two genetic factors emerge (Jang 2002 L): one driven by N2 (anger/hostility) and one carrying the remaining core facets (general distress).  
\- Domain-level heritability well-established (\~.41–.50 twin; .08–.16 SNP). Briley & Tucker-Drob (2014 XL): domain-level genetic architecture indistinguishable across Big Five traits.  
\- Within-domain co-development is the highest of any domain (r\_change \= .33; Schwaba 2022 M).  
\- The N1–N3 genetic near-identity (rG=.90) anchors the domain.  
\- Within-domain aspect correlation .61 (Haehner 2025 L) — the highest of any domain.  
\- All six facets declined together over 10 years (Bleidorn 2009 M).

What fractures it:  
\- N5 Impulsiveness is genetically marginal and structurally orphaned; behaves more like a C facet.  
\- N2 bridges N and A genetically.  
\- N6 has a genetic C component.  
\- The within-domain genetic factor structure does not cleanly match the published Volatility/Withdrawal aspect model. The genetic split is "Hostility (N2)" vs. "everything else" — not "Volatility" vs. "Withdrawal." N5 (proposed Volatility member) is absent from the Hostility factor.  
\- \[v1 noted that four of six facets load ambiguously on the two structural factors. v2 confirmed the within-domain genetic factor structure doesn't map to the phenotypic aspect structure.\]

Assessment: Neuroticism is a real domain with a genetically coherent core {N1, N3, N4, N6}, a distinctive anger component {N2} that bridges to A, and one member {N5} that probably doesn't belong. The domain is moderately incoherent at the internal-structure level despite high domain-level coherence. It has the highest within-domain factor correlation (.61) of any Big Five domain, which sounds like good news for coherence — but this high correlation means the two-factor solution is poorly separated, and it masks substantial facet-level heterogeneity.

The Volatility/Withdrawal structural split is phenotypically supported but genetically complicated. The BFI-2 and BFAS evidence clearly separate anxiety/depression/withdrawal content from anger/volatility content at the phenotypic level. But the genetic structure within N does not produce a clean two-factor solution matching this split:  
\- The genetic split is "Hostility (N2)" vs. "everything else" — not "Volatility" vs. "Withdrawal."  
\- N5 (the other proposed Volatility member) is genetically absent from the Hostility factor.  
\- N1 loads on both genetic factors in Canada.  
The {N1, N3} core is the most coherent subunit at the genetic level. Everything else is either noisy (N4), boundary-crossing (N2, N6), or misplaced (N5).

The two corpuses disagree on E2 (flat in v1 vs. declining in v2) and E3/E4 direction (E3 rises in both but E4 declines in v1 vs. increases in v2). The discrepancy likely reflects different age ranges (v1 studies extended past age 60; v2's single study capped at 59\) and different study designs.

  The v1 developmental pattern is more robustly supported because it draws on multiple large studies, but the v2 distinction — that E4 increased in the 18–59 range but declined thereafter — is potentially informative about the age-graded nature of E4 Activity (possibly a different phenomenon in midlife vs. old age).\]

  Clustering B — Structural Mapping Only  
  {E1, E2, E6} \= Enthusiasm. Moderate coherence: E1 is slightly more stable than E2/E6 in some studies, and E6 behaves more like a domain marker than an aspect-specific facet (genetically correlated equally with Enthusiasm- and Assertiveness-adjacent constructs).  
  {E3} \= Assertiveness core. Strong developmental and structural identity but no genetically validated group membership.  
  E4 structurally contested: Assertiveness in DeYoung 2007; Social Vitality in Roberts 2006 (XL); Enthusiasm-adjacent in BFI-2 (Soto & John 2017 XL). Three taxonomies, three different placements.  
  E5 structurally homeless: excluded from BFI-2 (Soto & John 2017 XL). Peripheral loading in DeYoung 2007\. No clean HEXACO equivalent.

  Clustering C — Genetic Evidence Only  
  Two genetic factors exist within E (Jang 2002 L — single-factor model rejected), but their content shifts across cultures. In Germany, Factor 2 resembles Assertiveness; in Canada it does not. No E-internal genetic cluster at rG \> .50 exists (Pilia S2 data). The COMT molecular data (Mitrovic 2024 S) provide the only evidence cleanly supporting the Enthusiasm/Assertiveness split at a biological level: Val+ allele associated with E1, E2, E6 (Enthusiasm) but not E3, E4, E5 (Assertiveness). Weak signal — single candidate gene, small effects.

\#\#\# Summary  
The strongest convergence signal: E2 and E3 are separable across all three streams — developmental, structural, and genetic all converge on separating E2 from E3. E2 is the prototypical Enthusiasm facet (declines/flat, defines sociability genetic factor, COMT-associated). E3 is the prototypical Assertiveness facet (increases, genetically multi-domain, COMT-unassociated). Their dissociation is the cleanest evidence for within-E structure.

The published Enthusiasm grouping {E1, E2, E6}: structural convergence is strong (all three mapped to Enthusiasm); developmental convergence is partial (E1 and E6 cohere as stable, but E2 diverges in v2's data); genetic convergence is weak (they don't load on the same single genetic factor — E2 is Factor 1, E1 and E6 load on both factors).

The published Assertiveness grouping {E3, E4, E5}: achieves zero convergence for E3–E5 and E4–E5 across any stream. E3 and E4 converge developmentally in v2's Bleidorn data (both increase 18–59) and partially genetically in the German sample, but E4's primary genetic signal is its C cross-loading, not its E placement. \[v1 called this "the weakest published aspect grouping in any domain examined." v2 confirmed this assessment with genetic evidence.\] Within-domain co-development r \= .15 (Schwaba 2022 M) suggests even E facets that group together develop somewhat independently.

\#\#\# Proposed Within-Domain Groupings

Group 1: Enthusiasm — {E1, E2, E6} (two-stream convergence: developmental \+ structural).  
Internal coherence: moderate. E1 is slightly more stable than E2/E6 in some studies. E6 behaves more like a general Extraversion domain marker — its age-heterogeneity pattern matches E4 and C (not E1), and its unique genetic correlation with intelligence sets it apart from E1 and E2. E6 appears to be the "positive affect" core of the domain rather than a facet sitting specifically on the Enthusiasm aspect.  
This grouping is real but not tight.

Group 2: Assertiveness-Proper — {E3} (strong developmental \+ structural signal, but it is a singleton).  
E3 is the only facet that consistently increases through midlife. Clean structural mapping to Social Dominance / HEXACO Social Boldness. Cannot form a "group" with its published aspect-mates because those mates behave completely differently on every evidence stream.

Misbehaving facets that resist grouping:  
\- E4 Activity: Structurally and genetically misplaced. Genetically clusters with C (XL quality), developmentally aligns with Social Vitality (declining, not increasing like Social Dominance). Does not belong with E3 on any evidence stream. Its strongest affinity is with C facets (genetic) or very loosely with E2/E6 (developmental direction in v1 data).  
\- E5 Excitement-Seeking: Structurally homeless (excluded from BFI-2). 68–70% facet-specific genetic variance. Phenotypically disconnected from E in external criterion studies (r \= .05 with life satisfaction). Content overlaps more with impulsivity dimensions than with social dominance or warmth. Candidate for domain removal.

\--- Step 4: Misbehaving Facets \---

\#\#\# E4 Activity — genetic C facet with an E phenotype  
E4 is flagged by every study that examines it closely:  
\- Genetic: Primary genetic loading on C (.65) \> E (.47; Pilia S3 XL). Genetic C cross-loading .48–.54 in three countries (Yamagata XL). Clusters genetically with C4 Achievement Striving at rG \= .775 (Pilia S2 XL). Absent from E-domain genetic cluster. Age-heterogeneity pattern matches C (h² higher in young; Pilia XL).  
\- Developmental: Steepest decline of any E facet, accelerating in late life. Only E facet responding to retirement (Löckenhoff 2009 S). Pattern matches Social Vitality (Roberts 2006 XL), not Assertiveness/Social Dominance.  
\- Structural: Classification disagreement across three major taxonomies (Assertiveness in DeYoung; Social Vitality in Roberts; Enthusiasm in BFI-2).  
\- Molecular: Not COMT-associated, unlike Enthusiasm facets (Mitrovic 2024 S). BFI-2 Energy Level pulls toward C in 2/3 Swedish samples (Zakrisson 2025 L).  
\- Measurement concern: Item content ("often feel as if I'm bursting with energy") may increasingly measure physical vitality rather than personality in older adults. Multiple authors flag this (Terracciano 2005, Brandt 2023, Löckenhoff 2009). This confound is unresolved — no study directly tests it (e.g., by controlling for objective health measures).  
Cross-domain affinity: E4 → C. The strongest case for cross-domain reassignment in the corpus.

\#\#\# E5 Excitement-Seeking — structurally peripheral, genetically unique  
Excluded from BFI-2. 68–70% facet-specific genetic variance (Jang 2002 L). Genetic A cross-loading −.43 (Pilia S3 XL). r \= .05 with life satisfaction. Developmental trajectory opposite to aspect-mates. Strongest domain-removal candidate in E.

\#\#\# E3 Assertiveness — genetically multi-domain  
Phenotypically clean Assertiveness marker. But genetically loads on four domains (Yamagata XL). May represent cross-domain "agency" construct.

\#\#\# E1 Warmth — E/A genetic boundary facet  
Genetic A loading .45 alongside E loading .72 (Yamagata XL). Loads on both within-E genetic factors. Phenotypically Enthusiasm, genetically half-A.

\--- Step 5: Domain Coherence \---

Extraversion has a recoverable domain-level genetic factor (congruence .92–.96 across three countries; Yamagata XL), but its internal aspect structure is not genetically stable.

Evidence for domain-level coherence:  
\- Genetic E factor replicates across Canada, Germany, Japan (Yamagata XL).  
\- Common pathways model fits E genetically (Briley 2012 M; Johnson & Krueger 2004).  
\- All E facets load ≥.45 on genetic E (Yamagata 2006).  
\- Highest domain-level rank-order stability in children (r \= .72; Brandes 2020 L).

Evidence against clean aspect-level structure:  
\- Two genetic factors exist but shift content across cultures (Jang 2002 L).  
\- No E-internal genetic cluster at rG \> .50 (Pilia S2).  
\- E4's genetic signal is dominated by its C cross-loading.  
\- E5 is 68–70% genetically unique.  
\- E3 is genetically spread across four domains.  
\- Haehner (2025 L): Enthusiasm–Assertiveness r \= .49; diverge on political orientation.  
\- Enthusiasm aspect had poor unidimensional CFA fit in German (CFI \= .74).

Assessment: Extraversion's domain-level factor is genetically real and cross-culturally stable. The two-aspect structure has modest structural support, one piece of molecular evidence (COMT, S quality), and no stable genetic support across cultures. The strongest within-E signal is the E2–E3 dissociation (sociability vs. assertiveness) — the rest is noise, boundary-crossing, or facet-specific variance.

The best-supported structure from the evidence base is:  
\- A core Sociability/Warmth group {E1, E2} with moderate coherence.  
\- A Positive Emotionality domain marker {E6} that belongs to E but not specifically to either aspect.  
\- An Assertiveness/Social Dominance singleton {E3} with strong developmental identity but no genetically validated group membership.  
\- A Physical Vitality/Energy facet {E4} with strong cross-domain affinity to C.  
\- A Sensation-Seeking facet {E5} that is structurally peripheral to E.

This is not a clean two-aspect structure. It somewhat resembles the BFI-2's three-facet solution (Sociability, Energy Level, Assertiveness as three separate facets rather than two aspects). The evidence that independently supports a three-way rather than two-way split: (a) developmental trajectories don't cleanly bifurcate into two groups, (b) genetic evidence supports a single common factor plus specifics rather than two aspect-level factors, (c) cross-taxonomy mapping shows three distinguishable content clusters. The evidence against: (a) E6 doesn't fit cleanly into any of the three, (b) factor-analytic evidence consistently yields two intermediate factors, not three.

\--- Step 6: Evidence Gaps \---

\- Developmental evidence severely underpowered in v2 corpus (only Bleidorn 2009 M). v1 had richer developmental data (Terracciano L, Brandt L).  
\- No within-E genetic correlation matrix. Jang 2002 provides factor loadings but not pairwise rG.  
\- Cross-cultural instability of E's genetic Factor 2 unresolved.  
\- No study tests whether two-aspect genetic models fit E better than one-factor.  
\- COMT finding (Mitrovic 2024\) is unreplicated.  
\- No genetic data on E5 beyond univariate h²; rG(E5, N5) untested.  
\- E4 Activity measurement confound unresolved.  
\- E6 structural status unresolved (Enthusiasm facet vs. general E marker).  
\- No adolescent developmental data at NEO-PI-R facet level.

\#\#\# What the convergence shows

The only pairing with multi-stream support is {A2, A4} — they cluster on developmental trajectories (both linear increase), structural placement (both Politeness), and genetic factor (both define the genetic A core; v2). They are the core of whatever "Politeness" is. All other pairings are supported by at most one or two evidence streams.

The published Compassion aspect {A1, A3, A6} has essentially no multi-stream convergence. A1 and A3 are domain markers that dual-load, A6 is the sole clean Compassion marker, and genetically A1 and A6 are dissimilar (Jang 2001 L). This is a structurally supported grouping that receives no developmental or genetic reinforcement.

The published Politeness aspect {A2, A4, A5} has structural convergence, and v2 adds genetic convergence (both A2 and A5 load on the Politeness genetic factor). But A5 breaks from the group developmentally (flat while A2 and A4 increase) and shows multiple genetic anomalies (reversed MZ/DZ, cross-cultural instability). The tight core is {A2, A4}; A5 is a questionable member.

\#\#\# Proposed Within-Domain Groupings

1\. {A2, A4} — the behavioral-compliance core.  
Three-stream convergence (v2). Both increase developmentally. Both structural Politeness markers. Both define genetic A-factor core. Tightest unit in the domain. Caveat: A4 loads genetically on the Compassion-like subfactor in Jang 2002 (L, German data), but Yamagata 2006 (XL) five-factor model puts A4 cleanly on A. Weight the XL finding higher.

2\. {A3, A6} — phenotypic Compassion, genetically weak.  
Structural Compassion grouping (Soto & John 2017 XL). No developmental convergence (A3 flat, A6 increases). Genetically they don't cluster — A3 bridges to C/E (.34–.36 C cross-loading in three countries), A6 is 56–58% facet-specific.

3\. A1 Trust — domain marker / N-boundary facet.  
Structurally orphaned (dual-loads .42/.42; standalone in BFI-2). Genetically split between A and low-N (in Canada, N loading −.59 exceeds A loading .49). Orphaned from all genetic clusters (Pilia S2). Only A facet with genetic influence on developmental change (Bleidorn 2009 M). Developmental: inverted-U with late-life decline, unlike other A facets.

4\. A5 Modesty — culturally variable Politeness marker.  
Clean Politeness marker structurally in Western samples but genetic A loading drops to .23 in Japan (Yamagata 2006 XL). Developmentally inert. Excluded from BFI-2. May not be a personality "trait" in the endogenous sense — reversed MZ/DZ correlations and cross-cultural heritability instability suggest it may be more culturally shaped than genetically driven.

Self-check on pattern-matching: The grouping isolates A5 Modesty, consistent with published critiques of the Politeness aspect. Independent evidence supporting A5's isolation: (1) flat trajectory while A2 and A4 increase (Terracciano L, Schwaba M); (2) no heritable specific variance (Jang 1998 L); (3) reversed MZ/DZ correlations (Jang 1996 M); (4) cross-cultural heritability instability (Jang 1998 L); (5) genetic loading drops to .23 in Japan (Yamagata 2006 XL). Evidence against: its structural loading on Politeness is clean (DeYoung 2007), and it loads on the Politeness genetic factor (Jang 2002 L). One evidence stream (structural) says it belongs; two-three streams (developmental, genetic stability, twin pattern) say it doesn't.

\--- Step 5: Domain Coherence \---

Agreeableness is the least internally coherent of the Big Five.

Genetic incoherence: Schwaba (2025 XL): lowest h²\_SNP, lowest cross-geography rG, lowest cross-instrument rG, only domain without PFC gene expression enrichment. Cross-national genetic congruence \= .86 Canada–Japan (Yamagata XL; lowest of any domain). A5 drops to .23 genetic loading in Japan; A4 drops to .43.

Developmental incoherence: Most variable rank-order stability (Brandt 2023 L). Weakest within-domain co-development (r\_change \= .21; Schwaba 2022 M). Every changing A facet has a different change etiology (Bleidorn 2009 M).

Structural ambiguity: Two of six facets (A1, A3) are domain markers dual-loading on both aspects. DeYoung (2007) noted no good NEO markers for Compassion specifically. BFI-2 resolved by making Trust standalone and redefining Compassion with non-NEO items.

Within-aspect incoherence: Neither published aspect holds together across evidence streams.  
\- Compassion {A1, A3, A6}: A1 and A6 are genetically dissimilar (Jang 2001 L); A1 dual-loads structurally and is genetically more N than A in Canada; A3 has no heritable specific variance and is genetically diffuse. This "aspect" has essentially no multi-stream convergence — it is a structurally supported grouping that receives no developmental or genetic reinforcement.  
\- Politeness {A2, A4, A5}: A5 dissociates from A2 and A4 on both developmental trajectory (flat vs. increasing) and genetic profile (no heritable specificity, cross-cultural instability, reversed twin correlations). The tight core is {A2, A4} only.

What holds together: {A2, A4} is the tight core — the only pairing in the domain with three-stream convergence (v2). Beyond that, the domain is more a loose confederation than a structured hierarchy. The Compassion/Politeness distinction is real as a structural observation (the two aspects land in different HEXACO domains — Emotionality vs. Agreeableness), but it maps poorly onto the NEO facets that are supposed to populate it. Compassion–Politeness r \= .34 (Haehner 2025 L) — lower than all other within-domain aspect pairs except Openness–Intellect (.29).

Self-check on pattern-matching: The finding that A is the least coherent domain is consistent with some published critiques of Big Five Agreeableness. Evidence independently supporting this assessment: (a) Schwaba 2025 XL GWAS molecular-genetic data showing A is the genetic outlier among the Big Five; (b) Yamagata 2006 XL showing A has the lowest cross-national genetic congruence; (c) Jang 2001 L showing A facets have heterogeneous genetic correlations with N; (d) Brandt 2023 L showing A facets have the most variable rank-order stability. Evidence against: the phenotypic factor structure does replicate cross-culturally, and the BFI-2 three-facet model works at the phenotypic level.

\- No facet-level GWAS.  
\- A2 genetic correlations with other facets not reported.  
\- No longitudinal observer-report data for A.  
\- A5 Modesty: why flat when HH content shows steep increase — unexplained.  
\- No childhood-to-adulthood developmental continuity data.  
\- Within-person co-development of A facets poorly powered (Haehner 2025 CI: −.47 to .86).  
\- No HEXACO-to-NEO genetic correlations.  
\- Non-Western, non-Japanese genetic data absent.  
\- A5 Modesty structural home in three-facet-per-domain models undefined.

  Clustering A — Developmental Trajectories Only  
  v1 (based on Terracciano L, Schwaba M, Brandt L, and others): Three groups:  
  \- Declining: {O1 Fantasy, O3 Feelings, O4 Actions}. All three decline across adulthood, though for different reasons (O1: imaginative decline; O3: affective decline; O4: behavioral/possibly physical-capacity decline).  
  \- Stable/preserved: {O2 Aesthetics, O5 Ideas}. Both stable across the lifespan, replicated in childhood data (Brandes L). This is a cross-aspect developmental convergence — O2 is Openness-aspect, O5 is Intellect-aspect, yet they share a trajectory.  
  \- Unclassifiable: {O6 Values}. Contradictory evidence across studies (Terracciano: strong decline; Brandt/Schwaba: increase). Cannot be assigned a direction.

  v2 (based on Bleidorn 2009 M): Stable {O2, O3, O5, O6} vs. Declining {O1, O4}.  
  Key discrepancy: O3 is classified as declining in v1 but stable in v2. This matters because v2's O3 classification shifts the {O2, O3} pair to three-stream convergence (developmental \+ structural \+ genetic). The discrepancy likely reflects different study designs and age ranges. v1's classification draws on more studies; v2's is from a single longitudinal study with stronger internal validity. Both should be noted.

  Pattern-matching flag: v1 noted that the {O2, O5} developmental pairing does not correspond to either published aspect. The grouping is driven purely by developmental convergence — both are "cognitively/perceptually preserved" content. Whether this reflects a meaningful latent structure or simply that stable-content facets happen to share a trajectory is an open question.

  Clustering B — Structural Mapping Only  
  Openness aspect: {O1 (dual-loads .64/.45 — highest cross-aspect loading in O), O2, O3}. Intellect aspect: {O5 (clean anchor), O4 (weakly — excluded from BFI-2), O6 (variably assigned — excluded from BFI-2)}.  
  The aspect factor correlation (.51 in DeYoung 2007\) is the lowest of any domain, meaning these are the most separable pair of aspects in the Big Five. External criteria cleanly differentiate them: musical sensibility (O2 beta=.44, O1 beta=.07), intelligence (O5 rG=.38, O2 rG=−.05). Ludeke 2019 (L) confirms the split in HEXACO space.

  Clustering C — Genetic Evidence Only  
  Jang 2002 (L): Two genetic factors within O. Factor 1 (Openness-like): O1, O2, O3. Factor 2 (Intellect-like): O4, O5, O6. O5 cross-loads on Factor 1 in the Canadian sample (.52). O6 is mostly independent (51–59% facet-specific). The German sample shows the cleanest aspect-aligned genetic split of any domain — the best match to the later-published aspect model. However, O4 loads 100% on Factor 2 genetically but on Openness phenotypically (a phenotypic-genetic mismatch).

\#\#\# Proposed Within-Domain Groupings

Group 1 — Openness core: {O2, O3}  
Three-stream convergence (v2). Stable developmentally. Clean structural Openness markers. Anchor genetic Openness factor. Most confident grouping in O.

Group 1 extended — {O1 Fantasy}  
Two-stream convergence with O2/O3 (structural \+ genetic). Developmental trajectory (declining) dissociates. Good but imperfect member.

Group 2 — Intellect-adjacent: {O5 Ideas}  
Structural anchor of Intellect aspect. Genetically semi-independent (44–55% specific, cross-loads). Coherent construct with weak domain integration. Stands somewhat alone.

Peripheral / unclassifiable: {O4, O6}

\--- Step 4: Misbehaving Facets \---

\#\#\# O4 Actions — genetically homeless  
O4 is the most structurally and genetically problematic O facet. The evidence is convergent across streams:  
\- Excluded from BFI-2 (Soto & John 2017 XL).  
\- Genetic loading drops from .65 → .43 → .37 across Canada/Germany/Japan (Yamagata 2006 XL). Fails to reach .40 on any genetic factor in Japan. This is the most cross-culturally unstable genetic loading in the entire corpus.  
\- In the Jang 2002 German sample, O4 loads 100% on genetic factor 2 (Intellect-like), zero on factor 1 (Openness-like). This is a clean genetic placement on Intellect — but in phenotypic structure, DeYoung 2007 assigned O4 to Openness (.54 loading). Phenotypic-genetic mismatch.  
\- Declines developmentally while its genetic factor-mates (O5, O6) are stable.  
\- Content (willingness to try new activities, variety-seeking) is more behavioral than cognitive or perceptual. It may be a behavioral-engagement facet at the boundary of O rather than in its core. The same physical-capacity confound flagged for E4 Activity may apply here — items about behavioral variety/adventurousness may increasingly measure physical capacity in older adults.  
Flag for cross-domain synthesis: O4's behavioral content may overlap with E-domain facets (activity, novelty-seeking).

\#\#\# O6 Values — genetically adrift  
O6 has the most dissociation flags of any O facet:  
\- Near-zero implied heritability from twin correlations (Jang 2002 L). MZ correlations (.35/.34) barely exceed DZ (.30/.31).  
\- 51–59% facet-specific genetic variance — most of whatever genetic influence exists is not shared with the domain.  
\- Excluded from BFI-2 (Soto & John 2017 XL).  
\- Weakest structural loading in the domain (.44; DeYoung 2007).  
\- Content (tolerance, open-mindedness, willingness to re-examine values) is social-attitudinal, not cognitive or perceptual — qualitatively different from the rest of the O domain.  
\- Elevated DZ correlations suggest shared environment effects (Jang 2002 L), atypical for adult personality.  
\- Contradictory developmental evidence (Terracciano L: strong decline; Brandt/Schwaba: increase). Cannot be assigned a direction.  
\- Excluded from O genetic cluster (Pilia S2 XL) despite adequate O loading (.70).  
\- Jackson 2009 (M): Conventionality (≈ reverse O6) has a unique late-onset developmental trajectory unlike any standard C facet.  
\- Cross-culturally unstable heritability (Canada h²=.61, Germany h²=.38; Jang 1998 L).  
O6 is the strongest candidate for domain removal or reclassification in the entire O domain. Its content might be better understood as a social-attitude dimension influenced primarily by shared environment.  
Flag for cross-domain synthesis: O6's social-attitudinal content may cluster with A-domain facets. Its unique developmental behavior suggests it may capture a dimension not well-represented in any Big Five domain.

\#\#\# O5 Ideas — coherent construct, weak domain integration  
Clean Intellect anchor. But 44–55% facet-specific genetic variance. Cross-loads on Openness genetic factor in Canada. Aspect correlation \= .29 — nearly independent from Openness. May be housed in O because of phenotypic correlation rather than deep biological connection.

\#\#\# O3 Feelings — content at domain boundary  
Content overlaps N and A. But genetically purest O facet (.81). Developmental stability (v2) places it with O2. Not misplaced by evidence despite content concerns.

\#\#\# O1 Fantasy — split allegiances  
Highest cross-aspect structural loading (.64/.45). Intelligence rG aligns with Intellect. Declines like Openness facets. Sits at the boundary in every stream but doesn't consistently lean one direction.

\--- Step 5: Domain Coherence \---

Openness has the clearest within-domain structural split of any Big Five domain — but the two sides are unequal in support.

The Openness aspect {O1, O2, O3} has multi-stream convergence:  
\- Structural: clean loadings.  
\- Genetic: anchor the genetic Openness factor (Jang 2002 L; Yamagata 2006 XL).  
\- The {O2, O3} core achieves three-stream convergence (v2).  
\- Aspect correlation with Intellect \= .29 (Haehner 2025 L).  
\- External correlates diverge sharply from Intellect.

The Intellect aspect {O4, O5, O6} is fragmented:  
\- Only O5 is a clean structural marker.  
\- O4 and O6 excluded from BFI-2.  
\- Genetically: O4 cross-culturally unstable, O5 cross-loads, O6 mostly independent.  
\- No pairing within {O4, O5, O6} achieves more than one-stream convergence.

The domain as a whole: Genetic five-factor structure replicates but with the lowest factor congruence (.87 in Pilia; weakest O4 loadings in Yamagata). Worst CFA fit of any BFI-2 domain (Zakrisson 2025 L). Only metric (not scalar) cross-cultural invariance.

The developmental evidence crosscuts the structural aspects: the developmental fault line is {O2, O5 stable} vs. {O1, O3/O4 declining}, which doesn't follow the structural boundary at all. O2 (Openness aspect) clusters developmentally with O5 (Intellect aspect), not with its structural aspect-mate O1. The genetic evidence mostly supports the structural split (German data is the best aspect-level genetic match of any domain; Jang 2002 L) but O4 and O6 are unstable members, and O5 cross-loads in Canada.

Comparison to other domains:  
\- N had one confident pairing ({N1, N3}) with strong convergence (structural \+ genetic \+ partial developmental). O has nothing comparable — its three-stream pair {O2, O3} depends on v2's developmental classification of O3 as stable.  
\- E had one decent grouping ({E1, E2, E6}) with two-stream convergence. O's Openness core {O2, O3, O1} achieves similar two-stream support (structural \+ genetic) with developmental tension.  
\- A was the weakest domain. O is better — it has a clear structural split and identifiable developmental groups, even if they don't align.  
\- C had moderate coherence but no three-stream pairings. O is similar.

O is in the middle of the pack for domain coherence. The structural Intellect/Openness distinction is among the best-documented within-domain splits in the Big Five. But it is supported primarily by one evidence stream (structural mapping \+ external criteria). The other two streams either contradict it (development) or partially support it with complications (genetics).

Assessment: O is a domain with one well-supported aspect (Openness: {O2, O3} core, O1 extended) and one fragmented aspect (Intellect: O5 stands alone, O4/O6 peripheral). The two aspects are the most separable in the Big Five (r \= .29), which is both the strongest evidence that the split is real and a warning that these might be better understood as two weakly related constructs sharing a domain label than as two halves of a coherent whole.

\--- Step 6: Evidence Gaps \---

\#\#\# Critical:  
\- No full within-O genetic correlation matrix (Jang 2002 has factor loadings, not pairwise rG).  
\- No facet-level GWAS (Schwaba 2025 is domain-level).  
\- Developmental evidence thin — only Bleidorn 2009 (M) in v2 corpus for all six O facets.  
\- No cross-domain genetic models for O facets with N/C/A facets.  
\- O6 Values heritability needs resolution (near-zero from Jang 2002; elevated DZ correlations; replication needed).  
\- O6 developmental trajectory unresolved (Terracciano vs. Brandt/Schwaba contradiction).

\#\#\# Moderate:  
\- BFI-2 Creative Imagination ≠ NEO Fantasy (r \= .38).  
\- HEXACO genetic structure for O underresolved (Lewis & Bates 2014 M: 2–3 items per facet, precision too low).  
\- No facet-level molecular data beyond COMT null finding (Mitrovic 2024 S).  
\- No within-family heritability at facet level (Schwaba 2025 found domain-level O has unique pop vs. within-family discrepancy).  
\- Limited developmental data for ages 18–30 and 60+.  
\- No observer-rated data.  
\- No data on: O facet × non-O facet genetic correlations (except O × intelligence from Nikolasevic S), O facet responses to life events (none responded to retirement), whether Openness/Intellect factor correlation varies by age/sex/culture, item-level genetic architecture, or differential heritability by sex.

\#\# Conscientiousness Domain Synthesis (C1–C6) — Merged

\--- Step 1: Facet Profiles (dissociation flags only) \---

  C1 Competence: Genetically bridges C and low N. Yamagata (2006 XL): N cross-loading −.48 to −.53 in Canada/Germany — the largest of any C facet. Three-country replication. Shares genetic variance with IQ (Luciano 2006 M: \~2% shared variance; Nikolasevic 2021 S: rG \= \+0.16; replicated). No other C facet shows this. Residual heritability barely above zero (.11; Jang 1998 L) — lowest of any C facet. Wellbeing profile resembles N/E more than C (Røysamb 2018 M). Developmental trajectory (peak-and-decline) differs from C4 (flat) and C5 (late-life decline). Change more environmental than C4/C5 (Bleidorn 2009: a² \= 6 vs. C4's 13, C5's 11). Shares COMT association with C4 but not C5 (Mitrovic 2024 S).

  C2 Order: Developmentally flat while Orderliness aspect-mates (C3, C6) increase — most consistent within-Orderliness dissociation (replicated across 5+ studies). Strongly negative genetic correlation with intelligence (rG \= −0.28; Nikolasevic S) while C3 is near zero and C6 mildly negative. Heritability massively unstable across samples (Jang 2002 L: .08 Canada, .70 Germany). Moderate heritable specificity (.26; Jang 1998 L).

  C3 Dutifulness: The straddler — straddles Industriousness/Orderliness boundary. DeYoung (2007) loadings .63/.50 (closest to equal of any C facet). Soto & John (2017 XL) pulled it out as standalone "Responsibility." Highest genetic specificity of any C facet (.65 proportion of reliable variance; Jang 1998 L). Genetically bridges C and A: Yamagata (2006 XL) A cross-loading in 2/3 samples; Zakrisson (2025 L) C/A dual-loading. Shares D variance with C1 (Luciano 2006 M). Only C facet with CE model in Bleidorn (2009). Genetically firmly C despite phenotypic A overlap.

  C4 Achievement Striving: Most genetically independent C facet (41–43% facet-specific; Jang 2002 L). No heritable specific variance (CE residual; Jang 1998 L). Developmentally flat — resists maturation. Shares COMT association with C1 (Mitrovic 2024 S). Pulled from C-domain genetic cluster by Pilia S2 (XL) and placed with E4 at rG \= .775.

  C5 Self-Discipline: Strongest Industriousness marker phenotypically (.86 loading). But genetically clusters with Orderliness in German data, not Industriousness (Jang 2002 L) — strongest genetic challenge to published C aspect assignments. Not COMT-associated (unlike C1/C4). Heritability massively unstable (Canada .18, Germany .62; Jang 2002 L). Inverted-U developmental trajectory with late-life decline. Moderate heritable specificity (.28; Jang 1998 L). May phenotypically express as "getting things done" while genetically driven by "behavioral regulation."

  C6 Deliberation: Diverges dramatically from Orderliness aspect-mates in development — steepest increase (+1.70 T/decade, Terracciano L; Impulse Control d13 \= 0.88, Jackson M). Replicated across 5+ studies. Stronger developmental affinity with N5 (different domain) than with C2 (same aspect). Genetic architecture diverges: only C facet without age-related heritability decline (Pilia 2006 XL); no heritable specific variance (CE residual; Jang 1998 L). HEXACO Prudence (≈ C6 content) genetically independent of other C facets (Lewis & Bates 2014 M). Most genetically unstable C facet across samples (Jang 2002, Yamagata 2006). Impulse-control content may blend planfulness and impulse inhibition that are developmentally and genetically dissociable.

\--- Step 2: Clusterings (verdicts only) \---

  Clustering A — Developmental Trajectories Only  
  Three developmental patterns emerge, and they crosscut the Industriousness/Orderliness aspect boundary:  
  \- Flat/inert: {C2 Order, C4 Achievement Striving}. Both resist normative C maturation. C2's flatness is replicated across 5+ studies. C4 is less well-documented but consistently shows minimal change. These are in different aspects (C2 \= Orderliness, C4 \= Industriousness).  
  \- Increasing: {C3 Dutifulness, C6 Deliberation}. Both increase across adulthood, though with very different magnitudes. C6's increase is the steepest developmental signal in the C domain (+1.70 T/decade; Terracciano L). C3's trajectory is less clear — studies disagree (Schwaba: steep increase; Brandt: null; Terracciano: modest then decline). C3 is dual-loading; C6 is primarily Orderliness.  
  \- Peak-and-decline: {C1 Competence, C5 Self-Discipline}. Both show inverted-U trajectories with late-life decline. Both involve self-evaluative content that may peak mid-life. Both are Industriousness facets. This is the one developmental grouping that aligns with the published aspect structure — but even here, C4 (also Industriousness) is flat rather than inverted-U.

  Clustering B — Structural Mapping Only  
  Industriousness: {C1 (boundary — correlates broadly across facets), C4, C5}. C4 and C5 have the highest within-domain phenotypic intercorrelation (r \= .67). C1 is the boundary member.  
  Orderliness: {C2, C3 (boundary — dual-loading .63/.50), C6}. C2 is the purest Orderliness marker. C3 was spun off as standalone "Responsibility" in BFI-2. C6 loads .66 on Orderliness.

  Clustering C — Genetic Evidence Only  
  v2 (Jang 2002 L, primarily German data): Two genetic factors emerge. Factor 1: {C2, C3, C5, C6} — the "behavioral regulation/constraint" cluster. Factor 2: {C1, C4} — the "proactive achievement" cluster. The critical finding: C5 Self-Discipline, the strongest Industriousness marker phenotypically (.86 loading), loads genetically with Orderliness-side facets. This is the strongest genetic challenge to the published C aspect assignments.  
  v1 (from Jang 1998 L residual models): A third crosscutting pattern based on heritable specificity: facets with heritable specific variance {C2, C3, C5} vs. facets whose genetics come entirely from the domain factor {C1, C4, C6}. This grouping does not map to either the aspect structure or the developmental clustering — it is a third independent dimension of variation within C.

\--- Step 4: Misbehaving Facets \---

\#\#\# C5 Self-Discipline — genetically misassigned  
C5 presents the single most important facet misalignment in the C domain. It is the strongest Industriousness marker phenotypically (.86 loading) but genetically clusters with Orderliness-side facets in the German sample (Jang 2002 L). Not COMT-associated (unlike its putative Industriousness grouping-mates C1 and C4; Mitrovic 2024 S). C5 may phenotypically express as "getting things done" (Industriousness content) while being genetically driven by "behavioral regulation" (Orderliness-like genetic architecture). The discrepancy between phenotypic expression and genetic architecture is the most dramatic in C. The Jang 2002 Canadian data is ambiguous for C5 — the German finding needs replication in a third independent sample.

\#\#\# C3 Dutifulness — the straddler  
Most structurally misplaced C facet. Dual-loading (.63/.50). BFI-2 standalone "Responsibility." Highest genetic specificity. Shares D factor with C1 across aspects. Genetically bridges C and A (Yamagata 2006 XL A cross-loading; Zakrisson 2025 L). Belongs to C but not cleanly to either aspect.

\#\#\# C6 Deliberation — cross-domain developmental affinity  
C6's developmental trajectory (steep monotonic increase) has more in common with the inverse of N5 Impulsiveness (N domain) than with its Orderliness aspect-mates C2 (flat) and C3 (modest increase). Three independent studies confirm the C6–N5 developmental convergence: Terracciano (2005 L), Brandt (2023 L), Jackson (2009 M — Impulse Control d13 \= 0.88 vs. Orderliness d13 \= 0.07). C6 also has: a unique genetic age profile (only C facet without age-related heritability decline; Pilia 2006 XL); no heritable specific variance (CE residual; Jang 1998 L); and maximal genetic instability across samples (Jang 2002, Yamagata 2006). HEXACO Prudence (≈ C6 content) is genetically independent of other C facets (zero common genetic factor loading; Lewis & Bates 2014 M). NEO C6 items may blend two distinct functions — planfulness and impulse inhibition — that are developmentally and genetically dissociable. C6 is an Orderliness facet by factor loading, but its developmental and genetic behavior is more aligned with impulse-control content that crosses the N–C boundary.

\#\#\# C1 Competence — the cognitive overlap  
Genetic N cross-loading −.48 to −.53 in three countries (Yamagata XL). Shares genetic variance with IQ (replicated). Near-zero residual heritability. Wellbeing profile resembles N/E. Self-evaluative content overlaps O/Intellect boundary.

\#\#\# C2 Order and C4 Achievement Striving — the developmentally inert  
Both resist maturation despite being in different aspects. Neither has strong cross-domain signal. Well-behaved domain members that simply don't change.

\--- Step 5: Domain Coherence \---

Domain-level coherence: Moderate.

What holds together:  
\- Genetic C factor replicates across three countries with high congruence (.93–.97; Yamagata 2006 XL). All six facets load primarily on genetic C.  
\- Moderate within-domain co-development (r\_change \= .31; Schwaba 2022 M — second-highest after N).  
\- Domain-level heritability consistent (\~.44–.49).

What fractures:  
\- Published Industriousness/Orderliness split not recovered genetically. C5 on wrong side. Genetic split closer to {C1, C4} vs. {C2, C3, C5, C6}.  
\- Industriousness–Orderliness r \= .39 (Haehner 2025 L). Industriousness correlates more strongly with Withdrawal (−.54) than with Orderliness. External correlates diverge sharply (LS: .44 vs .14; SE: .48 vs .13).  
\- Three of six C facets are boundary facets (C1 → N/C, C3 → C/A, C6 → unstable/content-heterogeneous).  
\- Common pathway model rejected for C (Lewis & Bates 2014 M; converges with Jang 2002 requiring two genetic factors).  
\- Developmental trajectories split three ways and crosscut aspects.  
\- Briley & Tucker-Drob (2012 M) rejected common pathways model — genetic effects on C facets cannot be represented through a single latent variable.

One important framing note: the domain-level coherence statistic (Schwaba's within-domain r\_change \= .31) is moderate and real. Facets within C co-develop more than facets across domains. The domain is not arbitrary. But the internal structure — how facets relate to each other within C — is not well-captured by a single split. The best-supported structure may involve no clean binary partition at all: {C1, C4} form a genetic/structural dyad, {C2} is a pure Orderliness anchor, {C3} straddles both aspects, {C5} is phenotypically Industriousness but genetically Orderliness, and {C6} is a cross-domain impulse-control construct.

Assessment: C is a phenotypically coherent domain with a genetically complex internal structure that does not match its phenotypic aspect assignments. The two-aspect model is a useful structural simplification but misplaces C5 genetically and cannot account for C3's orphan status or C6's content heterogeneity. External correlates underscore the asymmetry: Industriousness carries the domain's psychological weight (wellbeing, self-esteem) while Orderliness contributes little (LS: .44 vs .14; SE: .48 vs .13; Haehner 2025 L).

\--- Step 6: Evidence Gaps \---

\- No bivariate genetic correlations between C facets. The pairwise rG between C5 and C2 vs. C5 and C4 would directly test C5's genetic home.  
\- No targeted aspect-level genetic model.  
\- C6–N5 genetic overlap not tested (developmental convergence well-documented but no rG).  
\- Longitudinal NEO-PI-R data limited (Bleidorn 2009 N \= 344; disagreement between Bleidorn, Jackson, and other sources on C2 and C3 trajectories).  
\- No observer-report genetic data. C1's genetic N cross-loading could reflect self-evaluation bias.  
\- Jang (2002) Canadian vs. German discrepancy for C5 unresolved — third independent sample needed.  
\- C3 Dutifulness developmental trajectory inconsistent across studies (Schwaba: steep increase; Brandt: null; Terracciano: modest then decline).  
\- C1 × O5 bivariate genetic model untested (would test C1–Intellect overlap).  
\- No data on C6 × N5 genetic correlations.  
\- Childhood C development represented by one study only (Brandes 2020, non-NEO instrument).
