# Factor Whac-a-Mole

The *Pan* personality literature contains a puzzle that is usually treated as a methodological nuisance but which the resolution mechanism reframes as the primary finding.

The same 54-item Hominoid Personality Questionnaire has been administered to chimpanzees in US zoos (King & Figueredo, 1997; N=100), Japanese zoos (Weiss et al., 2009; N=157), US research laboratories (Weiss et al., 2007, N=175 at Yerkes; Freeman et al., 2013, N=99 at MD Anderson), and in the wild at Gombe (Weiss et al., 2017; N=128). The same instrument has been administered to captive bonobos across 16 US and European facilities (Weiss et al., 2015; N=154) and to wild bonobos at Wamba (Garai et al., 2016; N=16). In every case, six factors are extracted. In no two cases is the factor structure identical.

What “shows up” means: some factors replicate cleanly across populations — the same items coalesce, load in the same direction, and achieve congruence coefficients (Tucker’s φ) above .85 when one population’s solution is rotated toward another’s. Other factors fail congruence: the items still exist, they still vary across individuals, but they distribute across different factors rather than cohering as a distinct dimension. The factor doesn’t vanish from the species. It dissolves into the other factors, like a dye dispersing into water.

Here is what happens, population by population:

**Dominance** emerges cleanly in zoo chimpanzees, lab chimpanzees, and wild chimpanzees. It is the most stable chimpanzee factor. In bonobos, it is relabeled **Assertiveness** and its composition shifts: the top loaders become reversed anxiety (−.81), reversed timidity (−.76), and reversed fearfulness (−.75), with independence (.71) and dominant (.68) loading alongside them. Weiss et al. noted this maps better onto the Assertiveness facet of human Extraversion than onto competitive dominance. In wild bonobos, no clear Dominance/Assertiveness factor emerged at all — though the sample (N=16) was too small for reliable factor analysis. The sex difference reverses across species: males score higher in chimpanzees, females score higher in bonobos.

**Neuroticism** emerges in zoo chimpanzees but not in lab chimpanzees, where its items merge into a broader Reactivity/Undependability dimension. It does not emerge in bonobos at all — captive or wild. In bonobos, the items that define chimpanzee Neuroticism (anxious, fearful, vulnerable, excitable) load as reversed Assertiveness; “depressed” loads as reversed Extraversion. The trait content is present and varies. The factor does not cohere.

**Attentiveness** emerges only in bonobos. Its defining items — intelligent (.68), not disorganized (−.71), not clumsy (−.68), not thoughtless (−.68), not distractible (−.59) — achieve congruence coefficients of .66 to .79 against all three chimpanzee reference samples, uniformly below the .85 threshold. In chimpanzees, these same items scatter across Dominance (intelligent, decisive, perceptive) and Conscientiousness (organized, not clumsy). The items covary in chimpanzees. They do not coalesce.

**Extraversion** exists in both species but is compositionally reorganized. In chimpanzees, it loads on active, playful, and sociable. In bonobos, “active” and “playful” migrate to Openness, and Extraversion is defined primarily by the absence of social withdrawal: not solitary (−.67), not depressed (−.67). Congruence coefficients range from .69 (against Yerkes chimps) to .85 (against ChimpanZoo) — borderline at best.

**Conscientiousness, Openness, and Agreeableness** are the stable core. They achieve fair-to-good congruence across most cross-species comparisons and replicate within chimpanzees across zoo, lab, and wild populations.

The standard interpretation treats the unstable factors as measurement problems — captive environments distort expression, sample sizes are too small, or the instrument is anthropocentrically biased. These concerns are real. But the pattern is also systematic: *which* factor fails to cohere is not random. Neuroticism dissolves in bonobos and lab chimps but coheres in zoo chimps. Attentiveness coheres in bonobos but dissolves in chimps. Dominance coheres strongly in chimps, shifts composition in captive bonobos, and disappears in wild bonobos. The instability tracks something about the ecology of each population, not just the sample size.

The resolution mechanism explains this directly. The four-primary architecture is always present. The three-way intersections are always structurally available. But a three-way intersection only separates from the Domesticated center — only becomes statistically recoverable as a distinct factor — when the complementary primary’s boundary is ecologically sharpened. In populations where that primary isn’t under pressure, the three-way intersection’s items don’t disappear. They disperse back into the adjacent two-way domains and the center, producing the characteristic pattern: items present, variance present, factor absent.

This is not whac-a-mole as failure. It is whac-a-mole as signal.

# The Ape Stack: Ecology, Need, and Factor Resolution

## **Architecture**

The model is a four-primary Venn diagram operating at the ape level (Systems 0.2, 0.3, 1, and 2). The primaries are **Boldness** (System 0.2, bilaterian approach/avoid), **Mastery** (System 0.3, vertebrate reinforcement learning), **Affiliation** (System 1, endotherm attachment), and **Autonomy** (System 2, primate mentalizing/metacognition). These are arranged in a ring topology where only adjacent pairs form pairwise intersections.

Pairwise overlaps produce six two-way domains:

* Boldness × Mastery \= Conscientiousness  
* Boldness × Affiliation \= Core Self-Concept  
* Mastery × Affiliation \= Agreeableness  
* Affiliation × Autonomy \= Extraversion  
* Autonomy × Boldness \= Openness  
* Autonomy × Mastery \= Strategic Self-Concept

Three-way overlaps produce four domains, each defined by the primary they *lack*:

* Missing Autonomy \= **Neurotic**  
* Missing Affiliation \= **Impulsive**  
* Missing Mastery \= **Dominant**  
* Missing Boldness \= **Deceptive**

The four-way center — where all four primaries overlap — is **Domesticated** (the self-domestication syndrome: suppressed reactive aggression with all four systems coordinated simultaneously).

### **Containment**

Each two-way domain contains exactly two three-way domains (those that share both its primaries) plus the Domesticated center:

* Conscientiousness contains Impulsive, Neurotic, Domesticated  
* Agreeableness contains Neurotic, Deceptive, Domesticated  
* Extraversion contains Deceptive, Dominant, Domesticated  
* Openness contains Dominant, Impulsive, Domesticated  
* Core Self-Concept contains Dominant, Neurotic, Domesticated  
* Strategic Self-Concept contains Deceptive, Impulsive, Domesticated

## **The Resolution Mechanism**

The circles in the Venn diagram do not move across ecologies. The architecture is fixed. What changes is *which primary’s boundary becomes clearly defined*.

When ecological pressure creates a high need for a given primary, that primary’s circle sharpens — its boundary becomes crisp. One consequence: the three-way intersection that lives just *outside* that primary also becomes clearly differentiated from the Domesticated center, because the sharpened boundary now separates it.

When the need is low, the boundary is fuzzy, and the corresponding three-way factor bleeds back into the undifferentiated center.

This means ecology does not create new factors or rearrange the architecture. It modulates the *statistical resolution* of factors that are always structurally present. The mechanism is counterintuitive: you’d naively expect high mastery to sharpen Conscientiousness. Instead, high mastery sharpens *Dominant* — because mastery pulling away from the center is what gives the remaining three-way blend (Boldness × Affiliation × Autonomy, absent Mastery) its own identity.

## **Generational Archetypes and Pan Personality**

The Worldliness framework describes a four-phase generational cycle rotating around the ring topology. Each phase is defined by one primary being high and its neighbor being low. Each phase maps onto a specific three-way personality factor that should emerge most clearly under that ecology — and onto findings from the *Pan* (chimpanzee and bonobo) personality literature.

### **1\. Reactive Nomads (high Mastery, low Affiliation) → Dominance**

**Generational archetype:** Coalitionary competition, hierarchical enforcement, resource control. High mastery need sharpens the mastery boundary. The three-way factor missing mastery — **Dominant** (Boldness × Affiliation × Autonomy) — differentiates from the center.

**Pan evidence:** Dominance is the most robust species-differentiating personality factor in chimpanzees. The foundational six-factor chimpanzee structure (King & Figueredo, 1997; N=100 zoo chimpanzees) placed Dominance alongside the human Big Five, and it has replicated across Japanese zoo populations (Weiss et al., 2009; N=157), laboratory populations (Freeman et al., 2013; N=99), and wild Gombe chimpanzees (Weiss et al., 2017; N=128). Behavioral validation is strong: Dominance predicts display, intervention, and proximity behaviors in captive chimps, and in wild chimpanzees at Gombe, higher Dominance and lower Conscientiousness predict rank, with Dominance predicting siring success.

Chimpanzee ecology is defined by male coalitionary competition. Males participated in a mean of 74.5% of border patrols at Gombe over 30 years, and coalitionary aggression targeted females in 13.2% of cases compared to just 1% in bonobos. Chimpanzees are more risk-prone than bonobos across multiple studies, more patient in delayed-reward tasks, and show superior spatial memory — a cognitive profile consistent with a mastery-sharpened ecology where tracking resources, rivals, and coalitional debts is fitness-relevant.

The sex difference in Dominance is telling: males score substantially higher than females in chimpanzees, directly mapping onto male-dominated hierarchical competition. This reverses entirely in bonobos (see below).

### **2\. Civic Heroes (high Affiliation, low Autonomy) → Impulsive/Attentive**

**Generational archetype:** Prosocial coordination, egalitarian structure, group cohesion prioritized over individual self-governance. High affiliation need sharpens the affiliation boundary. The three-way factor missing affiliation — **Impulsive** (Boldness × Mastery × Autonomy) — differentiates from the center.

**Pan evidence:** The bonobo personality structure (Weiss et al., 2015; N=154 captive bonobos from 16 facilities, \~80% of the captive population) identified six factors, of which the most species-distinctive is **Attentiveness** — defined by intelligent (.68), not disorganized (−.71), not clumsy (−.68), not thoughtless (−.68), and not distractible (−.59). This factor has no chimpanzee analogue: congruence coefficients across all three chimpanzee reference samples were uniformly below .80, making it the cleanest species-differentiating factor in the dataset.

In chimpanzees, the items that define Attentiveness distribute across Dominance (intelligent, decisive, perceptive) and Conscientiousness (organized, not clumsy). The items vary in chimpanzees but do not coalesce into a distinct dimension. Weiss et al. interpreted this as human Conscientiousness splitting into two factors in bonobos: one capturing deliberation and low impulsive aggression (Conscientiousness) and one capturing order, focus, and self-discipline (Attentiveness). The model reinterprets this: Attentive is the *wild-type* expression of the Impulsive factor — what Impulsive looks like when it differentiates under affiliative rather than competitive pressure.

Bonobo ecology fits the civic-hero template. Female bonobos achieve higher rank through coalition formation and occupy more central social positions. The Dominance/Assertiveness sex reversal is dramatic: females score higher than males in bonobos, directly inverting the chimpanzee pattern. The bonobo factor labeled “Assertiveness” (top loaders: anxious \[−.81\], timid \[−.76\], fearful \[−.75\], independent \[.71\], dominant \[.68\]) more closely resembles reversed social withdrawal than competitive dominance — Weiss et al. explicitly noted it maps better onto the Assertiveness facet of human Extraversion in the Costa & McCrae framework than onto a pure competitive-dominance construct.

Bonobos show a distinctive prosocial signature: voluntary food sharing with unfamiliar individuals, help for strangers obtaining food, and contagious yawning that responds equally to group-mates and strangers (chimpanzees show in-group bias). But the “categorically more tolerant” narrative has been substantially complicated: a partial replication of the foundational co-feeding study found only a trend-level species difference, one study directly reversed it, and sanctuary bonobos and chimpanzees console at equal rates.

Neurobiologically, bonobo amygdalae contain more than twice the density of serotonin transporter-immunoreactive axons compared to chimpanzees, and bonobos have more gray matter in empathy-related regions (right dorsal amygdala, right anterior insula, ventral anterior cingulate) with a larger amygdala-to-ACC pathway. Bonobos entirely lack the AVPR1A DupB deletion that is polymorphic in chimpanzees, making their vasopressin receptor promoter region more similar to humans. These neurochemical and neuroanatomical profiles are consistent with an ecology that sharpens the affiliation boundary.

### **3\. Adaptive Artists (high Autonomy, low Boldness) → Neuroticism**

**Generational archetype:** Risk-averse, inward-focused, autonomy needs salient — often through frustration. High autonomy need sharpens the autonomy boundary. The three-way factor missing autonomy — **Neurotic** (Boldness × Mastery × Affiliation) — differentiates from the center.

**Pan evidence:** Neuroticism is the most environmentally unstable factor in the *Pan* personality literature. It emerges in zoo-housed chimpanzees (King & Figueredo, 1997; Weiss et al., 2009\) but not in laboratory-housed chimpanzees, where it merges into a broader Reactivity/Undependability dimension (Weiss et al., 2007; Freeman et al., 2013). It does not emerge at all in bonobos. This is not because the constituent items are absent: in bonobos, the items that define chimpanzee Neuroticism (anxious, fearful, vulnerable, excitable) load as reversed Assertiveness, and “depressed” loads as reversed Extraversion. The trait content exists and varies. What changes across ecologies is whether it coheres into a distinct dimension.

The model explains this instability: Neuroticism is the triple intersection missing Autonomy. It differentiates from the Domesticated center only when the autonomy boundary is crisp — when autonomy needs are ecologically salient. Zoo environments, which systematically constrain autonomy (no fission-fusion dynamics, no natural foraging, no predator pressure, no emigration options), create exactly this condition. Laboratory environments, which constrain autonomy even further but also strip out much of the social complexity that makes autonomy *salient*, may paradoxically fail to sharpen the boundary because the need isn’t activated so much as eliminated.

The architecture predicts that Neuroticism is the factor whose emergence signals autonomy-frustration — it is what you see when what’s missing is the capacity for self-governance. In captivity, what’s missing is autonomy. In bonobos, whose egalitarian ecology doesn’t sharpen the autonomy boundary in the first place, the Neurotic factor never separates from the center.

Neuroticism’s instability even *within* chimpanzees (emerging in zoo but not lab populations) is itself a strong test of the model: the same species, the same items, different factor structure depending on whether the ecology makes autonomy needs salient.

### **4\. Idealistic Prophets (high Boldness, low Mastery) → Deceptive**

**Generational archetype:** High-risk signaling, moral authority, vision over competence. High boldness need sharpens the boldness boundary. The three-way factor missing boldness — **Deceptive** (Mastery × Affiliation × Autonomy) — differentiates from the center.

**Pan evidence:** This is the most speculative mapping. No existing *Pan* personality study has identified a clean “Deceptive” factor. This is conspicuous because the genus is the highest-scoring primate taxon for tactical deception in Byrne & Whiten’s comparative database, and experimental work (Hare et al., 2006\) demonstrates flexible use of competitor-knowledge in chimpanzees. Yet no study has systematically measured individual variation in deceptive behavior, and no study has tested whether personality dimensions predict deception in either species. The model predicts that this factor would emerge most clearly in ecologies where boldness is at a premium — conditions where the boundary between indirect strategic manipulation and the Domesticated center becomes sharp.

**Machiavellian intelligence connection:** The Machiavellian Intelligence Hypothesis (Byrne & Whiten, 1988, 1997\) argued that primate cognitive complexity evolved to navigate social manipulation, deception, and coalition tracking. The model locates Machiavellian intelligence as specifically *Deceptive-factor* intelligence — the triple intersection missing Boldness. This is strategic, competent, socially calibrated, autonomous action that avoids direct confrontation. Everything is indirect because the Boldness channel is missing. Cold, patient, calculating.

Terrence Deacon’s treatment of Machiavellian intelligence provides a critical qualification. Deacon argues that symbols are *not required* for Machiavellian cleverness — indexical communication (reading behavioral “symptoms,” anticipating an opponent’s plan) is sufficient for the majority of complex social relationships. Social animals are “lightning calculators” of behavioral symptoms. He even suggests that non-symbolic species like dogs are often better at reading a person’s true behavioral predispositions than humans are, because human symbolic thought can actually distract from nonverbal signals.

Deacon describes a “runaway” evolutionary process driven by reproductive stakes, where disinformation (deception) and assessment (detection) escalate in tandem. In “tournament systems” where males compete aggressively, communication becomes an elaborate process of checking for signs of inconsistency in an opponent’s display. This social arms race can drive increases in cognitive and communicative complexity, but in Deacon’s view it does not push a species across the *symbolic threshold*.

This matters for the model because it means the Deceptive factor at the ape level (System 2\) operates *indexically*. Tactical deception — concealing information, deploying false signals, exploiting others’ knowledge states — is sophisticated mentalizing, but it references things that are perceptually present (behavioral cues, social configurations, resource locations).

**The Prophet transition:** Deacon argues that the transition to symbolic thought was driven by a specific socio-ecological predicament: the need to represent *social contracts* — marriage, reciprocity, abstract obligations — that regulate reproduction in a hunting/scavenging niche. Unlike behavioral manipulation (indexical), a social contract refers to abstract commitments and future obligations that cannot be seen or pointed to. While Machiavellian social intelligence could make an ape “smarter,” only the requirement of an abstract contract could force the brain across the difficult threshold into symbolic reference.

The Prophet archetype — high Boldness, low Mastery — is the generational phase most associated with this kind of move: moral codes, symbolic legislation, abstract commitments that reorganize the social field. Moses, not Machiavelli. The model suggests that the Deceptive factor occupies the structural position from which the symbolic threshold is crossed. At the ape level (System 2), it is Machiavellian tactical deception — indexical manipulation of others’ knowledge states. At the human level (System 3), the same structural position transforms: Deacon’s social contracts, moral codes, and abstract obligations operate from the same Mastery × Affiliation × Autonomy intersection, but the *type of reference* shifts from indexical to symbolic.

Deacon adds one further observation: symbolic capacity takes Machiavellianism to a uniquely human level. Because symbols allow us to “trade places” in imagination with another mind, they enable profound empathy — but also uniquely human forms of cruelty, such as using the imagined suffering of another as a means of coercion. The Deceptive factor at the symbolic level is not just strategic manipulation. It is the capacity for both moral authority and moral corruption — the structural position from which prophets and tyrants emerge.

This would make the Prophet archetype the hinge point of the entire accretion story — the moment where the Deceptive factor’s content transforms from Machiavellian manipulation into symbolic legislation. Same architecture, qualitatively different cognitive operation. Whether this transformation is best understood as a continuous elaboration or a genuine phase transition remains an open question.

## **The Broader Empirical Landscape**

Several features of the *Pan* personality data provide additional support for the resolution mechanism.

**Extraversion is structurally different across species** — it exists in both chimps and bonobos but is compositionally reorganized. In chimpanzees, Extraversion loads on active, playful, and sociable. In bonobos, “active” and “playful” shift to Openness, and Extraversion is defined primarily by the absence of social withdrawal. The model would predict this: Extraversion sits at the Affiliation × Autonomy intersection, and its internal composition should shift depending on which adjacent primaries are sharpened.

**The AVPR1A genetic architecture differs dramatically.** Bonobos entirely lack the DupB deletion that is polymorphic in chimpanzees, and they show 57% more fixed substitutions in four neurochemical receptor genes (AVPR1A, OXTR, DRD2, HTR1A), while chimpanzees show 31% more polymorphic coding variation. More fixed substitutions in bonobos suggests stronger directional selection (consistent with affiliation-sharpening). More polymorphic variation in chimpanzees suggests maintained diversity (consistent with frequency-dependent selection under a mastery-sharpened ecology where multiple strategies coexist).

**The self-domestication hypothesis partially maps.** Hare et al. (2012) proposed bonobos underwent selection against reactive aggression producing correlated trait changes. This has been challenged by wild data showing bonobos engage in 2.8× more aggressive interactions than chimpanzees — but bonobo aggression is exclusively male-male and individualistic, while chimpanzee aggression targets females and uses coalitions. The resolution mechanism reframes this: bonobos are not “more domesticated” than chimps. They have a different primary sharpened (affiliation vs. mastery), which produces a different factor emerging from the Domesticated center, not a closer proximity to it.

**Wild vs. captive divergence.** In wild bonobos (N=16), the factor structure was markedly different from captive bonobos, with no clear Dominance/Assertiveness factor emerging. Though the sample is far too small for reliable factor analysis, this is consistent with the model: captive environments alter which primaries are ecologically salient, changing which factors differentiate.

**Hormonal profiles track the predicted ecological signatures.** Bonobo males respond to feeding competition with cortisol (stress framing); chimpanzee males respond with testosterone (dominance framing). Chimpanzee males show a clear developmental rise in testosterone; bonobo males show minimal pubertal increases. Bonobos retain juvenile levels of play into adulthood while chimpanzees show age-related declines. These hormonal and developmental profiles are consistent with the model’s prediction that chimpanzee ecology sharpens mastery (competition, reinforcement-based hierarchy) while bonobo ecology sharpens affiliation (social bonding, attachment-based coordination).

## **Summary**

The model makes a single core claim: **a fixed four-primary architecture produces variable factor structures depending on which primary is ecologically sharpened.** The sharpening mechanism is need — high ecological demand for a primary makes its boundary crisp, which differentiates the complementary three-way intersection from the undifferentiated center. The four generational archetypes (Nomads, Heroes, Artists, Prophets) represent four stable ecological configurations, each producing a characteristic personality factor structure.

The *Pan* personality literature — with its divergent factor solutions across chimps, bonobos, and captive vs. wild populations — provides the first empirical test case. The Machiavellian Intelligence Hypothesis and Deacon’s account of symbolic emergence provide the theoretical context for the most speculative vertex: the Deceptive factor, which the model places at the structural position where indexical social intelligence transforms into symbolic social legislation at the human level.

The model generates several testable predictions:

1. **Factor resolution tracks ecological need.** Any population where a specific primary is under strong ecological pressure should show cleaner factor separation for the complementary three-way intersection.  
2. **The Deceptive factor is unmeasured.** No *Pan* study has identified it despite the genus being the highest-scoring for tactical deception. Behavioral coding of deceptive acts linked to personality assessment would test whether it coheres under boldness-sharpened conditions.  
3. **Neuroticism’s instability is predictable.** It should cohere specifically in ecologies where autonomy is salient (frustrated or contested), not in ecologies where autonomy is either fully available or fully eliminated.  
4. **Cross-species factor congruence should track ecological similarity, not phylogenetic distance.** Two populations of the same species in different ecologies (zoo vs. wild chimps) should show less factor congruence than two species in similar ecologies.  
5. **The genetic architecture should differ by ecological regime.** Mastery-sharpened ecologies (chimpanzee-style) should maintain more polymorphic variation (frequency-dependent selection on multiple strategies). Affiliation-sharpened ecologies (bonobo-style) should show more fixed substitutions (directional selection toward prosocial coordination).
