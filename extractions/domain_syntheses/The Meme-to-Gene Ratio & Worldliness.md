# ***The Meme-to-Gene Ratio & Worldliness***

## ***A Working Document — April 2026***

***Context:** This document develops a specific empirical model downstream of the Evolutionary Embodied Information Processor Stack (the "Stack"). It addresses the question: What makes some individuals more representative of their generation than others? The model proposes a causal mechanism — the Meme-to-Gene Ratio — and a resulting personality construct — **Worldliness** — grounded in the Stack's framework of competing information-processing transmission channels.*

***Parent document:** The Evolutionary Embodied Information Processor Stack (summary for continuing conversations, April 2026\)*

## ***The Core Question***

*Why do some people seem to embody their generation while others from the same birth cohort feel like they belong to a different era — or to no era at all? What is the mechanism that produces generational representativeness as an individual difference?*

---

## ***The Foundational Insight***

*There is an ancient Arab proverb: **"People resemble their times more than they resemble their parents."***

*This saying, often cited as a summary of Karl Mannheim's (1928) generational theory, frames the question as a competition between two transmission channels. To the extent that an individual's personality, values, and worldview are shaped by family transmission (genetic \+ cultural), they resemble their parents. To the extent those traits are shaped by generational-cultural transmission, they resemble their times.*

***The key reframe:** Generational representativeness is not a fixed personality profile. It is a structural property — the degree of similarity between an individual's personality profile and their generational cohort mean — produced by a channel-competition during sensitive developmental windows. The mechanism is the **Meme-to-Gene Ratio.** The result is **Worldliness.***

---

## ***Worldliness***

***Worldliness** is the extent to which an individual's personality profile resembles their generation's profile.*

*Worldliness is a **second-order trait.** It does not describe what someone's personality looks like. It describes how much their personality looks like their generation's. A worldly Gen Xer and a worldly Gen Zer have entirely different personality profiles — but they share the structural property that their profiles are close to their respective cohort centroids. An unworldly person from either generation is farther from their cohort centroid — closer to their family centroid or to their own idiosyncratic temperament.*

### ***Analogy: Typicality in Cognitive Science***

*Worldliness is analogous to **typicality** — how good an exemplar something is of its category. A robin is a more typical bird than a penguin. A worldly person is a more typical member of their generation than an unworldly one. The content of the category changes across generations; the distance-from-centroid metric is stable and comparable.*

### ***Operational Definition***

*Worldliness can be computed from any dataset that contains both individual personality profiles and cohort-level data:*

***Worldliness \= profile similarity (e.g., intraclass correlation or cosine similarity) between an individual's personality profile and their generational cohort mean***

*Optionally, worldliness can be defined relative to family resemblance:*

***Relative Worldliness \= similarity to cohort centroid − similarity to family centroid***

*This relative form directly captures the channel-competition model: positive values indicate the generational signal dominated; negative values indicate the family signal dominated; values near zero indicate roughly equal influence (or an idiosyncratic profile distant from both).*

### ***The Mechanism–Outcome Distinction***

*The **Meme-to-Gene Ratio** is the independent variable — a developmental exposure measure during the formative window (roughly ages 10–22).*

***Worldliness** is the dependent variable — a stable, trait-like property of the resulting personality profile, crystallized by the end of the formative window.*

*They are correlated but not the same construct. The ratio is the cause. Worldliness is the effect. This separation allows each to be measured independently and their relationship tested empirically.*

### ***Why Worldliness Requires All Four Systems***

*Worldliness is the quadruple intersection because generational representativeness demands simultaneous coordination of all four primary drives:*

* ***Boldness** — willingness to engage with the world outside the family*  
* ***Mastery** — competence at absorbing and performing the generation's skills and norms*  
* ***Affiliation** — bonding with age-peers and absorbing their social conventions*  
* ***Autonomy** — sufficient self-governance to navigate the symbolic environment independently (without this, you're just compliant, not worldly)*

*Remove any one and you get something other than worldliness:*

* *Without Boldness → withdrawn, avoidant of generational culture*  
* *Without Mastery → exposed but incompetent at performing the generational style*  
* *Without Affiliation → intellectually engaged but socially disconnected from peers*  
* *Without Autonomy → absorbing family culture rather than generational culture*

### ***The Domesticated → Worldly Transition***

*The transition from Domesticated (ape center) to Worldly (human center) is not a replacement — it is a wrapping, consistent with the Stack's "tries to explain" principle. Domestication persists as the System 2 substrate. Worldliness wraps around it with a System 3 layer.*

*A worldly person is also domesticated (socially regulated, low reactive aggression, capable of complex group living). But they are domesticated in a culturally specific way — their social regulation is calibrated to their generation's norms, not just their local troop's.*

*An unworldly person may still be domesticated (socially functional, capable of group living) but calibrated to their family's norms, a different generation's norms, or their own idiosyncratic temperament rather than their cohort's symbolic package.*

### ***Cross-Cultural Instability of Neuroticism and Openness***

*This architecture explains a long-standing puzzle: why Neuroticism and Openness are the two least stable factors in cross-cultural personality research.*

***Neuroticism** is a triple intersection (Boldness × Mastery × Affiliation, absent: Autonomy). All four triple intersections — Neurotic, Dominant, Impulsive, Deceptive — require coordination of three systems and have more degrees of freedom in how the component systems combine. Different cultural environments may weight the components differently, producing factor structures where Neuroticism conflates with Dominance, Impulsivity, or Deceptiveness. The instability isn't measurement error — it reflects genuine architectural complexity at the triple-intersection level.*

***Openness/Intellect** faces an additional instability source: the Open aspect (System 2, ape-level) and the Intellect aspect (System 3, human-level) are architecturally layered rather than blended. Different cultures and different measurement instruments may tap one layer more than the other, producing inconsistent factor loadings.*

## ***Mannheim's Framework (Brief Summary)***

*Karl Mannheim (1928/1952) distinguished three levels of generational membership:*

1. ***Generation as location** — mere chronological co-presence (a birth cohort)*  
2. ***Generation as actuality** — those who participate in the characteristic social and intellectual currents of their period*  
3. ***Generation units** — subgroups within the generation who work up their shared experience in distinct ways*

*Critically, Mannheim stressed that not every birth cohort develops a distinctive generational consciousness. Whether one does depends on the pace of social change — times of upheaval produce stronger generational identities.*

*Stewart & Healy (1989) nuanced Mannheim's "formative period" into a four-part taxonomy: events in childhood/early teens shape fundamental values; those in early adulthood shape identity and life choices; those in mature adulthood shape behavior; those in later adulthood shape new pressures and reflections.*

## ***The Mechanism: Channel Competition***

*The Stack's inheritance gradient predicts that as you move up the evolutionary information-processing hierarchy, cultural transmission increasingly competes with genetic and parental transmission as a source of individual differences:*

| *Level* | *Dominant inheritance channel* |
| ----- | ----- |
| *System 0* | *Genetic* |
| *System 0.1–0.3* | *Genetic \+ parental effects* |
| *System 1* | *\+ epigenetic \+ social learning (indexical semiotics)* |
| *System 2* | *\+ strategic social transmission (mentalizing enables teaching with intent)* |
| *System 3* | *\+ **symbolic cultural transmission** (becomes dominant)* |

***Generational representativeness is the degree to which System 3 transmission wins the competition against System 0–2 transmission during the formative window.***

*The variable that sets the mixing ratio is not a personality trait. It is a developmental exposure variable: **the relative amount of time spent receiving socially transmitted symbolic content vs. time in family-only or solitary non-media contexts during sensitive periods of development (roughly ages 10–22).***

### ***The Sorting Principle***

*Activities during the formative window can be sorted along a continuum by asking a single question:*

***Does this activity expose you to other people's models of how to be?***

*Activities at the high end (peer interaction, media consumption, institutional participation) carry the generational signal. Activities at the low end (solitary non-media time, family-only time) either carry the family signal or allow the individual's own temperamental signal to express without external override.*

## ***The Meme-to-Gene Ratio***

*Following Richard Dawkins' original (1976) definition of the meme as the unit of cultural transmission, we define a ratio:*

***Meme-to-Gene Ratio \= (time in meme-carrying activities) / (time in gene-carrying activities)***

*Where:*

* ***Meme** \= activities that transmit the generational symbolic package*  
* ***Gene** \= activities that transmit the family signal or allow expression of individual temperament*

*The name uses "gene" loosely — the denominator is not literally genetic inheritance but the full bundle of non-cultural-transmission influences on personality (genetic predisposition \+ family-specific cultural transmission \+ autonomous self-development).*

### ***The Market-Basket Approach***

*Not all activities during the formative window can be cleanly classified. Following the logic of economic indicators like the Consumer Price Index (CPI), the Meme-to-Gene Ratio begins with a curated basket of **high-confidence items from the extreme ends of the continuum** — activities where classification is unambiguous. Ambiguous activities are excluded from v1.0, not because they don't matter, but because they cannot yet be reliably sorted. The basket improves over time as empirical work clarifies which ambiguous activities load more heavily on generational-signal vs. family/self-signal.*

*(CPI's "core" version strips out food and energy due to idiosyncratic volatility, even though they are obviously part of the cost of living. The same logic applies here.)*

## ***Predictions***

### ***Historical Predictions***

*The Meme-to-Gene Ratio has increased across historical periods due to successive media technologies progressively invading family space:*

| *Era* | *Primary generational-signal technology* | *Effect on ratio* |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| *Pre-industrial* | *Oral culture, limited institutional time* | *Low ratio; family channel dominates* |
| *19th century* | *Printing press \+ compulsory schooling \+ conscription* | *Ratio rises; Mannheim's generations begin to exist as a phenomenon* |
| *Early–mid 20th century* | *Radio, cinema, television* | *Ratio rises further; broadcast signal enters the home* |
| *Late 20th century* | *Cable TV, personal stereos, gaming consoles, earphones* | *Ratio rises; individuals can isolate from family while physically present* |
| *21st century (smartphones)* | *Always-on social media in the pocket* | *Ratio approaches maximum; "time away from family" decouples from physical absence* |

***Key historical claim:** Each new media technology does not merely increase information access. It shifts the Meme-to-Gene Ratio by bringing the System 3 symbolic channel closer to the individual during the formative window, progressively eroding the family channel's competitive advantage of physical proximity.*

### ***Variance Predictions***

*If personality variance is partially allocated between family-channel and generational-channel sources during the formative window, then the Meme-to-Gene Ratio predicts a historical shift in variance decomposition:*

* ***Pre-industrial prediction:** Less between-generation variability, more between-family variability in personality*  
* ***21st-century prediction:** More between-generation variability, less between-family variability in personality*

***Implication for behavioral genetics:** The standard finding that shared family environment contributes little to personality variance may be a historically contingent artifact of studying cohorts raised in high-Meme-to-Gene-Ratio environments, not a universal law. The same twin studies conducted in low-ratio populations (e.g., Amish communities, pre-industrial societies) might yield substantially larger shared-environment estimates.*

### ***Individual-Level Prediction***

*Within any given generation, individuals with higher Meme-to-Gene Ratios during their formative window should show higher Worldliness scores (greater profile similarity to their cohort centroid) when measured later in life. This is the core testable claim of the model: the mechanism (ratio) predicts the outcome (worldliness) without either being defined in terms of the other.*

*Worldliness should also correlate with existing measures of generational identity (e.g., Joshi et al.'s generational identity construct; the GIS-12), but the constructs are not identical — generational identity is a subjective sense of belonging; worldliness is an objective profile-similarity metric. A person could have high worldliness (their profile matches their cohort) without strong generational identity (they don't think of themselves in generational terms), and vice versa.*

### ***Disruption of Generational Cycling***

*The Strauss-Howe generational cycle model posits a four-phase rotation driven by parenting-mediated allostatic correction. Each generation's parenting style implicitly sets the Meme-to-Gene Ratio for the next generation by controlling (intentionally or not) how much time children spend in family-only vs. society-facing contexts.*

*If media technology pushes the ratio toward maximum regardless of parenting style — because the System 3 channel now lives in the child's pocket — then the parenting correction mechanism may lose its leverage. Helicopter parents (low intended ratio) may produce the same generational-signal absorption as absent parents (high intended ratio) because they cannot control the actual ratio.*

***Prediction:** The generational cycle may be disrupted or accelerated in the smartphone era, as the parenting-style dial becomes decoupled from the actual Meme-to-Gene Ratio experienced by children.*

## ***Open Questions***

1. ***Sensitive period boundaries.** The formative window is roughly 10–22, but is the ratio's effect uniform across this range, or does it have sub-windows with different functions (per Stewart & Healy's taxonomy)?*  
2. ***Sibling resolution.** Can sibling interaction be empirically decomposed into its family-channel and peer-channel components? Older siblings as System 3 Trojan horses inside the attachment relationship may be a testable claim.*  
3. ***Structured vs. unstructured.** Is unstructured peer \+ media time the real generational-signal variable, with structured institutional time being a partially conservative (prior-generation) force?*  
4. ***Cross-cultural calibration.** Does the ratio predict worldliness across cultures with different family structures (collectivist vs. individualist societies)?
