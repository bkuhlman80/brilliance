# Centrality vs Peripherality of Personality Facets Across Four Instruments

## Question

Within each instrument's facet/aspect space, which constructs are more **central** (high cross-domain loadings, connecting multiple domains) and which are more **peripheral** (loading almost exclusively on their own domain)?

## Method

**Cross-domain loading ratio** = sum(|cross-domain loadings|) / |primary loading|

- Ratio near 0 = peripheral (domain-defining anchor)
- Ratio near 1 = moderate (cross-loadings collectively equal primary)
- Ratio > 1 = central (more cross-domain than within-domain variance)

## 4. NEO-PI-R / NEO-PI-3 (30 facets × 5 factors)

### Sources
- LeCorff & Bhérer (2016) Table 2, French-Canadian NEO-PI-3 (N=1,313), **oblimin rotation** (oblique)
- Pilia et al. (2006) Table S3, Sardinian NEO-PI-R (N=6,148), **genetic** factor loadings (Procrustes varimax)

### Methodological notes

These two sources differ in important ways:

1. **LeCorff** uses **oblimin rotation** (oblique), producing **pattern loadings** — the unique contribution of each factor after partialling out correlated factors. This systematically inflates cross-loading ratios, especially for facets whose domains share substantial variance with other domains (e.g., E facets show low unique E loadings because E shares variance with A, N, and C).

2. **Pilia** uses **genetic factor loadings** — the heritable architecture stripped of environmental influences. Genetic loadings are not constrained by varimax orthogonality and can show large cross-domain loadings reflecting genuine pleiotropy.

Both sources yield higher ratios than varimax PCA (used for BFI-2, BFAS, HEXACO). The convergent patterns across these two very different methods are therefore especially informative.

## Summary: The periphery-center map

### Tier 1 — Peripheral (domain-defining anchors)

These constructs define what each domain IS. They have the lowest cross-loadings and the highest factor purity.

| Domain | Anchor construct | Mean ratio across instruments |
|---|---|---|
| C | Organization / Orderliness | ~.35 |
| N | Anxiety / Worry | ~.30 |
| E/X | Sociability / Social Boldness | ~.50 |
| O | Ideas / Aesthetics / Unconventionality | ~.45 |
| A | Compliance / Flexibility / Politeness | ~.55 |

### Tier 2 — Intermediate

These constructs carry domain-specific information but also connect outward.

| Example constructs | Typical ratio range |
|---|---|
| Trust, Productiveness, Creative Imagination | .55–.80 |
| Emotional Volatility, Aesthetic Sensitivity | .45–.70 |
| Perfectionism, Sentimentality, Gentleness | .65–.90 |

### Tier 3 — Central (cross-domain connectors)

These constructs sit at the intersections of multiple domains. They reveal how domains relate to each other.

| Construct | Typical ratio range | Domains bridged |
|---|---|---|
| **Assertiveness / Agency** | 1.10–2.89 | E↔O↔C↔A↔N |
| **Energy / Activity / Diligence** | .88–2.53 | E↔C |
| **Depression / Withdrawal** | .80–1.15 | N↔E |
| **Warmth / Compassion / Altruism** | .72–2.40 | E↔A (↔H in HEXACO) |
| **Trust** | .61–4.33 | A↔N↔E |
| **Fairness / Responsibility** | .73–1.43 | C↔A (↔H in HEXACO) |
| **Deliberation / Prudence** | .73–1.73 | C↔A↔N |
| **Impulsiveness** | 1.33–2.80 | N↔C (↔A) |

### Design implications

1. **To measure a domain cleanly**, use the peripheral anchors. Organization measures C. Anxiety measures N. Sociability measures E.

2. **To predict real-world outcomes**, the central facets may be more powerful because they capture cross-domain variance driving complex behaviors. Assertiveness predicts leadership, intelligence, and well-being simultaneously because it connects E, O, C, and low N.

3. **To understand domain inter-relationships**, examine the central facets. The E↔C bridge (Energy Level, Activity, Diligence) and the N↔E bridge (Depression, Withdrawal) reveal the structural joints of personality space.
