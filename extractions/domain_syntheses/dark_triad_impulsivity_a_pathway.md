# Dark Triad as Impulsive Interpersonal Behavior: The A-Pathway to Impulsivity

## Purpose

Show that the UPPS-P's weak Agreeableness arm (mean |r| = .156) is an instrument limitation, not a construct limitation. Reversed-A captures impulsive interpersonal behavior that the UPPS misses. This completes the argument that impulsivity touches all three BRILLIANCE II primaries (C/Agency, A/Warmth, E/Sociality).

## 2. UPPS-P x Big Five: The Structural Blindspot

| UPPS-P Facet | N | E | O | A | C |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Negative Urgency | **.51** | -.02 | .07 | -.24 | **-.38** |
| Positive Urgency | **.26** | .19 | .00 | -.19 | **-.22** |
| Lack of Premeditation | -.01 | .09 | -.10 | -.16 | **-.47** |
| Lack of Perseverance | .10 | -.16 | -.15 | -.05 | **-.52** |
| Sensation Seeking | -.09 | **.41** | **.22** | -.14 | -.06 |

A correlations range -.05 to -.24. Only Negative Urgency reaches -.24. The UPPS-P was explicitly derived from NEO-PI-R facets of N, E, O, and C — A facets were not source material.

**Sharma (2014, XL)** meta-analysis of 58 self-report impulsivity scales confirms: three personality factors emerge (E/PE, DvC/C, N/NE). **Agreeableness does not appear as a factor in the impulsivity measurement space.** The entire self-report impulsivity literature is structurally blind to antagonism.

## 3. A2 Straightforwardness: The Bridge in the NEO Matrix

From the 30x30 NEO correlation matrix (Costa & McCrae 1992, psychTools):

| Facet | r with A2 | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **E5 Excitement-Seeking** | **-.31** | Low A2 → sensation seeking |
| **N2 Angry Hostility** | **-.29** | Low A2 → hostility |
| **O1 Fantasy** | **-.28** | Low A2 → fantasy proneness |
| **N5 Impulsiveness** | **-.23** | Low A2 → impulsiveness |
| **C6 Deliberation** | **.24** | Low A2 → low deliberation |
| **C5 Self-Discipline** | .20 | Low A2 → low self-discipline |
| **E3 Assertiveness** | -.20 | Low A2 → dominance |
| **C3 Dutifulness** | **.34** | Low A2 → low dutifulness |

The person low on A2 (deceptive, manipulative) also tends to be impulsive (N5), sensation-seeking (E5), hostile (N2), low in deliberation (C6), and dominant (E3). This is essentially the subclinical psychopathy profile. The causal pathway runs through A (social exploitation), not through C (failure of behavioral control) — but the UPPS-P only measures the C pathway.

## 4. HiTOP: Externalizing Splits Into Antagonism and Disinhibition

Multiple XL-quality papers confirm:

**Krueger et al. (2021, XL Prestige — World Psychiatry):**
- Externalizing superspectrum splits into two spectra:
  - **Disinhibited externalizing**: impulsivity, SUDs, ADHD, conduct disorder → maps to **low C**
  - **Antagonistic externalizing**: manipulativeness, callousness, aggression → maps to **low A**
- Different neural substrates: vlPFC (disinhibition) vs. OFC/VMPFC + reduced amygdala volume (antagonism)
- General externalizing factor ~80% heritable

**Ringwald, Forbes & Wright (2023, XL):**
- Meta-analysis (35 studies, N = 120,596) confirms 5 factors
- Hierarchically, Externalizing splits into **Antagonism** and **Disinhibition** at the spectrum level

**Widiger et al. (2019, XL Prestige):**
- HiTOP spectra map onto FFM: antagonism = low A, disinhibition = low C
- These are the two personality dimensions underlying the externalizing superspectrum

## 5. Samo et al. (2026): Integrity + Impulsivity = Disinhibition

Bottom-up TGA of IPIP-NEO-300 (N = 149,337) recovers a novel meta-trait:

| Meta-trait | Traits | Content |
|---|---|---|
| Stability | Neuroticism + Conscientiousness | N + C |
| Plasticity | Sociability + Openness | E+A blend + O |
| **Disinhibition** | **Integrity + Impulsivity** | **A+C morality + E/C/N impulsivity** |

When structure is not imposed, A+C morality content ("Integrity") and impulsivity content cluster together under a common Disinhibition meta-trait. This is the structural signature of the externalizing superspectrum: antagonism and disinhibition are correlated but separable.

## 6. "Cold" vs. "Hot" Impulsivity

| Type | Mechanism | Personality pathway | UPPS-P capture | Dark Triad capture |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hot impulsivity** | Emotion-driven action without reflection | N (urgency) | Strong (NU, PU) | Vulnerable narcissism |
| **Reward impulsivity** | Approach motivation overrides restraint | E (sensation seeking) | Strong (SS) | Grandiose narcissism (partial) |
| **Cold impulsivity** | Callous disregard / calculated exploitation | A (antagonism) | **Weak** (-.05 to -.24) | **Strong** (Mach, psychopathy, grandiose narc) |
| **Control failure** | Top-down regulation breakdown | C (premeditation) | Strong (LP, LPers) | Weak (C near-zero for grandiose narc) |

The UPPS-P captures the hot, reward, and control-failure pathways. It misses the cold pathway almost entirely.

**Carver et al. (2017, L)** make the critical theoretical distinction: cognitive impulsiveness → internalizing; behavioral impulsiveness → externalizing. But "cold" impulsivity doesn't fit either category — it's interpersonally destructive behavior that may be fully deliberate in execution but impulsive in its disregard for others' welfare.

## 7. HEXACO Evidence

**Ashton & Lee (2007, XL Prestige):** HEXACO better predicts workplace delinquency, sexual harassment, and Dark Triad traits than the Big Five. Honesty-Humility is the strongest personality predictor of unethical behavior and dark personality.

**Ashton, Lee & de Vries (2014, XL Prestige):** Big Five A and N are 45-degree rotations of HEXACO H, A, and E. Honesty-Humility captures the variance that Big Five A misses regarding exploitation and manipulation.

**Burtaverde & Ene (2025, L):** Big Five predicted only 20% of Honesty-Humility variance — meaning 80% of the manipulation/exploitation dimension is invisible to the Big Five as typically measured.

This suggests the A-pathway gap is even wider than the NEO matrix shows: the HEXACO's Honesty-Humility captures antagonistic-impulsive behavior that even NEO Agreeableness partially misses.

## 9. Gaps

### Papers NOT in the repo that would strengthen the argument:
- **Lynam & Widiger (2001)** — FFM expert prototype of psychopathy (primarily low A with secondary low C). *Critical gap.*
- **Paulhus & Williams (2002)** — Original Dark Triad paper with foundational Big Five correlations
- **Patrick, Fowles & Krueger (2009)** — Triarchic model (Boldness, Meanness, Disinhibition). Meanness = the A-pathway explicitly.
- **Vize, Lynam, Collison & Miller (2018)** — DT differences are primarily A-facet differences
- **Sleep et al. (2019)** — UPPS-P captures C variance but not A variance explicitly demonstrated
- **Hare (2003) / Neumann** — PCL-R Factor 1 (interpersonal/affective = antagonistic) vs. Factor 2 (lifestyle/antisocial = disinhibited)

### Papers that would complicate the argument:
- **Decuyper et al. (2009)** — Meta-analysis showing psychopathy has BOTH low A AND low C, meaning the pathways are correlated, not fully independent. The claim that reversed-A captures something UPPS-P "misses" is complicated by the fact that psychopathic individuals ARE also impulsive on conventional measures.
- UPPS-P Negative Urgency correlates -.24 with A — not trivial. The "blind spot" is partial, not total.

## 10. Caveats

1. **"Cold impulsivity" may not be impulsivity at all.** Calculated exploitation is deliberate. Calling it "impulsive" stretches the construct beyond its ordinary meaning. The argument is stronger if framed as "impulsive in its consequences" rather than "impulsive in its mechanism."

2. **The A and C pathways are correlated.** Psychopathy involves both low A and low C. The pathways are separable but not independent. Grandiose narcissism is the cleanest case of pure-A antisociality (C ≈ 0), but it's an outlier among Dark Triad constructs.

3. **No direct DT x UPPS-P correlation table exists in the repo.** The argument that DT captures impulsivity variance BEYOND UPPS-P is theoretical, supported by structural evidence (HiTOP, Samo) but not by an incremental validity test in the data we have.

4. **HEXACO Honesty-Humility is the better measure for this argument than NEO Agreeableness.** 80% of H-H variance is not captured by Big Five A (Burtaverde & Ene 2025). If the A-pathway to impulsivity is real, it may be even wider than the NEO matrix suggests — but we'd need H-H x UPPS-P data to confirm.
