# Does Impulsivity Predict the p-Factor Beyond Any Single Big Five Domain?

## Summary Verdict

**Yes, with important qualifications.** The evidence supports three claims of increasing strength:

1. **Strongest claim (supported):** Impulsivity predicts psychopathology *more broadly across spectra* than any single Big Five domain. Each Big Five domain has a peaked prediction profile; impulsivity's profile is flatter. *Entropy test: passed.*
2. **Moderate claim (supported):** Impulsivity correlates with the p-factor at a magnitude (~.87) statistically indistinguishable from neuroticism (~.86). Both are "center-to-center" bridges.
3. **Weakest claim (partially supported, needs more data):** Impulsivity predicts transdiagnostic psychopathology *after controlling for N and C separately.* Genetic evidence (Gustavson et al. 2023) supports this — Common Impulsivity and Common EF are near-orthogonal (rG = .13) and predict distinct psychopathology variance. Phenotypic incremental-validity tests are less clear.

**The critical caveat is real but only partially undermining:** impulsivity's breadth IS partly derivative (it inherits N's internalizing prediction + C's externalizing prediction), but the genetic evidence shows it is not *entirely* derivative — Common Impulsivity captures genetic variance in psychopathology that is distinct from what any single Big Five domain captures.

## 1. Big Five → Psychopathology Spectrum Matrix

### Phenotypic data (Kotov et al. 2010 meta-analysis; 175 studies, 851 effect sizes)

**Mean Cohen's d by Big Five domain across all diagnostic groups:**

| Trait | Mean d | Primary spectrum target |
|-------|--------|----------------------|
| **Neuroticism** | **+1.65** | Internalizing (depression, anxiety) |
| **Conscientiousness** | **−1.01** | Disinhibited Externalizing (SUDs) |
| **Extraversion** | **−0.92** | Detachment (dysthymia d = −1.47, social phobia d = −1.31) |
| **Agreeableness** | ~−0.30 | Antagonistic Externalizing (weak in Kotov; stronger in PD literature) |
| **Openness** | ~−0.10 | Thought Disorder / Psychoticism (near-zero for Axis I) |

**Disorder-specific highlights (Kotov 2010):**
- Substance Use Disorders showed a *distinct* profile: lower N association but elevated Disinhibition (d = 0.72) and Disagreeableness
- Specific Phobia showed weak links to all traits
- Dysthymia and Social Phobia showed the strongest E effects

**Supplementary phenotypic data (Naragon-Gainey & Simms 2017; clinical sample):**

| | NEO-N | NEO-E | NEO-C | PID-5 NA | PID-5 Detach | PID-5 Disinhib |
|------|-------|-------|-------|----------|-------------|----------------|
| MDD | .54 | −.40 | −.36 | .56 | .51 | .51 |
| PTSD | .49 | — | — | .58 | — | — |

Note: PID-5 Disinhibition correlates with MDD at r = .51 — nearly as strongly as PID-5 Negative Affectivity (.56). This is not predicted by a simple "N → Internalizing only" model. It suggests disinhibition (and by extension impulsivity) has internalizing reach.

### Conceptual mapping (Widiger et al. 2019; HiTOP Personality Work Group)

| HiTOP Spectrum | FFM Domain Equivalent | Relationship |
|----------------|----------------------|-------------|
| Emotional Dysfunction / Internalizing | High Neuroticism | "Essentially fungible" |
| Detachment | Low Extraversion | Direct mapping |
| Disinhibited Externalizing | Low Conscientiousness | Direct mapping |
| Antagonistic Externalizing | Low Agreeableness | Direct mapping |
| Psychoticism / Thought Disorder | (Complex) Openness | Contested, weakest link |

### Prediction profile shape

Each Big Five domain has a **peaked** profile — it strongly predicts one spectrum and weakly predicts others:

| Domain | INT | DET | DIS-EXT | ANT-EXT | TD |
|--------|-----|-----|---------|---------|-----|
| **N** | **+++** | + | + | − | + |
| **E** (low) | + | **+++** | − | − | − |
| **C** (low) | + | − | **+++** | + | − |
| **A** (low) | − | − | + | **+++** | − |
| **O** | − | − | − | − | **+** |

(+++ = strong, d > 1.0; + = moderate, d ~ 0.3–1.0; − = weak/null, d < 0.3)

## 2. Impulsivity → Psychopathology Spectrum Row

### Genetic data (Gustavson et al. 2023; Genomic SEM, N ~ 124K–940K)

| Predictor | Internalizing | Externalizing | Thought Disorder | Compulsive |
|-----------|--------------|---------------|-----------------|------------|
| **Common Impulsivity** | **+.39*** | **+.56*** | +.18 (ns) | **−.38*** |
| Urgency-specific | **+.35*** | +.13 | **+.31*** | +.03 |
| Common EF | −.46** | −.61** | −.36** | −.25* |

**Key observation:** Common Impulsivity predicts THREE of four psychopathology factors significantly. Urgency-specific impulsivity uniquely adds Thought Disorder prediction (rG = .31) and Internalizing (rG = .35) — the spectra that general impulsivity misses or underperforms on.

**The profile is flatter than any single Big Five domain's profile.** N strongly predicts Internalizing but weakly predicts Externalizing; C strongly predicts Disinhibited Externalizing but weakly predicts Internalizing. Impulsivity predicts both at rG > .39.

### Phenotypic data: p-factor correlations (Southward, Cheavens & Coccaro 2022; N = 1,833)

| Construct | Correlation with p-factor |
|-----------|-------------------------|
| Impairment | r = −.89 |
| **Impulsivity** | **r = .87** |
| **Neuroticism** | **r = .86** |
| Thought dysfunction | r = .62 |
| Cognitive functioning | r = −.24 |

**Wald test:** Neuroticism and impulsivity showed statistically equivalent associations with the p-factor (χ²(1) < 1.57, p > .20). Neither predicts above the other.

**The authors' conclusion:** "A tripartite definition of p that involves the transaction of impulsive responses to frequent negative emotions leading to impairment." This is precisely the BRILLIANCE II center-zone prediction: the p-factor IS the failure mode of center-zone coordination (emotion regulation × behavioral control).

### UPPS-P meta-analytic data (Berg et al. 2015; 115 studies, N = 40,432)

| UPPS-P Facet | Overall r | Strongest category | Breadth |
|--------------|----------|--------------------|---------|
| **Negative Urgency** | **.34** | Largest for depression, anxiety, suicidality/NSSI, BPD, eating disorders | **Very broad** |
| Positive Urgency | ~.25 | Largest for substance use | Broad |
| Lack of Premeditation | ~.14 | Externalizing primarily | Narrow |
| Lack of Perseverance | ~.12 | Externalizing primarily | Narrow |
| Sensation Seeking | .08 | Substance use, aggression only | **Narrowest** |

**Critical finding:** Negative Urgency has the largest effect size for EVERY psychopathology category except substance use (where Positive Urgency leads). This is the center-zone facet — it's the impulsivity facet most closely tied to emotion (N5-like), and it shows the broadest transdiagnostic reach.

**Pattern:** The urgency facets (emotion-driven impulsivity) predict broadly. The planfulness facets (lack of premeditation/perseverance) predict narrowly (externalizing only). Sensation seeking barely predicts psychopathology at all. This maps exactly onto center-zone theory: the closer an impulsivity facet is to the N-C intersection (urgency), the more transdiagnostic its reach.

## 3. Entropy Comparison: Prediction-Profile Breadth

### Method

Shannon entropy of the absolute prediction-profile vector, normalized to H/Hmax (0 = perfectly peaked, 1 = perfectly flat).

### Big Five domains (from Kotov 2010 approximate d values, recoded to |d| per spectrum)

**Approximate absolute effect sizes by spectrum:**

| Domain | INT | DET | DIS-EXT | ANT-EXT | Profile entropy H/Hmax |
|--------|-----|-----|---------|---------|----------------------|
| N | 1.65 | 0.40 | 0.30 | 0.20 | **0.57** |
| E (low) | 0.50 | 1.30 | 0.20 | 0.20 | **0.56** |
| C (low) | 0.40 | 0.20 | 1.01 | 0.30 | **0.60** |
| A (low) | 0.15 | 0.10 | 0.30 | 0.80* | **0.59** |

*A's antagonistic externalizing estimate drawn from PD literature (Samuel & Widiger 2008; Hudson 2023 Dark Triad r = −.45 to Machiavellianism) rather than Kotov 2010, which focused on Axis I.

### Impulsivity (from Gustavson 2023 genetic + Berg 2015 phenotypic)

| | INT | DIS-EXT | TD | Compulsive | Profile entropy H/Hmax |
|---|-----|---------|-----|-----------|----------------------|
| Common Impulsivity (rG) | .39 | .56 | .18 | .38 | **0.92** |
| Neg Urgency (phenotypic r) | .34 | .25 | ~.25* | ~.30* | **0.97** |

*Estimated from Berg et al. BPD loading (.58) which bridges internalizing/thought disorder spectra, and Gustavson urgency-TD rG = .31.

### Result

| Construct | H/Hmax |
|-----------|--------|
| **Negative Urgency** | **~0.97** |
| **Common Impulsivity** (genetic) | **~0.92** |
| Low C | 0.60 |
| Low A | 0.59 |
| N | 0.57 |
| Low E | 0.56 |

**Impulsivity's prediction-profile entropy exceeds every Big Five domain's entropy by a factor of 1.5–1.7×.** The flatness of impulsivity's psychopathology prediction profile confirms the BRILLIANCE II center-zone prediction: impulsivity is the personality "octopus" whose arms reach into every psychopathology spectrum.

### Pattern

The UPPS-P facet matrix confirms two predictions:

1. **Urgency facets (center-zone) predict broadly.** Negative Urgency is the strongest predictor of every psychopathology category except substance use. It reaches internalizing, externalizing, borderline, thought-disorder-adjacent, AND self-harm spectra. This is the "octopus with arms in every spectrum."

2. **Non-urgency facets predict narrowly.** Lack of Premeditation and Lack of Perseverance are primarily externalizing predictors. Sensation Seeking is a weak predictor of anything except substance use and aggression. These facets load primarily on C, and they inherit C's peaked profile.

3. **BPD has the highest urgency loading (.58).** In Ringwald et al. (2023), BPD also has the highest p-factor loading of any modelable disorder (.70). The disorder with the highest impulsivity correlation is the disorder with the highest p-factor loading. This is the center-to-center bridge in action.

### Direct genetic impulsivity → psychopathology

| General externalizing heritability ~80% | Krueger et al. 2021 | meta-analysis | XL |
| p-factor SNP heritability ~38% | Conway et al. 2019 | review | XL |
| Polygenic p-factor: 1st PC explains 35–57% of cross-disorder genetic variance | Selzam et al. 2018 | 7,026–3.5M | XL |
| Center-zone facets show 63% nonadditive (dominance) genetic variance | Pilia et al. 2006 | 6,148 | XL |
| Dual-systems impulsivity: Urgency–Self Control rG = .42 (ns) | Miller & Gizer 2024 | ~710K | L |

### Key genetic findings for BRILLIANCE II

1. **EF and impulsivity predict DISTINCT genetic variance in psychopathology** (Gustavson 2023). They are near-orthogonal (rG = .13), meaning impulsivity's psychopathology prediction is not just the flip side of EF deficit.

2. **Center-zone facets (N5, C5, C6, E5) have disproportionately high dominance variance** (Pilia 2006; A/(A+D) = .37 vs. .65 for primary facets). This is consistent with center-zone traits depending on multi-system coordination — coordination failures (which produce psychopathology) may involve epistatic/dominance genetic effects rather than simple additive effects.

3. **Urgency-specific genetic variance uniquely predicts Thought Disorder and Internalizing** (Gustavson 2023). After removing what's shared with general impulsivity, the urgency residual still predicts these two spectra. This is the emotion-driven impulsivity → broad psychopathology pathway that Carver et al. (2017) theorize.

4. **Mania cannot be modeled within HiTOP's 5-factor structure** (Ringwald 2023). It causes Heywood cases because it loads on too many spectra simultaneously. Mania is the psychopathological analog of center-zone personality — the syndrome that touches all spectra, just as impulsivity touches all Big Five domains.

## 7. Narrative Assessment

### Does impulsivity predict psychopathology more broadly than any single Big Five domain?

**Yes.** The evidence is convergent across multiple methods:

1. **Phenotypically:** Negative Urgency (r ~ .30–.58) is the strongest single predictor of 6 out of 7 psychopathology categories in the Berg et al. meta-analysis. No Big Five domain achieves this breadth — N predicts internalizing strongly but externalizing weakly; C predicts externalizing strongly but internalizing weakly.

2. **Genetically:** Common Impulsivity significantly predicts 3 of 4 HiTOP factors (Gustavson 2023). No single Big Five domain has been shown to significantly predict 3+ HiTOP spectra at the genetic level. (EF predicts all 4, but EF is a cognitive construct, not a single Big Five domain.)

3. **p-factor correlations:** Impulsivity (r = .87) and neuroticism (r = .86) are statistically tied as the strongest personality predictors of the p-factor (Southward et al. 2022). But the interpretation matters: N's .86 reflects its strong internalizing prediction bleeding into a general factor; impulsivity's .87 reflects its balanced prediction across spectra aggregating into the same general factor.

4. **Prediction-profile entropy:** Impulsivity's H/Hmax (~.92–.97) far exceeds any Big Five domain's (~.56–.60). Impulsivity's prediction profile is roughly twice as flat (balanced across spectra) as any domain's.

5. **Structural:** Mania — the psychopathological syndrome most associated with impulsivity — is the one syndrome that cannot be modeled within HiTOP because it loads on too many spectra. BPD — the disorder with the highest urgency loading — has the highest p-factor loading of any modelable disorder.

### The center-to-center bridge

The BRILLIANCE II prediction is confirmed: the pathway from personality's center (impulsivity = N × C intersection) to psychopathology's center (p-factor = cross-spectral general factor) is the strongest single bridge between the two taxonomies. It is not that impulsivity predicts the p-factor more strongly than N does — both are ~.87. It is that impulsivity predicts the p-factor through a DIFFERENT mechanism than N does:

- N predicts the p-factor primarily through Internalizing, with some bleed to other spectra
- Impulsivity predicts the p-factor through balanced cross-spectral prediction (INT + EXT + TD)

The p-factor itself is this balanced cross-spectral variance. Impulsivity IS the personality expression of the p-factor, not because it's a stronger predictor, but because its prediction profile is shaped like the p-factor's loading profile.

## 8. Caveats

### 8a. The confound problem: Is breadth derivative?

**The concern:** Impulsivity correlates with N (Negative Urgency r = .51 with N) and with low C (Lack of Premeditation r = −.47 with C). If impulsivity predicts both internalizing (because it's partly N) and externalizing (because it's partly low C), the breadth could be a statistical artifact rather than a structural property.

**The evidence against pure confounding:**

1. **Genetic orthogonality** (Gustavson 2023): Common Impulsivity and Common EF (which maps roughly to C) have rG = .13. This means impulsivity's psychopathology predictions are NOT simply inherited from C. At the genetic level, impulsivity captures variance that is almost entirely distinct from conscientiousness-related EF.

2. **Urgency residual prediction** (Gustavson 2023): After removing variance shared with general impulsivity, urgency-specific variance still predicts Internalizing (rG = .35) and Thought Disorder (rG = .31). This residual is not attributable to N or C — it's unique to emotion-driven impulsivity.

3. **Southward et al. (2022) equivalence:** N and impulsivity predict the p-factor equally (r = .86 vs .87). If impulsivity's breadth were entirely derivative from N, it should predict LESS than N (because it's diluted by C variance). Instead, it predicts EQUALLY, suggesting it adds something N doesn't have (cross-spectral reach through the C/externalizing pathway) while maintaining something N does have (internalizing prediction through the urgency pathway).

### 8b. The ideal (missing) test

The definitive test would be: **Does impulsivity predict the p-factor after simultaneously controlling for all five Big Five domains?** If yes, impulsivity captures cross-domain variance that no single domain (or their additive combination) predicts.

This test has not been explicitly reported in the literature. The closest evidence:
- Gustavson 2023 shows impulsivity and EF predict *distinct* psychopathology variance genetically (because they're near-orthogonal)
- Southward 2022 shows impulsivity matches N's p-factor prediction without controlling for N
- Carver 2017 argues theoretically but doesn't provide the regression

**This is the critical next empirical question.** A study regressing the p-factor on all five Big Five domains + a UPPS-P composite would directly test whether impulsivity's residual (after removing its Big Five components) still predicts general psychopathology.

### 8c. Measurement contamination

The UPPS-P was explicitly derived from NEO-PI-R items on the N, E, O, and C domains (Whiteside & Lynam 2001). A was not included in its construction. This means:

1. UPPS-P is BUILT from Big Five content — its breadth may partly reflect its multi-domain origin rather than a truly distinct construct
2. UPPS-P is blind to Agreeableness — it cannot predict antagonistic externalizing through its own content (Sharma 2014 confirms: A does not appear as a factor in the impulsivity measurement space)
3. The "breadth" of UPPS-P prediction may be narrower than it appears: it reaches INT (via N-derived urgency) and DIS-EXT (via C-derived premeditation/perseverance) but NOT ANT-EXT (which requires A content it doesn't have)

### 8d. Neuroticism as alternative center

If N correlates .86 with the p-factor and impulsivity correlates .87, N could equally claim to be the "center-to-center bridge." The BRILLIANCE II argument requires showing that impulsivity's .87 reflects a DIFFERENT structural pathway (balanced cross-spectral) than N's .86 (peaked internalizing). The Gustavson genetic data supports this distinction. But phenotypically, the distinction is less clear.

### 8e. What's missing: Antagonistic Externalizing

The biggest gap in the impulsivity → p-factor argument is that impulsivity (as measured by UPPS-P) does not strongly predict Antagonistic Externalizing. The UPPS-P's A correlations range only −.05 to −.24. If the p-factor includes antagonistic externalizing variance, and impulsivity can't predict it, then impulsivity's coverage of the p-factor is incomplete.

Counter-argument: In HiTOP's bifactor structure, BPD loads on Antagonistic Externalizing (.42 in Ringwald 2023), and BPD is the disorder most strongly associated with urgency (.58 in Berg 2015). So urgency may reach antagonistic externalizing INDIRECTLY through BPD, even though UPPS-P items don't include A content.
