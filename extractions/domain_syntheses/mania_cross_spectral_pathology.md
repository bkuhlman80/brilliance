# Mania as the Most Cross-Spectral Pathology: Evidence for Center-Zone Psychopathology

## Purpose

Test the claim that mania is the psychopathological analogue of impulsivity's position in personality space. Just as impulsivity is the construct no single Big Five domain owns (center of the BRILLIANCE II Venn diagram), mania is the syndrome no single HiTOP spectrum owns. Both are what the system looks like when multi-system coordination breaks down catastrophically.

---

## 1. The Headline Finding: Mania Broke the HiTOP Model

**Ringwald, Forbes & Wright (2023, XL)** — Meta-analysis of HiTOP structure (35 studies, 48 samples, N = 120,596).

**Bipolar disorder had to be REMOVED from the model for the five-factor structure to converge.** When bipolar was included, models produced Heywood cases (impossible parameter estimates). Only after removing bipolar and dependent PD could the five-factor structure (Internalizing, Detachment, Thought Disorder, Antagonism, Disinhibition) achieve acceptable fit (CFI = .92, RMSEA = .07, SRMR = .03).

Heywood cases are the statistical signature of a variable that loads substantially on too many factors simultaneously. Bipolar is the only major syndrome that caused this problem.

The paper states: "its placement in the overall structure is uncertain" and "additional research is needed to investigate questions such as whether bipolar disorders reflect internalizing and/or thought disorder spectra."

---

## 2. Mania Is "Interstitial" — Watson et al. (2022, XL)

Watson et al. (2022, World Psychiatry), the most comprehensive HiTOP review of the emotional dysfunction superspectrum, explicitly uses the word **"interstitial"**:

- "The mania subfactor is **interstitial** between internalizing and thought disorder, sharing a number of neural abnormalities with psychotic disorders."
- "mania is **interstitial** and shows important connections to both internalizing and thought disorder"
- "it seems likely that **specific symptom dimensions within mania (e.g., emotional lability, euphoric activation) fall in different parts of the HiTOP hierarchy**"

Mania is formally placed within the Emotional Dysfunction superspectrum but acknowledged to bridge into the Psychoticism/Thought Disorder superspectrum.

## 3. Mania's Spectral Loading Profile

| HiTOP Spectrum | Mania loads? | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Internalizing** | YES | Placed within emotional dysfunction superspectrum; shares distress features; mood episode core | Watson et al. (2022, XL) |
| **Thought Disorder / Psychoticism** | YES | Shares neural abnormalities with psychotic disorders; genetic rg with schizophrenia = .70; grouped with psychoticism in cognition models | Watson (2022, XL); Lee et al. (2019, cited); Ringwald (2025, XL) |
| **Disinhibited Externalizing** | YES | Response inhibition deficit g = .52; impulsive reactivity; behavioral disinhibition features | Carver et al. (2017, L) |
| **Antagonistic Externalizing** | YES | Dominance motivation; grandiosity; agentic extraversion; willingness to prioritize power over social costs | Johnson et al. (2021, S) |
| **Detachment** | NO (opposite) | Bipolar characterized by HIGH positive emotionality, not low; distinguished from depression by NOT being introverted | Widiger et al. (2019, XL) |
| **Somatoform** | No evidence | — | — |

**Mania touches 4 of 6 HiTOP spectra** — Internalizing, Thought Disorder, Disinhibited Externalizing, and Antagonistic Externalizing. It is actively OPPOSITE to Detachment (high approach motivation, high sociality).

## 5. p-Factor Evidence

### Selzam et al. (2018, L) — Genetic p-factor

First genetic PC explained 22–57% of variance. **Schizophrenia, bipolar, and MDD had the highest loadings** across methods. Oblique rotation placed schizophrenia and bipolar together on one factor.

### Ringwald et al. (2023)

General factor loadings from the one-factor model (bipolar excluded):
- BPD: .70 (highest modeled)
- Paranoid PD: .64
- Schizotypal PD: .60
- MDD: .58

Bipolar was not estimated, but its cross-spectral nature suggests it would have loaded very high — potentially matching or exceeding BPD's .70.

### Harden et al. (2020, L)

p-factor heritability: 49–72%. Genetic correlation with EF: rA = -.25 to -.38. Does not give disorder-specific p loadings.

### Carver et al. (2017, L)

The theoretical bridge: Carver interprets the p-factor as reflecting "impulsive reactivity to emotion — not emotional intensity per se." If the p-factor IS regulation failure, and mania has the highest p-loading, then mania = maximum regulation failure = center-zone psychopathology. This directly parallels: impulsivity = maximum cross-loading = center-zone personality.

## 6. Personality Correlates of Mania

### Implied Big Five Profile (synthesized from repo evidence)

| Domain | Level | Evidence | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **N** | **HIGH** | Core of emotional dysfunction superspectrum; irritability; emotional lability | Watson (2022, XL); Beauchaine & Tackett (2020, L) |
| **E** | **HIGH** | Agentic extraversion; dominance motivation; positive emotionality; NOT introverted (unlike depression) | Widiger (2019, XL); Johnson (2021, S) |
| **O** | **Elevated** | Psychoticism/unconventionality dimension; thought disorder features | Watson (2022, XL) |
| **A** | **LOW** | Grandiosity; dominance; antagonistic features; prioritizing power over social costs | Johnson (2021, S); Krueger (2021, XL) |
| **C** | **LOW** | Response inhibition deficit g = .52; impulsive reactivity; behavioral disinhibition | Carver (2017, L) |

**This is a genuinely unusual profile.** Most internalizing conditions show HIGH N + LOW E. Mania shows HIGH N AND HIGH E — which is part of why it is structurally anomalous. It combines the emotional distress of internalizing with the approach motivation of externalizing + the antagonism of the dark personality space + the disorganization of thought disorder.

### Johnson et al. (2021, S) — Hypomanic Personality Scale

HPS correlates with "higher explicit and implicit dominance motivation, self-rated power, agentic extraversion, willingness to prioritize the pursuit of power over social costs, and observer-rated dominant behavior." Higher mania scores → positive affect and comfort as leader; discomfort as subordinate.

## 7. Mania × Impulsivity

### Carver et al. (2017, L) — The central evidence

- Bipolar response inhibition deficit: meta-analytic **g = .52**
- EF deficits persist even after remission
- Unaffected first-degree relatives show response inhibition deficits (Stroop d = .51) — heritable, not state-dependent
- Emotion-triggered impulsivity (Negative Urgency, Positive Urgency) linked to both internalizing AND externalizing AND bipolar
- Key theoretical claim: "impulsive reactivity to emotion — not emotional intensity per se — is the critical transdiagnostic vulnerability"
- Bipolar involves BOTH cognitive impulsiveness (internalizing pathway) AND behavioral impulsiveness (externalizing pathway)

### Connection to impulsivity subtypes

| Impulsivity subtype | Present in mania? | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **Hot / Urgency** | YES | Emotional lability → action without reflection; both positive and negative urgency |
| **Reward / Sensation** | YES | Elevated approach motivation; reward sensitivity; excitement-seeking |
| **Control failure / Premeditation** | YES | Response inhibition deficit g = .52; heritable EF impairment |
| **Cold / Antagonistic** | YES | Grandiosity; dominance; willingness to exploit; disregard for social costs |

Mania appears to involve ALL FOUR impulsivity subtypes simultaneously — consistent with center-zone total coordination failure rather than single-system failure.

BPD is the strongest competitor for "most cross-spectral syndrome":

| Feature | BPD | Mania |
|---|---|---|
| Spectra loaded (≥ .30) in Ringwald 2023 | 2 (Int, Ant) | Not modelable |
| General factor loading | .70 (highest modeled) | Not estimated (would be ≥ .70) |
| Described as "interstitial" | Not explicitly | YES (Watson 2022) |
| Caused model convergence failure | No | YES |
| Personality profile | High N, low A, low C | High N, HIGH E, low A, low C, elevated O |
| Spectra touched (theoretical) | 2–3 (Int, Ant, Dis) | 4 (Int, Tht, Dis, Ant) |

BPD sits at the Internalizing × Externalizing boundary — a pairwise intersection in BRILLIANCE II terms. Mania sits at a higher-order intersection touching an additional spectrum (Thought Disorder) that BPD does not strongly load on. The key differentiator: mania includes psychotic features (thought disorder) and elevated positive emotionality (approach motivation) that BPD lacks.

## 12. Caveats

1. **The cross-spectral claim is supported indirectly.** No paper explicitly models mania's simultaneous loadings across all HiTOP spectra. The evidence is convergent — Heywood cases (Ringwald 2023) + "interstitial" designation (Watson 2022) + multi-domain personality profile + multiple impulsivity subtypes engaged — but not a single definitive analysis.

2. **Mania vs. manic episode vs. bipolar disorder.** The HiTOP literature often discusses "bipolar disorder" (the DSM category) rather than "mania" (the state/syndrome). The cross-spectral argument is strongest for the manic episode itself, which simultaneously involves mood elevation (internalizing), psychotic features (thought disorder), behavioral disinhibition (disinhibited externalizing), and grandiosity/antagonism (antagonistic externalizing). Depressive episodes in bipolar are straightforwardly internalizing.

3. **The Antagonistic Externalizing loading is the weakest arm.** The evidence for mania loading on antagonistic externalizing comes primarily from Johnson et al. (2021, S quality, N = 81) on dominance motivation. Grandiosity is clinically recognized but its structural placement on the Antagonism spectrum (vs. a separate mania-specific dimension) is not well established in the repo data.

4. **The Thought Disorder loading is genetic, not purely phenotypic.** The strongest evidence for mania–psychoticism overlap is the genetic correlation (rg = .70 with schizophrenia, cited but not directly present in repo). Phenotypically, psychotic features occur in mania but are not universal. The "interstitial" designation reflects this partial overlap.

5. **Creativity gap is total.** The repo has no data on the mania-creativity link. This section of the BRILLIANCE II argument will need external sourcing.
