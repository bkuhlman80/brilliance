# Personality Structures by Species

> Comprehensive survey of all personality factor structures reported in the bibliography, organized by species. Includes trait names, intercorrelations where available, study quality, and notable structural findings.
>
> Last updated: 2026-04-07

## Great Apes

### Chimpanzee (*Pan troglodytes*) — Captive

| Study | N | # | Factors | Instrument | Quality |
|-------|---|---|---------|------------|---------|
| King & Figueredo 1997 | 100 | 6 | Surgency, Agreeableness, Dependability, Emotionality, Openness, **Dominance** | 43 adjectives | Structural: M |
| Weiss, King & Figueredo 2000 | 145 | 6 | Same (CPQ) | CPQ 43 items | Heritability: M |
| Weiss et al. 2009 (Japan) | 146 | 6 | Dominance, Extraversion, Conscientiousness, Agreeableness, Neuroticism, Openness | HPQ (Japanese) | Structural: L |
| Freeman et al. 2013 | 99 | 6 | Reactivity/Undependability, Dominance, Extraversion, Openness, Agreeableness, Methodical | 41-item combined | Structural: M |

**Cross-cultural congruence (US→Japan):** Overall .85 (E .91, D .89, C .89, A .89; N .69, O .71 weakest).

**Heritability (Weiss et al. 2000):** Only Dominance significantly heritable (h² = .63); all others near zero. Shared zoo environment negligible for all six factors.

**SWB correlations (Weiss et al. 2009):** D r = .50, E r = .49, N r = −.36, A r = .22, O r = .22.

**Notable:** Dominance has no human FFM equivalent. Methodical (Freeman et al.) may refine Conscientiousness. Six-factor structure consistently recovered across instruments and cultures.

---

### Chimpanzee — Wild (*P. t. schweinfurthii*)

| Study | N | # | Factors | Instrument | Quality |
|-------|---|---|---------|------------|---------|
| Weiss 2025 | 40 | **4** | Dominance, Disagreeableness, Openness, Extraversion | HPQ 54 items | Structural: M |

**Conscientiousness and Neuroticism ABSENT.** C items loaded on Dominance/Disagreeableness. Extraversion narrow (activity + gregariousness only, α = .31). First full HPQ study of wild chimpanzees.

**Weiss et al. 2023 (Gombe, N = 34 males, 37 years):** Uses personality for fitness prediction, not de novo structure. Dominance predicted both rank (91% variance explained) and siring success (OR = 2.59 beyond rank).

### Bonobo (*Pan paniscus*)

| Study | N | # | Factors | Instrument | Quality |
|-------|---|---|---------|------------|---------|
| Weiss et al. 2015 | 154 | 6 | Assertiveness, Conscientiousness, Openness, Attentiveness, Agreeableness, Extraversion | HPQ | Structural: L |

**Procrustes with chimpanzees:** Good for 4 factors (Assertiveness .85–.95, C .92–.96, A .91–.93, O .79–.95); poor for Attentiveness (.66–.79) and Extraversion (.69–.85).

**Attentiveness and Extraversion are likely derived traits** (evolved after bonobo–chimpanzee split ~1–2 MYA). Repeatability over ~5.6 years: .17 (A, ns) to .62 (Assertiveness).

### Orangutan (*Pongo* spp.)

| Study | N | # | Factors | Instrument | Quality |
|-------|---|---|---------|------------|---------|
| Weiss, King & Perkins 2006 | 152 | 5 | Extraversion, Dominance, Neuroticism, Agreeableness, **Intellect** (C+O blend) | OPQ 48 items | Structural: L |

**No distinct Conscientiousness factor** — Intellect blended C + O markers. Suggests C evolved after orangutan–African ape split (~14 MYA).

**SWB correlations:** E r = .47, A r = .50, N r = −.41; Dominance and Intellect ns.

### Great Apes — Multimethod (4 species)

| Study | N | # | Factors | Instrument | Quality |
|-------|---|---|---------|------------|---------|
| Uher & Asendorpf 2008 | 20 (5 per species) | 19 trait constructs | Bottom-up mono-polar traits (not FFM factors) | GAPI-B (34 items), GAPI-A (17 items), 76 behavior-situation variables | Structural: S |

Three assessment methods: behavior measures (α = .96), behavior ratings (α = .81), adjective ratings (α = .83). Temporal stability: mean r = .78–.88. Behavior ratings fully mediated behavior–adjective relationships in 6/7 traits. Bottom-up approach generated traits **beyond FFM** (food orientation, sexual activity).

## Old World Monkeys

### Macaques (6 species)

| Study | N | # | Factors | Instrument | Quality |
|-------|---|---|---------|------------|---------|
| Adams et al. 2015 | 432 (6 spp., 7 sites) | Variable | Friendliness + Openness universal; aggression/competence varies by social style | HPQ | Structural: L |

Dimensions related to aggression/social competence mapped onto **social style (despotic vs. egalitarian)** rather than phylogeny. Grade 1 despotic species (rhesus, Japanese) clustered together. Sex × Social Style interaction for Friendliness (β = .08, p = .015). Inter-rater reliabilities .45–.89.

REFA sensitivity analysis across 2–6 factor solutions. Friendliness and Openness predicted serial cognitive task performance consistently.

## New World Monkeys

### Brown Capuchin (*Sapajus apella*) — Captive

| Study | N | # | Factors | Instrument | Quality |
|-------|---|---|---------|------------|---------|
| Morton et al. 2013 | 127 (7 sites) | 5 | Assertiveness, Openness, Neuroticism, Sociability, Attentiveness | HPQ | Structural: L |
| Morton 2014 (thesis) | 127 / 18 | 5 | Same | HPQ + behavioral + social network | Structural: M |

**Cross-species comparisons:** More similar to great apes than rhesus for Assertiveness (Williams's t = 4.27) and N (t = 3.37). Attentiveness ≈ chimpanzee Conscientiousness — supports **convergent evolution** in large-brained primates.

**Behavioral validation:** Assertiveness–aggression r = .72, Openness–play r = .62, N–attention span r = −.56.

**Social network (Morton 2014):** Centrality predicted by rank (r = .625) and Sociability (partial r = .589). Better learners less socially successful (r = −.64); driven by Assertiveness.

### Yellow-breasted Capuchin (*S. xanthosternos*) — Wild

| Study | N | # | Factors | Instrument | Quality |
|-------|---|---|---------|------------|---------|
| Fernandez-Bolaños et al. 2020 | 26 | 3 (HPQ) / 3 (behav.) | O-N merged, Assertiveness, Attentiveness-Sociability / Prosociality, Aggressiveness, Reactivity to Humans | HPQ + ethogram | Structural: S |

**Openness and Neuroticism merged in wild** (separate in captivity). Vigilance correlated with Assertiveness (r = .62) — interpreted as bold anti-predator behavior, opposite of captive pattern where vigilance loads on N.

### Coppery Titi Monkey (*Plecturocebus cupreus*)

| Study | N | # | Factors | Instrument | Quality |
|-------|---|---|---------|------------|---------|
| Rebout et al. 2025 | 54 | 5 | Aggressive, Cautious, Cool, Innovative, Helpful | HPQ | Structural: M |

**Mate similarity:** Aggressive r = .45, Helpful r = .45 (both p = .02). Speed-dating pairs higher in Helpful (t = 8.16, p = .014). Three personality clusters: gentle/cool/stable; anxious/fearful/excitable; reckless/persistent/dominant.

### Callitrichids (3 species)

| Study | N | # | Factors | Instrument | Quality |
|-------|---|---|---------|------------|---------|
| Masilkova et al. 2020 | 65 (3 spp.) | 5 cross-sp. facets | Activity/exploration, aggression, signals, grooming, sociability | Behavioral ethograms | Structural: M |

**Phylogenetically distant species** (golden-handed tamarins + marmosets) had **more similar structures** than either had with cotton-top tamarins — ecology > phylogeny. Repeatabilities: .60–.72.

## Cetaceans

### Bottlenose Dolphin (*Tursiops truncatus*)

| Study | N | # | Factors | Instrument | Quality |
|-------|---|---|---------|------------|---------|
| Morton et al. 2021 | 134 (15 facilities, 8 countries) | 4 | Openness, **Directedness** (high C / low N), Sociability (E+A blend), Disagreeableness | DPQ 49 items | Structural: L |

## Humans — Factor Structure Studies

### The Standard Big Five (FFM)

The canonical five-factor model (N, E, O, A, C) with 30 facets (6 per domain) is the default personality taxonomy in the bibliography, operationalized primarily via the NEO-PI-R (Costa & McCrae 1992) and BFI-2 (Soto & John 2017). Numerous studies replicate this structure; only those with novel structural findings are listed below.

**Cross-cultural replication:**
- McCrae & Costa 1997 (N = 7,134, 6 cultures): NEO-PI-R facet structure replicates. Quality: Prestige XL.
- McCrae et al. 2004: Replicates in Russian and Czech (Slavic cultures).
- Yamagata et al. 2006 (N = 1,910 twin pairs; Canada, Germany, Japan): Phenotypic, genetic, and environmental factor structures highly congruent across 3 countries. **Big Five emerge from genetic covariance.** Quality: Structural XL.

**BFI-2 development (Soto & John 2017):**
- N ~ 3,600, 3 studies. Hierarchical 5 domains + 15 facets (3 per domain). Quality: Structural XL.

**Genetic factor structure within domains (Jang et al. 2002):**
- N = 1,255 twin pairs (Canada + Germany). Multiple genetic factors within each Big Five domain; supports hierarchical model with genetically distinct facets. NEO-PI-R 30 facets. Quality: Structural L.

### Big Five Aspect Scales (BFAS) — 10 Aspects

| Study | N | # | Factors | Instrument | Quality |
|-------|---|---|---------|------------|---------|
| DeYoung, Quilty & Peterson 2007 | 481 + 480 | 10 | **Volatility/Withdrawal** (N), **Enthusiasm/Assertiveness** (E), **Intellect/Openness** (O/I), **Compassion/Politeness** (A), **Industriousness/Orderliness** (C) | NEO-PI-R facets → 100-item BFAS | Structural: L |
| Weisberg, DeYoung & Hirsh 2011 | ~900 | 10 | Same | BFAS | Structural: M |
| Haehner et al. 2026 | 4,492 (5 waves) | 10 | Same (German, French, Italian) | BFAS, BFAS-40 | Structural: L |
| Ludeke et al. 2019 | 1,586 (4 samples) | 10 → 6 | BFAS aspects mapped to HEXACO domains | BFAS + HEXACO-PI-R | Structural: L |

**DeYoung et al. 2007:** Factor analysis of 6 NEO-PI-R facets within each domain consistently yielded 2-factor solutions. Aspects correspond to genetically distinct variance components (cross-referenced with Jang et al. 2002). The 10 aspects occupy an intermediate hierarchical level between facets and domains.

**Ludeke et al. 2019 (BFAS–HEXACO bridge):** Politeness maps to Honesty-Humility (r ~ .51); Compassion to HEXACO-A (r ~ .58). Shows how 10 aspects translate across the two major personality taxonomies.

### Metatraits / Higher-Order Factors (Big Two)

| Study | N | # | Factors | Quality |
|-------|---|---|---------|---------|
| Digman 1997 | Review | 2 | **Alpha/Stability** (N⁻, A, C) and **Beta/Plasticity** (E, O) | Prestige: XL |
| DeYoung 2006 | Multi-informant | 2 | Stability and Plasticity | Prestige: L |
| Ashton, Lee, Goldberg & de Vries 2009 | Simulation + empirical | — | Argues Alpha/Beta and GFP are **artifacts** of evaluative content and acquiescence bias | Prestige: XL |
| Thalmayer et al. 2025 | 2,496 + 61,760 | 2 | **Communion/Social Self-Regulation** and **Agency/Dynamism** | Structural: XL |
| Ringwald et al. 2024 | 645 (5 waves, ages 14–23) | 2+5+15 | Stability/Plasticity → 5 domains → 15 BFI-2 facets (codevelopment tracked) | Developmental: L |

**Thalmayer et al. 2025 (Cross-Cultural Big Two):** Derived from 11 global lexical studies. Communion/SSR = warmth, morality, industriousness; Agency/Dynamism = competence, sociability, positive mood. 45-item CCB2I instrument.

**Ashton et al. 2009 critique:** Blended scales produce spurious higher-order structure even from uncorrelated primary factors. Challenges the reality of both metatraits and GFP.

### HEXACO (6 factors)

| Study | N | # | Factors | Instrument | Quality |
|-------|---|---|---------|------------|---------|
| Ashton & Lee 2007 (review, 12 languages) | Varies | 6 | **Honesty-Humility**, Emotionality, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Conscientiousness, Openness | HEXACO-PI | Prestige: XL |
| Ashton, Lee & Goldberg 2004 (English lexical) | 310 | 6 (or 7) | Big Five + H-H (+ Religiosity at 7) | 1,710 adjectives | Structural: M |
| Ashton & Lee 2009 (HEXACO-60) | 1,670 | 6 | HEXACO domains | HEXACO-60 | Structural: M |

## Cross-Cutting Patterns

### 1. Dominance is ubiquitous in nonhuman primates, absent in humans
Every great ape and most monkey studies recover a Dominance factor with no direct FFM equivalent. Likely reflects centrality of social rank in primate life.

### 2. Conscientiousness is evolutionarily recent
- **Absent:** orangutans (Weiss et al. 2006), wild chimpanzees (Weiss 2025), dolphins (Morton et al. 2021), cats (Litchfield et al. 2017)
- **Present:** bonobos and captive chimpanzees
- May require complex, stable social environments to emerge as a distinct dimension

### 3. Wild vs. captive yields different structures
- Wild chimps: 4 factors (not 6); C and N absent
- Wild capuchins: 3 factors; O-N merged
- Captivity may impose environmental regularity enabling finer personality differentiation

### 4. Ecology shapes structure as much as phylogeny
- Macaque structures map to social style, not clade (Adams et al. 2015)
- Callitrichid structures violate phylogenetic predictions (Masilkova et al. 2020)
- Human factor count varies with socioecological niche diversity (Smaldino et al. 2019)

### 5. Only 2–3 factors are pan-culturally robust in humans
- **Consistently replicate:** Extraversion, Agreeableness, Conscientiousness
- **Do NOT cohere cross-linguistically:** Neuroticism, Openness/Intellect (De Raad et al. 2010; Cutler & Condon 2022)
- NLP semantic analysis confirms only 3 factors recoverable from language structure

### 6. Friendliness/Sociability and Openness are the most phylogenetically conserved primate dimensions
Appear across great apes, Old World monkeys, New World monkeys, and cetaceans.

### 7. Higher-order factors (metatraits, GFP) remain contested
- Digman 1997 / DeYoung 2006 propose Stability and Plasticity
- Ashton et al. 2009 argue these are statistical artifacts
- Thalmayer et al. 2025 propose alternative two-factor structure (Communion/Agency) from global lexical evidence
- Samo et al. 2026 find 3 metatraits (including novel Disinhibition) via bottom-up TGA

### 8. Bottom-up approaches challenge top-down Big Five
- Samo et al. 2026: TGA yields 6 traits ≠ Big Five (Sociability, Integrity, Impulsivity are new)
- Irwing et al. 2024: 70 facets go well beyond the 30 NEO-PI-R facets
- Henry & Mottus 2025: >60 personality nuances fall outside Big Five/HEXACO coverage
