# Structured Extractions — Prior Proposals of a Third Metatrait

Faithful, per-paper extractions of repo papers that propose a third higher-order factor or metatrait alongside Stability/Plasticity (Alpha/Beta), or that offer a substantively interpreted three-factor higher-order solution. No cross-paper synthesis; each section reports only what the paper itself claims, finds, and argues.

Ordering: Tier 1 first, then Tier 2; chronological within each tier.

---

## Tier 1 — Papers proposing a third higher-order factor alongside Stability/Plasticity (or Alpha/Beta)

---

## Schreiber & Marcus (2020) — Place of the Dark Triad in general personality

**Citation:** Schreiber, A., & Marcus, B. (2020). The place of the 'Dark Triad' in general models of personality: Some meta-analytic clarification. *Psychological Bulletin, 146*(11), 1021–1041. https://doi.org/10.1037/bul0000299

**Tier:** 1 — Argues meta-analytically that the Dark-Triad common core forms a higher-order factor "D" that lies at the same level as, and is not absorbed by, Digman's Alpha (Stability) and Beta (Plasticity).

**Third construct proposed:** A higher-order "D" factor representing the common variance of narcissism, Machiavellianism, and psychopathy. Content is callous-manipulative antagonism plus low constraint; under HEXACO it is anchored most strongly by the low pole of Honesty-Humility (latent ρ ≈ –.80). The authors explicitly position D as a third higher-order factor sitting beside Alpha and Beta, while denying that any single general factor of personality (GFP) sits above all three.

**Theoretical motivation:** Recent meta-analyses had described Dark-Triad–FFM/HEXACO links only at the bivariate level, leaving unsettled whether D is (a) a redundant restatement of Alpha (low Stability), (b) a blend of Alpha and Beta, or (c) a distinct higher-order construct. The authors argue that bivariate correlations cannot adjudicate this and that two-stage SEM is required to determine where D lives in the higher-order space.

**Empirical evidence:** Two-stage SEM on a pooled correlation matrix of *k* = 108 samples (total *N* = 40,818). Findings: (a) all three DT traits load substantially on a higher-order D factor; (b) D cannot be reproduced by Alpha plus Beta — model fit requires a separate higher-order factor; (c) no general factor underlies Alpha, Beta, and D jointly; (d) Beta cross-loads onto narcissism (the "bright" facet), while D-core callous content sits outside Beta; (e) when HEXACO is added, the higher-order structure is preserved and low Honesty-Humility becomes an integral part of D. Moderator analyses: original DT measures and the Short Dark Triad show structural equivalence; the Dirty Dozen shows reduced equivalence.

**Sample:** Meta-analytic — *k* = 108 samples, total *N* = 40,818. Composition described as a heterogeneous mix of student, community, and online samples drawn from prior DT primary studies.

**Instrument:** NPI, MACH-IV, SRP, SD3, Dirty Dozen × NEO-PI/FFI, BFI, IPIP × HEXACO-PI-R/HEXACO-60.

**Statistical model:** Two-stage SEM (TSSEM) on pooled correlation matrices; competing higher-order CFAs comparing models with vs. without a separate D factor and with vs. without a top-level general factor.

**Reception:** The repo contains both engagement and critical context. Samo et al. (2026, in this repo) cite Schreiber & Marcus's argument that D sits outside Alpha/Beta and connect their own "Disinhibition" meta-trait to the same externalizing/Honesty-Humility space. Beyond this, the repo does not contain a clear citation network for this paper, so I cannot say from the repo alone how widely the D-as-third-higher-order claim has been adopted vs. challenged. The authors recommend integrating DT into HEXACO rather than treating it as fully separate, which positions the proposal as additive to existing taxonomy rather than as a replacement.

**Methodological wrinkles:** TSSEM aggregates heterogeneous primary instruments and reporting conventions; equivalence across DT measures was not perfect (Dirty Dozen as outlier). The cross-loading of Beta onto narcissism complicates a clean reading of D as orthogonal to Plasticity. The argument that "D is not covered by Alpha and Beta" is a model-comparison claim, sensitive to the particular CFA specifications. Self-report only.

**Direct quotes (≤3, ≤40 words each):**
- "Common elements among DT traits constitute a higher-order factor D that is not covered by Alpha and Beta."
- "No common general factor underlies all three higher-order traits."
- "After adding HEXACO dimensions, the higher-order structure remains basically intact, but the low pole of HEXACO honesty-humility becomes an integral part of higher-order D."

**Page references:** Abstract (p. 2 of preprint); higher-order modelling and D-vs-Alpha/Beta argument run through the Results and Discussion of the published version (pp. 1021–1041).

## Samo, Garrido, Abad, Golino, McAbee, & Christensen (2026) — Revisiting the IPIP-NEO hierarchy with Taxonomic Graph Analysis

**Citation:** Samo, A., Garrido, L. E., Abad, F. J., Golino, H., McAbee, S. T., & Christensen, A. P. (2026). Revisiting the IPIP-NEO personality hierarchy with taxonomic graph analysis. *European Journal of Personality, 40*(2), 369–390. https://doi.org/10.1177/08902070251352590

**Tier:** 1 — Explicitly proposes a novel third meta-trait, *Disinhibition*, alongside Stability and Plasticity, derived bottom-up from item-level network analysis of the 300-item IPIP-NEO.

**Third construct proposed:** *Disinhibition* — a third meta-trait at the top of an empirically recovered 3-level hierarchy (28 facets → 6 traits → 3 meta-traits). Content: composed of two second-level dimensions, *Integrity* (Honesty + Fairness + low Manipulativeness, with content drawn from theoretical Agreeableness-Morality and Conscientiousness-Dutifulness facets) and *Impulsivity* (Recklessness, Excitement-seeking, Cautiousness, Immoderation; spanning Conscientiousness, Extraversion, and Neuroticism content). The authors describe it as "a broad dispositional tendency characterized by reduced behavioral and emotional regulation, integrating aspects of both externalizing and self-regulatory processes."

**Theoretical motivation:** Top-down approaches impose Big-Five structure on item pools and never test whether the same hierarchy emerges from below. The authors argue that conventional psychometric procedures (item-total correlations, dimension-by-dimension factor analysis, theoretically parceled facet scores) silo items, hide cross-domain relations, and obscure structures that only appear when the entire item pool is analyzed simultaneously and when local independence violations and wording effects are explicitly handled. They argue that Disinhibition is exactly the kind of broad regulatory dimension that gets invisibly redistributed across A, C, and E in top-down treatments and re-emerges as a coherent meta-trait when items are allowed to associate freely.

**Empirical evidence:** TGA (Taxonomic Graph Analysis), a 7-step network psychometrics pipeline addressing local independence, wording effects, dimensionality, and structural robustness, applied to the 300-item IPIP-NEO. Yields: 28 first-level dimensions (from 221 retained items, 79 dropped), 6 second-level dimensions (Neuroticism, Conscientiousness, Openness, Sociability [E+A mix], Integrity, Impulsivity), and 3 third-level meta-traits (Stability, Plasticity, Disinhibition). A fourth level (general factor of personality) was not supported (uniqueness *u* = 0.49). Stability and Plasticity retained their conventional content despite Agreeableness not emerging as a distinct second-level dimension. Disinhibition is composed of Integrity + Impulsivity. The authors link the proposal to prior work on Disinhibition vs. Constraint as a "major superordinate dimension spanning normal and maladaptive traits" and connect it to externalizing pathology and HEXACO Honesty-Humility.

**Sample:** *N* = 149,337 from the open-source Johnson IPIP-NEO repository (single-country U.S. sample).

**Instrument:** IPIP-NEO-300 (300 items; Costa & McCrae base, IPIP item pool).

**Statistical model:** Taxonomic Graph Analysis — a network psychometrics pipeline combining Unique Variable Analysis (local-independence detection), wording-effect mitigation, Exploratory Graph Analysis (community detection on partial-correlation networks), and bootstrap stability assessment; applied iteratively to recover hierarchical levels.

**Reception:** Published 2026, so reception cannot be inferred from this repo. The paper itself situates Disinhibition within an existing literature it cites (e.g., Mullins-Sweatt et al., 2019; Sharma, Markon, & Clark, 2014; Markon, Krueger, & Watson, 2005) on Disinhibition/Constraint as a superordinate dimension, framing the contribution as confirming a structurally recurring construct in a Big-Five item pool rather than inventing one ex nihilo.

**Methodological wrinkles:** Single-country U.S. sample; single instrument (IPIP-NEO) — the authors flag mono-operation bias and call for replication on adjective and behavior-based instruments. Only 16 of 30 theoretical facets emerged empirically (53.3%), so the recovered hierarchy is a substantial reorganization of, not a re-confirmation of, IPIP-NEO scoring. Agreeableness did not emerge as a distinct second-level dimension. The TGA framework itself is recently developed and the paper acknowledges that no prior application has used item pools as large as IPIP-NEO. No general factor was supported.

**Direct quotes (≤3, ≤40 words each):**
- "The final third-level dimension, Disinhibition, is a novel addition to the IPIP-NEO and Big Few personality hierarchies, and is composed of Integrity and Impulsivity."
- "This structure aligns with prior hierarchical models of personality that have consistently identified Disinhibition versus Constraint as a major superordinate dimension."
- "Our results do not support a single, fourth-level dimension (i.e., a general factor of personality)."

**Page references:** Hierarchy summary p. 380, Figure 3; second-level structure pp. 381–382; third-level structure pp. 382–383 (Disinhibition discussion); limitations p. 383.

## Markon, Krueger, & Watson (2005) — Integrative hierarchy of normal and abnormal personality

**Citation:** Markon, K. E., Krueger, R. F., & Watson, D. (2005). Delineating the structure of normal and abnormal personality: An integrative hierarchical approach. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 88*(1), 139–157. https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.88.1.139

**Tier:** 2 — Recovers a five-level personality hierarchy (Big Two → Big Three → Big Four → Big Five) and offers explicit substantive interpretations of the three-factor higher-order level: Negative Emotionality, Disinhibition, and Positive Emotionality. The Big Two is Digman's Alpha/Beta; the Big Three sits below it but above the Big Five and is treated as a substantive level, not a methodological artifact.

**Third construct proposed:** *Disinhibition* as the third factor at the Big Three level of the hierarchy. Content: high Disinhibition reflects callousness, manipulativeness, oppositionality, conduct problems, low Conscientiousness, and low Agreeableness; at the Big Four level it bifurcates into "Disagreeable Disinhibition" (mapped to A) and "Unconscientious Disinhibition" (mapped to C). At the top of the hierarchy, Disinhibition + Negative Emotionality both load on Alpha (path coefficients .71 and .71 in Study 1), and Positive Emotionality sits under Beta. Thus the Big Three are a substantive intermediate level *between* Alpha/Beta and the Big Five, not a competing top-level alternative.

**Theoretical motivation:** Prior work had treated normal and abnormal personality structures as parallel but separate (e.g., Eysenck's Big Three for abnormal, Big Five for normal) and had not produced a single hierarchy showing how they integrate. The authors argue that joint analysis of normal-personality measures (NEO-PI, BFI, EPQ) plus abnormal-personality measures (DAPP, MPQ, SNAP, TCI) reveals a replicable hierarchy in which the Big Three (Negative Emotionality, Disinhibition, Positive Emotionality) is a structurally meaningful tier rather than a measurement-narrowed view of the Big Five.

**Empirical evidence:** Two studies. Study 1: meta-analytic factor analysis of correlation matrices from 52 studies. Study 2: empirical sample with joint administration of EPQ, BFI, NEO-PI, DAPP, SNAP. Across both, EFA solutions at 2, 3, 4, and 5 factors recover the hypothesized levels with high replicability. Hierarchical relations (Figure 1, p. 148): at the three-factor level, Negative Emotionality and Disinhibition both fall under Alpha (.71, .71); Positive Emotionality falls under Beta (.70). At four factors, Disinhibition splits into Disagreeable Disinhibition (under A) and Unconscientious Disinhibition (under C). The five-factor level recovers the Big Five. The hierarchy replicates across the meta-analytic and empirical samples, across normal and abnormal instruments, and across EFA and maximum-likelihood factor methods.

**Sample:** Study 1 — meta-analysis of 52 published correlation matrices. Study 2 — empirical sample (the paper reports specific *N*; from the repo summary: replication sample with joint instrument battery). Both samples include normal and abnormal-personality measures.

**Instrument:** EPQ, BFI, NEO-PI, DAPP, SNAP, MPQ, TCI (across studies).

**Statistical model:** Exploratory factor analysis (varimax rotation) at 2-, 3-, 4-, and 5-factor levels, with maximum-likelihood replication; correlations between subordinate and superordinate factors used to construct the hierarchy diagram (Figure 1).

**Reception:** This paper is widely cited within the repo. Samo et al. (2026) cite it explicitly when situating their own Disinhibition meta-trait as part of a "consistent" hierarchical pattern. Schreiber & Marcus (2020) frame their D factor against the same Alpha/Beta backdrop that Markon's hierarchy systematizes. Broader uptake within the repo includes references in HiTOP-related work (Ringwald 2023, 2024 papers), where Markon's framework is treated as a structural foundation. The hierarchy is generally engaged positively as a structural model rather than critiqued.

**Methodological wrinkles:** EFA-based hierarchy is sensitive to the particular set of instruments included; the authors note that representativeness of Openness measures matters because narrow Openness coverage suppresses Beta–O loadings (a difference from Digman 1997). Varimax-rotated solutions are forced orthogonal at each level, which constrains the interpretation of cross-level correlations. The "three-factor level as substantive" interpretation depends on accepting that the Big Three replicates better than alternative non-hierarchical three-factor structures (e.g., Eysenck's PEN at primary level). All studies are self-report.

**Direct quotes (≤3, ≤40 words each):**
- "Our results indicate that the Big Two are related to the Big Three in that Alpha comprises Negative Emotionality and Disinhibition content, and that Beta is essentially isomorphic with Big Three Positive Emotionality."
- "The Big Three identified by Digman (1997)... factors of the Big Three 'belong at the Big Five level'... Our results indicate, in contrast, that the Big Three factors occupy a distinct level of the hierarchy."
- "It is not only the Big Five, Big Four, Big Three, or Big Two that replicate, but also their hierarchical relationships with one another."

**Page references:** Three-factor model results pp. 145, 149 (Tables 4 and 8); hierarchy diagram p. 148, Figure 1; three-factor-as-distinct-level argument pp. 148, 150.

## Coverage notes

**Notable third-metatrait proposals not found in repo:** Several proposals are referenced inside the repo papers but not present as primary sources:
- Eysenck's Big Three (P-E-N), with Psychoticism as the antecedent of later "Disinhibition/Constraint" higher-order factors. Cited by Markon et al. (2005) and Samo et al. (2026); the original Eysenck monographs are not in the repo.
- Tellegen's MPQ Big Three (Positive Emotionality, Negative Emotionality, Constraint). Cited by Markon et al. (2005) via the MPQ analyses; the Tellegen (1985) chapter and Tellegen & Waller MPQ writings are not in the repo.
- Zuckerman, Kuhlman, & colleagues' (1988, 1991) "alternative Big Three / Big Five" with Impulsive-Unsocialized-Sensation-Seeking as a higher-order factor. Cited by Markon et al. (2005); originals not in repo.
- Mullins-Sweatt et al. (2019) on Disinhibition as a broad maladaptive personality dimension. Cited by Samo et al. (2026); original not in repo.
- Cloninger's TCI Self-Transcendence as a higher-order character dimension. Items appear in Markon et al.'s analyses; the Cloninger TCI source papers themselves are not present as standalone entries.
- Ashton & Lee's HEXACO Honesty-Humility as an additional broad dimension at the domain (not metatrait) level. Repo contains Ashton & Lee 2009 (HEXACO-60) and Romano et al. (2023) HEXACO scale work, but neither argues for H-H as a third *metatrait*; they argue for it as a sixth *primary* domain.

**Density of coverage by sub-area:**
- Direct third-metatrait proposals (Tier 1): 2 papers (Schreiber & Marcus 2020; Samo et al. 2026).
- Three-factor higher-order solutions with substantive interpretation (Tier 2): 1 paper (Markon, Krueger, & Watson 2005).

**Reception patterns:** Coverage is too thin to support a strong reception-pattern statement. Within this repo, Markon et al. (2005) is the most cited of the three; both Schreiber & Marcus (2020) and Samo et al. (2026) build on it explicitly. Schreiber & Marcus frame D as positioned alongside Alpha/Beta but recommend integrating Dark Triad content into existing taxonomies (especially HEXACO) rather than treating D as a wholly independent construct space. Samo et al. position Disinhibition as a *novel* meta-trait but immediately link it to Markon's prior Disinhibition factor and to the Mullins-Sweatt et al. externalizing literature, framing the contribution as bottom-up confirmation rather than novel invention. Beyond these direct lines of engagement, the repo does not contain enough downstream citations to characterize broader field-level reception.

**Notable absences:** The repo contains no entries that:
- Explicitly compare Schreiber & Marcus's D with Samo et al.'s Disinhibition (or with Markon's Disinhibition) at the construct-validity level.
- Test a three-metatrait model behavior-genetically (e.g., a twin-study heritability decomposition of Stability, Plasticity, and a candidate third meta-trait jointly).
- Replicate a three-metatrait higher-order structure cross-culturally outside Anglophone/European samples.
- Provide longitudinal stability or rank-order continuity estimates for any proposed third meta-trait.

Cross-cultural three-factor *primary* (domain-level) structures are present in the repo — De Raad et al. (2014) propose Dynamism / Affiliation / Order as a pan-cultural three-factor kernel from 11 psycholexical studies, and Mai & Church (2023) report that the Big Three replicates moderately to strongly in Vietnamese while the Big Five and Big Six do not — but these are primary-level domain solutions, not higher-order metatraits, and so fall outside the Tier 1/Tier 2 scope. They are noted here for downstream relevance.
