# **Activation versus integration: a reconnaissance of whether binding quality is an independent personality axis**

**The field has generated substantial indirect evidence that binding/integration quality is separable from trait activation, but the direct orthogonality test — simultaneously modeling both and checking for independent predictive variance — has essentially never been performed.** The strongest evidence comes from three convergent lines: (1) the Stability meta-trait captures unique variance in white matter integrity and health outcomes that individual traits (including Neuroticism) lose once Stability is modeled; (2) Common EF, a \~99% heritable GABAergic construct, predicts psychopathology transdiagnostically after controlling for IQ and specific EFs; and (3) successful versus unsuccessful psychopathy shows identical trait activation but divergent prefrontal integrity, executive function, and life outcomes. However, the critical bridges between these lines — Common EF → Stability, residual p-factor → neural integration substrates, representational geometry → personality — remain untested. The field is under-tested rather than resolved.

---

## **Section A: Psychometric and behavioral-genetic convergence**

### **Stability carries unique variance that Neuroticism alone does not explain**

The case for Stability as more than relabeled inverse-Neuroticism rests on three empirical pillars. First, DeYoung, Tai, Chou, and Mlačić (2026, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*) systematically controlled for three artifact sources — acquiescence (via ipsatization), evaluative bias (via multi-informant ratings), and blended variables (via ESEM allowing cross-loadings) — and found that **Big Five latent correlations survived all three controls simultaneously**, reproducing the standard Stability and Plasticity metatraits. This directly addresses the strongest psychometric counterargument: Ashton, Lee, Goldberg, and de Vries (2009, *Personality and Social Psychology Review*) demonstrated that a blended-variable model fit BFAS data better than a higher-order factor model (SRMR ≈ .069–.078 vs. .138–.155), arguing the apparent metatrait reflects overlapping item content rather than causal structure. DeYoung's 2026 ESEM results, which explicitly model cross-loadings, undercut this interpretation.

Second, partialling studies reveal a striking pattern. Lewis et al. (2016, *Social Cognitive and Affective Neuroscience*; N \> 550, Lothian Birth Cohort 1936\) found that Stability as a latent factor predicted left uncinate fasciculus fractional anisotropy (**β \= 0.27, p \< .001**), but once Stability was modeled, the residual variance in Agreeableness, Conscientiousness, and Neuroticism showed **zero independent association** with uncinate FA (all βs \< ±.02, all ps \> .88). Manuck et al. (2022, *Psychosomatic Medicine*; N \= 798\) replicated this architecture in a health domain: Stability predicted carotid artery intima-media thickness, but **N, A, and C were individually unrelated to atherosclerosis after extracting Stability variance**. These partialling results are the inverse of what one would expect if Stability were merely Neuroticism relabeled — the individual traits, including N, lose predictive power once the shared integration-like variance is removed.

Third, genomic evidence is emerging. Veltman et al. (2026, *medRxiv* preprint; Genomic SEM on N \= 279,240–682,707) identified **81 genome-wide significant loci for a genetic Stability factor**, with enrichment specifically in **highly conserved genomic regions (GERP, FDR \< 0.02)** — regions under strong negative selection. Critically, these conserved-region enrichments overlap with those reported for the p-factor of general psychopathology, suggesting a shared genetic regulatory layer. This contrasts with the Neuroticism GWAS signal (Nagel et al., 2018, *Nature Genetics*; N \= 449,484), which implicates serotonergic neurons and dopaminergic neuroblasts. The divergent enrichment patterns suggest Stability's genetic architecture is not simply the Neuroticism GWAS inverted. ⚠️ The Veltman preprint is not yet peer-reviewed, and identification issues arose for the Plasticity factor (Heywood case requiring constraints).

**Critical gap:** No study has directly partialled Neuroticism from Stability and tested whether the residual Stability variance (the A \+ C shared component net of N) retains predictive power. The existing partialling evidence works in the opposite direction — removing Stability to show individual traits lose prediction. Additionally, no study has directly compared Stability-composite GWAS enrichment against Neuroticism-only GWAS enrichment at the SNP level.

### **The p-factor's unique variance: strong neural case, weak psychometric case**

The psychometric evidence for p as a genuine unique construct is surprisingly fragile. Watts, Poore, and Waldman (2019, *Clinical Psychological Science*; N \= 2,498 youth) found that the bifactor p-factor **did not incrementally predict external criteria** (personality, aggression, antisociality) beyond what correlated factors already explained. Forbes et al. (2023, *Psychological Medicine*) reported that while p showed high reliability (ωh\* \= .92) and dominated common variance (ECV \= .92), it correlated **r \= .88 with neuroticism** and **r \= .87 with impulsivity** — both activation-type constructs, not coordination constructs. Greene et al. (2021, *International Journal of Environmental Research and Public Health*) tested bifactor models across 63 population subgroups: **none fit the original bifactor model**; only the correlated-factors model (no separate p) was universally applicable.

The genetic evidence is similarly equivocal. Grotzinger et al. (2022, *Nature Genetics*; 11 disorders, average N ≈ 156,771) found that a hierarchical p-factor GWAS identified only **2 genome-wide significant hits**, while the QSNP heterogeneity test flagged **69 SNPs operating through specific factors rather than p**. This indicates most genetic effects on psychopathology are heterogeneous — they route through specific spectra, not a common pathway. GABAergic cell-type enrichment was found for the Psychotic factor specifically, not the p-factor. However, the earlier, narrower Genomic SEM (Grotzinger et al., 2019, *Nature Human Behaviour*; 5 disorders) found more genuine common-factor signal, with gene annotation pointing to **synaptic communication, dendrite development, and dendritic spine formation** — integration-relevant biology. The divergence between the 5-disorder and 11-disorder results suggests p's genetic coherence degrades as phenotypic breadth increases.

Caspi and Moffitt's own recent work (2024, *Clinical Psychological Science*; E-Risk, N \= 2,232 twins) acknowledged that p performs identically regardless of extraction method (one-factor, higher-order, or bifactor), which undermines claims that bifactor p captures unique variance. The persistent collapse of the Thought Disorder specific factor into p (observed in the original 2014 Dunedin paper and replicated since) is, however, consistent with the integration-failure hypothesis: thought disorder symptoms — the most disorganization-laden phenotypes — load most strongly on p.

Heinrich et al. (2023, *Assessment*) documented that symmetrical bifactor models produce anomalous results in **≥61% of applications**, with the meaning of p shifting across studies depending on which specific factor collapses. This substantially weakens confidence in p as a stable construct with a fixed interpretation.

**Bottom line for p psychometrics:** The unique variance of p beyond specific spectra is either negligible (Watts) or dominated by neuroticism/impulsivity overlap (Forbes). The field has not yet performed the decisive test: extracting residual p-variance after partialling specific spectra and testing whether it retains its neural integration signature.

### **Common EF bridges to psychopathology integration but has not been tested against personality integration**

Common EF from Friedman and Miyake's unity/diversity model is the cleanest operationalization of a binding construct in the individual-differences literature. Its credentials are formidable: **\~99% heritable** at the latent level (Friedman et al., 2008, *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*; 582 twins), genetically distinct from Updating-Specific and Shifting-Specific (different genes influence each), and it absorbs **all individual-differences variance in inhibition** — there is no separable Inhibiting-Specific factor. Engelhardt et al. (2015, *Psychological Science*; 505 child twins) replicated the 100% heritability finding in middle childhood.

The bridge to psychopathology is well-established. Hatoum et al. (2018, *Development and Psychopathology*; 885 twins) showed Common EF was associated with the p-factor in males and **explained 32% of the covariance between internalizing and externalizing**. Harden et al. (2020, *Journal of the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry*; 1,913 child twins) found genetic correlations of **rg \= −0.20 to −0.38** between general EF and transdiagnostic psychopathology. Gustavson et al. (2024, *Clinical Psychological Science*; Genomic SEM with N \> 100,000) confirmed that low Common EF is genetically associated with **all four psychopathology domains** (internalizing, externalizing, thought disorders, compulsive disorders), while common impulsivity showed a more differentiated pattern. Friedman et al. (2020, *Clinical Psychological Science*; two twin samples) demonstrated that Common EF and impulsivity are **genetically separable** (rg ≈ −.15) and independently predict externalizing psychopathology — they are not redundant.

**The critical missing test:** No published study crosses the unity/diversity EF bifactor model with a hierarchical personality model including the Stability meta-trait. The indirect evidence is suggestive — Common EF genetically links to conscientiousness-adjacent constructs (Gustavson et al., 2015, *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General*: Common EF linked to goal-management; Castellanos-Ryan et al., 2016, *Journal of Abnormal Psychology*: p-factor correlates with both low EF and personality traits mapping onto low Stability). But the direct test — does Common EF correlate with Stability after partialling specific EFs? — has not been published. This is arguably the single highest-value empirical gap identified in this reconnaissance. A genomic SEM analysis crossing Hatoum et al.'s cEF GWAS with a Stability latent factor from personality GWAS appears feasible with existing summary statistics.

### **Dark Triad activation versus integration: the most developed empirical dissociation**

The Dark Triad literature contains the most explicit and well-replicated activation-versus-integration dissociation, though it is not typically framed in those terms. Two structural models converge on the same architecture across psychopathy and narcissism.

In psychopathy, the triarchic model (Patrick, Fowles, & Krueger, 2009, *Development and Psychopathology*) decomposes the construct into **boldness** (social dominance, emotional resilience), **meanness** (callous exploitation), and **disinhibition** (impulse control failure). Boldness and disinhibition are uncorrelated but each overlaps with meanness. Yang et al. (2005, *Biological Psychiatry*; community sample) found that unsuccessful psychopaths had a **22.3% reduction in prefrontal gray matter** compared to controls, while successful psychopaths showed **no PFC deficit** — despite equivalent PCL-R scores. Ishikawa et al. (2001, *Journal of Abnormal Psychology*; 55 community participants) showed successful psychopaths had **superior WCST performance** and heightened autonomic reactivity, while unsuccessful psychopaths showed impaired executive function and blunted autonomic response. Same trait activation, different system integrity.

In narcissism, Crowe, Lynam, Campbell, and Miller (2019, *Journal of Personality*; N \= 591; bass-ackwards analysis of 303 items from 46 scales) identified three factors: **Agentic Extraversion** (organized, effective expression), **Self-centered Antagonism** (the shared core), and **Narcissistic Neuroticism** (disorganized, distressed expression). Antagonism is the activation dimension shared across grandiose and vulnerable narcissism; what differentiates them is whether the flanking factor is agentic extraversion (integration+) or neuroticism (integration−). Edershile and Wright (2021, *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*; EMA across 3 samples, total N ≈ 600+) found that dispositional vulnerability predicted **greater within-person variability** in narcissistic states — instability of expression as a signature of poor integration.

The meta-analytic finding from Schreiber and Marcus (2020, *Psychological Bulletin*; k \= 108, N \= 40,818) is structurally decisive: the common Dark Triad factor "D" is **orthogonal to the Stability/Alpha meta-trait**. This means Dark Triad activation level and personality integration occupy independent dimensions in the joint factor space — precisely the two-axis architecture the core question asks about. Lasko et al. (2019, *Personality Neuroscience*; two MRI samples) found that primary psychopathy was positively associated with **ventrolateral PFC gray matter density** (r ≈ .20–.30), interpreted as a compensatory neural mechanism allowing "successful" psychopaths to regulate high trait activation.

**Gap:** No genetic studies decompose trait level from trait integration quality within Dark Triad phenotypes. The narcissism neuroscience literature is extremely thin (only 34 studies total as of Jauk et al.'s 2021 systematic review, with only 2 addressing vulnerable narcissism neurally). Machiavellianism is largely neglected in this framework.

## **Section B: Neural and genetic substrate evidence**

### **White matter tracts as integration substrates show the clearest dissociation from trait-specific activation**

The neural evidence for p-factor mapping to integration hardware is considerably stronger than the psychometric evidence for p as a unique construct. Romer et al. (2018, *Molecular Psychiatry*; Duke Neurogenetics Study, N \= 1,246) found higher p-factor scores associated with **reduced fractional anisotropy in pontine white matter** — the relay station where prefrontal cortical information is integrated before reaching the cerebellum via the cerebello-thalamo-cortical circuit (CTCC). This finding **replicated** in the Dunedin population-representative cohort (Romer et al., 2021, *Molecular Psychiatry*; N \= 875, β \= −0.092, p \= .005). The CTCC is precisely the circuit responsible for coordinating, monitoring, and binding information across modalities.

Hinton et al. (2019, *NeuroImage: Clinical*; 410 young adult twins) provided the closest approximation to a dissociation test using bifactor modeling. The general p-factor was uniquely associated with FA in the **body of the corpus callosum** (the primary interhemispheric integration tract), while specific externalizing mapped to splenium, corona radiata, and internal capsule, and specific internalizing mapped to the fornix. **Different factors, different tracts** — supporting the idea that the general/binding dimension and specific/activation dimensions have separable neural substrates.

Neumann et al. (2020, *Journal of the American Academy of Child and Adolescent Psychiatry*; Generation R, N \= 3,030) found global white matter microstructure predicted the general psychopathology factor (β \= −0.07, p \< .01) but — counterintuitively — predicted **increased** specific externalizing (β \= \+0.07, p \< .01). This directional reversal across general versus specific factors is strong evidence that they are neurally dissociable constructs. The p-factor's association was with white matter **globally**, not restricted to specific tracts, consistent with a widespread integration substrate.

For the Stability meta-trait, Lewis et al. (2016) showed the uncinate fasciculus — connecting temporal-limbic structures (Neuroticism-relevant) with prefrontal cortex (Conscientiousness-relevant) — mapped specifically to Stability, not to individual traits. This white matter tract is anatomically positioned to serve the cross-system integration that an integration-quality measure should predict.

### **GABAergic pathways and Common EF provide a molecular mechanism for binding**

Hatoum et al. (2023, *Biological Psychiatry*; UK Biobank, N \= 427,037) identified **129 genome-wide significant lead variants** for Common EF, with gene-based analyses implicating **fast synaptic transmission: GABAergic, synaptic, and potassium channel pathways**. This was unexpected — prior candidate gene work had emphasized dopaminergic pathways. GABA is the primary inhibitory neurotransmitter mediating excitation/inhibition balance, the mechanism through which cortical networks synchronize via gamma-band oscillations. GABAergic interneurons orchestrate the precise temporal binding of neural representations across distributed cortical areas and regulate thalamocortical information flow via the thalamic reticular nucleus.

The Common EF GWAS was genetically correlated with IQ (rg \= .743) but **significantly less than 1.0**, indicating specific genetic variance. Critically, **cEF was more strongly genetically correlated with schizophrenia and bipolar disorder than IQ was** (non-overlapping confidence intervals), and predicted psychopathology even after controlling for speed and IQ via Genomic SEM. This demonstrates that Common EF's integration-quality variance has unique predictive power for psychopathology beyond what general cognitive ability captures.

The GABAergic connection creates a potential mechanistic bridge to the thalamocortical integration literature. Wolff and Halassa (2024, *Neuron*; review) described how MD thalamus amplifies prefrontal functional connectivity via VIP+ interneurons operating through a GABAergic disinhibition circuit — linking the molecular substrate of Common EF to the thalamic hub architecture.

⚠️ **However, no study has directly measured GABA concentrations (via MRS) alongside personality traits and executive function performance simultaneously** to test whether GABA predicts personality after controlling for EF, or vice versa. The small MRS literature is inconsistent: Hasegawa et al. (2010, *Psychiatry Research: Neuroimaging*; N \= 41\) found frontal GABA correlated with extraversion but not neuroticism; Winkler et al. (2019, *Translational Psychiatry*; N \= 110\) found prefrontal glutamine (not GABA) correlated with neuroticism, and GABA was nonsignificant.

### **The thalamus as integration hardware: strong mechanism, absent personality bridge**

Hwang et al. (2017, *Journal of Neuroscience*; N \= 235, Brain Genomics Superstruct Project) established that most thalamic subdivisions display **connector hub properties** capable of integrating multimodal information across cortical functional networks, with anterior, medial, and dorsal thalamus exhibiting the strongest hub properties. Hwang et al. (2021, *eLife*; 20 thalamic lesion patients vs. 42 controls) demonstrated that lesions to thalamic subregions with stronger hub properties caused **widespread multi-domain cognitive deficits** (executive, language, memory), while lesions to weaker hub regions caused domain-specific deficits. Lesion size did not predict breadth of impairment — **hub quality did**. Multi-domain lesion sites had higher density of matrix thalamocortical projection cells (CALB1+), which have diffuse cortical projections — a cellular mechanism for integration.

This is structurally analogous to the binding-quality hypothesis: integration capacity (hub quality) predicts breadth of outcome (multi-domain impairment) independently of "amount" (lesion size). But the translation to personality individual differences has not been made. **No study measures individual differences in MD thalamus hub connectivity alongside personality or psychopathology traits in a healthy sample.** Thalamocortical dysconnectivity is well-documented in schizophrenia (Woodward et al., 2012, *American Journal of Psychiatry*; reduced prefrontal-thalamic \+ increased sensorimotor-thalamic connectivity), including in first-degree relatives, suggesting a trait-like vulnerability substrate. But the personality-relevant individual-differences study has not been conducted.

### **Representational geometry: a theoretically promising but empirically nascent lead**

High-dimensional cortical representational geometry — measuring the geometric structure of neural population codes rather than activation magnitudes — offers a theoretically clean operationalization of "binding quality" versus "activation level." Fascianelli et al. (2024, *Nature Communications*; 2 rhesus monkeys, dlPFC single-unit recordings \+ RNN modeling) demonstrated that two animals with identical average task performance had **strikingly different representational geometries** in PFC, and these geometric differences predicted behavioral patterns (reaction times) invisible to standard performance metrics. Lee et al. (2022, *Science Advances*; N \= 468, fMRI) showed that greater representational dimensionality in face-selective regions predicted better episodic memory, and this effect was **specific to episodic memory, not general cognitive abilities** — a partial orthogonality demonstration.

⚠️ **No study has measured representational geometry alongside personality traits in humans.** This is the thinnest area in the entire reconnaissance. The conceptual case for representational geometry as a binding-quality metric is strong (Kriegeskorte's RSA framework, Stringer et al.'s high-dimensional population coding work), but the translation to individual-differences personality science is entirely absent. The HCP dataset, with its rich fMRI and personality battery, is the most obvious vehicle for this test.

### **Successful versus unsuccessful psychopathy provides the neural existence proof**

The most direct neural evidence for the activation-versus-integration dissociation comes from the psychopathy literature. Yang et al. (2005, *Biological Psychiatry*) showed **22.3% prefrontal gray matter reduction** in unsuccessful psychopaths but intact PFC in successful psychopaths — despite equivalent PCL-R scores. Lasko et al. (2019, *Personality Neuroscience*) found community-dwelling individuals high in primary psychopathy had **increased** VLPFC gray matter density, consistent with a compensatory model where greater prefrontal structure enables successful regulation of high trait activation. Lasko and Chester (2021, *Personality Disorders*; Pathways to Desistance, N \= 1,354, 7-year follow-up) demonstrated longitudinally that high grandiose-manipulative psychopathy was associated with **steeper increases in inhibitory control over time** — the compensatory trajectory was specific to the bold/agentic facets, not the disinhibited facets.

Nenadic et al. (2023, *Scientific Reports*; N \= 267–296, DTI) showed grandiose narcissism associated with FA in the **anterior thalamic radiation** (fronto-striatal circuits for social dominance), while vulnerable narcissism mapped to cingulum tracts overlapping with neuroticism/negative-emotionality networks. Same core antagonism, different white matter architecture.

### **The orthogonality test remains unperformed**

Across all five targets, **no study was found that simultaneously models both trait-specific activation level and neural integration/binding quality as predictors, testing whether integration predicts personality or psychopathology outcomes after controlling for trait-specific activation.** The closest approximations are:

* **Bifactor white matter studies** (Hinton 2019, Neumann 2020): General and specific psychopathology factors have differential, sometimes opposing, white matter correlates — structural analog of the orthogonality test  
* **Hatoum cEF GWAS** (2023): Common EF predicts psychopathology after controlling for IQ and processing speed — binding construct with residual predictive power beyond general cognitive activation  
* **Hwang lesion study** (2021): Hub quality, not lesion size, predicts breadth of impairment — integration metric operates beyond amount  
* **Fascianelli geometry study** (2024): Representational geometry predicts behavior beyond performance level — geometry as independent axis from activation

Evidence quality for the specific orthogonality hypothesis: approximately **1.5 out of 5**. This represents a genuine empirical gap rather than negative evidence.

## **Resolution: one axis, two axes, or under-tested?**

The field's current evidence does not cleanly favor either the **one-axis answer** (activation and integration are psychometrically inseparable) or the **two-axis answer** (binding quality operates as an orthogonal dimension). Instead, it reveals an asymmetric evidence landscape where three parallel literatures have each generated strong suggestive evidence for dissociability but have not been formally connected.

**Evidence favoring two axes (dissociability):** The strongest case comes from converging structural parallels across targets. Common EF is **genetically distinct** from specific EFs, **99% heritable**, **GABAergic** in molecular architecture, and predicts psychopathology transdiagnostically after controlling for IQ. The Stability meta-trait captures white matter integrity variance that individual traits lose once Stability is modeled. Successful versus unsuccessful psychopathy shows **identical trait scores but 22% prefrontal gray matter divergence**. The Dark Triad common factor "D" is **orthogonal to the Stability meta-trait** in meta-analytic factor space. The p-factor maps to integration-critical neural structures (pontine white matter, corpus callosum body, sensory-to-transmodal cortical gradient) that are not classical emotional-reactivity substrates. White matter studies show the general psychopathology factor and specific factors have **differential and sometimes opposing** tract associations.

**Evidence favoring one axis (inseparability):** The p-factor correlates **r \= .88 with neuroticism** — an activation construct — raising the possibility that what is called "general psychopathology" is mostly emotional reactivity variance. Genetic heterogeneity testing (QSNP) shows most molecular genetic effects on psychopathology route through specific factors, not through a common pathway. Bifactor models produce anomalous results in \>61% of applications. No study has shown that Common EF's integration variance predicts personality outcomes after controlling for trait-specific activation — the bridge from cognitive binding to personality binding is assumed, not demonstrated.

**The honest assessment: the question is under-tested.** Three critical experiments have not been conducted:

1. **Common EF → Stability test:** A genomic SEM or phenotypic SEM analysis crossing the unity/diversity EF model with a hierarchical personality model including the Stability meta-trait. Does Common EF correlate with Stability after partialling specific EFs? This is feasible with existing GWAS summary statistics (Hatoum cEF \+ Gupta personality GWAS).

2. **Residual p-factor → neural integration test:** After partialling internalizing, externalizing, and thought disorder variance from p, does the residual retain its association with pontine white matter, corpus callosum body, and thalamocortical connectivity? This is feasible in ABCD and Generation R datasets.

3. **Representational geometry → personality test:** Do individual differences in cortical representational dimensionality or geometric quality predict personality traits or psychopathology orthogonally to activation-level measures? This is feasible in HCP data but has never been attempted.

Until these tests are performed, the field has strong *suggestive* evidence for dissociability from multiple converging lines, but the formal orthogonality demonstration — the definitive test that would distinguish two constructs from one construct measured at different resolutions — remains absent. The reconnaissance verdict is that the two-axis hypothesis is more parsimonious with the existing neural and genetic evidence (especially the GABAergic cEF finding, the differential white matter associations, and the successful/unsuccessful psychopathy dissociation), but the psychometric evidence alone cannot distinguish it from a methodological artifact of hierarchical factor extraction. The resolution will likely come from cross-domain studies that simultaneously measure binding-quality substrates and trait-specific activation in the same individuals — exactly the studies that the field has not yet conducted.
