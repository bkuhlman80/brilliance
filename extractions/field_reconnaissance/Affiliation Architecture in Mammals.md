# A Two-Axis Affiliation Architecture in Mammals? PNS × HPG as a Parallel to Koolhaas — A Comprehensive Map

## Bottom-Line Answer

**No mammalian study has explicitly tested, as its primary aim, a two-axis (parasympathetic × hypothalamic-pituitary-gonadal) architecture for affiliative/attachment individual differences in the way that Koolhaas et al. tested SNS × HPA dissociation for stress-coping styles.** The closest empirical instance is Porges, Smith, and Decety (2015), which directly examined within-individual covariation of vagal tone with testosterone reactivity, but in a small all-male sample (n = 40) framed around aggression/challenge rather than affiliation. The affiliative-physiology literature is rich but architecturally one-dimensional: most studies measure either a PNS marker (RSA/HRV) or a gonadal-steroid marker against affiliation, rarely both in the same individuals with the orthogonality question explicitly posed.

What the assembled evidence supports at varying tiers:

- **(i) Empirically settled:** Vagal tone (resting RSA/HRV) shows substantial heritability (~30–60%), good temporal stability, and reliable individual-difference covariation with affiliative/social-regulatory behavior. Gonadal steroids (baseline T, E2, P4) show stable individual differences and reliable, if context-dependent, links to mating, parenting, and pair-bonding behavior across mammals. Within the prairie vole, autonomic and endocrine systems are both engaged during pair-bond formation and dissolution.
- **(ii) Plausibly inferred:** The two axes (PNS, HPG) are *partially* dissociable in mammals because (a) they have distinct heritabilities, distinct neuroanatomical substrates, and (b) the only direct test (Porges et al., 2015) found low-baseline RSA predicting *elevated* T reactivity — a negative within-individual coupling that implies some shared variance but not collinearity. A multi-axis architecture is therefore biologically plausible.
- **(iii) Genuinely contested:** Whether the mammalian myelinated vagus constitutes a discrete "social engagement system" (Porges) or is anatomically/phylogenetically continuous with vertebrate cardio-vagal control (Grossman, Taylor, Neuhuber, Berthoud). Whether peripheral oxytocin measurements index central function. Whether dual-hormone (T × C) effects are reproducible.
- **(iv) Thin/untested:** The central architectural question — whether individuals' positions on a PNS axis and an HPG axis (i) are orthogonal across the population, (ii) jointly predict a structured set of affiliative behaviors, and (iii) produce qualitatively different clusters depending on the combination — has *not been directly tested in any mammal*, including humans. The Bowlby–Ainsworth attachment-physiology tradition and the Koolhaas stress-coping tradition have not been integrated.

The honest summary is that the Koolhaas-analogue architecture for affiliation is **biologically credible but evidentially unbuilt**.

## 1. Has Anyone Proposed or Tested a PNS × HPG Architecture for Affiliation?

**No — not under that framing.** Searches across the relevant traditions reveal no paper that (a) explicitly invokes Koolhaas's two-tier dissociation framework, (b) substitutes PNS for SNS and HPG for HPA, and (c) tests the orthogonality of axes plus their joint prediction of affiliative behavior in a mammalian sample.

The closest conceptual approximations use different vocabularies:

- **Porges's polyvagal framework** treats the ventral vagal complex as a "social engagement system" but does not pair it with an HPG axis; when it touches HPG, it is through the lens of testosterone *reactivity to challenge* (Porges, Smith, & Decety, 2015), not affiliative individual differences.
- **Feldman's biobehavioral synchrony framework** (Feldman, 2007, 2012, 2017) explicitly models multiple physiological substrates of affiliation (vagal tone, OT, cortisol) but treats them as a *coordinated* synchrony system rather than as orthogonal axes. The framework is integrative, not dissociative.
- **The dual-hormone hypothesis** (Mehta & Josephs, 2010; Mehta & Prasad, 2015) is the existing two-axis hormonal architecture in the literature, but it pairs HPG (T) with HPA (C), not with PNS, and targets status/dominance rather than affiliation.
- **The challenge hypothesis** (Wingfield et al., 1990; reviewed for mammals by Marler & Trainor, 2020) frames T as a slow-axis modulator of an affiliation–competition trade-off, but does not cross it with an autonomic axis.
- **Koolhaas's own group** (Koolhaas et al., 1999, 2010; Koolhaas, de Boer, Buwalda, & van Reenen, 2007) explicitly acknowledged that beyond the SNS×HPA dissociation, "some studies also included the individual difference in the hypothalamic–pituitary–gonadal axis (HPG) and the balance between the sympathetic and parasympathetic branch of the autonomic nervous system" — but in the service of coping style, not affiliation.

**Finding (tier iv, thin/untested):** The PNS × HPG architecture for affiliation has not been proposed under any vocabulary as an explicit dissociation hypothesis. It exists only implicitly, scattered across non-overlapping literatures.

## 2. Within-Individual Covariation of Vagal Tone with HPG Measures

The empirical base is thin and mostly indirect.

- **Porges, Smith, and Decety (2015)** — *Frontiers in Psychology*, doi:10.3389/fpsyg.2015.00019. n = 40 adult males viewing violent or control sports video. Measured EDA, HR, RSA pre/during; cortisol and testosterone pre/post (saliva). **Finding:** Within the violent-video group, lower baseline RSA predicted *larger* T increases (significant negative correlation). Cortisol did not change overall. Authors interpret as "high parasympathetic tone dampens testosterone reactivity." **Architectural relevance:** This is the most direct mammalian individual-difference test of within-person PNS × HPG covariation that exists in a behavior-relevant paradigm — but the paradigm is challenge/aggression, not affiliation, and the sample is small and single-sex. *Caveat:* The correlation is between *baseline* RSA and *reactivity* T, not between two stable trait measures.
- **Mehta & Josephs (2010)** and the dual-hormone follow-ups (Mehta & Prasad, 2015; Sarkar, Mehta, & Josephs, 2019; Zilioli, Ponzi, Henry, & Maestripieri, 2015) establish that T × C interactions predict status-relevant behaviors; this is a precedent for hormone × hormone architectures but does not address PNS × HPG.
- **Koeneman, Demetriou, Boer, Boer, and Riese (2014, and related Dutch twin work)** show that HRV indices and basal cortisol are *weakly correlated at best* across individuals — a pattern that supports a Koolhaas-style dissociation between an autonomic and an HPA axis. The analogous PNS × HPG analysis has not been published.
- **Marler and Trainor (2020)** (*Hormones and Behavior*; doi:10.1016/j.yhbeh.2019.104645) review California mouse work showing that T pulses respond to social interaction in ways modulated by pair-bond status; vagal indices are not reported.

**Synthesis:** Across typical mammalian populations, there is no direct, individual-level, trait-by-trait correlational map of resting vagal tone × baseline gonadal steroids. The Porges et al. (2015) negative correlation is the lone direct datum, and it is reactivity-based. The two axes are **likely partially independent** (different heritabilities, different anatomical substrates, different temporal kinetics), but a formal independence test in the affiliative domain is missing.

## 4. Attachment Classification on a Two-Axis Plane

**No study has placed attachment classifications on a PNS × HPG plane.** What exists:

- **Attachment × HPA:** Maunder, Lancee, Nolan, Hunter, and Tannock (2006); Quirin, Pruessner, and Kuhl (2008); Kidd, Hamer, and Steptoe (2013, *Psychophysiology*); and Pierrehumbert et al. (2012) link insecure attachment (particularly preoccupied/fearful) to flatter or elevated diurnal cortisol and exaggerated reactivity in TSST-type paradigms. The effect sizes are small.
- **Attachment × vagal/HRV:** Diamond, Hicks, and Otter-Henderson (2008); Sbarra and Borelli (2013, *Int J Psychophysiology*) on attachment avoidance × HRV during separation; Bryant and Hutanamon (2018, *PLOS ONE*) on attachment-figure priming enhancing HF-HRV but only in low-avoidance participants; Dozier and Kobak (1992, *Child Development*) on physiological deactivation in dismissing AAI classifications.
- **Attachment × HPG:** Almost entirely absent. Edelstein, Stanton, Henderson, and Sanders (2010) and Edelstein, Chopik, and Kean (2011) found that attachment avoidance predicts lower progesterone reactivity to social closeness primes in women — a candidate HPG-affiliation linkage — but did not measure vagal tone in parallel.

**Architectural reading:** The attachment-physiology literature is overwhelmingly *single-axis*: insecure attachment is paired either with HPA dysregulation or with vagal anomalies, almost never with HPG steroids, and essentially never with both physiological axes in the same individuals. Different attachment classifications have not been demonstrated to map to *combinations* of fast-axis and slow-axis positions; current evidence supports only single-axis differences.

**Tier (iv, thin/untested) for the central architectural claim within the attachment literature.**

## 5. Pair-Bonding, Parental Responsiveness, Social Buffering

Here the evidence is single-axis-rich and dissociation-poor.

- **Grippo, Lamb, Carter, and Porges (2007)** *Biological Psychiatry* / **Grippo et al. (2007, 2014)** in *Psychosomatic Medicine* and *Stress* — telemetry studies in prairie voles showing that pair-bond disruption reduces HRV and increases sympathetic-parasympathetic imbalance. Gonadal steroids were not the primary outcome. doi:10.1016/j.biopsych.2007.03.011 and related.
- **Kenkel et al. (2013)** *PLoS One*, "Autonomic Substrates of the Response to Pups in Male Prairie Voles" — alloparental responsiveness in male prairie voles is accompanied by increased HR but also altered RSA; T is not measured in the same animals.
- **Feldman, Weller, Zagoory-Sharon, and Levine (2007, 2010)** in *Psychoneuroendocrinology* — peripheral OT during pregnancy predicts mother–infant bonding; vagal tone (Feldman & Eidelman, 2007, *Developmental Psychobiology*, doi:10.1002/dev.20220) of the neonate predicts later synchrony with both parents. T is not the focus.
- **Gettler, McDade, Feranil, and Kuzawa (2011)** *PNAS*, doi:10.1073/pnas.1105403108, n ≈ 624 Filipino men — declines in T predicted fathering involvement, a slow-axis individual-difference linkage to affiliation. No autonomic measure.
- **Storey, Walsh, Quinton, and Wynne-Edwards (2000)** *Evolution and Human Behavior* — expectant fathers show declines in T and rises in cortisol associated with paternal responsiveness. No autonomic measure.
- **Bergamasco et al. (2010)** *Applied Animal Behaviour Science* — HRV and cortisol drop together in shelter dogs after positive human interaction (social buffering). Both axes measured, but no HPG.

**Synthesis:** Pair-bonding and parental affiliation are predicted by *single*-axis measures (either RSA or T, rarely both). The classic challenge-hypothesis prediction — that lower T in fathers indexes affiliation — has received limited meta-analytic support (Grebe, Sarafin, Strenth, & Zilioli, 2019, *Psychoneuroendocrinology*, doi:10.1016/j.psyneuen.2019.04.013), suggesting the slow-axis story is more complicated than originally proposed. No published study tests whether the joint position on PNS and HPG predicts affiliation incrementally better than either alone.

## 6. Heritability and Developmental Stability of the Axes

This is one of the strongest evidentiary cells, supporting the *axes-as-traits* assumption.

- **HRV/RSA heritability** (Anokhin, Heath, Myers, Ralano, & Wood, 2005; De Geus, Kupper, Boomsma, & Snieder, 2007; Neijts, van Lien, Kupper, Boomsma, Willemsen, & de Geus, 2014; Wang, Su, Snieder, et al., 2009): twin-derived heritabilities of 30–64% for RSA and time-domain HRV; the Lifelines cohort study (n > 29,000 families) reports similar estimates. Reactivity heritability under stress is comparable or slightly higher; most of stress-reactivity heritability is shared with rest-level genetics (Wang et al., 2009).
- **Stability:** 24-h ambulatory HRV shows high temporal consistency (Neijts et al., 2015). RSA reactivity is moderately consistent across tasks (Wang et al., 2009: r² = .82–.88 for RMSSD across three stressors).
- **HPG heritability:** Basal testosterone is heritable (~40–60% in adult men; Harris, Vernon, & Boomsma, 1998; Hong, Wessels, Daly, Lamont, & Sahin, 2001; Kuijper et al., 2007), as is cycle-adjusted estradiol in women.
- **Reactivity vs. trait:** Tonic RSA is a stronger trait marker than RSA reactivity, which is paradigm-dependent (Beauchaine, 2015; Beauchaine & Thayer, 2015). For HPG, baseline T is more stable than reactive T pulses.

**Both axes qualify as trait-like at the population level.** This is a necessary precondition for the architectural hypothesis and is empirically satisfied (tier i).

## 8. Developmental Inputs

- **Early caregiving × vagal tone:** Propper and Moore (2006); Beauchaine, Gatzke-Kopp, and Mead (2007); Calkins and Keane (2004) — sensitive caregiving in infancy predicts higher baseline RSA and more competent RSA reactivity; maltreatment predicts blunted vagal withdrawal (Daches et al., 2017).
- **Early caregiving × HPG:** Sparser. Belsky, Steinberg, and Draper (1991) life-history work, and Ellis (2004) reviews, link early adversity to earlier pubertal timing and altered adult sex-steroid trajectories. Direct individual-difference linkage between caregiving and adult basal T or E2 reactivity is weaker than for cortisol.
- **Same or different inputs?** This is genuinely *unknown*. The most plausible reading is that the two axes are shaped by *partially overlapping* developmental inputs — early caregiving acts strongly on vagal regulation and HPA, more weakly and indirectly on HPG — but no within-cohort, longitudinal study has decomposed the variance.

**Tier ii (plausibly inferred) for partial-overlap.** Tier iv (untested) for the formal decomposition.

## 9. Comparison to Koolhaas SNS × HPA Dissociation

Two questions are nested here. First, does the proposed PNS × HPG architecture *mirror* the structure of Koolhaas's SNS × HPA model? The structural ingredients (a fast autonomic axis, a slow endocrine axis, putative dissociation) are present, but the comparison runs into a substantive complication: the **HPA and HPG axes interact non-orthogonally at baseline** (cortisol suppresses GnRH; sex steroids modulate cortisol clearance; reviewed in Viau, 2002, *J. Neuroendocrinol.*). Thus, even before testing the PNS × HPG architecture for affiliation, the slow-endocrine axis cannot be cleanly substituted. The dual-hormone literature (Mehta & Prasad, 2015) is the existing precedent for *handling* this non-orthogonality through interaction modeling.

Second, do individuals' positions on the affiliation-axis pair (PNS, HPG) relate to their positions on the stress-coping pair (SNS, HPA)? No mammalian study has measured all four axes in the same individuals against both behavioral domains. Indirect evidence:
- Resting RSA correlates negatively (modestly) with resting SNS markers (skin conductance, pre-ejection period), so PNS and SNS are not independent.
- Plateau pika (Récapet, Daunt, Cabello-Vergel, et al., 2019, *Physiology & Behavior*, doi:10.1016/j.physbeh.2018.11.027) and related field studies are explicit Koolhaas two-tier tests in non-laboratory mammals; they support behavior–HR coupling and HPA independence, but do not extend to affiliation or to HPG.

**Tier iv (untested) for the four-axis question.**

## 10. Cross-Species Evidence

### Rodents — voles (strongest single lineage)

- **Grippo et al. (2007a)** *American Journal of Physiology — Heart and Circulatory Physiology*; doi:10.1152/ajpheart.00280.2006. Established that the prairie vole has high vagal tone (atropine reveals large reductions in RSA, HRV) — making it a translational autonomic model. Females, n ≈ 12. Architectural use: provides species characterization, not individual-difference dissociation.
- **Grippo, Trahanas, Zimmerman, Porges, and Carter (2009)** and **Grippo, Sgoifo, Mastorci, McNeal, and Trahanas (2010)**: social isolation in pair-bonded prairie voles produces both autonomic imbalance and altered HPA reactivity. Both axes are engaged, but covariation at the individual level is not the analytical focus.
- **Kenkel et al. (2013)** PLoS One, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0072450: alloparental male prairie voles show RSA changes during pup exposure; provides one of the few mammalian datasets where vagal tone is mapped onto a clearly affiliative behavior.
- **Vole comparative work** (Young, Wang, and Insel; Carter and colleagues): the prairie vole vs. meadow vole divergence is built almost entirely on neuropeptide-receptor distribution (OT, AVP); autonomic and gonadal-steroid individual differences across these species are not the central architectural variable.

### Non-human primates

- **Sapolsky** primate dominance/cortisol/testosterone tradition (e.g., Sapolsky, 2005, *Science*, doi:10.1126/science.1106477) establishes that baboon T and C track dominance and social status; affiliative behavior (grooming, social network position) is increasingly studied separately. No within-animal autonomic data.
- **Maestripieri and colleagues** on rhesus macaque mother–infant relationships: cortisol, T, and behavior are reported, autonomic indices are sparse.

### Carnivores — dogs

- **Topál, Miklósi, Csányi, and Dóka (1998)** *J. Comp. Psychology*, doi:10.1037/0735-7036.112.3.219, established the Strange Situation in dogs.
- **Gácsi, Maros, Sernkvist, Faragó, and Miklósi (2013)** *PLoS One*, doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0058475 — owner-as-safe-haven effect on dog HR during threatening stimuli.
- **Bergamasco et al. (2010)** *Applied Animal Behaviour Science*, doi:10.1016/j.applanim.2010.03.002 — shelter dog HRV + cortisol after human interaction.
- **Riemer, Assis, Pike, and Mills (2016)**; **Solomon et al. (2019)** *Frontiers in Behavioral Neuroscience*, doi:10.3389/fnbeh.2019.00162 — attachment classification × physiology in the SSP. Insecure-attached dogs show higher cortisol and HR but the joint axis question is not posed.
- **No dog study measures T together with vagal tone in an attachment paradigm.**

### Ungulates

- Sheep, cattle (Wageningen/INRA tradition; Boissy, Veissier, et al.) provide individual-difference data on cortisol and HR reactivity but typically index temperament/coping, not affiliation, and rarely measure HPG.

### Other rodents (rats, mice)

- California mouse (Marler & Trainor, 2020) is the best candidate for an HPG-affiliation linkage with pair-bond modulation; autonomic measures are minimal in this lineage.

## 11. Where the Architecture Has Been Most Tested vs. Conspicuously Unaddressed

**Most tested (but indirectly):** Prairie vole (Grippo lab) — both axes activated during pair-bond disruption, but the within-animal dissociation question is not the analytical question. Human father studies (Feldman lab; Gettler et al.) — both physiology and HPG are reported but rarely in the same individuals.

**Conspicuously unaddressed:** 
- Adult human attachment classifications × HPG steroids × vagal tone within the same sample.
- Non-human primates outside the Sapolsky-dominance frame.
- Dogs: no published study integrates SSP attachment classification, RSA/HRV, and gonadal steroids.
- Heritability decomposition of joint PNS × HPG variance with affiliative phenotypes.

## Implicit vs. Explicit Testing — The Structural Gap Finding

The two-axis architecture is **implicit in scattered findings but explicit in none**. Specifically:

- Implicit in Porges et al. (2015), but framed as a single-mechanism vagal-dampens-T finding rather than a two-axis dissociation.
- Implicit in Grippo's prairie-vole bond-disruption work, where both axes are engaged but the variance decomposition is not run.
- Implicit in Feldman's biobehavioral synchrony framework, where multiple physiological substrates are listed but treated as coordinated rather than orthogonal.
- Implicit in the dual-hormone hypothesis precedent — but pairing the wrong axes (HPA, not PNS).
- Implicit in the Koolhaas group's own 2010 statement acknowledging HPG and PNS contributions, but never developed for affiliation.

The Bowlby–Ainsworth attachment tradition (developmental psychology, clinical) and the Koolhaas stress-coping tradition (behavioral physiology, behavioral ecology) have **largely not produced co-authored or even cross-cited work**. This is a structural gap: the disciplines that would jointly motivate the two-axis affiliation question have not converged.

## OXT/AVP Section (Bracketed — Sub-Questions 3 and 7)

### Where OXT/AVP sits relative to the PNS × HPG question

The user's instruction to bracket OXT/AVP from the main PNS × HPG analysis is well-supported by the structure of the literature. OXT and AVP do not cleanly belong to either the PNS axis or the HPG axis:

- **OXT mechanically modulates vagal output** at the dorsal-vagal complex / NTS (Porges and colleagues; Higa, Mori, Viana, Penatti, & Branco, 2002; Grippo et al., 2009): in this sense it acts as an upstream modulator of the PNS axis rather than a parallel third axis.
- **OXT/AVP and the HPG axis** are partly entangled: gonadal steroids modulate OT receptor expression, and OT release is regulated in part by reproductive state (lactation, parturition). OT is not fully orthogonal to HPG.
- **OXT/AVP and the HPA axis**: OT inhibits HPA activity; AVP can potentiate it (Neumann, 2008).

Best architectural reading: **OXT/AVP is most parsimoniously modeled as a coupling/modulator system that integrates the affiliation-relevant axes (PNS, HPG, HPA), not as a discrete third axis.** It is closer to being a *cross-axis coupling layer* than to being parallel.

However, this conclusion is constrained by severe measurement-validity problems described below.

### Measurement-validity problems (per user instruction to flag)

- **Peripheral vs. central OXT:** Leng and Sabatier (2017) and Leng and Ludwig (2016) and the Quintana group's empirical work (Martins et al., 2020, *eLife*, doi:10.7554/eLife.62456) establish that peripheral OT (plasma, saliva, urine) is a poor proxy for central OT release. Plasmatic and salivary OT show low test–retest reliability and are not reliable trait markers.
- **Assay variability:** Different commercial ELISAs yield non-correlated values; radioimmunoassay with extraction is more conservative but still uncertain. Many high-profile papers used unextracted ELISAs (Szeto et al., 2011; McCullough, Churchland, & Mendez, 2013).
- **Intranasal administration controversy:** Walum, Waldman, and Young (2016); Lane, Luminet, Nave, and Mikolajczak (2016); Mierop et al. (2020) — interactive intranasal-OT effects on psychosocial outcomes are heterogeneous, frequently unreplicated, often underpowered. Quintana et al. (2021, *Molecular Psychiatry*, doi:10.1038/s41380-020-00864-7) provides a measured assessment. Declerck, Boone, Pauwels, Vogt, and Fehr (2020, *Nature Human Behaviour*) registered-replication failure on OT-trust effect is the canonical empirical landmark.
- **OXTR genetic individual differences** (Bakermans-Kranenburg & van IJzendoorn, 2014) are a more methodologically stable handle, but cell-tissue-specific OXTR expression in humans remains opaque.

### Feldman cohort: the strongest OXT-affiliation lineage

Feldman, Gordon, Schneiderman, Weisman, and Zagoory-Sharon (2010, *Psychoneuroendocrinology*); Feldman et al. (2011, *Developmental Science*, doi:10.1111/j.1467-7687.2010.01021.x); Feldman (2017, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, doi:10.1016/j.tics.2016.11.007) — the strongest integrated peripheral-OT-and-vagal-tone-and-behavior program. Plasmatic and salivary OT correlate with parent–infant synchrony; vagal tone of the neonate independently predicts synchrony; OT-administration to parents alters infant physiology. **However:** the Feldman lab's findings inherit the peripheral-OT measurement caveats; the variance-decomposition question (do OT and vagal tone account for *separable* variance in synchrony?) is not the analytical frame.

### Architectural verdict for OXT/AVP

Tier iii (genuinely contested). If the measurement-validity issues are resolved, OT/AVP would most plausibly enter the architecture as a **modulator coupling layer** rather than a third orthogonal axis. The question of whether parasympathetic–OXT coupling mirrors sympathetic–cortisol coupling (i.e., whether OXT is to the PNS what cortisol is to the SNS) is intriguing but **untested as a within-individual architectural claim in mammals**.

## Map of the Polyvagal Dispute (No Verdict)

**Porges (1995, 2007, 2011, 2021, 2023, 2025) and the Polyvagal Institute position:** A mammalian-specific ventral vagal complex with myelinated efferents originating in the nucleus ambiguus integrates with branchiomotor nuclei of cranial nerves V, VII, IX, X, XI to constitute a "social engagement system." RSA is defined narrowly as the NAmb-mediated, respiration-gated cardiac signal — not as a generic index of cardiorespiratory coupling. The theory has been clarified in successive papers in response to critiques.

**Grossman and Taylor (2007), Taylor, Wang, and Leite (2022, *Biological Psychology*, doi:10.1016/j.biopsycho.2022.108382), Grossman (2023):** Evidence from fish, reptiles, amphibians, lungfish (Monteiro et al., 2018) shows that myelinated cardiac vagal fibers originating from a ventral nucleus are present in non-mammalian vertebrates, contradicting any strict mammalian-exclusivity claim. The dorsal-vs-ventral distinction in functional output is overstated; RSA reflects respiration-mediated modulation of cardiac vagal output more than a pathway-specific functional system; the equation of RSA amplitude with global vagal tone is overgeneralized (target-organ specificity).

**Neuhuber and Berthoud (2022, *Auton. Neurosci.*):** Conceptual and anatomical objections about the way the ventral vagal complex is described as integrating branchiomotor nuclei; the "polyvagal" framing should be abandoned in favor of language emphasizing brainstem-network coordination. The social-engagement-system *concept* is plausible and useful clinically; the *anatomical-evolutionary specifics* are not well supported.

**Porges (2023, 2025) responses:** The critiques rest on misreadings — the theory does not claim anatomical exclusivity, only mammalian-specific *integration*; RSA is pathway-specific in the theory's own formulation; recent transcriptomics support functional specialization.

**Architectural relevance:** The dispute does not invalidate the empirical observation that resting vagal tone shows reliable individual differences and tracks affiliative/social-regulatory behavior in mammals. It does undermine using polyvagal theory's evolutionary-anatomical claims as a *grounding* for the PNS-as-affiliation-substrate hypothesis. The two-axis architecture question can be pursued independent of which side of the polyvagal dispute is correct. (Map-only, no verdict rendered.)

## Meta-Analytic Feasibility (Sub-Question to Surface Separately)

**Could existing literature be assembled to test the PNS × HPG architecture post hoc?**

*Partially.* The components exist:
- Individual-level RSA/HRV data are extractable from many psychophysiology datasets.
- Individual-level T, E2, P4 data are extractable from many endocrine-behavior datasets.
- Affiliation measures (attachment questionnaires, parenting observations, social network position) exist.

**Obstacles:**
- The overlap (same individuals measured on both axes plus affiliation) is rare. The Whitehall II HRV/cortisol/attachment data have these elements but T is not the focus.
- Individual-level data sharing is patchy.
- Axis measurement is heterogeneous (RSA vs. RMSSD vs. HF-HRV; saliva T vs. serum T; baseline vs. reactivity).
- Studies are not pre-registered around the dissociation question, raising garden-of-forking-paths concerns.

**Realistic assessment:** A two-stage meta-analysis is feasible — (a) pool studies that report both PNS and an affiliative outcome; (b) pool studies that report both HPG and an affiliative outcome; (c) check whether effect sizes for one axis are conditional on the level of the other in studies that report both. The third step is data-poor. A definitive test would require an individual-participant-data (IPD) consortium akin to the Lifelines cohort (which already has HRV) augmented with T assays and attachment instruments. This consortium does not currently exist.

---

## Gaps in the Literature (Primary Deliverable)

Empty cells, ranked by importance to the architectural question:

1. **No study measures resting RSA, baseline T (and/or E2, P4), and an explicit affiliative phenotype in the same individuals with adequate power for a dissociation test.** This is the central gap.
2. **The Bowlby–Ainsworth–Feldman tradition and the Koolhaas–de Boer tradition do not co-cite or co-author.** The structural disciplinary gap that prevents the architectural question from being asked.
3. **No mammalian dataset has all four axes (SNS, PNS, HPA, HPG) measured against both stress-coping and affiliation in the same individuals.** This is the relationship-of-architectures question.
4. **No longitudinal study has decomposed developmental inputs to PNS vs. HPG individual differences against later affiliative phenotypes.**
5. **OXT measurement validity remains the binding constraint** on extending the architecture to include OT/AVP as a coupling layer.
6. **No dog, primate, or ungulate study integrates attachment classification with both axes.** The dog literature is the most underused mammalian model for this question.
7. **Variance-decomposition formal modeling** (mixed-effects with random slopes for axis position; structural equation models testing orthogonality) is rarely applied even when both physiological axes are measured.
8. **Sex-specificity:** Most data are male (Porges et al., 2015 is all-male). The HPG axis has strong sex-specific structure (cycle phase, contraception, parity) that has not been systematically integrated with PNS measures.

## Methodological Caveats

- **Baseline vs. reactivity:** The Porges et al. (2015) result is *baseline-RSA × reactivity-T*. These are not the same architectural construct as *baseline-PNS × baseline-HPG*. The literature conflates these.
- **Tonic vs. phasic RSA** (Beauchaine, 2015) — high tonic RSA is adaptive; excessive RSA *reactivity* (suppression) marks dysregulation. A two-axis architecture must specify which.
- **Sample sizes** in the relevant individual-difference studies (often n = 30–80) are typically inadequate for reliable correlation estimates between physiological axes (Schönbrodt & Perugini, 2013, suggesting n > 250 for stable r estimates).
- **Mixed-effects modeling** with appropriate variance decomposition is rare; most studies use simple correlation or moderated regression.
- **Affiliation vs. prosociality:** The distinction (affiliation = pair-bonding, attachment, social engagement; prosociality = cooperation, trust, generosity) is often elided. Different physiological correlates may apply.
- **Sex, age, cycle, circadian timing:** HPG measures are highly sensitive; many published studies report inadequate control. Cycle-phase synchronization is rare. Salivary vs. serum T comparability is imperfect.
- **HPA–HPG non-orthogonality** at baseline complicates substituting HPG for HPA in the Koolhaas frame; the dual-hormone literature has shown that interaction modeling is essential.
- **Heterogeneity of affiliation measurement:** AAI, ECR, Strange Situation, parenting observation, social network position, romantic-pair-bond questionnaires — these likely tap partially distinct phenotypes.
- **Publication bias and replication concerns** are substantial in both the polyvagal-applications literature and the OT-behavior literature.
- **Most polyvagal-applications studies are *not* explicitly framed as testing a two-axis architecture**, which means architectural claims are inferred from data collected for other purposes.
