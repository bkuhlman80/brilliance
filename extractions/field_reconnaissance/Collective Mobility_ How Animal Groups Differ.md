# **A Theory-Neutral Reconnaissance of Vectors Along Which Animal Groups Differ in Collective Mobility**

## **Orientation**

This inventory reports how the movement-ecology, behavioural-ecology and collective-behaviour literatures of roughly 2015–2026 organise variation in the mobility of animal groups across land, water and air. A caveat is warranted at the outset: the recent literature does *not* present a single, unified typology of "group mobility." Instead, three partially overlapping research programmes dominate:

1. A **movement-ecology programme** (descendants of [Nathan et al. 2008](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0800375105)) that indexes populations along continuous movement metrics (displacement, tortuosity, periodicity, residency) and derives syndromes from them ([Abrahms et al. 2017, Movement Ecology](https://movementecologyjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40462-017-0104-2); [Seidel et al. 2022](https://movementecologyjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40462-022-00365-y)).  
2. A **migration-biology programme** (descendants of Dingle, Newton, Fryxell) that produces categorical typologies — obligate/facultative, complete/partial, differential, leapfrog/chain, altitudinal, irruptive, diadromous, capital/income — whose meaning is largely domain-specific.  
3. A **collective-motion programme** (Couzin, Krause, Vicsek, Berdahl, Strandburg-Peshkin, Farine, King, Sueur) that treats the group itself as the unit and parameterises it by polarisation, cohesion, alignment, leadership distribution and information-pooling.

These programmes do not map cleanly onto each other. The vectors below are compiled from all three, ranked by how widely they are invoked in recent reviews; each is flagged as either **theoretically load-bearing** (does real explanatory work in multiple synthesis pieces) or **descriptive convenience** (recurs as terminology but rarely anchors causal argument). Where competing typologies coexist, I report them side by side rather than harmonising them.

A second caveat: many vectors are defined on individuals and then aggregated to groups (e.g. migratory connectivity, dispersal syndrome). The literature rarely provides strictly "group-level" analogues; authors typically treat the group as the carrier of whatever individual-level variation defines the vector. I follow that convention.

## **Ranked Inventory of Vectors**

### **1\. Residency vs. migration vs. nomadism vs. dispersal (the "movement-type spectrum")**

**Definition.** The core partition of spatial behaviour over a lifetime into (a) range residency — remaining within a bounded home range, (b) seasonal migration — repeated round-trips between discrete ranges, (c) nomadism — irregular wandering tracking unpredictable resources, and (d) dispersal — once- or few-in-a-lifetime directed relocation.

**Type.** Hybrid: traditionally categorical, increasingly operationalised as a continuous parameter space.

**Scholarly basis.** This is the single most invoked axis in recent movement-ecology synthesis. [Teitelbaum & Mueller (2019, *Trends in Ecology & Evolution*)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169534719300527) argue explicitly for nomadism as a distinct type along the frequency–predictability spectrum, distinguishing full, seasonal, phase, irruptive and partial nomadism, with environmental variability (low intra-annual vs. high inter-annual) providing the functional logic. [Abrahms et al. (2017, *Movement Ecology*)](https://movementecologyjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40462-017-0104-2) derived four idealised syndromes — central-place foraging, territoriality, nomadism, migration — directly from GPS metrics across 13 vertebrates and recovered them as statistically distinct clusters. [Kauffman et al. (2021, *Annual Review of Ecology, Evolution, and Systematics*)](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-012021-011516) use the same partition as the organising spine of ungulate-migration research. [Singh et al. (2012)](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/224960764) had earlier shown that the same moose populations shift between migration, nomadism and residency along a latitudinal gradient — empirically establishing the continuum interpretation.

**Domain applicability.** Cross-domain and load-bearing in all three media. Terrestrial ungulates ([Kauffman et al. 2021](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-012021-011516)), pelagic fish and marine megafauna ([Sequeira et al. 2018, *PNAS*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1716137115)), and birds ([Newton 2012, *Journal of Ornithology*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10336-011-0765-3)) are all mapped onto this spectrum.

**Contested edges.** The key live dispute is whether nomadism is a distinct type or a noisy form of facultative migration; Teitelbaum & Mueller argue the former on pattern-plus-driver grounds, but Newton and the bird-migration tradition treat irregular wandering as one pole of a migration continuum rather than a separate kind ([Newton 2006, *Ardea*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/287833436)). Dispersal (once-in-a-lifetime) and migration (repeated) are distinguished chiefly by periodicity, yet partially migratory populations and dispersive migrations blur that line ([Chapman et al. 2011, *Oikos*](https://nsojournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1600-0706.2011.20131.x)). **Status: load-bearing.**

### **2\. Partial migration (proportion migrating within a population)**

**Definition.** The coexistence within one population of migrant and resident individuals, quantified as the migrant fraction and/or as the distribution of distances moved.

**Type.** Continuous index (proportion), often sub-categorised by mechanism (obligate, facultative, conditional).

**Scholarly basis.** A major synthesis programme: [Chapman et al.'s 2011 *Oikos* thematic issue](https://nsojournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1600-0706.2011.20131.x) established "partial migration" as ubiquitous across fish, birds and mammals; [Chapman et al. (2012, *Journal of Fish Biology*)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1095-8649.2012.03349.x) classifies fish partial migration into non-breeding, breeding and skipped-breeding subtypes. [Hsiung et al. (2018, *Biological Reviews*)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/brv.12435) use partial migration as a central axis of their altitudinal-migration review. [Chapman et al. (2019, *Frontiers in Ecology & Evolution*)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2019.00403/full) extend the framework to insects. Its theoretical importance derives from the predation–growth trade-off model, which the partial-migration literature treats as the best-supported general driver across taxa.

**Domain applicability.** Cross-domain and unusually portable: the same functional logic applies to salmonids, songbirds, ungulates and insects.

**Contested edges.** The distinction between "partial migration" (individuals differ) and "differential migration" (classes differ by sex or age) is not consistently drawn; some authors subsume the latter ([Newton 2012](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10336-011-0765-3)). Whether partial migration should be classified by pattern (who moves) or by mechanism (fixed/condition-dependent/genetic) is unresolved. **Status: load-bearing.**

### **3\. Migratory connectivity (strength of breeding–nonbreeding linkage)**

**Definition.** The degree to which individuals or populations that share one season's range also share another season's range; often quantified as a Mantel correlation ($r\_M$) or a transition-probability-based metric (MC).

**Type.** Continuous index, with standard values from \~0 (full mixing) to \~1 (tight spatial linkage).

**Scholarly basis.** Initiated by [Webster et al. (2002, *Trends in Ecology & Evolution*)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Links-between-worlds:-unraveling-migratory-Webster-Marra/6ab78de75fa01bbc960567f01e8cf719ff128827), formalised by [Cohen et al. (2018, *Methods in Ecology & Evolution*)](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.12916), and extended to insects by [Knight et al. (2020)](https://d-nb.info/1225026407/34). [Finch et al. (2017, *Journal of Animal Ecology*)](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/1365-2656.12635) showed that weak connectivity predominates among long-distance migrant birds. The functional logic is that connectivity strength determines population-level vulnerability, responses to localised threats and evolutionary divergence between breeding populations.

**Domain applicability.** Originally avian but now applied across domains: marine turtles and pelagic fish, insects, bats. Travels well cross-domain because it is defined at the network level.

**Contested edges.** Estimates are sensitive to how populations are delineated a priori ([Cohen et al. 2018](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.12916)). Whether "connectivity" is a property of individuals, populations, or a species' entire network is handled inconsistently. **Status: load-bearing for conservation biology; descriptive for eco-evolutionary theory.**

### **4\. Leadership structure / consensus architecture (despotic ↔ shared ↔ distributed)**

**Definition.** How directional decisions during group travel are distributed across members: unshared (despotic), partially shared, or equally shared (democratic/distributed).

**Type.** Hybrid: categorical types, each operationalisable as continuous initiation/following statistics.

**Scholarly basis.** A central plank of the collective-motion programme. [Strandburg-Peshkin et al. (2015, *Science*)](https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/Shared-decision-making-drives-collective-movement-Strandburg-Peshkin-Farine/02e94e5383943eb44e93cb07148ce55e2a4234bf) documented shared decision-making in wild baboons; [Strandburg-Peshkin et al. (2018, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B*)](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2017.0006) then proposed a generalised framework distinguishing local from global influence and outlining methods to infer both. [King & Sueur (2011, *International Journal of Primatology*)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10764-011-9526-7) organise primate group-travel research around this axis. [Couzin et al. (2005, *Nature*)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12520299/) is the canonical theoretical anchor; Seeley's honeybee programme (summarised in [Seeley's *Annual Review*](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-ento-033121-100228) retrospective) shows distributed consensus by scouts. [Westley et al. (2018, *Phil. Trans. B*)](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2017.0004) frame the whole thematic issue around collective decisions in nature.

**Domain applicability.** Cross-domain: fish schools, primate troops, pigeon flocks, bee swarms and ant emigrations are all placed along the same axis, though the mechanisms (quorum thresholds, waggle dance, hierarchical initiation) are medium-specific.

**Contested edges.** Whether observed "shared" decision-making actually reflects deliberative consensus or self-organising interaction rules without individual preferences is contested; the Strandburg-Peshkin framework tries to neutralise this by focusing on influence patterns rather than cognition. **Status: load-bearing.**

### **5\. Fission–fusion dynamics (temporal variability in group composition)**

**Definition.** The degree to which group size, composition and cohesion vary over short timescales through splitting (fission) and merging (fusion).

**Type.** Originally categorical ("fission-fusion societies"), now explicitly a continuous gradient.

**Scholarly basis.** [Aureli et al. (2008, *Current Anthropology*)](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/586708) proposed the paradigm shift from treating fission–fusion as a type to treating it as a dimension on which any social system can be scored. The reframe has been consolidated by [Silk et al. (2014, *Ibis*)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ibi.12191) for birds, [Ramos-Fernandez et al. (2018, *Proc. B*)](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rspb.2018.0532) with quantitative uncertainty metrics, and [Madsen & de Silva (2024, *Phil. Trans. B*)](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2023.0175), who embed fission–fusion in a complex-adaptive-systems framework emphasising scale. Fission–fusion is invoked to explain why group-level mobility is noisy, information transfer is fragmented and disease transmission is episodic.

**Domain applicability.** Documented in primates, cetaceans, elephants (both *Loxodonta* species and *Elephas*), ungulates, bats, corvids, squamates. Mostly terrestrial/aquatic; the aerial literature is thinner, although [Silk et al. 2014](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/ibi.12191) makes the case for birds explicitly.

**Contested edges.** Quantifying fission–fusion remains methodologically unsettled: association-index approaches, network modularity, and duration-of-subgroup metrics yield non-comparable scores ([Ramos-Fernandez et al. 2018](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rspb.2018.0532)). Whether "stable" vs. "fluid" groups constitute endpoints of a single continuum or separate phenomena is debated ([Madsen & de Silva 2024](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2023.0175)). **Status: load-bearing.**

### **6\. Collective-motion parameter space (polarisation, cohesion, alignment, density, speed)**

**Definition.** The suite of continuous group-level variables — order parameter (polarisation), nearest-neighbour distances (cohesion), turning-angle alignment, density, and group speed — used to classify moving groups in physics-inspired models.

**Type.** Continuous multi-dimensional parameter space; discrete "collective states" (swarm, mill, polarised flock) are attractors within it.

**Scholarly basis.** Descends from the [Vicsek model](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1140/epjst/e2016-60066-8) as reviewed by Ginelli (2016); [Vicsek & Zafeiris (2012, *Physics Reports*)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0370157312000968) provide the canonical synthesis. Biological application: [Herbert-Read (2016, *J. Exp. Biol.*)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5091654/) reviews how trajectory data yield inferred interaction rules; [Papadopoulou et al. (2023, *Phil. Trans. B*)](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2022.0068) directly compare polarisation, cohesion and shape across stickleback shoals, pigeon flocks, goat herds and baboon troops.

**Domain applicability.** Cross-domain in principle but disproportionately applied to fish schools, bird flocks and insect swarms. Terrestrial mammalian groups fit awkwardly: [Papadopoulou et al.](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2022.0068) show that baboons and goats violate several standard Vicsek-style assumptions.

**Contested edges.** Whether these parameters are theoretically fundamental or merely statistical summaries is unresolved; [Gordon's ecology-of-collective-behavior programme](https://journals.plos.org/plosbiology/article?id=10.1371/journal.pbio.1001805) argues that ecological context — not local interaction rules — is primary. **Status: load-bearing in physical/theoretical work; descriptive in field ecology.**

### **7\. Capital vs. income migration (energetic fuelling strategy)**

**Definition.** The continuum from migrants that store fuel before departure and expend it en route (capital) to those that forage continuously along the way (income).

**Type.** Continuous continuum with intermediate ("mixed") strategies.

**Scholarly basis.** [Evans & Bearhop (2022, *Journal of Animal Ecology*)](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1365-2656.13800) propose this as the first truly taxon-independent organising framework for migratory strategies, explicitly aimed at cross-taxon comparability, and show its alignment with theoretical flight-range models and its predictive leverage for climate-change responses. The Results demonstrate that phylogeny plus body plan constrain where on the continuum a lineage sits (e.g. capital migration is rare in walking terrestrial mammals because of poor walking economy).

**Domain applicability.** Fully cross-domain — explicitly covers insects, fish, reptiles, birds and mammals.

**Contested edges.** "Capital" and "income" were originally defined for breeding, not migration, and some authors resist the extension. Many migrants are intermediate, and the categorical labels may obscure more than they reveal for populations near the middle. **Status: load-bearing as an integrative axis, though its adoption is still recent.**

### **8\. Memory and cultural transmission of routes (naïve ↔ experienced ↔ culturally inherited)**

**Definition.** The degree to which group movement relies on within-individual spatial memory, socially learned information from conspecifics, or cross-generational cultural transmission of routes and sites.

**Type.** Hybrid: continuous (information content) overlaid on categorical levels (individual memory / social learning / culture).

**Scholarly basis.** [Fagan et al. (2013, *Ecology Letters*)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/ele.12165) synthesise the memory–movement interface; [Berdahl et al. (2018, *Phil. Trans. B*)](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2017.0009) integrate collective navigation with migratory culture, distinguishing individual navigation, social learning and the emergence of culture. [Jesmer et al. (2018, *Science*)](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aat0985) provided the key empirical test: translocated bighorn sheep and moose did not migrate; populations regained migratory propensity and green-wave surfing ability only after decades, implicating cultural transmission. [Merkle et al. (2025, *Phil. Trans. B*)](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rstb/article/380/1925/20240131) consolidates this into a formal framework linking cultural transmission, phenotypic diversity and migration maintenance.

**Domain applicability.** Strongest empirical support in ungulates (routes) and cetaceans (vocal cultures, feeding grounds); theoretical support in fish and birds; plausible but lightly tested in insects.

**Contested edges.** Distinguishing socially transmitted routes from environmentally cued route convergence is methodologically hard; the Jesmer translocation design is considered the strongest evidence but remains a single study system ([Kauffman et al. 2021, *AREES*](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-012021-011516)). **Status: load-bearing and rising.**

### **9\. Resource tracking regime (predictable seasonal waves ↔ stochastic pulses ↔ static)**

**Definition.** The spatiotemporal structure of the resource landscape the group tracks, classified along axes including abundance, predictability, ephemerality, spatial configuration and variance.

**Type.** Continuous multi-dimensional.

**Scholarly basis.** [Abrahms et al. (2021, *Trends in Ecology & Evolution*)](https://www.cell.com/trends/ecology-evolution/fulltext/S0169-5347\(20\)30310-4) propose six formal resource-landscape axes and argue that different movement modes (migration, nomadism, residency) map onto distinct regions of this space. The [green wave hypothesis](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rspb.2016.0456) (Merkle et al. 2016, *Proc. B*) is a specialisation of this for ungulates and phenology; [Aikens et al. (2017, *Ecology Letters*)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/ele.12772) quantify "greenscape" surfing; [Aikens et al. (2020, *Current Biology*)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0960982220308484) show the wave-like pattern itself predicts whether populations migrate. The parallel "frost-wave" hypothesis extends the logic to retreating snow; avian analogues in [Xu & Si, synthesised in Kauffman et al. 2021](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-012021-011516). A key negative finding: [Wang et al. (2019, *Nature Communications*)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-09971-8) use stochastic spatio-temporal simulations on waterfowl and find green-wave surfing is *not* ubiquitous, with only a minority of 14 populations tracking plant phenology after controlling for correlated effects.

**Domain applicability.** Cross-domain in principle; strongest in terrestrial herbivore literature and pelagic fish (where prey patchiness plays the analogous role). Aerial insectivores and seabirds are comparatively undertheorised on this axis.

**Contested edges.** The universality of green-wave surfing is contested ([Wang et al. 2019](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-019-09971-8); Bischof et al. 2012 [jumping vs surfing](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/667590)). Whether resource predictability or absolute variability is the more fundamental axis is unresolved. **Status: load-bearing.**

### **10\. Group size and composition (as it bears on mobility)**

**Definition.** Numerical size, age/sex composition and kin structure of the moving group, understood as a constraint on collective movement dynamics.

**Type.** Continuous (size) and compositional.

**Scholarly basis.** [Papageorgiou et al. (2021, *eLife*)](https://elifesciences.org/articles/59902) document a non-linear (quadratic) relationship between group size and home-range size / daily path length in vulturine guineafowl, identifying an intermediate optimum — this is the cleanest recent field test of the "information-pooling vs. coordination-cost" trade-off. [Jolles et al. (2020, *Trends in Ecology & Evolution*)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169534718300557) synthesise how phenotypic composition (personality mix, age structure) shapes collective behaviour. [Kao & Couzin (2014)](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-021-01517-w) show theoretically that intermediate sizes often optimise collective accuracy. [Fryxell et al. (2018, *Phil. Trans. B*)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5882983/) connect group-size distributions in gazelles to fission–fusion and mobility fitness trade-offs.

**Domain applicability.** Cross-domain, though the shape of the size–mobility relation is taxon-specific.

**Contested edges.** Whether group-size effects on mobility are primarily information-based ("many eyes/heads") or ecological (competition, collective swamping of predators) is disputed; most models treat them additively. **Status: load-bearing; rising in prominence because of continuous-tracking technology.**

### **11\. Multilevel / nested social organisation**

**Definition.** Whether the moving social unit is a single cohesive group or a hierarchy of nested units (core units → bands → clans → population) that move with different degrees of independence.

**Type.** Categorical (single-level vs. multilevel), with continuous sub-metrics of modularity.

**Scholarly basis.** [Grueter et al. (2020, *Trends in Ecology & Evolution*)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169534720301336) provide a unifying definition and terminology, arguing multilevel societies recur across papionins, colobines, equids, elephants, sperm whales, cetaceans and some birds. [Papageorgiou et al. (2019, *Current Biology*)](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822\(19\)31244-3) documented multilevel society in a small-brained bird (vulturine guineafowl). [Schreier et al. (2025, *Phil. Trans. B*)](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rstb/article/380/1922/20230274/45796) frame multilevel societies as performing "different tasks at different levels" — including long-range movement coordination.

**Domain applicability.** Terrestrial mammals (primates, ungulates, elephants), aquatic (sperm and killer whales), now documented in birds. Insects sit awkwardly because colonies are the obvious higher level; the framework has not been fully integrated.

**Contested edges.** Whether multilevel societies form a discrete category or lie on a continuum with fission–fusion systems is unresolved ([Grueter et al. 2020](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169534720301336)). **Status: load-bearing for social mobility; descriptive for ecological mobility.**

### **12\. Altitudinal vs. latitudinal axis of seasonal movement**

**Definition.** Whether seasonal range shifts occur primarily along elevational gradients, latitudinal gradients, or both.

**Type.** Hybrid: categorical in practice, continuous in the proportion of displacement along each axis.

**Scholarly basis.** [Hsiung et al. (2018, *Biological Reviews*)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/brv.12435) is the major recent synthesis across birds, mammals and insects; [Vander Pluym et al. (2024, *Ecology and Evolution*)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ece3.70240) attempt to build a comparative framework and argue that altitudinal migration is chronically under-detected because standard definitions exclude populations that also move latitudinally. [Barçante et al. (2017)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofo.12234) compile the bird species list.

**Domain applicability.** Terrestrial and aerial (birds, bats, some insects, ungulates). Aquatic analogues (diel vertical migration) are conceptually parallel but are usually considered out of scope for group-mobility typologies.

**Contested edges.** Working definitions are contested: fitness-based ([Vander Pluym et al. 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ece3.70240)) vs. distance/elevation thresholds. Tropical regions are undersampled. **Status: descriptive convenience at the global scale; load-bearing in montane ecology and conservation.**

### **13\. Differential migration patterns: leapfrog, chain, telescopic**

**Definition.** The geometric arrangement of breeding and nonbreeding ranges across a species' populations — reversed latitudinal sequence (leapfrog), parallel sequence (chain), or nested (telescopic).

**Type.** Categorical.

**Scholarly basis.** The classic framework (Salomonsen 1955; Alerstam) is consolidated by [Newton (2024, *The Migration Ecology of Birds*, 2nd ed.)](https://shop.elsevier.com/books/the-migration-ecology-of-birds/newton/978-0-12-823751-9) and re-tested with modern tracking: [Hedh et al. (2024, *Ecology*)](https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ecy.4379) test competition-based hypotheses; [Briedis et al. (2020, *Evolution*)](https://academic.oup.com/evolut/article/74/10/2377/6727314) document chain migration in aerial insectivorous common swifts; [Pedersen et al. (2022, *Journal of Avian Biology*)](https://nsojournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jav.02924) test chain migration in curlews.

**Domain applicability.** Primarily avian; occasionally extended to ungulates and fish but without a strong general framework.

**Contested edges.** Improved tracking has flipped prior classifications — some textbook leapfrog migrants are now re-scored, with greylag geese shifting from chain to leapfrog as a result of short-stopping ([Nilsson et al. 2022](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ece3.8740)). **Status: descriptive convenience — widely used but rarely load-bearing theoretically beyond ornithology.**

### **14\. Dispersal syndrome (phenotype–movement covariation)**

**Definition.** A correlated set of morphological, physiological, behavioural and life-history traits that co-vary with dispersal propensity within a population.

**Type.** Continuous (trait axes), sometimes categorised into disperser/resident morphs.

**Scholarly basis.** [Clobert et al. (2009, *Ecology Letters*)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1461-0248.2008.01267.x) and [Ronce & Clobert (2012, in *Dispersal Ecology and Evolution*)](https://academic.oup.com/book/27547/chapter/197538423) established the framework; [Bonte et al. (2012, *Biological Reviews*)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1469-185X.2011.00201.x) synthesise costs. Integrated with the [pace-of-life syndrome](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00265-018-2473-y) (Dammhahn et al. 2018\) for multi-trait architectures. [Spiegel et al. (2020, *Movement Ecology*)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40462-020-00216-8) extend this to within-population movement variation from tracking data.

**Domain applicability.** Cross-domain, with strong insect/spider, small-mammal and lizard literatures.

**Contested edges.** The group-level relevance is indirect: syndromes describe individuals, not groups. Whether POLS applies to group-mobility variation (e.g. in social insects) is actively debated. **Status: load-bearing at individual/population scale; indirect for group-level mobility.**

---

### **15\. Obligate vs. facultative migration**

**Definition.** Migration governed by fixed, genetically encoded seasonal programmes (obligate) vs. migration that is condition-dependent and flexible year-to-year (facultative).

**Type.** Continuous axis despite typically being presented as a dichotomy ([Newton 2012](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10336-011-0765-3) explicitly argues for the continuum interpretation).

**Scholarly basis.** Anchored by Newton (2006, 2012\) for birds; extended to general physiological regulation by [Ramenofsky & Wingfield (reviewed in Reichard et al. 2023, *Hormones and Behavior*)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0018506X23001630). Irruptive migration is a limiting case of facultative migration. Functional logic: predictability of cues determines whether hard-wired programmes or condition-dependent decisions are favoured.

**Domain applicability.** Birds foremost; increasingly fish, ungulates and insects.

**Contested edges.** Partial migration blurs the distinction: facultative partial migrants are individual-level decisions; obligate partial migration reflects fixed polymorphism. **Status: load-bearing in ornithology and endocrinology; descriptive elsewhere.**

### **16\. Colony relocation dynamics (in social insects and colonial species)**

**Definition.** Whether the group-level nest or base is sessile, periodically relocated (army-ant nomadic/statary cycle, honeybee swarm, ant nest-moving), or distributed across multiple simultaneous sites (polydomy).

**Type.** Categorical, with continuous sub-metrics (relocation frequency, distance).

**Scholarly basis.** [McGlynn (2012, *Annual Review of Entomology*)](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-030-28102-1_84) is the canonical review of nest-movement ecology in social insects. [Franks' army-ant programme](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00302894) (Eciton cycle of statary and nomadic phases) remains the anchor example, extended recently to convergent mass-raiding evolution ([Kronauer et al. 2021, *PNAS*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2026534118)). [Robinson et al. (2016, *Proc. B*)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5310590/) review methods for identifying polydomy (multiple simultaneous nests of one colony). Honeybee swarm relocation is synthesised in Seeley's programme ([Annual Review retrospective](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-ento-033121-100228)).

**Domain applicability.** Eusocial insects foremost; analogues in colonial seabirds and bats are weaker. Very poorly integrated with vertebrate mobility typologies.

**Contested edges.** Whether polydomy is "one group moving" or "multiple groups coordinated" is definitionally unsettled; the methodological review ([Ellis & Robinson 2016](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5310590/)) highlights that resource-sharing, spatial and genetic criteria give different answers. **Status: load-bearing within entomology; a partly separate theoretical universe from vertebrate movement ecology.**

### **17\. Phase polyphenism (density-dependent morphological/behavioural switching)**

**Definition.** Density-triggered transitions between solitarious and gregarious phenotypes that enable or disable collective mobility — classically, locust gregarisation.

**Type.** Hybrid: behaviourally categorical (solitarious vs. gregarious) but with a continuous density-threshold dimension.

**Scholarly basis.** [Pener & Simpson (2009)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0065280608360019) and [Cullen et al. (2017, *Advances in Insect Physiology*)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0065280617300231) provide the canonical reviews. Phase change is the mechanism that flips locusts from cryptic non-movers to collectively migrating bands/swarms. [Anstey et al. (2009, *Science*)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19131626/) established the serotonin mechanism; [Cease et al. (2010)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20493192/) propose gregarisation-risk assessment as a management framework.

**Domain applicability.** Primarily terrestrial insects (locusts, some crickets and armyworms), but conceptually extends to density-dependent switching in other swarming invertebrates.

**Contested edges.** Whether the mechanism is phylogenetically conserved across acridid lineages or independently evolved is contested ([Song et al.](https://oaktrust.library.tamu.edu/handle/1969.1/193004)). **Status: load-bearing in its taxon but rarely integrated with vertebrate typologies.**

---

### **18\. Site fidelity / return accuracy**

**Definition.** The probability that individuals return to the same breeding, stopover or non-breeding site across seasons; extended to groups as proportion of members returning to inherited ranges.

**Type.** Continuous index.

**Scholarly basis.** [Morrison et al. (2021, *Journal of Animal Ecology*)](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1365-2656.13425) synthesise drivers of site fidelity in ungulates; Börger et al. (2008, *Ecology Letters*) review home-range mechanisms; [Silva et al. (2022, *MEE*)](https://ecoevorxiv.org/repository/object/4026/download/7985/) formalise range-residency detection via autocorrelated kernel-density estimation. Newton's irruptive-migration tables (summarised in [Newton 2006, *Ardea*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/287833436)) show site fidelity differs by an order of magnitude between regular and irruptive migrants.

**Domain applicability.** Cross-domain.

**Contested edges.** How to separate fidelity to sites from fidelity to routes, and whether "site" means a point, a region or a network of nodes, is inconsistent across studies. **Status: load-bearing as a diagnostic, descriptive as an explanatory vector.**

### **19\. Range expansion / contraction under landscape change**

**Definition.** The direction and rate at which a population's collective space use is expanding, shrinking or shifting in response to disturbance (human footprint, climate, barriers).

**Type.** Continuous rate, sometimes categorised (expanding, stable, contracting, shifting).

**Scholarly basis.** [Tucker et al. (2018, *Science*)](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aam9712) is the canonical synthesis: 57 mammal species, showing movement extents in high human-footprint areas are one-half to one-third those in low footprint areas. [Gomez et al. (2025, *Journal of Animal Ecology*)](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/1365-2656.70040) synthesise follow-up work. The [Kauffman et al. (2021, *Science*)](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abf0998) Global Initiative on Ungulate Migration builds a coordinated mapping programme around contraction of migratory corridors.

**Domain applicability.** Primarily terrestrial mammals in recent synthesis; marine and aerial analogues (range shifts under warming) are handled in separate literatures.

**Contested edges.** Whether range contraction under human footprint is primarily behavioural plasticity or demographic filtering (loss of wide-ranging individuals) is actively debated ([Tucker et al. 2018](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aam9712) endorse both). **Status: load-bearing for conservation biology.**

---

### **20\. Energetic formation use (aerodynamic/hydrodynamic coordination)**

**Definition.** Whether groups move in arrangements that extract energetic benefit from neighbours' wakes (V-formation, drafting, diamond school) versus neutral/disordered aggregations.

**Type.** Categorical formation types overlaid on continuous spacing variables.

**Scholarly basis.** [Beaumont et al. (2024, *Applied Sciences*)](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/14/13/5402) narratively review aerodynamic mechanisms. Quantitative estimates of energy savings range from \~10% to \~36% depending on species and method. Extended to fish schools ([Marras et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00265-014-1856-y)).

**Domain applicability.** Primarily aerial (large flocking birds) and aquatic (schools, fish migrations). Terrestrial analogues (single-file movement, drafting in caribou) are comparatively poorly characterised.

**Contested edges.** Field studies find high variation in bird positioning within supposedly optimal formations ([Cutts & Speakman 1994; Speakman & Banks 1998](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/agricultural-and-biological-sciences/v-formation)), with only a minority of birds at the predicted optimal position. Whether cetacean echelon formations are aerodynamic analogues or primarily social is unsettled. **Status: descriptive convenience for most of the movement-ecology literature; load-bearing in biomechanics.**

---

### **21\. Naïve vs. informed group composition (proportion of experienced individuals)**

**Definition.** The fraction of the moving group that carries prior experience of the route/destination; the "informed minority" in collective-navigation models.

**Type.** Continuous proportion.

**Scholarly basis.** [Couzin et al. (2005, *Nature*)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12520299/) established that small informed minorities can steer large naïve groups. [Berdahl et al. (2018, *Phil. Trans. B*)](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2017.0009) extend the logic to long-distance migratory groups; [Berdahl et al. (2016, *Fish and Fisheries*)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/faf.12084) apply it to salmonid homing. This axis provides the link between leadership structure (\#4) and cultural transmission (\#8).

**Domain applicability.** Cross-domain — tested in fish, pigeons, ungulates (via translocations).

**Contested edges.** Empirical tests are challenging because "informed" and "naïve" individuals are rarely distinguishable from trajectory data alone. **Status: load-bearing theoretically; empirically thin outside experimental systems.**

---

### **22\. Spatial and temporal scale of mobility (periodicity and displacement)**

**Definition.** The characteristic scales of the group's movement — daily range to annual migration distance, hours to years — used as descriptive axes in comparative analyses.

**Type.** Continuous (multiple orders of magnitude).

**Scholarly basis.** [Abrahms et al. (2017)](https://movementecologyjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40462-017-0104-2) and [Seidel et al. (2022)](https://movementecologyjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40462-022-00365-y) operationalise these as metrics that differentiate movement syndromes; [Sequeira et al. (2018, *PNAS*)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1716137115) show that the distribution of speeds and turning angles across 2,600+ marine megafauna individuals converges between coastal (search-like) and open-ocean (ballistic) habitats, cutting across phylogeny. The multi-scale movement-syndrome (MSMS) framework of [Seidel et al. 2022](https://movementecologyjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40462-022-00365-y) explicitly hierarchises step → path → life-history-phase scales.

**Domain applicability.** Cross-domain and increasingly used as the skeleton of comparative movement ecology.

**Contested edges.** Whether scale should be treated as a descriptive covariate or a defining axis of movement types is unsettled; MSMS treats it as defining. **Status: load-bearing methodologically; often descriptive theoretically.**

## **Superseded or Collapsed Vectors**

* **"Fission–fusion societies" as a discrete category.** [Aureli et al. (2008, *Current Anthropology*)](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/586708) explicitly retired the categorical use, arguing that all social systems show fission–fusion dynamics to some degree; the quantitative gradient framework has largely displaced the type label in subsequent literature ([Madsen & de Silva 2024](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2023.0175)).  
* **"Hard-wired" vs. "learned" migration as a dichotomy.** Collapsed into the obligate–facultative continuum by [Newton (2012)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10336-011-0765-3) and then further reframed by the cultural-transmission literature ([Jesmer et al. 2018](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aat0985); [Merkle et al. 2025](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rstb/article/380/1925/20240131)), which treats the two as non-exclusive mechanisms acting at different phylogenetic scales.  
* **Pure "migrant" vs. "resident" dichotomy.** Absorbed into the partial-migration framework ([Chapman et al. 2011](https://nsojournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1600-0706.2011.20131.x)), which treats populations as mixtures and individuals as expressing graded strategies.  
* **"Fixed home range" as a universal assumption.** Autocorrelated kernel-density estimation ([Silva et al. 2022](https://ecoevorxiv.org/repository/object/4026/download/7985/); Fleming & Calabrese) has redefined home-range analysis around the testable property of range residency, replacing implicit stationarity assumptions.  
* **"Telescopic migration" and some of the older avian labels** have been quietly superseded or are now treated as descriptive rather than causal, with chain vs. leapfrog retained as the main useful contrast ([Newton 2024, 2nd ed.](https://shop.elsevier.com/books/the-migration-ecology-of-birds/newton/978-0-12-823751-9)).  
* **"Swarm / flock / school" as taxon-level markers.** Collective-motion physics treats these as instances of the same state in the polarisation–cohesion parameter space ([Vicsek & Zafeiris 2012](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0370157312000968)); the taxon-specific terms persist but no longer carry theoretical weight.

## **Cross-Domain Unification**

Substantial convergence has occurred in the last decade, especially:

* **The residency–migration–nomadism–dispersal spine** ([Teitelbaum & Mueller 2019](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0169534719300527); [Abrahms et al. 2017](https://movementecologyjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40462-017-0104-2)) works across walking, swimming and flying animals, and is now the nearest thing to a lingua franca.  
* **Migratory connectivity** ([Cohen et al. 2018](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/2041-210X.12916)) has been deliberately generalised from birds to insects, fish and marine turtles.  
* **Capital–income migration** ([Evans & Bearhop 2022](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1365-2656.13800)) is explicitly pitched as a taxon-independent axis.  
* **Collective-motion parameter space** ([Papadopoulou et al. 2023](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2022.0068)) is being applied to fish, birds, goats and primates together.  
* **Marine-megafauna movement patterns converge across phylogeny** by habitat (coastal vs. open ocean) rather than by taxon ([Sequeira et al. 2018, *PNAS*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1716137115)), providing indirect support for medium-transcending typologies.

Meaningful domain-specific distinctions persist where:

* **Diadromy (anadromy/catadromy/amphidromy)** is necessarily aquatic; cross-domain analogues (e.g. amphibians between water and land) are sometimes discussed but are not integrated.  
* **Aerodynamic formation-flight** literature is necessarily aerial (with hydrodynamic drafting as the wet analogue).  
* **Phase polyphenism** is a terrestrial insect phenomenon with no clear vertebrate analogue, despite superficial resemblance to density-dependent shoaling.  
* **Altitudinal migration** is a terrestrial/aerial phenomenon; diel vertical migration in aquatic systems is treated as a distinct literature despite formal similarities.  
* **Colony relocation in social insects** remains largely siloed from the vertebrate movement-ecology programme.

## **Thin Coverage**

Several areas are notably under-reviewed at the synthesis level:

* **Social invertebrate group mobility beyond army ants, honey bees, and locusts.** Termite, wasp, and most ant groups lack comparative-typological treatment; [McGlynn (2012)](https://link.springer.com/rwe/10.1007/978-3-030-28102-1_84) remains the major reference and is now over a decade old.  
* **Tropical and Southern Hemisphere altitudinal migration.** Both [Barçante et al. (2017)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofo.12234) and [Hsiung et al. (2018)](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/brv.12435) flag sub-Saharan Africa, SE Asia and South America as critically undersampled.  
* **Reptile group mobility.** Squamate sociality reviews are recent but reptile collective movement is largely absent from synthesis literature; most fission–fusion reviews acknowledge this.  
* **Amphibian collective movement.** Essentially untreated at the synthesis level beyond breeding migrations (Semlitsch 2008).  
* **Aerial insect migration at the group level.** Strong individual-level work, but very few reviews treat insect migrant aggregations as groups in the sense used for flocks/schools ([Chapman et al. 2019, *Frontiers in E\&E*](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2019.00403/full) is a partial exception).  
* **Bat movement ecology synthesis** is notably thinner than avian; altitudinal reviews flag bats as underdescribed.  
* **Multilevel societies in non-mammals.** [Papageorgiou et al. (2019)](https://www.cell.com/current-biology/fulltext/S0960-9822\(19\)31244-3) in vulturine guineafowl was the first clear bird case; the framework is extending only slowly.  
* **Cultural transmission of routes outside ungulates and cetaceans.** Fish, seabirds, bats and insects have scattered evidence but no recent comprehensive synthesis.  
* **Freshwater fish group mobility.** Partial migration in fishes is well reviewed ([Chapman et al. 2012a,b](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1095-8649.2012.03349.x)), but collective-motion and fission–fusion syntheses for freshwater systems are comparatively thin.  
* **Cross-domain formalisation of resource-tracking regimes outside terrestrial vertebrates.** [Abrahms et al. (2021)](https://www.cell.com/trends/ecology-evolution/fulltext/S0169-5347\(20\)30310-4) is explicit that pelagic and aerial versions of the six-axis resource landscape need more work.  
* **Ontogenetic integration.** How natal dispersal, juvenile dispersive migration and adult ranging combine across a lifetime is sketched in several reviews but rarely operationalised as a single framework — the Seidel et al. (2022) multi-scale syndrome is the nearest attempt.

## **Closing Reflection on the Structure of the Field**

The recent literature organises group mobility along three partially independent axes that the prompt's framing does not fully capture: (a) a *what* axis (movement type: residency, migration, nomadism, dispersal and their partial versions), (b) a *how* axis (leadership, consensus, cultural transmission, memory, formation), and (c) an *in response to what* axis (resource-tracking regime, environmental predictability, human footprint). The three generate most of the more specific vectors above. The field has not yet produced a fully integrated framework spanning all three, despite repeated calls for one — [Nathan et al. (2008)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.0800375105), [Evans & Bearhop (2022)](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1365-2656.13800), [Abrahms et al. (2021)](https://www.cell.com/trends/ecology-evolution/fulltext/S0169-5347\(20\)30310-4), [Seidel et al. (2022)](https://movementecologyjournal.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40462-022-00365-y) and [Kauffman et al. (2021)](https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-ecolsys-012021-011516) all push in this direction. The single largest conceptual move of the last decade has been from categorical typologies to continuous, multi-axis parameter spaces — particularly visible in the treatment of fission–fusion, partial migration, obligate/facultative migration, and movement syndromes. The single largest unresolved integration problem is how to connect the social-insect colony-level mobility literature to the vertebrate group-mobility literature: they use different vocabularies, measure different things, and rarely cite each other.
