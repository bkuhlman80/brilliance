# **A Map of What Is and Is Not Known: Behavioral, Ecological, Cognitive, and Social Innovations Across the Mammal → Primate → Hominin → *Homo sapiens* Transitions (2025–2026 Reconnaissance)**

## **Reading Note on This Map**

This report is intentionally structured as a *map of evidence and disagreement* rather than a synthesis. Within each sub-question, claims are sorted into **EMPIRICALLY SETTLED**, **PLAUSIBLY INFERRED**, **GENUINELY CONTESTED**, and **THIN/UNTESTED**. Where the field has clearly *moved* in the last 10–15 years (e.g., FOXP2, the savanna hypothesis in its strict form, the cooking hypothesis), the report describes the older claim, the newer evidence, and the direction of movement — without rendering a verdict on disputes still actively contested. Per the user's calibration, non-primate convergent cognition is invoked only where it materially constrains a claim about primate-specific innovations.

A few structural caveats up front:

* Several "prime mover" hypotheses (cooperative breeding, embodied capital, grandmother) are **internally consistent and empirically active but mutually competing**. The current state is multi-causal and unresolved.  
* Behavioral inferences from the deep hominin record (\>2 Ma) depend heavily on a small number of sites (Olduvai, Koobi Fora, Hadar, Aramis, Sterkfontein, Swartkrans) and a small number of methods (dental microwear, stable isotopes, taphonomy). When those sites or methods change, conclusions shift.  
* Single-author theoretical frameworks (Bingham's throwing hypothesis, Wrangham's cooking hypothesis in its strong form, Dunbar's number as a hard cognitive constraint) have all been challenged by independent quantitative work in the last decade. Where independent corroboration is thin, this is flagged.

## **Block 1 — Primates as a Distinctive Mammalian Lineage**

### **What actually distinguishes primates from other social mammals?**

**EMPIRICALLY SETTLED:**

* Primates have unusually large brains for body size compared to other vertebrates, and there is a quantitative relationship between brain size and *typical* social group size *within primates*. Outside primates, the social-brain relationship takes a qualitatively different form: among non-primate mammals and birds tested (carnivores, ungulates, bats, many bird taxa), it is **monogamous pair-bonding species** that show disproportionately enlarged brains, not large-group species ([Dunbar & Shultz 2007 / Shultz & Dunbar; Dunbar 2009 PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19575315/)). This is a structural difference between primate and non-primate cognitive ecology, not a difference of degree.  
* The deep mammalian baseline includes **lactation, parental care, hair, and endothermy** — traits that paleoneurological and comparative-genomic work increasingly trace to non-mammalian synapsids in the late Permian and Triassic, not to crown Mammalia. Lactation in particular appears to predate true mammals and to derive from apocrine-like glands associated with hair follicles ([Oftedal 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12751889/); [Benoit et al. 2023, *Progress in Brain Research*](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36841570/)). Parental care, gregariousness, and even possible parental investment are now reconstructed for some Permian therapsids on paleoneurological grounds ([Benoit et al. 2024, *Journal of Paleontology*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-paleontology/article/150-years-of-synapsid-paleoneurology-the-origins-of-the-mammalian-brain-behavior-sense-organs-and-physiology/500B0F6CD3E934B1B9D29D5EBF2B1C83)).

**PLAUSIBLY INFERRED:**

* Primates appear distinctive in extending **pair-bond-like affiliative relationships ("friendships") to non-reproductive same-sex and cross-sex partners**, not just mates. This is the primary candidate explanation for why the primate social-brain relationship takes a quantitative rather than qualitative form ([Dunbar 2009](https://www.sciencedirect.com/topics/psychology/social-brain-hypothesis)).  
* DeCasien et al. (2017, *Nature Ecology & Evolution* 1:0112) found, with a very large primate sample, that **diet (frugivory vs. folivory) predicts primate brain size better than sociality does** when body size is controlled. This was a substantial empirical challenge to a strong-form social-brain hypothesis and partially restored ecological-intelligence framings.

**GENUINELY CONTESTED:**

* Whether the primate social-brain relationship is itself robust. Several large recent re-analyses argue that the regression-line basis for "Dunbar's number" carries enormous confidence intervals — Bayesian and PGLS approaches yield mean group-size predictions that range from \~16 to \~109 with 95% CIs spanning 2–520, depending on method and sample ([critical review in Revista Internacional de Sociología, 2016](https://revintsociologia.revistas.csic.es/index.php/revintsociologia/article/download/651/758?inline=1)). Dunbar (2024, *Annals of Human Biology* "Thirty years on") defends the framework, with grade-corrected r values \>0.95 within hominoid grades, and notes 23 ethnographic and digital-network studies that recover community sizes near 150\. Critics (DeCasien et al., Lindenfors et al., and others discussed in Dunbar 2024\) argue the underlying statistical and grade structure is being reified.  
* **Whether primate cognitive performance is meaningfully different from convergent corvid/cetacean/elephant performance** in the same task families. Cognitive test batteries comparing parrots, corvids, primates, dolphins, and elephants increasingly find domain-general performance overlap, especially on physical-cognition tasks; large parrots can perform comparably to or below great apes on Primate Cognition Test Battery (PCTB)-style tasks depending on whether tasks are visually vs. mechanically loaded ([Krasheninnikova et al. 2019; review in *Frontiers in Psychology*, 2020](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.01835/xml/nlm); review of avian convergence in *Learning & Behavior*, 2020, [DOI: 10.3758/s13420-020-00434-5](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13420-020-00434-5)).

**Direction of field movement (last 10–15 years):**

* The strong-form claim that primate social complexity is uniquely demanding has *weakened*. The newer position is that primates extend dyadic-bond cognition broadly through groups, while convergent cognition in corvids, cetaceans, and elephants achieves comparable physical-cognitive and limited social-cognitive performance via different neuroanatomy ([Marino 2002](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12097858/); [Marino, *Cetaceans Have Complex Brains for Complex Cognition*, PLoS Biology PMC1868071](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1868071/)).

### **Cooperative breeding, alloparenting, and food sharing — where do great apes sit?**

**EMPIRICALLY SETTLED:**

* Among primates, **callitrichids (marmosets and tamarins) are the genuine cooperative breeders**. Captive marmosets share food proactively in over one-third of transfers during peak provisioning periods, with \~10% of all shared items going to other adults ([Burkart & van Schaik 2010, *Animal Cognition*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10071-009-0263-7); [Martins & Burkart 2013](https://zslpublications.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jzo.12322)).  
* Great apes do **not** show callitrichid-style proactive food sharing in the wild between adults. Chimpanzee meat sharing is highly variable across populations: at Gombe, sharing is best explained as "sharing-under-pressure" or tolerated theft (Gilby 2006, *Animal Behaviour* 71:953–963); at Taï, sharing tracks coalition partners and hunting roles, with hunters of more skilled roles receiving more meat ([Boesch 2002, *Human Nature*](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12110-002-1013-6); [Samuni et al. 2018, *Communications Biology*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-018-0142-3)). Bonobo plant-food sharing exists but is non-reciprocal and largely passive.

**PLAUSIBLY INFERRED:**

* The Hrdy/Burkart/van Schaik **Cooperative Breeding Hypothesis (CBH)** in its life-history form — that humans evolved short interbirth intervals plus prolonged dependency *only* with allomaternal help — is well-supported by ethnographic and demographic data ([Hrdy 2009; Burkart, Hrdy & van Schaik 2009, *Evolutionary Anthropology* 18:175–186](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/evan.20222)).

**GENUINELY CONTESTED:**

* The cognitive extension of CBH (that cooperative breeding *causes* enhanced socio-cognitive performance) was challenged by **Thornton & McAuliffe (2015, *Journal of Zoology*)**, who pointed out that (a) non-human cooperatively breeding primates have *smaller* brains than independently-breeding primates; (b) reports of adult food transfers in wild callitrichids are largely "theft by the dominant female" rather than proactive prosociality. Burkart & van Schaik (2016) replied by restricting CBH to the primate lineage and by reframing the mechanism as releasing energetic constraints rather than directly selecting cognition; Thornton et al. (2016) replied that this revised version is unfalsifiable ([Thornton et al. 2016 reply, PMC4982024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4982024/)). The dispute is unresolved.  
* Whether chimpanzee food-sharing is *primarily* tolerated theft (Gilby's Gombe data) or *primarily* reciprocal exchange among bonded partners (Taï data; Samuni et al. 2018 finding selective sharing with friends and elevated oxytocin after sharing) is unsettled. The most defensible current statement is that **chimpanzee sharing is genuinely variable across populations**, which is itself an important finding for any reconstruction of the LCA ([Wittig & Crockford lab, MPI-EVA, 2018](https://www.mpg.de/12338783/wild-chimpanzees-share-food-with-their-friends)).

**THIN/UNTESTED:**

* Direct paleoanthropological signatures of alloparenting in the hominin record. Inferences are largely indirect (life-history reconstructions from dental development, encephalization, weaning isotopes).

## **Block 2 — The Hominin Transition From Other Apes**

### **Do great apes pool food at any group level? Is hominin food sharing structurally distinctive or different in degree?**

**EMPIRICALLY SETTLED:**

* Great apes do not pool food at the group level. Sharing among adults is sporadic, dominated either by harassment-driven transfers or by selective dyadic transfers tied to bonds and cooperative roles ([Gilby 2006](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/222996278_Meat_sharing_among_the_Gombe_chimpanzees_Harassment_and_reciprocal_exchange); [Boesch 2002](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12110-002-1013-6)).  
* Hadza, Ache, Hiwi, and other extensively-studied foraging populations show **routine pooling of large-game meat at the band level**, with food flowing primarily *outward* from the most-skilled hunters ([Hawkes, O'Connell & Blurton Jones 2001 *EHB* on Hadza meat sharing; Kaplan & Hill 1985 on Ache; review in Gurven & Hill 2009 *Current Anthropology*](https://anthro.vancouver.wsu.edu/media/documents/Venkataraman2024.pdf)).

**PLAUSIBLY INFERRED:**

* The transition from chimpanzee-style dyadic sharing to forager-style band-wide pooling is therefore **structurally distinctive to hominins**, not merely a quantitative shift. It is best dated only indirectly: Taï-style coalition sharing in chimpanzees provides the "preadaptation," but no chimpanzee population approaches the band-pooling pattern of foragers.

### **When does central-place provisioning appear, and how confidently can it be separated from meat-eating, scavenging, and cooking?**

**EMPIRICALLY SETTLED:**

* Cut-marked bones documenting hominin butchery extend to \~2.6 Ma in Ethiopia and \~1.95 Ma at Koobi Fora ([Bunn 1981 *Nature* 291:574–577](https://www.nature.com/articles/291574a0)). At sites such as FLK Zinjanthropus (Olduvai, 1.84 Ma), bone assemblages show systematic butchery, marrow extraction, and concentration of skeletal material in spatially restricted areas distinct from surrounding paleolandscape densities ([Bunn & Kroll 1986 *Current Anthropology* 27:431–452](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/203467); [Domínguez-Rodrigo et al. 2010, 2014; Uribelarrea et al. 2014](https://cir.cenieh.es/bitstream/20.500.12136/387/1/Geo-archaeological%20and%20geometrically%20corrected%20reconstruction%20of%20the%201.84%20Ma%20FLK%20Zinj%20paleolandscape%20at%20Olduvai%20Gorge,%20Tanzania_Uribelarrea_et_al_2014.pdf)). New AGS site at Olduvai (1.84 Ma) extends the spatial range of butchery loci ([Stancampiano et al. 2022 *Sci Rep*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-022-14031-1)).

**GENUINELY CONTESTED:**

* Whether FLK-Zinj-type sites reflect *true central-place foraging with food sharing* (Isaac's "food-sharing model" → "central-place model") or **near-kill processing locations** with high mobility and limited transport. Faith et al. (2009, *Journal of Human Evolution* 56:247–256) used optimal-foraging skeletal-element analysis on five Bed I assemblages and found no preferential transport pattern, consistent with **short-distance carcass transport** rather than long-distance provisioning of a residential hub. Pickering, Domínguez-Rodrigo and colleagues argue the converse based on cut-mark and percussion-mark frequencies. The debate is methodologically genuine — different proxies generate different inferences.

**PLAUSIBLY INFERRED:**

* Even on the most parsimonious reading, hominins \~2 Ma were transporting carcasses to specific localities and butchering them with stone tools — the *capacity* for central-place foraging exists by \~2 Ma. Whether it became *obligate* and *socially organized* is a separate question that the archaeological record does not yet resolve.

### **Cooking hypothesis (Wrangham): current evidentiary status**

**Older claim (Wrangham 1999; *Catching Fire* 2009):** Cooking became obligate in *Homo erectus* by \~1.9 Ma, drove the reduction in tooth and gut size, freed energy for brain growth, and underwrote a sexual division of labor.

**Newer evidence:**

* Archaeological evidence for habitual fire use is now solid by \~1.0 Ma (Wonderwerk Cave, [Berna et al. 2012, *PNAS* 109:E1215–E1220](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1117620109)), and pushed by the AI/spectroscopy approach of Stepka et al. to \~1.0–0.8 Ma at Evron Quarry, Israel ([Stepka et al. 2022, *PNAS* 119:e2123439119](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2123439119)).  
* Habitual, unambiguous fire use with hearths is established by \~0.79 Ma at Gesher Benot Ya'aqov (Goren-Inbar et al.).  
* Cave-context, well-preserved hearth evidence is abundant only from \~400 ka onward (Qesem, Schöningen, Beeches Pit; Roebroeks & Villa 2011).  
* Wrangham himself acknowledges this gap and has argued the biological evidence (raw-foodist energy deficits, anatomical changes in *H. erectus*) constrains the timing more tightly than the archaeology does ([Wrangham 2017, *Current Anthropology* 58:S303–S313](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/692113)).

**EMPIRICALLY SETTLED:**

* *Sapiens* is metabolically dependent on processed/cooked food. Raw-foodist studies show severe energy deficits and reproductive suppression on all-raw diets even in industrial settings.  
* Habitual hearth-based fire use is documented only from \~400 ka.

**PLAUSIBLY INFERRED:**

* Sporadic or opportunistic fire use occurred between \~1.0 Ma and \~400 ka, but whether it was a *cognitive trait under selection* (as Wrangham's model requires) or *occasional exploitation of natural fires* is not directly resolvable from the current archaeological signal.

**GENUINELY CONTESTED:**

* Whether the *H. erectus* anatomical signal (small teeth, reduced gut, larger body and brain at \~1.9 Ma) demands cooking as the explanation, or whether intensified meat-eating, marrow extraction, and mechanical food processing (pounding, slicing) suffice. Zink & Lieberman (2016, *Nature*) showed that simple slicing and pounding alone yield substantial reductions in chewing demand on raw meat, weakening the *necessity* of cooking by 1.9 Ma.  
* Field movement: the strong-form claim (cooking → *H. erectus*) has lost ground; the weak-form claim (cooking became important sometime in the mid-Pleistocene and shapes modern human biology) is widely accepted. The *date* of the transition is what is now contested.

### **Brain expansion timing relative to dietary shifts, tool use, and cooperative foraging**

**EMPIRICALLY SETTLED:**

* Hominin brain-size increase across \~3 Ma is now best characterized as **gradual within-species (anagenetic) increase**, supplemented by clade-level effects from origination of larger-brained taxa and extinction of smaller-brained ones ([Du et al. 2018, *Proc R Soc B* 285:20172738](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rspb/article/285/1873/20172738/78823/Pattern-and-process-in-hominin-brain-size); [DeSilva et al. 2024, *PNAS* 121, "within-species encephalization"](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2409542121); [Püschel et al. 2019, *eLife*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6379089/)). Australopith brains (\~400–500 cc) remain near chimp range for \~1.5 Myr; substantial expansion follows the appearance of *Homo*.  
* Pliocene C4/CAM shift in hominin diet predates significant brain expansion. *Australopithecus afarensis* (\>3.4 Ma at Hadar) already shows substantial C4/CAM consumption, distinct from extant chimpanzees ([Wynn et al. 2013 *PNAS*](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1222559110); [Sponheimer et al. 2013 *PNAS*](https://www.pnas.org/content/110/26/10513); [Lee-Thorp et al. 2010 *Phil Trans B*; Cerling et al. 2013](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20855312/)). By \~2.37 Ma in the Lower Omo, the C4 shift is shared across *Paranthropus*, *Australopithecus*, and early *Homo* ([2020 PNAS Lower Omo update](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.2006221117)).  
* Dental microwear of *H. erectus* (and Dmanisi early *H. erectus* at 1.8 Ma) suggests a *broader* dietary range than habilines or australopiths but no specialization on extremely hard or tough foods ([Ungar 2012 *Current Anthropology* 53:S6](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/666700); [Pontzer et al. on Dmanisi](https://www.academia.edu/23105962/Dental_microwear_texture_analysis_and_diet_in_the_Dmanisi_hominins)).

**PLAUSIBLY INFERRED:**

* Brain expansion *within species* is the dominant signal — not stepwise speciation events. This is consistent with a multi-causal driver landscape (energetic, ecological, social, technological) rather than a single mid-Pleistocene "trigger."

**GENUINELY CONTESTED:**

* Whether the within-species rate accelerates over time. Rosenberger (Rosenberger et al. 2019, *eLife*, PGLS approach) and Du et al. 2018 disagree on whether the data support strict gradualism or accelerating directional evolution. DeSilva et al. (2024 PNAS) argue accelerating intraspecific evolution.

### **Cumulative cultural ratcheting — when does it appear, and does it precede or follow language?**

**EMPIRICALLY SETTLED:**

* Tomasello's "ratchet effect" (Tomasello, Kruger & Ratner 1993; [Tennie, Call & Tomasello 2009 *Phil Trans B* 364:2405–2415](https://royalsocietypublishing.org/doi/10.1098/rstb.2009.0052)) is now operationalized as: high-fidelity copying \+ innovation \+ sustained transmission. Modern human children show partial ratcheting from age \~4 (Tennie et al. 2014, *Journal of Experimental Child Psychology*).  
* Chimpanzee tool traditions exist (Whiten et al. 1999, *Nature*) but are best characterized as exploitations of a "zone of latent solutions" — variants within an existing cognitive repertoire — rather than progressive accumulation.

**GENUINELY CONTESTED:**

* When in the hominin record cumulative culture begins. Archaeological signatures candidates:  
* **Acheulean handaxe stasis** (\~1.7 Ma–\~300 ka): consistent across vast time and space, often cited as evidence of *transmission* but not *ratcheting*.  
* **Late Acheulean / Levallois transition** (\~500–300 ka): standardization and technique refinement may indicate ratcheting onset.  
* **Middle Stone Age and Still Bay industries** (\~100–70 ka in southern Africa): clear progressive innovations (heat-treatment of silcrete, pressure flaking, hafting, complex bone tools) suggest unambiguous ratcheting by this time ([Brown et al. 2009; Mourre, Villa & Henshilwood 2010 on pressure flaking at Blombos](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.ads2797)).  
* Whether ratcheting requires language (Tomasello) or whether weaker forms of contingent social learning produce a "primary ratchet" without language (Boyd & Richerson; [Boyd 2017 bioRxiv](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/134247v2.full)) is debated. The current consensus is that *some* ratcheting can exist without language but *cumulative* technology of late-Pleistocene complexity probably could not.

**THIN/UNTESTED:**

* Direct fossil markers of language. Inferences are anatomical (hyoid, vocal-tract, hypoglossal canal) and genetic (FOXP2, NOVA1) and remain indirect.

### **Neanderthal cognition, symbolic behavior, and material culture — implications for *sapiens* distinctiveness**

**EMPIRICALLY SETTLED (via uranium-thorium dating, accepted by most of the field):**

* Three Iberian cave sites (La Pasiega, Maltravieso, Ardales) contain pigment-applied art with U-Th minimum ages \>64,800 years, predating the arrival of *H. sapiens* in Iberia by \>20 ka. Authorship by Neanderthals is the parsimonious inference ([Hoffmann et al. 2018 *Science* 359:912–915, doi:10.1126/science.aap7778](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.aap7778)).  
* Symbolically-elaborated marine shell ornaments at Cueva de los Aviones and Cueva Antón date to \~115 ka, also Neanderthal context (Hoffmann, Angelucci, Villaverde, Zapata & Zilhão 2018 *Science Advances* 4:eaar5255).  
* Bruniquel Cave: deep-cave construction of stalagmite-piece rings 336 m from entrance, dated by U-series of regrowth speleothems and burnt bone to 176.5 ± 2.1 ka — unambiguously Neanderthal ([Jaubert et al. 2016 *Nature* 534:111–114, doi:10.1038/nature18291](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature18291)).  
* Specialized bone tools (lissoirs) made by Neanderthals (Soressi et al. 2013, *PNAS*).

**PLAUSIBLY INFERRED:**

* Neanderthal symbolic capacity is no longer reasonably described as absent or qualitatively inferior. Whether Neanderthal symbolic *systems* matched *sapiens* in scale, productivity, and cumulative elaboration remains an open empirical question.

**GENUINELY CONTESTED:**

* Whether the U-Th dates on calcite overlying Iberian pigment are reliable proxies for the underlying paint (concerns raised by some specialists about sample stratigraphy and U-mobility); the dispute has narrowed but not closed (Pettitt critiques referenced in [Smithsonian coverage](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/science-nature/were-neanderthals-earliest-cave-artists-new-research-spain-points-possibility-180968236/)).  
* Whether Neanderthal cognitive capacity for figurative representation is comparable to *sapiens* (no figurative Neanderthal art has been confirmed, in contrast to Sulawesi pigs at \>45.5 ka and recently redated to \>50 ka: [Brumm et al. 2021 *Sci Adv* 7:eabd4648](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/sciadv.abd4648); [2024 redating in *Nature*](https://www.haaretz.com/archaeology/2024-07-03/ty-article-magazine/earliest-art-is-much-older-than-thought-sulawesi-pigs-painted-over-51-000-years-ago/00000190-77af-d14c-a1dd-ffafd7170000)).

**Field movement:** The older claim that symbolic cognition is uniquely modern-*sapiens* has clearly *lost ground*. The emerging position is that symbolic capacity has deeper roots in the genus *Homo*, with Neanderthals and *sapiens* both inheriting the capacity and elaborating it differently.

## **Block 3 — Sexual Division of Labor and Pair-Bonding**

### **Sex-based division of labor in ethnographic foragers**

**EMPIRICALLY SETTLED:**

* Some form of gendered division of labor exists in *every* contemporary forager society documented, but **gender roles are flexible**, not rigid. Men spend more time hunting medium-to-large game; women spend more time gathering and on childcare; both sexes fish, gather shellfish, and engage in food processing ([Marlowe 2007; Kelly 2013 *Foraging Spectrum*; Gurven & Hill 2009 *Current Anthropology* 50:51–74](https://anthro.vancouver.wsu.edu/media/documents/Venkataraman2024.pdf)).  
* Women's hunting is documented in many foraging societies, including big-game hunting in some.

**The Anderson et al. 2023 / Venkataraman et al. 2024 dispute:**

* Anderson, Chilczuk, Nelson, Ruther & Wall-Scheffler (2023 *PLOS ONE* 18:e0287101) reported that women hunt in 79% of 63 foraging societies sampled, and big game in 33%, on the basis of explicit ethnographic descriptions, and used this to challenge the existence of any meaningful gendered division of labor.  
* Venkataraman, Hoffman, Farquharson, Davis, Hagen, Hames, et al. (2024 *Evolution and Human Behavior*) re-coded the same source ethnographies and identified: (a) inclusion of horticulturalist and agriculturalist societies miscoded as foragers (Kikuyu, Wopkaimin, Iroquois, Tsimane, Matses); (b) selection of 22/63 societies from outside the D-PLACE database with a reported 81% positive rate, indicating selection bias; (c) exclusion of D-PLACE societies that contradict the narrative (e.g., Ju/'hoansi, where women are categorically prohibited from hunting); (d) coding errors where ethnographic statements explicitly contradicted the Anderson et al. binary coding. Venkataraman et al. estimate the actual prevalence of women's hunting in their re-coding at much lower frequencies (\~7% of foraging time on average, per Gurven & Hill 2009).

**EMPIRICALLY SETTLED (post-dispute):**

* Women hunt in many foraging societies, especially small game and via traps and snares; women's hunting is not zero, contrary to a strawman version of "Man the Hunter."  
* A flexible but real gendered division of labor is empirically robust across nearly all studied foraging societies. The Anderson et al. 79% figure does not survive methodological re-analysis.

**Direction of field movement:** The strawman version of "Man the Hunter" (women never hunt) has been definitively rejected — but this position was already abandoned by professional hunter-gatherer ethnographers decades ago. The Anderson et al. 2023 study was widely covered in popular media but its methodological problems are now well-documented.

### **Paternity-certainty / pair-bonding-as-prime-mover hypotheses**

**Older claim:** Provisioning hypothesis (Lovejoy 1981 *Science*, others) — male provisioning of mates and children selected for pair-bonding, paternal investment, sexual division of labor, and concealed ovulation as a package. Variants emphasize paternity certainty (Chapais 2008, *Primeval Kinship*).

**Newer evidence and challenges:**

* Lukas & Clutton-Brock (2013, *Science* 341:526–530) and Opie et al. (2013, *PNAS*): comparative phylogenetic analyses indicate that across mammals, **paternal care is a consequence rather than a cause of pair-living**. Pair-living evolved when females were dispersed/solitary, making mate guarding more profitable than multiple mating. Paternal care subsequently evolved on a pair-bonded substrate.  
* **Hawkes / grandmother-hypothesis alternative**: Coxworth, Kim, McQueen & Hawkes (2015, *PNAS* "Grandmothering life histories and human pair bonding") argued that male-biased adult sex ratios produced by grandmothering (which extends female longevity but not female fertility) would generate selection for male mate-guarding regardless of whether paternal care became important. This reframes pair-bonding as a *consequence* of grandmothering rather than provisioning. ([PNAS doi:10.1073/pnas.1599993112](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1599993112)).  
* **Kramer & Russell 2015** ("Was monogamy a key step on the hominin road?", *Evolutionary Anthropology* 24:73–83) argue the monogamy-as-prime-mover claim is stronger than the data support.  
* **Schacht & Bell 2016** (*Scientific Reports*): formal model of partner scarcity → mate guarding → pair-bonding → opportunity for paternal care, supporting an intermediate-step view.

**EMPIRICALLY SETTLED:**

* Human pair-bonding is real, cross-culturally pervasive, and integrated with cooperative provisioning of children — but with substantial cross-cultural variation in form (monogamous, polygynous, with extra-pair paternity).  
* Some form of pair-bonding existed in the LCA of *sapiens* and Neanderthals (genetic data on inbreeding avoidance, mate exchange).

**GENUINELY CONTESTED:**

* Causal direction. Did pair-bonding evolve to support paternal provisioning, or for mate-guarding under partner-scarcity, with provisioning emerging later? The phylogenetic-comparative work of Lukas & Clutton-Brock and Opie et al. supports the latter ordering for mammals broadly.  
* Whether *cooperative breeding* (Hrdy/Burkart/van Schaik) or *embodied capital with paternal provisioning* (Kaplan/Hill — see Block 5\) or *grandmothering* (Hawkes/O'Connell/Blurton Jones) best explains hominin life-history evolution. These are mutually competing live frameworks.

### **Inter-group marriage exchange and exogamy in foragers — what does it actually do?**

**EMPIRICALLY SETTLED:**

* Hill, Walker, Bozicević, Eder, Headland, Hewlett, Hurtado, Marlowe, Wiessner & Wood (2011 *Science* 331:1286–1289) analyzed band composition in 32 forager societies (n=5,067 individuals; mean band size \~28 adults) and found: (a) either sex may disperse or remain in natal group (no consistent patrilocal/matrilocal pattern); (b) adult brothers and sisters often co-reside; (c) **most individuals in residential groups are genetically unrelated**. This is structurally distinct from any non-human primate group composition. ([Science doi:10.1126/science.1199071](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21393537/)).  
* Walker, Hill, Flinn & Ellsworth (2011 *PLOS ONE* 6:e19066) used phylogenetic comparative methods on hunter-gatherer marriage practices and recovered: low polygyny, reciprocal exchange of mates and resources between kin groups, evidence of arranged marriages in many Eurasian-derived foragers (with several African foragers showing courtship marriage). The picture is of a deep history of regulated reciprocal exogamy.

**PLAUSIBLY INFERRED:**

* Exogamy in foragers serves *multiple* simultaneous functions: alliance and exchange networks across bands (consistent with cumulative-culture transmission across larger populations, per Hill et al. 2011), inbreeding avoidance (genetic-fitness data), and demographic viability (small effective populations require partner exchange). Recent integrated genomic-ethnographic work on Central African hunter-gatherers (Macao and Minganga, 2025 bioRxiv) shows that homozygosity is kept similarly low across populations through *different* combinations of mobility and exogamy norms — reinforcing that multiple functions are simultaneously at work.

**GENUINELY CONTESTED:**

* The relative weighting of paternity-certainty / kinship / alliance / demographic functions. No single-function model is supported by the comparative evidence.

## **Block 4 — Language and Symbolic Cognition**

### **When did symbolic cognition emerge? What evidence is diagnostic?**

**Sites and dates considered diagnostic by most of the field:**

**EMPIRICALLY SETTLED:**

* Symbolic behavior of multiple kinds (pigment use, deliberate engraving, perforated ornaments, deep-cave construction, figurative art) is documented well before the European Upper Paleolithic.  
* The "Eurocentric Upper Paleolithic revolution" model has been *decisively rejected*. African MSA evidence at 75–100 ka and earlier, plus Neanderthal evidence at \>64 ka and \>115 ka, plus Sulawesi figurative art at \>45.5 ka, all preclude a sudden 40 ka European emergence as the origin event.

**PLAUSIBLY INFERRED:**

* Symbolic cognition was likely present in the LCA of *sapiens* and Neanderthals (\~500–700 ka). The deep cave constructions at Bruniquel (176 ka) and the Iberian pigment art (\>64 ka) push this back independently.

**GENUINELY CONTESTED:**

* Whether ostrich eggshell engravings, ochre processing, or perforated shells are *diagnostic* of symbolic representation, or merely of the ability to make non-symbolic geometric marks and ornaments. The methodological question of what *is* a symbolic threshold remains active.  
* Whether the U-Th dates on Iberian Neanderthal art are robust. The method has been validated by independent labs but pigment-stratigraphy concerns persist for some specialists.

### **Is symbolic representation a precondition for, or consequence of, cooperative practices?**

**THIN/UNTESTED:**

* This is the directionality question and is **largely speculative**. The strongest empirical anchors are:  
* Cooperative breeding and food sharing patterns are documented archaeologically only via central-place foraging signatures, which exist by \~2 Ma (well before symbolic-art evidence).  
* Symbolic markers in MSA contexts (75–100 ka) postdate established hominin cooperation and provisioning by hundreds of thousands of years.  
* This temporal pattern is *consistent with* cooperative practices preceding symbolic representation, but the absence of symbolic preservation does not equal absence of symbolic capacity. The directionality cannot be settled with current evidence.

### **Language origins: gestural-first vs. vocal-first; gradual vs. saltational**

**EMPIRICALLY SETTLED:**

* Both gestural and vocal-tract systems are involved in modern human language. Apes can be trained on rudimentary sign-like systems (Kanzi etc.) but not on vocal speech, suggesting gestural and vocal systems are dissociable.  
* Most contemporary specialists no longer hold a strict gesture-first or speech-first view. The dominant position is **multimodal coevolution** — kinesic, vocal, and possibly sung modalities developing together with neither taking strict precedence ([Kendon 2017 *Psychonomic Bulletin & Review* "Reflections on the gesture-first hypothesis"](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-016-1117-3); [Fitch 2017 same issue](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-016-1130-6); Corballis 2010 *WIREs Cognitive Science*, doi:10.1002/wcs.2).

**FOXP2 — a substantial recent revision:**

* *Older claim* (Enard et al. 2002 *Nature* 418:869–872): Two human-specific amino acid substitutions in FOXP2 were under recent positive selection in *sapiens*, and constituted a textbook "language gene" sweep within the last \~100 ka.  
* *Krause et al. 2007*: Neanderthal FOXP2 sequencing showed Neanderthals carry the same two amino acid substitutions, requiring revision of timing.  
* *Atkinson, Audesse, Palacios, Bobo, Webb, Ramachandran & Henn 2018* (*Cell* 174:1424–1435; [doi:10.1016/j.cell.2018.06.048](https://www.cell.com/cell/fulltext/S0092-8674\(18\)30851-1)): re-analyzed FOXP2 in hundreds of globally distributed genomes, found **no evidence of recent positive or balancing selection at FOXP2**. The original 2002 sweep signal was an artifact of sample composition (mostly Eurasian, n=20). They identified an intronic region that may have undergone loss of function in humans.  
* Subsequent work (Lauterbur et al. 2023 with Flex-sweep methodology) finds FOXP2 as a candidate for older selection but supports the Atkinson et al. revision of the recent-sweep claim.  
* *Direction of movement*: FOXP2 is not a recent human-specific selective sweep. It has functional importance for vocal-motor control (speech production), but its role in *language as a cognitive faculty* was overstated in the 2002–2010 popular and academic narrative.

**NOVA1 — a new candidate:**

* Tajima, Vargas, Ito et al. (2025 *Nature Communications* 16:1542; [doi:10.1038/s41467-025-56579-2](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-025-56579-2)) generated humanized mice carrying the *sapiens*\-specific I197V substitution in NOVA1 (an RNA-binding splicing factor in the CNS). Result: humanized mice show altered ultrasonic vocalization patterns as pups and altered courtship vocalization complexity in adults; CLIP analysis identified vocalization-related genes as binding targets. The I197V variant is fixed in modern humans (650,052/650,058 modern genomes carry it) and absent from Neanderthals and Denisovans. Selection-coefficient analysis using ARGweaver-D / CLUES2 yields s ≈ 0.00082 in the modern-human lineage. **Caveat**: mice are not vocal learners; the behavioral significance for human speech is suggestive, not demonstrated. This is a 2025 finding still being absorbed by the field.

**Anatomical and endocast evidence:**

* Hyoid morphology of Neanderthals (Kebara 2\) shows a modern human–like form (D'Anastasio et al. 2013 *PLOS ONE*).  
* Endocranial reconstructions support broadly modern Broca's area expansion in genus *Homo* by \~2 Ma (Falk and others).

**GENUINELY CONTESTED:**

* Whether language is gradual (most genetic and anatomical evidence) or saltational (some Chomskyan formal-linguistic frameworks). Current empirical work overwhelmingly supports gradualism, but some prominent linguists hold to a recent (\~50–100 ka) saltational origin.

### **Neanderthal symbolic behavior — implications for *sapiens* uniqueness**

Already covered above. Net effect on the question: **claims of symbolic uniqueness for *Homo sapiens* are no longer defensible in their strong form**. Whether *sapiens* and Neanderthals had quantitatively different symbolic systems, or merely different elaborations of a shared capacity, remains an open question with the data favoring the latter for now.

## **Block 5 — Meta-Questions About Prime Movers**

### **Is the field single-cause or multi-cause now?**

**EMPIRICALLY SETTLED:**

* The field is decisively multi-causal. No single prime mover currently has the empirical traction to displace the others. Recent integrative work (Lieberman *The Story of the Human Body*; Antón, Potts & Aiello 2014 *Science* on early *Homo* origins; Wood and many others) explicitly frames hominin evolution as a coevolving complex of energetics, ecology, social structure, life history, and technology.

### **Where specific "prime movers" have lost ground or been revised**

| Hypothesis | Older claim | Current status / direction |
| ----- | ----- | ----- |
| **Savanna hypothesis (strict grasslands form)** | Bipedalism evolved on open African grasslands as a thermoregulatory and locomotor adaptation. | *Lost ground.* Australopith fossils (Aramis, Hadar) and Miocene-Pliocene paleoecology now show mosaic woodland-savanna habitats, not open grasslands ([Domínguez-Rodrigo 2014 *Current Anthropology* 55:1; Reynolds, Wilkinson, Marston & O'Regan 2015 *Trans R Soc S Africa*](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/10.1086/674530)). Drummond-Clarke et al. 2022 *Sci Adv* on Issa Valley savanna chimpanzees found that mosaic savanna habitats *do not* select for terrestrial bipedalism in chimpanzees — bipedalism in trees was actually higher in savanna chimps. This empirically undermines the simple "open ground → bipedalism" narrative. The mosaic / wooded-savanna form of the hypothesis remains live. |
| **Social brain hypothesis (Dunbar)** | Group size drives primate brain size; "Dunbar's number" of \~150 reflects a hard cognitive constraint. | *Partial revision.* DeCasien et al. 2017 found diet predicts primate brain size better than sociality; Lindenfors et al. and others find huge confidence intervals on group-size predictions. Dunbar 2024 (*Annals of Human Biology*, "Thirty years on") defends the framework with grade-corrected analyses and 23 ethnographic-network studies. The quantitative SBH applies *primarily to anthropoid primates*, not to other vertebrate clades, where pair-bonding rather than group size predicts brain size (Shultz & Dunbar 2007). |
| **Cooking hypothesis (Wrangham)** | Habitual cooking by *H. erectus* at \~1.9 Ma drove tooth, gut, and brain changes. | *Lost ground in its strong form.* Earliest unambiguous fire \~1.0 Ma at Wonderwerk (Berna et al. 2012); habitual hearth use only by \~400 ka. Zink & Lieberman 2016 showed slicing/pounding of raw meat alone explains much of the chewing reduction. Wrangham 2017 himself acknowledges the archaeological gap. Weak form (cooking matters for *sapiens* metabolism and was important in mid-Pleistocene) is mainstream. |
| **Throwing hypothesis (Bingham, Young, Calvin; Roach)** | High-speed throwing was a key driver in genus *Homo* evolution; shoulder anatomy of *H. erectus* enabled novel projectile use. | *Mixed.* Roach, Venkadesan, Rainbow & Lieberman (2013 *Nature* 498:483–486; [doi:10.1038/nature12267](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature12267)) provided the strongest biomechanical case to date — three anatomical features (low humeral torsion, lowered/widened shoulders, expanded waist) appear together in *H. erectus* and enable elastic-energy storage at the shoulder. *But* Larson 2007/2009 questioned the *H. erectus* shoulder reconstruction and the throwing inference (based on KNM-WT 15000 short clavicle). The Bingham 2000 social-coercion-via-throwing variant has minimal independent empirical support. The biomechanical-anatomical version (Roach/Lieberman) is partially corroborated but contested on fossil interpretation. |
| **Cooperative breeding (Hrdy/Burkart/van Schaik)** | Allomaternal care permitted human life-history pattern (short IBI \+ long dependency) and selected for prosociality and theory-of-mind. | *Live but contested.* The life-history version is well-supported. The cognitive-extension version was sharply challenged by Thornton & McAuliffe 2015 / Thornton et al. 2016, with Burkart & van Schaik 2016 retreating to a "primate-only, energetic-release" version. Whether the revised CBH is falsifiable remains debated. |
| **Predicament-of-meat / paternity-uncertainty** | Pair-bonding evolved to manage paternity certainty under male meat-provisioning. | *Partial revision.* Lukas & Clutton-Brock 2013 / Opie et al. 2013 phylogenetic comparative work indicates paternal care across mammals is generally a *consequence* of pair-living, not a cause; Coxworth et al. 2015 PNAS reframe pair-bonding as mate-guarding under male-biased ASR generated by grandmothering. The paternity-certainty-as-prime-mover model has lost ground. |
| **Embodied capital (Kaplan, Hill, Lancaster, Hurtado)** | Long juvenile dependency, late peak production, large brains, and extended lifespan coevolved as a complex driven by skill-intensive foraging on high-quality foods. | *Live, contested by grandmother-hypothesis advocates.* The Kaplan et al. 2000 *Evolutionary Anthropology* 9:156–185 framework remains a major synthesis. Hawkes and colleagues (Hawkes 2003, 2013, 2025; O'Connell & Hawkes 2023\) explicitly argue against the embodied-capital weighting of paternal provisioning. The two frameworks make different predictions about hunter-gatherer male productivity timing and male-female resource flows that have been actively tested in Tsimane, Hadza, Ache, and Pumé populations. |
| **Expensive tissue hypothesis (Aiello & Wheeler 1995\)** | Smaller hominin gut compensated for larger brain via shared metabolic budget. | *Largely lost ground in its broad mammalian form.* Navarrete, van Schaik & Isler (2011 *Nature* 480:91–93) tested 100 mammals and found no negative correlation between brain size and gut size. They proposed the **expensive brain / energy trade-off hypothesis** (brain trades against fat stores, reproduction, growth duration, etc.), which is broader. Aiello and Wheeler responded that the original hypothesis was specifically about anthropoid primates. Within primates, some support persists (Tsuboi et al. 2015 in cichlids; Liao et al. 2016 in anurans support gut-brain trade-off). Most recent comprehensive review: [Wells et al. 2024 *Evolutionary Human Sciences*](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/evolutionary-human-sciences/article/testing-the-expensivetissue-hypothesis-prediction-of-intertissue-competition-using-causal-modelling-with-latent-variables/1F09838BF0A9305C7759B8EA9AEA7608) finds suggestive but not strong support for inter-tissue competition in modern humans. |
| **Grandmother hypothesis (Hawkes)** | Postmenopausal female longevity evolved because grandmothers who provisioned weaned grandchildren increased their daughters' fertility. | *Live and active.* Kim, Coxworth & Hawkes 2012 *Proc R Soc B* 279:4880–4884 simulation supports the model; Engelhardt et al. (Université de Sherbrooke pre-industrial Quebec data) and Chapman et al. (Finnish historical data) provide empirical support; Lahdenperä et al. archival pre-industrial data supports fertility benefits. Critics argue the longevity-vs-reproductive-effort trade-off in formal models is not adequately handled and that male-centered models produce different results. Hawkes 2025 (*AJHB* "Revisiting Grandmothers and the Evolution of Human Longevity") provides the most recent integration. |

### **Where the field still disagrees, what evidence would settle it?**

**THIN/UNTESTED — proposed lines of evidence the field is actively pursuing:**

* Direct paleogenomic signatures of hunter-gatherer life-history loci (longevity, growth, reproduction).  
* More refined isotopic and trace-element work on weaning ages and dietary breadth across hominin species (calcium isotopes from enamel, recent CSIA-AA on amino acid nitrogen).  
* Better Pliocene-Pleistocene paleoecological reconstructions to test mosaic-vs-grassland models site by site.  
* Larger samples of dental microwear texture across hominin species (current N for several taxa is single digits).  
* AI/spectroscopy detection of pyrotechnology at additional Lower Paleolithic sites (the Stepka/Natalio Evron approach).  
* Whole-genome ancient DNA from Middle Pleistocene specimens beyond Sima de los Huesos.

## **Block 6 — Overarching Sequencing Summary**

The **best-supported ordering** of major innovations, with major uncertainty windows flagged:

### **From late Paleozoic (\~310 Ma) to mammals**

* Synapsid-sauropsid divergence (\~310–315 Ma): glandular integument; mammary-precursor secretions in apocrine-like glands; hair likely originating well before crown Mammalia.  
* Permian therapsids: increasing parental investment, possible gregariousness, whisker-like tactile sensors, enhanced olfaction and audition (Benoit et al. 2023).  
* Late Triassic / Early Jurassic mammaliaforms: nocturnal small body size; substantial brain enlargement *not* immediate at origin of Mammaliaformes — the major brain-size jump occurred later in cynodonts.  
* *Empirical settling*: Most defining mammalian features (lactation, hair, parental care, complex sensorium) predate crown Mammalia and arose stepwise within synapsid evolution.

### **From mammals to primates (\~75–55 Ma)**

* Frugivorous foraging, grasping hands and feet, forward-facing eyes, large relative brain size for body size.  
* Extension of pair-bond-like affiliative relationships to non-mate dyads (the structural feature that distinguishes primate sociality from carnivore/ungulate sociality and is the mechanistic basis of the quantitative form of the social brain hypothesis).  
* *Plausibly inferred*: Frugivory-driven cognitive demands (DeCasien et al. 2017\) at least partially drove primate encephalization.

### **From other apes to early hominins (\~7–4 Ma)**

* Bipedalism: appearing in some form in *Sahelanthropus*, *Orrorin*, *Ardipithecus*; obligate striding bipedalism by *Homo erectus*.  
* Mosaic savanna-woodland habitat (not open grassland).  
* C3-dominated diet (*Ardipithecus*, *Au. anamensis*) shifting to mixed C3/C4 by *Au. afarensis* (\>3.4 Ma).  
* *Empirically settled*: the C4 dietary shift documented by Sponheimer/Cerling/Lee-Thorp/Wynn et al. is one of the clearest landmark events in hominin paleoecology.

### **From australopiths to genus *Homo* (\~3.0–1.8 Ma)**

* Stone tool manufacture (Lomekwian \~3.3 Ma; Oldowan \~2.6 Ma).  
* Cut-marked bones documenting butchery from \~2.6 Ma; concentrated butchery loci from \~2 Ma (Olduvai FLK Zinj).  
* Beginning of substantial brain expansion within and across species.  
* Anatomical changes for high-speed throwing (Roach et al. 2013\) by \~2 Ma (contested but plausibly inferred).  
* C4 dietary shift extends across all hominin taxa by \~2.37 Ma.  
* *H. erectus* emerges (\~1.9 Ma), shows reduced gut and tooth size, broader dietary range (microwear evidence).  
* *Plausibly inferred*: meat-eating, dietary breadth expansion, and stone-tool processing precede systematic fire use; this is increasingly accepted.

### **Mid-Pleistocene (\~1.0 Ma–\~300 ka)**

* Earliest unambiguous fire (Wonderwerk \~1.0 Ma; Evron Quarry \~1.0–0.8 Ma; Gesher Benot Ya'aqov \~790 ka).  
* Bruniquel Cave Neanderthal stalagmite construction at \~176.5 ka — deep cave use, planning, fire in deep settings, possibly ritualized.  
* Sima de los Huesos hominins (\~430 ka) genetically identified as early Neanderthals via nuclear DNA ([Meyer et al. 2016 *Nature* 531](https://www.nature.com/articles/nature17405)).  
* Habitual hearth use becomes archaeologically common from \~400 ka.  
* Jebel Irhoud (Morocco) early *H. sapiens* at \~315 ± 34 ka with modern facial morphology but archaic neurocranium ([Hublin et al. 2017 *Nature* 546:289–292](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28593953/); [Richter et al. 2017 *Nature* 546:293–296](https://www.mpg.de/11322481/oldest-homo-sapiens-fossils-at-jebel-irhoud-morocco)). Pan-African origin.

### **Late Pleistocene (\~300–40 ka)**

* Symbolic markers: Skhul/Qafzeh shell ornaments (\~100 ka), Blombos engraved ochre (\~75–100 ka) and shell beads (\~75 ka), Pinnacle Point silcrete heat-treatment (\~71+ ka), Diepkloof eggshells (\>55 ka), Iberian Neanderthal cave art (\>64.8 ka), Cueva de los Aviones Neanderthal shell ornaments (\~115 ka).  
* Recurrent Neanderthal–*sapiens* interbreeding (\~250 ka and \~50 ka episodes) leaving \~1.5–4% Neanderthal ancestry in non-Africans ([Villanea & Schraiber 2019 *Nature Ecol Evol* 3:39–44](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41559-018-0735-8); [Iasi et al. 2024 *Science*](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.adi1768)).  
* Out-of-Africa expansion of behaviorally modern *sapiens* (\~60–50 ka) with Sulawesi figurative art at \~45.5–51 ka.  
* NOVA1 I197V *sapiens*\-specific selection (Tajima et al. 2025; date uncertain, possibly \~250–300 ka).

### **Holocene**

* Brain size has *decreased* on average over the last \~3,000 years (DeSilva et al. 2021 in *Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution* — somewhat speculative as to mechanism; cumulative-culture / cognitive-offloading hypotheses proposed).  
* Population scaling and cumulative culture explosion (Henrich; Boyd & Richerson cultural-evolution program).

### **Where multiple orderings remain defensible**

1. **Cooking timing**: \~1.9 Ma (Wrangham) vs. \~400 ka (archaeology-strict) — both defensible, with empirical preference for the later date but biological arguments for an earlier transition that may be archaeologically invisible.  
2. **Symbolic-cognition emergence**: Continuous with archaic *Homo* (Bruniquel \+ Iberian art interpretation) vs. *sapiens*\-specific elaboration. The continuous-deep-roots ordering is currently better supported.  
3. **Pair-bonding origin**: Mate-guarding-first (Lukas & Clutton-Brock 2013; Coxworth et al. 2015\) vs. provisioning-first (Lovejoy 1981 and successors). The phylogenetic-comparative weight is on mate-guarding-first across mammals.  
4. **Brain expansion driver**: Cooked-food energetics (Wrangham/Carmody) vs. meat-and-marrow (Aiello-Wheeler-style) vs. cooperative-breeding-released energy (Burkart/van Schaik) vs. embodied-capital skill-intensive foraging (Kaplan/Hill) vs. grandmothering-extended development (Hawkes/O'Connell). All four remain live; the field is multi-causal.

## **Honest Gap Reporting (Structural Findings)**

Several sub-areas in this map are **structurally thin** rather than just under-researched:

1. **Direct fossil markers of language** essentially do not exist. All inferences are indirect (anatomical proxies, genetic candidates with uncertain functional weighting, archaeological proxies for symbolic capacity). NOVA1 in 2025 represents real progress but requires substantial follow-up before it can be evaluated as a "language gene."

2. **Direct evidence of cooperative breeding or alloparenting in the deep hominin record** is essentially absent. All current arguments are based on life-history reconstruction from dental development, encephalization, and inferred weaning ages. Whether *Homo erectus*, *Homo heidelbergensis*, or earlier *Homo* species had alloparental systems comparable to modern foragers is not directly testable with current methods.

3. **Direct evidence on whether pre-Holocene cooperative practices were band-wide pooling or dyadic exchange** is absent. The transition from Taï-style coalition sharing to forager band-wide pooling is structurally distinctive but archaeologically invisible.

4. **The directionality of symbolic representation vs. cooperative provisioning** cannot be settled with current evidence. Cooperative provisioning has clearer archaeological signatures earlier (\~2 Ma), but absence of preserved symbolic markers does not equal absence of symbolic capacity.

5. **The number of fossil specimens for several key hominin species used in dental-microwear and isotope work is small** (often single digits). Conclusions about *Au. anamensis*, *Au. sediba*, *Paranthropus aethiopicus*, *H. naledi* dietary ecology rest on samples that would be flagged as inadequate in any other comparative biology context.

6. **The Anderson et al. 2023 / Venkataraman et al. 2024 dispute** is not a closed scientific question only because of methodology — it is also an active sociological debate about what counts as evidence in human-behavioral-ecology, and the methodological re-coding by Venkataraman and colleagues has not received the same media attention as the original PLoS ONE paper.

7. **Brain-size decrease in the Holocene** is empirically observed but the explanation (cumulative-culture cognitive offloading; population scaling; agricultural-diet effects; eusociality analogies to ants per DeSilva et al. 2021\) is genuinely speculative.

8. **Dunbar's number specifically** is in an unusual position: defended actively by its proponent with 23 confirmatory studies cited (Dunbar 2024), but with statistical-methodological critiques (Lindenfors et al.) producing wildly different point estimates with massive confidence intervals. The honest map of this debate is that the *general principle* (cognitive limits on stable social-network size) is more robust than the *specific number* (\~150).
