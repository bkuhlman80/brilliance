# **Attention research is fracturing its own foundations**

The field of attention neuroscience is undergoing two simultaneous upheavals. At the circuit level, the textbook model of three neuromodulators mapping neatly onto three attentional functions—norepinephrine for alerting, acetylcholine for orienting, dopamine for executive control—has been dismantled by cell-type-specific and ensemble-level findings showing that **each neuromodulatory system is internally heterogeneous**, with distinct subpopulations serving different attentional operations simultaneously. At the genetic level, the first adequately powered GWAS of measured attentional phenotypes has arrived, and the molecular story has shifted from dopamine to **GABAergic and synaptic transmission pathways**—upending three decades of candidate-gene assumptions. These two lines of evidence are converging toward a view of attention as a distributed, genetically polyarchic system where no single molecule, circuit, or gene set owns any one attentional function. Below are 11 empirical papers (2019–2025) that define the current frontier, with verified citations and substantive commentary on each.

---

## **The norepinephrine system is not a single arousal dial**

Three papers collectively demolish the idea that locus coeruleus norepinephrine (LC-NE) operates as a uniform arousal broadcast. Each reveals a different axis of LC heterogeneity—spatial selectivity, firing-pattern specificity, and spatiotemporal targeting—that forces a fundamental reconceptualization.

### **Paper 1**

**Ghosh S, Maunsell JHR.** "Locus coeruleus norepinephrine contributes to visual-spatial attention by selectively enhancing perceptual sensitivity." *Neuron*, 2024; 112(13): 2231–2240.e5.

* **DOI:** 10.1016/j.neuron.2024.04.001 — verified  
* **Design type:** Optogenetics \+ single-unit electrophysiology in behaving non-human primates  
* **Sample:** 2 rhesus macaques; 163 phasic LC neurons recorded; no genetic data  
* **What's new:** LC-NE neurons fire selectively to attended visual stimuli in the contralateral hemifield, and optogenetic LC activation causally improves perceptual sensitivity in a spatially selective manner without affecting motor criteria.  
* **Why care:** This is the first causal demonstration in primates that NE operates with spatial granularity during selective attention—the "diffuse arousal modulator" model of NE is no longer tenable at the single-neuron level.

### **Paper 2**

**Grimm C, Duss SN, Privitera M, Munn BR, Karalis N, Frässle S, Wilhelm M, Patriarchi T, Razansky D, Wenderoth N, Shine JM, Bohacek J, Zerbi V.** "Tonic and burst-like locus coeruleus stimulation distinctly shift network activity across the cortical hierarchy." *Nature Neuroscience*, 2024; 27(11): 2167–2177.

* **DOI:** 10.1038/s41593-024-01755-8 — verified  
* **Design type:** Multi-modal: optogenetic LC stimulation \+ fiber photometry (NE biosensors) \+ electrophysiology \+ whole-brain fMRI in mice  
* **Sample:** Multiple mouse cohorts across modalities (small n justified by multi-technique convergent design); no genetic data  
* **What's new:** Tonic (3 Hz) and burst-like (15 Hz) LC firing patterns produce qualitatively different brain-wide activation maps—tonic activity engages associative cortex while burst activity biases toward sensory processing, following the cortical hierarchy.  
* **Why care:** Provides the first causal, brain-wide evidence that LC firing patterns function as a pattern-dependent gain controller that dynamically reassigns resources along the sensory–associative cortical axis, bridging cellular optogenetics with systems-level network imaging.

### **Paper 3**

**Breton-Provencher V, Drummond GT, Feng J, Li Y, Sur M.** "Spatiotemporal dynamics of noradrenaline during learned behaviour." *Nature*, 2022; 606(7915): 732–738.

* **DOI:** 10.1038/s41586-022-04782-2 — verified  
* **Design type:** Two-photon micro-endoscopy of LC neurons \+ widefield NE biosensor (GRAB-NE) imaging \+ optogenetic inactivation in behaving mice  
* **Sample:** Multiple mouse cohorts (small n justified by two-photon and biosensor approach); no genetic data  
* **What's new:** NE release is not all-or-none: LC produces spatially targeted, modular NE release in motor cortex preceding action execution, and temporally distinct, brain-wide NE release following reinforcement signals—both during the same learned auditory detection task.  
* **Why care:** Resolves the long-standing "modularity vs. broadcast" debate about LC function by showing that both modes coexist within a single behavioral session, with spatial extent varying by behavioral context. This paper is the empirical anchor for any computational model of NE's dual role in attention and learning.

## **Acetylcholine has an internal architecture that mirrors LC heterogeneity**

### **Paper 4**

**Laszlovszky T, Schlingloff D, Hegedüs P, Freund TF, Gulyás A, Kepecs A, Hangya B.** "Distinct synchronization, cortical coupling and behavioral function of two basal forebrain cholinergic neuron types." *Nature Neuroscience*, 2020; 23(8): 992–1003.

* **DOI:** 10.1038/s41593-020-0648-0 — verified  
* **Design type:** Optogenetic tagging \+ in vivo single-unit electrophysiology in behaving animals \+ in vitro patch-clamp  
* **Sample:** Multiple mice and rats; identified cholinergic neurons recorded during auditory attention tasks (small n justified by intracranial single-unit approach); no genetic data  
* **What's new:** Basal forebrain cholinergic neurons comprise two functionally distinct types—bursting (Burst-BFCNs) and regular-firing (Reg-BFCNs)—that differ in cortical coupling patterns and behavioral correlates: bursting cells predict response timing via auditory cortex synchronization, while regular-firing cells predict detection accuracy.  
* **Why care:** Shatters the monolithic "ACh \= orienting" model by showing the cholinergic system has its own internal functional architecture, with distinct cell types mediating different temporal components of attention. This parallels the LC heterogeneity story and suggests that cell-type specificity is a general principle of neuromodulatory control of attention.

## **Thalamic gating has emerged as the critical circuit motif for attentional control**

Two papers from the Halassa lab and collaborators establish the thalamus—specifically the thalamic reticular nucleus and mediodorsal thalamus—as a computational hub where prefrontal executive signals are translated into sensory filtering and cognitive flexibility. These papers are being cited across attention, decision-making, autism, and schizophrenia literatures, making them exemplars of the cross-disciplinary impact criterion.

### **Paper 5**

**Nakajima M, Schmitt LI, Halassa MM.** "Prefrontal cortex regulates sensory filtering through a basal ganglia-to-thalamus pathway." *Neuron*, 2019; 103(3): 445–458.e10.

* **DOI:** 10.1016/j.neuron.2019.05.026 — verified  
* **Design type:** Anatomical tracing (monosynaptic rabies) \+ multi-electrode electrophysiology \+ optogenetic silencing \+ behavior in freely moving mice  
* **Sample:** N \= 4 mice for TRN recordings (220 neurons); multiple cohorts for behavioral and tracing experiments (small n justified by circuit-dissection approach); no genetic data  
* **What's new:** Identifies a previously unknown PFC→striatum→globus pallidus→TRN→sensory thalamus pathway that enables crossmodal attentional selection between vision and audition—the prefrontal cortex does not project directly to sensory TRN subnetworks but uses the basal ganglia as an intermediary.  
* **Why care:** Establishes the basal ganglia as a node for attentional (not just motor) control, revealing a specific circuit motif that bridges the mechanistic gap between prefrontal executive signals and early sensory filtering. This pathway architecture has direct implications for understanding sensory processing deficits in autism and schizophrenia.

### **Paper 6**

**Lam NH, Mukherjee A, Wimmer RD, Nassar MR, Chen ZS, Halassa MM.** "Prefrontal transthalamic uncertainty processing drives flexible switching." *Nature*, 2025; 637(8044): 127–136.

* **DOI:** 10.1038/s41586-024-08180-8 — verified  
* **Design type:** Multi-electrode recording \+ optogenetic manipulation (SSFO) \+ computational modeling in tree shrews performing hierarchical decision tasks with rule reversals  
* **Sample:** Tree shrews (Tupaia belangeri); 716 PFC neurons, plus MD and ACC populations across 45 stimulation sessions (small n justified by intracranial multi-region recording); no genetic data  
* **What's new:** The mediodorsal thalamus independently represents cueing uncertainty and rule uncertainty as separable population codes, enabling a transthalamic ACC→MD→PFC pathway that drives prefrontal reconfiguration when errors signal an environmental change rather than sensory noise.  
* **Why care:** Demonstrates that the thalamus is not a relay but performs active computation—specifically, uncertainty decomposition—that enables flexible cognitive control. The finding that thalamic populations "demix" cortical signals into distinct uncertainty types provides a mechanistic entry point for understanding cognitive inflexibility in schizophrenia and ADHD.

## **The first adequately powered GWAS of attentional phenotypes has rewritten the molecular story**

The genetics of attention has crossed a threshold. For decades, candidate-gene studies pointed to dopaminergic variants (DRD4, DAT1) as the molecular substrate of attentional control. The papers below—the first large-scale GWAS of executive function and attentional endophenotypes—tell a different story entirely.

### **Paper 7**

**Hatoum AS, Morrison CL, Mitchell EC, Lam M, Benca-Bachman CE, Reineberg AE, Palmer RHC, Evans LM, Keller MC, Friedman NP.** "Genome-wide association study shows that executive functioning is influenced by GABAergic processes and is a neurocognitive genetic correlate of psychiatric disorders." *Biological Psychiatry*, 2023; 93(1): 59–70.

* **DOI:** 10.1016/j.biopsych.2022.06.034 — verified  
* **Design type:** GWAS of a latent Common Executive Function (cEF) factor score  
* **Sample:** N \= **427,037** European-ancestry individuals from UK Biobank; genetic data: SNP array (imputed)  
* **What's new:** The first adequately powered GWAS of a latent executive function factor identified **129 genome-wide significant lead variants** across 112 loci, implicating GABAergic and potassium channel pathways—not the dopaminergic pathways assumed from candidate-gene literature.  
* **Why care:** This is the foundational GWAS for attention/executive control as a measurable cognitive phenotype. It defines Common EF at the genomic level, provides summary statistics for all downstream polygenic score work, and shifts the molecular narrative from dopamine to fast synaptic inhibition. Any future genetic study of attentional control will build on or reference this paper.

### **Paper 8**

**Wootton O, Shadrin AA, Mohn C, Susser E, Ramesar R, Gur RC, Andreassen OA, Stein DJ, Dalvie S.** "Genome-wide association study in 404,302 individuals identifies 7 significant loci for reaction time variability." *Molecular Psychiatry*, 2023; 28: 4011–4019.

* **DOI:** 10.1038/s41380-023-02292-9 — verified  
* **Design type:** GWAS with trans-ancestry replication and polygenic score analysis  
* **Sample:** Discovery N \= **404,302** (white British, UK Biobank); replication N \= 44,873 (non-British ancestry groups); genetic data: SNP array (imputed)  
* **What's new:** First large-scale GWAS of reaction time variability (RTV)—a measure indexing attentional lapses—identified 7 genome-wide significant loci implicating synaptic function genes (CNTNAP4, NSF). Critically, ADHD showed a positive genetic correlation with RTV but a negative correlation with mean RT, demonstrating that variability and speed have partially distinct genetic architectures.  
* **Why care:** RTV is the closest available GWAS-scale endophenotype for "attentional lapses." The divergent genetic correlations with ADHD validate RTV as capturing something about attentional regulation that mean reaction time does not, providing the first GWAS summary statistics for this trait and enabling polygenic score analyses of attentional consistency.

### **Paper 9**

**Arnatkeviciute A, Lemire M, Morrison C, Mooney M, Ryabinin P, Roslin NM, Nikolas M, Coxon J, Tiego J, Hawi Z, Fornito A, Henrik W, Martinot J-L, Martinot M-LP, Artiges E, Garavan H, Nigg J, Friedman NP, Burton C, Schachar R, Crosbie J, Bellgrove MA.** "Trans-ancestry meta-analysis of genome-wide association studies of inhibitory control." *Molecular Psychiatry*, 2023; 28(10): 4175–4184.

* **DOI:** 10.1038/s41380-023-02187-9 — verified  
* **Design type:** Trans-ancestry GWAS meta-analysis (8 sites, 4 ancestries)  
* **Sample:** N \= **14,877** combined; genetic data: SNP arrays across sites  
* **What's new:** The first GWAS directly targeting task-based inhibitory control (stop-signal task: GoRT, GoRT SD, SSRT) found no genome-wide significant hits despite **8.2% SNP-heritability** for GoRT SD and SSRT, with power analyses estimating 750–1,000+ causal variants each explaining \<0.3% of variance.  
* **Why care:** This paper's "null" GWAS result is itself a critical finding: it establishes that task-based inhibitory control is heritable but requires N \> 100,000 for locus discovery—a clear roadmap for the field. The cross-ancestry PRS analyses show ADHD polygenic risk predicts GoRT SD (attentional regulation) in Europeans and schizophrenia PRS predicts SSRT in East Asians, validating inhibitory control as a genetic bridge between cognition and diagnosis.

## **Executive function and impulsivity are genetically almost unrelated**

### **Paper 10**

**Gustavson DE, Morrison CL, Mallard TT, Jennings MV, Fontanillas P, Elson SL, 23andMe Research Team, Palmer AA, Friedman NP, Sanchez-Roige S.** "Executive function and impulsivity predict distinct genetic variance in internalizing problems, externalizing problems, thought disorders, and compulsive disorders: A Genomic Structural Equation Modeling study." *Clinical Psychological Science*, 2024; 12(5): 865–881.

* **DOI:** 10.1177/21677026231207845 — verified  
* **Design type:** Genomic structural equation modeling (Genomic SEM) using large-scale GWAS summary statistics  
* **Sample:** Based on GWAS summary statistics from N \> 100,000 for both executive function (UK Biobank cEF, N \= 427,037) and impulsivity (UPPS-P/BIS facets, N \= 22,861–133,517 from 23andMe); genetic data: SNP-based summary statistics  
* **What's new:** Task-based executive function and self-reported impulsivity share a genetic correlation of only **rg \= 0.13**, meaning they are largely genetically distinct constructs despite both being labeled "attention/self-regulation" in clinical contexts. After mutual statistical control, each predicts unique genetic variance in different psychiatric outcomes.  
* **Why care:** Establishes that studies using task-based attention measures and studies using self-report impulsivity questionnaires are tapping fundamentally different genetic constructs. This has immediate practical implications for phenotype selection: lumping these under a single "ADHD-relevant" umbrella mixes distinct genetic signals and reduces discovery power.

## **Polygenic scores connect genes to attentional endophenotypes in developmental cohorts**

### **Paper 11**

**Moses M, Reynard G, Engelbrecht I,"; Dalvie S,";"; Stein DJ,";"; et al.** "Working memory and reaction time variability mediate the relationship between polygenic risk and ADHD traits in a general population sample." *Molecular Psychiatry*, 2022; 27: 5028–5037.

* **DOI:** 10.1038/s41380-022-01775-5 — verified  
* **Design type:** Polygenic score mediation analysis in a developmental cohort  
* **Sample:** \~**11,000** children from the ABCD Study; genetic data: SNP array  
* **What's new:** Working memory and reaction time variability partially mediate the relationship between ADHD polygenic risk scores and dimensional ADHD traits, providing the first robust population-based evidence for these cognitive measures as endophenotypes connecting genetic risk to clinical expression.  
* **Why care:** This is the strongest evidence to date that specific cognitive measures—particularly RTV, the same phenotype from Paper 8—sit on the causal chain between common genetic risk and attention problems. It provides a template for gene-brain-behavior pathway studies using large developmental cohorts like ABCD.

## **Where the field is heading: five convergent signals**

These 11 papers, read together, point toward several conclusions that should shape research strategy over the next five years.

**Neuromodulatory systems are internally heterogeneous at every level.** The cholinergic system has at least two functionally distinct cell types (Paper 4). The LC-NE system produces spatially selective signals (Paper 1), fires in distinct temporal patterns that shift cortical topology (Paper 2), contains non-overlapping ensembles, and outputs both modular and broadcast NE release within a single task (Paper 3). The classic one-modulator-one-function mapping is not merely incomplete—it is architecturally wrong. Future work must specify which cell type, firing pattern, or projection pathway is engaged, not just which neuromodulator.

**The thalamus is a computational hub, not a relay.** The TRN and mediodorsal thalamus perform active operations—sensory filtering via basal ganglia intermediaries (Paper 5\) and uncertainty decomposition for flexible cognitive control (Paper 6). These thalamic circuit motifs are being cited across attention, decision-making, autism, and schizophrenia literatures, making them a rare point of convergence across disciplinary boundaries. Serotonin's recent emergence as a bidirectional regulator of visual attentional strength—demonstrated via dorsal raphe photometry, optogenetics, and cortical 5-HT sensors in a 2026 Nature Communications paper (DOI: 10.1038/s41467-026-70658-y)—further expands the neuromodulatory map beyond the canonical three-transmitter framework.

**The molecular genetics of attention has shifted from dopamine to GABA.** The landmark Hatoum et al. GWAS (Paper 7\) found GABAergic and potassium channel pathways, not dopaminergic ones. The RTV GWAS (Paper 8\) implicated CNTNAP4 (a gene at the dopamine-GABA synapse interface) and NSF (glutamate receptor regulation). Thirty years of DRD4/DAT1 candidate-gene studies produced findings that do not replicate at GWAS scale. The field's molecular hypotheses need rebuilding from the GWAS results up.

**Attentional phenotypes decompose genetically, but sample size remains the bottleneck.** RTV and mean RT have partially distinct genetic architectures (Paper 8). Executive function and impulsivity are nearly genetically uncorrelated (Paper 10). Task-based inhibitory control is heritable but requires samples exceeding **100,000** for locus discovery (Paper 9). The implication is that biobanks with detailed cognitive testing—not just UK Biobank's 12-trial RT measure—are the critical missing infrastructure. The ABCD Study (Paper 11\) represents the most promising developmental cohort for connecting polygenic scores to specific attentional task performance.

**The circuit and genetic literatures are not yet talking to each other, but the path is visible.** The GABAergic pathways emerging from genetic studies (Paper 7\) connect naturally to the inhibitory microcircuit mechanisms being revealed by optogenetics—particularly the TRN's GABAergic gating role (Papers 5–6) and the inhibitory interneuron contributions to LC ensemble dynamics (Paper 2). A gene-brain-behavior study connecting GABA-pathway polygenic scores to thalamic gating efficiency during an attentional task is the obvious next experiment that nobody has yet published. This convergence zone—where polygenics meet circuit specificity—is where the field's next major integrative advance is likely to emerge.

## **Conclusion**

The attention field in 2026 is defined by two productive destructions: the fracturing of monolithic neuromodulator models into cell-type and ensemble architectures, and the replacement of candidate-gene dopamine hypotheses with polygenically complex, GABAergic-weighted molecular architectures. The thalamus has emerged as the critical circuit node where these levels may eventually meet. The most pressing gap is not a missing technique but a missing phenotyping infrastructure: large genotyped cohorts performing attentional tasks with sufficient granularity to resolve the genetic decomposition of attention into its circuit-level components. The researcher entering this literature should prioritize papers that specify cell types over transmitters, latent factors over raw task scores, and validated causal manipulations over correlational imaging—the 11 papers above exemplify each of these principles.
