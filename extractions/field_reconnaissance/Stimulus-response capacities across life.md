# **Stimulus-response capacities across life, and the frameworks that claim to explain them**

This report has two methodologically distinct parts. **Part 1** is a landscape survey: a 9-capacity × 7-organism-group matrix with annotations, a direct answer to which capacities appear to be uniquely documented in centralized nervous systems, and strongest-case-from-each-side treatment of contested claims. **Part 2** is a theoretical survey: operational definitions of reflex arcs, predictive coding, and active inference, and the state of the literature on whether there is a necessary sequence among them — phylogenetic, logical, or mechanistic. Both parts prioritize peer-reviewed literature from 2010–2026 and explicitly flag thin literatures, definitional mismatches, and empirical-versus-definitional disagreements.

The bottom line for Part 1 is that **the cross-phyletic gap is smaller and more jagged than the older textbook picture implied, but genuine gaps remain — most clearly in well-controlled associative learning in aneural animals, plants, fungi, and bacteria**, with the 2023 cnidarian results (box jellyfish and *Nematostella*) being the most important recent development. The bottom line for Part 2 is that **no explicit adjudication of phylogenetic sequence exists**; the dominant theoretical move, led by Friston, Pezzulo, Parr, and by Cisek from a different angle, is to **dissolve** the three-way distinction by treating reflex arcs, predictive coding, and active inference as different depths of one hierarchical feedback-control/inference architecture, rather than distinct evolutionary stages. Critics (Aguilera et al., Bruineberg et al., Litwin & Miłkowski, Taiz et al.) dispute whether that unification is scientific, mathematical, or merely definitional.

## **1.2 Direct answer: is any capacity uniquely documented in centralized nervous systems?**

**The short answer is no — but with one important qualification.** Each of the nine capacities has at least one peer-reviewed demonstration in at least one non-centralized group that meets that field's operational criteria. The textbook picture of "only brains can do X" does not survive the 2010–2026 literature intact.

The qualification is that **well-controlled associative learning is the capacity most constrained to nervous systems**. As of 2026, no associative-learning demonstration outside animals meets the standards routinely applied in behavioral neuroscience. Pre-2023, associative learning was also missing from the aneural-animal and diffuse-NS literature. That gap closed in 2023: Bielecki et al. (*Current Biology*) demonstrated operant learning in *Tripedalia cystophora* box jellyfish — with learning localized to isolated rhopalia of \~1,000 neurons — and Botton-Amiot et al. (*PNAS*) demonstrated Pavlovian conditioning in the sea anemone *Nematostella vectensis* with appropriate unpaired, CS-only, and US-only controls. **Neurons appear to still be required, but a centralized nervous system does not.** The same-year publication of both results, independently, is the single most important empirical update to the landscape.

The remaining gap is between **animals with any nervous system** and **everything else**. In that gap, the strongest positive claim for a non-neural system is *Physarum polycephalum* habituation (Boisseau 2016, Boussard 2019), which meets several Rankin criteria but has not been tested for dishabituation or habituation-of-dishabituation. The strongest positive claim for plants is epigenetic stress memory, which is mechanistically secure but definitionally contested as "learning." Pea associative conditioning (Gagliano 2016\) is the one clean test of associative learning outside animals at the capacity level, and it failed replication (Markel 2020). Fungi have essentially not been tested with a Rankin-style battery.

The other capacities that older textbooks located exclusively in brains — cross-modal integration, state-dependent gating, anticipatory responses, memory beyond adaptation, and hierarchical override — all have clean non-neural demonstrations: Tagkopoulos's bacterial cross-modal coupling, quorum sensing, Mitchell's bacterial anticipation, Saigusa's *Physarum* anticipation, Reid's externalized slime memory, and Dexter et al.'s video-microscopy replication of Jennings's *Stentor* behavioral hierarchy. **The distribution of these capacities is not neatly partitioned by nervous-system architecture**; it is better understood as a mosaic with capacity-specific gaps.

The most honest headline is therefore: *the depth question* ("is centralized-NS behavior different in kind?") is live and requires comparing the integration depth and flexibility of associative learning in bilaterians against the narrower paradigms now available in cnidarians and the more restricted claims in non-neural systems. The *breadth question* ("is any capacity uniquely centralized-NS?") has largely been answered in the negative by the last fifteen years of work.

## **1.3 Contested findings, by capacity**

### **Habituation in plants (Mimosa pudica)**

The central claim is Gagliano, Renton, Depczynski & Mancuso 2014 (*Oecologia*): repeated 15-cm drops cause Mimosa leaflet-folding response to diminish in a way that persists ≥28 days, is faster and more persistent in low-risk (high-light) conditions, and is reportedly dishabituated by horizontal shaking. The strongest proponent case emphasizes the ≥28-day persistence (far beyond plausible turgor fatigue timescales), the ecologically coherent environment-dependence, and the fact that the design satisfies *several* Rankin criteria. The strongest critic case (Biegler 2018 reanalysis; Taiz et al. 2019; Robinson et al. 2023\) argues that the reported dishabituation is not statistically robust, that the design lacked an adequate fatigue control using a different-but-mechanically-equivalent stimulus, that curves are compatible with slow pulvinar ATP/turgor depletion, and that no lab outside the Gagliano–Mancuso network has independently replicated. **This is a mixed empirical–definitional dispute**: proponents and critics disagree both about what the data show and about whether the label "habituation" is appropriate absent a neural substrate.

### **Associative learning in pea plants**

Gagliano et al. 2016 (*Scientific Reports*) reported that pea seedlings in a Y-maze learned to grow toward an arm where a fan (CS) predicted blue light (US), even when tested with the fan alone. Markel 2020 (*eLife*) ran a pre-registered, larger-sample, blinded replication with additional controls and found no effect; Markel also noted that in parts of Gagliano's own data, plants did not reliably grow toward the last-illuminated arm, undermining the underlying US→UR contingency. Gagliano et al.'s 2020 eLife comment argued light-leakage and proximity differences invalidated the replication; Markel's response used histogram-matching to show comparable background light. **This is primarily an empirical replication dispute**, secondarily definitional. Earlier pre-1975 conditioning attempts on plants (Holmes & Gruenberg, Haney, Armus, Levy) also failed, reviewed by Adelman 2018\. As of 2026 there is no independent positive replication.

### ***Physarum*** **habituation and the transferability of learned behavior**

Boisseau, Vogel & Dussutour 2016 (*Proc R Soc B*) reported that *Physarum polycephalum* crossing bridges coated with quinine or caffeine initially slowed but recovered normal speed after nine days of exposure, with spontaneous recovery after a rest period and stimulus specificity between quinine and caffeine. Boussard et al. 2019 (*Phil Trans R Soc B*) replicated with mechanistic controls and showed the habituated state survives sclerotization. Vogel & Dussutour 2016 showed that cell–cell fusion transfers the habituated phenotype to naive receiver cells. The strongest proponent case emphasizes partial fulfillment of Rankin criteria, the chemical-retention mechanism, and the cell-fusion transfer as evidence of a concrete cytoplasmic substrate. The strongest critic case (canvassed in Smith-Ferguson & Beekman 2024\) argues that detoxification-via-uptake is itself the proximate mechanism, that dishabituation and habituation-of-dishabituation have not been tested, and that after cell fusion the entity is no longer two organisms, so framing the transfer as "between individuals" is semantic. **Empirical phenomenon robust; interpretive label contested.**

### ***Paramecium*** **associative conditioning**

Gelber's 1950s work, Hennessey et al. 1979, and Alipour et al. 2018 report Pavlovian-like conditioning in *Paramecium*; Gershman, Balbi, Gallistel & Gunawardena 2021 (*eLife*) argue the historical dismissal was overstated and the case is stronger than often believed. Critics point to Katz & Deterline 1958, Mirsky & Katz 1958, and Hinkle & Wood 1994 on *Stentor* tube-escape (confounded with gravity orientation), together with repeated replication difficulties. **This is a genuinely empirical dispute about replicability**, not merely definitional. Current state: unresolved, leaning skeptical.

### **Bacterial anticipation**

Mitchell et al. 2009 (*Nature*) showed *E. coli* exposed sequentially to lactose then maltose pre-induces maltose genes on lactose alone, reflecting gut transit statistics; yeast shows an analogous fermentation-sequence pre-induction; lab lines evolved without the coupling lose the trait. Tagkopoulos, Liu & Tavazoie 2008 (*Science*) showed transcriptional cross-modal coupling of temperature and oxygen. Proponents (Lyon 2015\) treat these as genuine predictive behaviors. Critics concede the empirical phenomena but argue the "anticipation" is evolutionarily hardwired rather than learned within a lifetime, so the label "prediction" is definitional. **Empirics are settled; the label is contested.**

### **Mycorrhizal "wood-wide web"**

Simard's tradition (Simard 1997; Beiler 2010; Song 2015; Gorzelak 2015\) reports carbon transfer between trees through common mycorrhizal networks (CMNs), preferential flow to stressed partners, and kin-biased signaling. Karst, Jones & Hoeksema 2023 (*Nature Ecology & Evolution*) systematically reviewed three popular claims — that CMNs are widespread in forests, that network-transferred resources increase seedling performance, and that mother trees preferentially provision kin — and found the first two inadequately supported by field evidence and the third **without any peer-reviewed empirical support**. They also document positive-citation bias. **This is largely an empirical dispute** about what field experiments actually show at the population level. The existence of CMNs is not disputed; magnitude, selectivity, and the "communication" interpretation are.

### **Adamatzky's "fungal language"**

Adamatzky 2022 (*R. Soc. Open Sci.*) reported species-specific electrical spike trains in four fungi with Zipf-like statistics and proposed a \~50-word lexicon. Critics (Buffi et al. 2025 *FEMS Microbiol Rev*; Fungal Biol Rev 2023 commentary) note that the spikes are reproducible but that the linguistic analogy lacks evidence of communication function or semantics, and many patterns are explicable by metabolic/growth dynamics. **Spiking is real; the language interpretation is disputed.**

### **Plant "neurobiology"**

Baluška, Mancuso, Trewavas, and Calvo (synthesized in Calvo et al. 2020, *Annals of Botany*) argue plants show propagating action and variation potentials, glutamate/GABA/acetylcholine signaling, anesthetic-reversible electrical activity (Yokawa et al. 2018), and synchronized root oscillations — supporting a functional plant "neurobiology." Alpi et al. 2007, Taiz et al. 2019, Draguhn et al. 2021, and Robinson et al. 2023 argue plants have no neurons or synapses, that their electrical signals are H⁺/Ca²⁺-driven and orders of magnitude slower than neuronal APs, that glutamate in plants is a wound/developmental signal not an excitatory neurotransmitter at a synapse, and that applying Feinberg & Mallatt's criteria only vertebrates, arthropods, and cephalopods qualify for consciousness (a separate but correlated claim). **Heavily definitional**, with layered empirical disputes about propagation speed, synaptic equivalence, and anesthetic specificity.

## **1.5 Cross-cutting caveats for Part 1**

Three patterns recur and deserve flagging. First, **the Rankin criteria are almost never fully satisfied outside animals**: *Physarum* and plant studies satisfy response decrement, spontaneous recovery, stimulus specificity, and sometimes intensity/frequency effects, but dishabituation and habituation-of-dishabituation are usually untested. Second, **cell-culture and molecular "memory" research uses the word "memory" for any persistent state change**, while animal-behavior research reserves it for a representation that guides a future response; this definitional slide is a recurring source of apparent disagreement that is not empirical. Third, **ctenophore and placozoan behavioral plasticity literatures are genuinely sparse**; Moroz's lab has explicitly flagged the absence of systematic paradigms, so "ctenophores lack documented associative learning" should be read as *not yet tested* rather than *tested and found absent*.

# **PART 2 — Necessary sequence between three frameworks**

## **2.1 Operational definitions and definitional drift**

**Reflex arcs.** In modern physiology, the reflex arc is the minimum anatomical-functional circuit producing a stereotyped motor or autonomic response to a sensory stimulus: receptor → afferent neuron → (optional interneurons) → efferent neuron → effector. Sherrington's 1906 *Integrative Action of the Nervous System* is the modern origin; the monosynaptic stretch reflex and polysynaptic withdrawal reflex are canonical. **Important definitional drift**: in active-inference treatments (Adams, Shipp & Friston 2013 *Brain Structure and Function*; Brown et al. 2013 *Cognitive Processing*) the reflex arc is reinterpreted as a proprioceptive prediction-error-suppressing loop in which α-motor neurons cancel descending predictions. In the philosophical critique of Keijzer 2015 (*Biology & Philosophy*) and Keijzer, van Duijn & Lyon 2013 (*Adaptive Behavior*), the reflex concept is rejected as the primitive unit of nervous-system evolution at all — the "skin-brain thesis" holds that nervous systems evolved for internal coordination of body-wide motility, not for stimulus-to-response transduction.

**Predictive coding.** In the Rao & Ballard 1999 (*Nature Neuroscience*) tradition, predictive coding is a hierarchical Bayesian algorithm in which higher cortical areas send top-down predictions and lower areas send forward only residual prediction error, with representations corresponding to posterior expectations over hidden causes of sensory input. Bastos et al. 2012 (*Neuron*) proposed the canonical cortical microcircuit mapping (superficial pyramidals as error units, deep pyramidals as prediction units). **Definitional variants**: Friston's variational/free-energy formulation (Friston 2005, 2008, 2010\) reformulates predictive coding as gradient descent on variational free energy under Gaussian assumptions, embedding it in the free-energy principle; Clark 2013, 2016 and Hohwy 2013 popularize "predictive processing" or "prediction-error minimization" as an umbrella claim about perception, attention, action, emotion, and cognition. Sprevak 2024 and Aitchison & Lengyel 2017 note these are claims at different Marr levels and that the commitments are not identical.

**Active inference.** In the Friston-lab sense, active inference extends the free-energy principle to action: an agent selects policies to minimize expected free energy, decomposing into an epistemic (information-gain) term and a pragmatic (preference) term. Two canonical formulations exist — continuous-state active inference (Friston et al. 2010 *Biological Cybernetics*), in which action enters only through classical proprioceptive reflex arcs suppressing prediction error, and discrete state-space active inference (Friston et al. 2017 *Neural Computation*; Da Costa et al. 2020 *Journal of Mathematical Psychology*), using partially observable MDPs with expected-free-energy-weighted policy posteriors. Parr, Pezzulo & Friston 2022 (*Active Inference*, MIT Press) is the book-length synthesis. **Drift**: popularized "active inference" as "acting to minimize prediction error" (Clark 2016; Hohwy 2020\) often lacks the mathematical commitments (expected free energy, precision, Markov blankets) of the technical framework.

## **2.2 Logical / conceptual sequence**

The **canonical Friston-school containment hierarchy** is FEP ⊇ active inference ⊇ predictive coding ⊇ reflex arcs as degenerate / shallow cases. The strongest explicit statements are Friston 2010 *Nature Reviews Neuroscience* ("The free-energy principle: a unified brain theory?"); Brown et al. 2013 (*Cognitive Processing*): *"active inference can be regarded as equipping a generalised predictive coding scheme with classical reflex arcs"*; and Parr, Pezzulo & Friston 2022, chapter 4, showing that continuous-state active inference reduces to predictive coding when action is held fixed, with reflex arcs as the minimal-depth limit where α-motor neurons function as error units. The strongest free-standing statement that all three are **different descriptions of the same underlying machinery at different levels of abstraction**, rather than architecturally distinct stages, is Pezzulo, Parr & Friston 2022 (*Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B* 377:20200531), arguing that reflex loops, predictive coding, and active inference differ only in hierarchical breadth and depth of the same generative-model architecture.

The **strongest case for the free energy principle as master framework** is Friston 2010, extended by Friston 2013 (*J. R. Soc. Interface*, "Life as we know it"), Ramstead, Badcock & Friston 2018 (*Physics of Life Reviews*, "Answering Schrödinger's question"), and Friston et al. 2023 (*Physics Reports*, "The free energy principle made simpler but not too simple"). The claim is that the FEP is a first principle of Bayesian mechanics for any system with a Markov blanket at non-equilibrium steady state, from which active inference derives as a corollary for systems with active states, predictive coding derives as its Gaussian continuous-state algorithm, and reflex arcs derive as the minimal-depth active-inference loop.

The **strongest cases against** are of three kinds. **Mathematical–technical critique**: Aguilera, Millidge, Tschantz & Buckley 2022 (*Physics of Life Reviews* 40:24–50) show in tractable linear stochastic systems that the FEP's required Markov-blanket and solenoidal-flow structure holds only in a narrow parameter regime, and that systems with perception–action asymmetry (most living organisms) typically violate the derivation conditions; Biehl, Pollock & Kanai 2021 (*Entropy*) show that different Markov-blanket definitions in Friston's corpus are non-equivalent and the original free-energy lemma is false as stated. Friston's group replies (Ramstead & Sakthivadivel 2022\) concede technical points while maintaining a path-integral survival of the principle. **Philosophical critique**: Bruineberg, Dołęga, Dewhurst & Baltieri 2022 ("The Emperor's New Markov Blanket," *BBS*) distinguish "Pearl blankets" (legitimate epistemic device) from "Friston blankets" (metaphysical claims about physical boundaries of agents) and argue the slide is unjustified; Colombo & Wright 2021 (*Synthese*) argue the FEP cannot simultaneously be a necessary truth about self-organising systems and a substantive scientific theory; Andrews 2021 (*Biology & Philosophy*) argues the FEP is a model *structure* to which empirical *construals* must be added, meaning non-neural applications are claims about construals, not about the FEP itself. **Unification-quality critique**: Litwin & Miłkowski 2020 (*Cognitive Science*, "Unification by Fiat") argue predictive processing is not general, simple, homogeneous, or systematic in the ways required of genuine theoretical unification; Cao 2020 (*Review of Philosophy and Psychology*, "New labels for old ideas") argues the containment hierarchy is partly terminological relabeling of pre-existing neuroscience concepts; Aitchison & Lengyel 2017 (*Current Opinion in Neurobiology*) argue predictive coding is only one algorithmic realisation of Bayesian inference — neither necessarily Bayesian nor necessarily tied to active inference — so the subsumption is a modelling choice rather than a forced logical entailment.

**Reflex-concept critique, separately**: Keijzer 2015 and Arnellos & Keijzer 2017 reject the reflex-arc-as-primitive premise itself, arguing that if reflex arcs were not the foundational unit of nervous systems, then subsuming them under active inference inherits the problematic input–output assumption rather than correcting it. Bruineberg, Kiverstein & Rietveld 2018 (*Synthese*, "The anticipating brain is not a scientist") argue that casting reflexive/sensorimotor loops as "inference" is a theoretical imposition.

**Summary of the logical sequence question.** Within the Friston school the logical containment is presented as mathematical derivation; outside it, the same containment is read as a modelling choice, a terminological move, or a category error. This is an **unresolved mix of empirical and definitional disagreement**. The empirical component is whether cortical microcircuits actually implement Rao–Ballard–Bastos predictive coding and whether prefrontal/subcortical systems implement expected-free-energy-based policy selection (Walsh et al. 2020 *Annals NY Acad Sci*; Schwartenbeck et al. 2015; Smith et al. 2023 — mixed evidence). The definitional component is whether the FEP is a physical principle, a modelling heuristic, or a tautology.

## **2.3 Phylogenetic sequence**

**There is no peer-reviewed paper in 2010–2026 that squarely adjudicates "reflex arc architecturally earlier than predictive coding / active inference" as a discrete empirical question.** The direct question is largely unaddressed in explicit form; adjacent literatures bear on it indirectly and converge on three distinguishable positions.

**Position 1: reflex-arc-first within nervous systems.** Jékely 2011 (*Proc R Soc B*) reconstructs the earliest nervous circuits as combined sensory-motor neurons translating input into output on ciliated or muscular effectors, with Platynereis larvae, cnidarian planulae, and sponge larvae retaining these as ancestral. Arendt 2021 (*Phil Trans B*) surveys both the vertical/reflex-arc origin hypothesis (Parker 1919, Mackie 1970\) and the horizontal/internal-coordination scenario (Pantin 1956; Passano 1963). This is the longer tradition and the "standard" view within comparative neurobiology, though Arendt notes Pantin's own rejection of the reflex arc as primitive.

**Position 2: reflex arcs are not the primitive unit — internal coordination is.** Keijzer, van Duijn & Lyon 2013 (*Adaptive Behavior*), Keijzer 2015 (*Biology & Philosophy*), and Arnellos & Keijzer 2017 (*Biology & Philosophy*) argue that nervous systems evolved for whole-body muscle coordination, not stimulus-to-response transduction, and that reflex arcs are a secondary elaboration. Jékely, Keijzer & Godfrey-Smith 2015 (*Phil Trans B*) is a rare joint statement framing input–output and internal-coordination views as axes of an option space rather than mutually exclusive stages.

**Position 3: predictive/inferential dynamics predate nervous systems.** Friston 2013 (*J. R. Soc. Interface*, "Life as we know it"), Kirchhoff et al. 2018, and Pezzulo, Parr & Friston 2022 argue that free-energy-minimising dynamics are a generic property of any Markov-blanketed self-organising system and that reflex loops are *already* shallow predictive loops. Lyon 2015 (*Frontiers in Microbiology*) and Lyon, Keijzer, Arendt & Levin 2021 (*Phil Trans B*) provide the basal-cognition empirical companion: bacteria already display anticipation, memory, valence, and decision-making (Mitchell 2009; Tagkopoulos 2008). Levin 2019 (*Frontiers in Psychology*) argues bioelectric signaling in non-neural cells was the substrate from which neural networks are an evolutionary exaptation and speed optimization. Corcoran, Pezzulo & Hohwy 2020 (*Biology & Philosophy*) grades cognition from allostatic reflex through counterfactual cognition as a continuum of model depth; Tschantz, Baltieri, Seth & Buckley 2020 (*PLOS Computational Biology*) model *E. coli* chemotaxis as active inference.

**Cisek's dissolving position.** Cisek 2019 (*Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics*) and Cisek 2022 (*Phil Trans B*) argue that neither the reflex arc nor predictive coding is the right-grained biological primitive; **negative feedback control** is. Early vertebrate tectal circuits are banks of species-typical action feedback loops, with predictive elaborations added via pallial and prefrontal expansions. In Cisek's framing, the reflex-vs-prediction dichotomy is a psychological-tradition artifact. Pezzulo, Parr & Friston 2022 and Cisek 2022 co-appear in the same *Phil Trans B* issue and are often read as complementary: Cisek emphasizes feedback-control-first, Pezzulo et al. emphasize that reflex loops *already are* shallow predictive loops.

**Skeptical counterweight.** Bruineberg, Kiverstein & Rietveld 2018 argue that the FEP's notion of "inference" is minimal — essentially generalized synchronization between coupled systems — so the claim that bacteria "do active inference" may reduce to "bacteria are dynamically coupled to environments," which does not establish predictive architecture as phylogenetically *prior* to reflex arcs in any substantive sense.

**Verdict on phylogenetic ordering.** The question is **contested and largely unaddressed in explicit form**. Most authors dissolve it: the Friston school by identifying reflex arcs with minimal active inference, Cisek by making feedback control the primitive, and the Keijzer school by rejecting reflex-arc-as-primitive at all. The strongest pressure against a "reflex-first" reading comes from the bacterial-anticipation and basal-cognition literatures, which locate anticipatory dynamics in aneural organisms predating any reflex arc — but whether those dynamics count as "predictive architecture" rather than as evolved regulation is exactly the definitional dispute at issue. There is a persistent **grain mismatch**: "reflex arc" is an anatomical-circuit claim, while "predictive coding" and "active inference" are computational-functional claims that can in principle be realized in many architectures.

## **2.4 Mechanistic / developmental sequence**

This is the **thinnest of the three orderings**. The major active-inference theorists treat the relation as phylogenetic and hierarchical, not developmental. The cleanest ontogenetic prediction — that reflex arcs scaffold later predictive-coding and active-inference circuitry within a single individual — is implied but rarely tested as a predictive-processing hypothesis.

**Developmental neuroscience** independently establishes that primitive reflexes (Moro, grasp, rooting, asymmetric tonic neck) are brainstem- and spinal-cord-mediated, present at term, and progressively gated by corticospinal maturation over months to years; the lateral corticospinal tract refines postnatally. This literature rarely frames itself in active-inference terms. **Infant predictive-processing work** (Köster et al. 2020 *Perspectives on Psychological Science*; Kouider et al. 2015 *Nature Communications*; Trainor et al. 2012\) detects mismatch-negativity-type prediction-error responses in newborns and early infants, showing that predictive processing is operative from the first months — concurrent with reflexive behavior, not a later addition. This **complicates the naive "reflex first, predictive coding later" developmental story**: the two run in parallel from early development rather than in strict sequence.

**The dominant theoretical move** is Pezzulo, Rigoli & Friston 2015 (*Progress in Neurobiology*) and Pezzulo, Parr & Friston 2022, which posit a phylogenetic (not ontogenetic) trajectory from homeostatic reflexes to goal-directed control via two formal operations — duplication (parallel sensorimotor loops) and allostasis (horizontal predictive links between modalities). Within ontogeny, the scaffolding claim is **implicit and under-tested**. Corcoran, Pezzulo & Hohwy 2020 provides the philosophical companion. The clearest explicit scaffolding claim outside neuroscience comes from Kuchling, Friston, Georgiev & Levin 2020 (*Physics of Life Reviews*), where primitive feedback loops in morphogenesis are the basal layer that more complex inference nests upon; this is non-neural developmental, not neural ontogenetic.

**Verdict on the mechanistic/developmental ordering.** Scaffolding of reflex → predictive coding → active inference within individual organisms is **largely unaddressed as a distinct empirical program**. Infant neuroscience supplies some evidence that prediction-error processing is concurrent with reflexive behavior from early postnatal life, weakening the strict-sequence reading. The dominant theoretical position — that the three frameworks are different depths of one architecture — makes the ontogenetic sequence question partly ill-posed.

## **2.5 Applicability to non-neural organisms**

This is **actively contested** and generated the highest-profile back-and-forth in the 2013–2024 literature, in *Physics of Life Reviews*, *Trends in Plant Science*, *Protoplasma*, and *J. R. Soc. Interface*. Two partially independent debates run in parallel: the mathematical/FEP controversy over whether the free-energy principle has empirical content for *any* self-organising system, and the biological plant/bacterial cognition controversy over whether non-neural organisms can be legitimately described as performing inference.

**Strongest case for non-neural applicability.** Friston 2013 ("Life as we know it") presents a heuristic derivation that any ergodic Markov-blanketed system minimizes variational free energy. Friston, Levin, Sengupta & Pezzulo 2015 (*J. R. Soc. Interface*, "Knowing one's place") applies active inference to planarian pattern regulation; Kuchling, Friston, Georgiev & Levin 2020 (*Physics of Life Reviews*, "Morphogenesis as Bayesian inference") is the flagship full Bayesian account of morphogenesis, with cells as active-inference agents inferring their location in a morphogenetic field. Baluška & Levin 2016 (*Frontiers in Psychology*, "On having no head") argues for substrate-independent cognition: memory, decision-making, and anticipation in bacteria, plants, somatic cells, planaria, and slime molds. Calvo, Baluška & Sims 2016 and Calvo & Friston 2017 (*J. R. Soc. Interface*, "Predicting green") argue plants are anticipatory engines embodying generative models of light, gravity, and moisture, with falsifiable experimental tests proposed. Lyon 2015 is the strongest empirical bacterial case.

**Strongest case against.** Aguilera, Millidge, Tschantz & Buckley 2022 is the strongest technical critique: in tractable systems, FEP's assumptions hold only in narrow parameter regimes, and perception-action asymmetry (characteristic of living organisms) typically violates them. Bruineberg et al. 2022 is the strongest conceptual critique, distinguishing Pearl blankets from Friston blankets and arguing the realist extension to non-neural agents is unjustified. Andrews 2021 reframes the debate by arguing the FEP is a model structure that predicts nothing without empirical construal — saving it from naive falsification but conceding it provides no independent empirical traction on bacteria or plants. Taiz et al. 2019 (*Trends in Plant Science*) and Draguhn, Mallatt & Robinson 2021 (*Protoplasma*) are the biological critique of plant-cognition-as-prediction, arguing plants lack neural anatomy and behavioral signatures, and that responses are genetically encoded rather than inferred online. Mallatt, Blatt, Draguhn, Robinson & Taiz 2021 provides a systematic twelve-point rebuttal of plant neurobiology. Litwin & Miłkowski 2020 ("Unification by Fiat") challenges the quality of predictive processing's unification claims at the general level.

**Is the question settled?** No — but the community has split into **three stable positions**: (1) FEP as mathematical tautology or model structure, applicable but vacuous without construal (Andrews, instrumentalist readings); (2) FEP as empirical theory applicable to non-neural systems, contested empirically and technically (Levin, Calvo, Baluška, Lyon, Friston in realist mode); (3) non-neural cognition/inference rejected as conceptually confused or empirically unsupported (Taiz, Draguhn, Mallatt, Robinson, Bruineberg, Aguilera). **Fungi are essentially absent from the peer-reviewed active-inference literature**, a genuine gap to flag, and direct active-inference formalism for slime molds is emerging but thin (largely conference proceedings rather than journal papers). Much of the dispute is **definitional rather than empirical**: whether "inference" and "prediction" require neural anatomy, whether mathematical analogy with Bayesian inference constitutes explanation, and whether the FEP is a physical principle or a modelling heuristic.

## **2.6 Principal competing positions**

The **pro-unification master-framework camp** includes Friston 2010, 2013, 2019, 2023a/b; Parr, Pezzulo & Friston 2022; Da Costa et al. 2020; Ramstead, Badcock & Friston 2018; Hohwy 2013, 2020; Clark 2013, 2016\. Its strongest claim is that the FEP licenses active inference as a corollary, predictive coding as its Gaussian algorithm, and reflex arcs as its minimal-depth case, providing a single unified theory applicable across all living systems.

The **deflationary camp** includes Colombo & Seriès 2012; Litwin & Miłkowski 2020; Miłkowski & Litwin 2022; Cao 2020; Aitchison & Lengyel 2017; Bowers & Davis 2012; Jones & Love 2011\. Its strongest claim is that Bayesian/predictive-coding models are useful instruments without a unifying realist metaphysics, and that the containment hierarchy is a modelling choice relabeling pre-existing concepts rather than a genuine theoretical unification.

The **technical-critique camp** includes Aguilera et al. 2022; Biehl, Pollock & Kanai 2021; Raja et al. 2021\. Its strongest claim is that the mathematical foundations of the FEP are narrower than advertised and require unstated assumptions.

The **ecological-enactive and philosophical-critique camp** includes Bruineberg, Kiverstein & Rietveld 2018; Bruineberg et al. 2022; Colombo & Wright 2021; van Es & Hipólito 2020; Williams 2020; Sims 2017; Klein 2018; Andrews 2021 (in reconstructive mode); Gallagher & Allen 2018\. Its strongest claim is that realist extensions of the FEP to agents' physical boundaries rest on an unjustified slide from epistemic to metaphysical usage, and that "inference" in the FEP sense is weaker than the cognitive-science sense and does not license non-neural cognition claims.

The **reflex-concept critique camp** (Keijzer 2015; Keijzer, van Duijn & Lyon 2013; Arnellos & Keijzer 2017\) holds the separate view that even the starting assumption of reflex-arc-as-primitive is historically and biologically unmotivated.

The **dissolving camps** (Pezzulo, Parr & Friston 2022; Cisek 2019, 2022; Jékely, Keijzer & Godfrey-Smith 2015\) argue by different routes that the three-way distinction among reflex arcs, predictive coding, and active inference is either depth-based (Pezzulo, Parr, Friston) or a psychological-tradition artifact (Cisek) or one dimension of a multi-axis option space (Jékely, Keijzer, Godfrey-Smith).

# **Cross-cutting conclusions**

Three non-summarising points deserve emphasis.

First, **the two questions interact asymmetrically**. Q1 is an empirical landscape question with a growing and mostly tractable evidence base; its central surprise is the 2023 cnidarian associative-learning results, which closed what had been the cleanest between-nervous-system-architecture gap. Q2 is a theoretical question where the dominant move in the literature is not to order the three frameworks but to **dissolve the ordering question**: Pezzulo, Parr & Friston argue reflex arcs are already shallow predictive loops, Cisek argues all three are elaborations of negative feedback control, and Jékely, Keijzer & Godfrey-Smith argue input–output and internal-coordination views are axes rather than stages. **The empirical findings of Q1 do not force a particular answer to Q2**, because any of the dissolving positions is compatible with the observed capacity distribution.

Second, **the empirical-versus-definitional distinction is load-bearing throughout**. Many apparent disagreements — about whether bacteria "anticipate," whether plants "learn," whether mycelia "decide," whether fungal spikes constitute "language," whether bacterial chemotaxis is "active inference" — are primarily disputes about the extension of cognitive-science vocabulary to non-neural systems, not about the underlying phenomena. The genuinely empirical disputes are narrower: whether Gagliano's pea conditioning replicates (it does not, per Markel 2020), whether Mimosa habituation survives fatigue controls (contested), whether *Paramecium* classical conditioning replicates (contested, leaning skeptical), and whether the FEP's technical assumptions hold for perception-action-asymmetric systems (contested, per Aguilera et al. 2022). Readers who conflate these two layers of dispute will misjudge the state of each literature.

Third, **the most productive research programs are the ones that pick a single capacity and test it rigorously in a single non-neural system with field-standard operational criteria**. The Dussutour lab's sustained work on *Physarum* habituation, the Dexter–Prabakaran–Gunawardena video-microscopy replication of Jennings's *Stentor* hierarchy, and the Bielecki and Botton-Amiot demonstrations of cnidarian associative learning are informative in a way that broad programmatic claims about "cognition everywhere" or "consciousness requires a brain" are not. The corresponding methodological gap is that **Rankin-style batteries have almost never been applied outside animals**, and field-standard associative-learning paradigms have almost never been attempted in fungi or archaea. These are the empirical frontiers where the landscape could change fastest in the next decade.
