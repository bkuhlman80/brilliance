## Test Case 1 (v2): N5 Impulsiveness

Big Five domain: N → V2 Bright Triad zone: **Center triangle (Brilliance)**
V1 prediction: N5 should resemble C facets (I×ES zone-mates).
V2 prediction: N5 should resemble center-triangle facets (E3, E4, E6, A1, A6).

### N5's trajectory (unchanged)
Monotonic linear decline across the full lifespan. No late-life upturn. Qualitatively unique among N facets. Three independent replications (Terracciano L, Brandt L, Jackson M).

### V1 comparison: N5 vs C facets (I×ES zone)
This was the strongest test case in v1. N5's trajectory mirrors C6 Deliberation — both monotonic, opposite directions, convergence toward reduced impulsivity. Three independent replications. **Strongly supported in v1.**

### V2 comparison: N5 vs Center-triangle facets

| Center facet | Trajectory | Shape | Match to N5? |
|---|---|---|---|
| E3 Assertiveness | Increases through midlife | Social Dominance pattern | ✗ Opposite direction |
| E4 Activity | Steepest monotonic decline | Accelerates late life | ✓ Both decline monotonically |
| E6 Positive Emotions | Stable or slight decline | -- | ✗ Different magnitude |
| A1 Trust | Inverted-U, late-life decline | Unique in A | Partial (both decline late-life) |
| A6 Tender-Mindedness | Increase (convex) | -- | ✗ Opposite direction |

N5 shares a declining direction with E4 (both monotonic declines), but E4's decline is attributed to physical vitality, not impulse control. E3 and A6 move in the opposite direction. E6 is near-flat. A1's inverted-U is a different shape.

### V2 verdict for N5: WEAKER than v1

The v2 center-triangle zone-mates are a poorer developmental match for N5 than the v1 C-zone-mates. N5's mirror-image relationship with C6 Deliberation — the strongest cross-domain developmental signal in the corpus — is no longer captured by the zone comparison, because C6 is in the intersection zone and N5 is in the center triangle.

The only center-triangle facet with a similar trajectory is E4 (both decline), but E4's decline has a different etiology (physical vitality vs. impulse control) and different curve shape (accelerating vs. linear).

**V1 result: ✓ ZONE-MATES (strong). V2 result: ✗ DOMAIN-MATES NOR ZONE-MATES (ambiguous).**

### Why v2 is WEAKER than v1 for H5

The v2 reassignments hurt H5 substantially because:

1. **N5's mirror-image with C6 is lost.** In v1, N5 (I×ES zone) was compared against C facets, and its trajectory perfectly mirrored C6 Deliberation — the single strongest developmental signal in the corpus. In v2, N5 is in the center triangle, and its new zone-mates (E3, E4, E6, A1, A6) do not share its trajectory. The N5–C6 developmental convergence still EXISTS — it just no longer counts as a "zone-mate" comparison.

2. **A1's inverted-U trajectory is idiosyncratic.** Moving A1 from the ES core to the center triangle doesn't change its trajectory, and it doesn't match center-triangle facets any better than it matched ES-core facets. A1 is a developmental outlier regardless of zone placement.

3. **The center triangle is developmentally heterogeneous.** The v2 center contains: one facet that increases (E3), two that decline (E4, N5), one that's stable (E6), one that increases convexly (A6), and one that peaks-then-declines (A1). There is no coherent developmental signature for the center triangle as a group.

### The key tension

H5 asks whether facets co-develop within BT zones. For N5, the strongest developmental affinity is with C6 (a v2 intersection facet), not with any center-triangle facet. This means either:
- The v2 zone assignment for N5 is wrong (it should stay in I×ES with C6), OR
- Co-development is not a good test of center-triangle membership (the center may cohere on genetic diffuseness, per H3, without sharing developmental trajectories)

The second interpretation is more consistent with the overall pattern: the center triangle's strongest support comes from genetic architecture (H3), not developmental dynamics (H5).

# Task H6 (v2): Developmental Rank-Order Stability by Bright Triad Zone

## Data Availability (unchanged from v1)

The corpus does NOT contain numerical rank-order stability estimates for individual NEO-PI-R facets. This fundamental limitation persists regardless of zone assignments.

Available data:
- Brandt (2023, L): Qualitative stability patterns from figures, no tabulated per-facet values
- Brandes (2020, L): Childhood domain-level stability only (ages 9–13, non-NEO instrument)
- Schwaba (2022, M): Within-domain co-development correlations (not rank-order stability)

## Prediction

V2 prediction: Primary cores > Intersection zones > Center triangle / Edge / Outside

## Domain-Level Analysis (fallback, v2)

Since facet-level zone means remain untestable, I check domain-level patterns.

V2 primary domains: N (Emotional Stability), E (Extraversion) — but the only primary-core E facet is E2. The E domain includes many intersection/center facets.

- But C4 (intersection) has HIGHER stability than its domain — intersection facets are not uniformly less stable ✗

## Verdict (V2): LARGELY UNTESTABLE — same as v1

The absence of numerical per-facet rank-order stability data prevents a direct zone comparison. The qualitative domain-level patterns are weakly consistent with the prediction:
- N (primary-dominated): high, uniform stability ✓
- A (mixed zones): most variable stability ✓
- But individual intersection facets (C4) can be highly stable ✗

The v2 reassignments don't change the verdict because the data limitation is the same — no facet-level stability values to compute zone means. The qualitative interpretation is marginally sharper in v2 because the N domain is now more purely "primary" (4 of 6 facets) and C is purely "intersection" (6 of 6), making domain-level patterns slightly more interpretable as zone-level patterns.
