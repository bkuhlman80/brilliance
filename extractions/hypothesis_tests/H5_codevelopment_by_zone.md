## Test Case 1: N5 Impulsiveness

Big Five domain: N → Bright Triad zone: I x ES (C zone, reversed)
Prediction: N5 trajectory should resemble C facets more than N facets.

### N5's trajectory
Monotonic linear decline across the full lifespan. NO late-life upturn. Qualitatively
unique among all six N facets. Three independent replications:
- Terracciano (2005, L): explicit monotonic decline
- Brandt (2023, L): decreased
- Jackson (2009, M): Impulse Control composite (≈ reversed-N5 + C6) shows d₁₃ = 0.88,
  the largest cumulative age signal of any C-domain facet

### Comparison to N domain-mates
| N facet | Direction | Shape | Match to N5? |
|---|---|---|---|
| N1 | Flat/increase | Non-declining | ✗ Opposite |
| N2 | Decline | Curvilinear, late-life upturn | ✗ Different shape (N5 has no upturn) |
| N3 | Decline (unclear) | Mixed | Partial direction match |
| N6 | Late-life increase | Reversal | ✗ Opposite in late life |

N5 shares only the direction (declining) with N2 and possibly N3, but its SHAPE
(monotonic, no upturn) is qualitatively different from every other N facet.

### Comparison to BT zone-mates (I x ES = C facets)
| C facet | Direction | Shape | Match to N5? |
|---|---|---|---|
| C6 Deliberation | Increase (+1.70 T/dec) | Monotonic | ✓✓ **MIRROR IMAGE** |
| C1 Competence | Peak then decline | Inverted-U | Partial (late-life convergence) |
| C2 Order | Flat | -- | ✗ |
| C4 Ach. Striving | Flat | -- | ✗ |
| C5 Self-Discipline | Peak then decline | Inverted-U | Partial |

**C6 Deliberation is the mirror image of N5.** Both are monotonic. N5 declines linearly;
C6 increases at the steepest rate in the C domain. They converge toward reduced
impulsivity / increased constraint. Three independent studies confirm this convergence
(Terracciano L, Brandt L, Jackson M). The authors of Terracciano (2005) explicitly note
the N5-C6 convergence.

When reversed, N5's trajectory (increasing impulse control) matches C6's trajectory
(increasing deliberation) almost exactly. No N domain-mate shows this pattern.

### Verdict: ✓ ZONE-MATES
N5's developmental trajectory is qualitatively different from all N domain-mates and
mirrors its BT zone-mate C6 Deliberation. This is the strongest test case — the
developmental convergence is the single best-documented cross-domain signal in the
corpus (three independent replications, Tier 1 evidence).

## Test Case 2: E4 Activity

Big Five domain: E → Bright Triad zone: Center triangle (leaning toward I x ES)
Prediction: E4 trajectory should resemble C facets more than E facets.

### E4's trajectory
Steepest monotonic decline of any E facet, accelerating in late life. Only E facet
responding to retirement (Löckenhoff 2009, S). Roberts (2006, XL) classifies E4 in
Social Vitality (declining), not Social Dominance (increasing).

Important complication: Bleidorn (2009, M) found E4 INCREASED in the 18-59 age range.
v1 studies (Terracciano L, Brandt L) covering ages past 60 found steep decline. The
discrepancy suggests E4 may increase in young adulthood then decline steeply from midlife
onward — a qualitatively different trajectory depending on age window.

### Comparison to E domain-mates
| E facet | Direction | Shape | Match to E4? |
|---|---|---|---|
| E1 | Flat | Stable | ✗ |
| E2 | Flat/slight decline | Stable | ✗ Different magnitude |
| E3 | Increases midlife | Opposite direction | ✗✗ |
| E5 | Decline | Different curvature | Partial |
| E6 | Stable/slight decline | -- | ✗ |

E4 is an outlier among E facets. E5 also declines but with different timing and
curvature. E3 moves in the opposite direction. E4 does NOT resemble its E domain-mates.

### Comparison to BT zone prediction (C facets)
| C facet | Direction | Shape | Match to E4? |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 Competence | Peak-and-decline | Inverted-U | Partial (both decline late life) |
| C2 Order | Flat | -- | ✗ |
| C3 Dutifulness | Increase | -- | ✗ Opposite |
| C4 Ach. Striving | Flat | -- | ✗ |
| C5 Self-Discipline | Peak-and-decline | Inverted-U, late decline | Partial |
| C6 Deliberation | Steepest increase | Monotonic | ✗✗ Opposite |

E4's steep late-life decline doesn't match C facets either. C facets are mostly flat
or increasing. C1 and C5 show late-life decline (inverted-U), but their peak is in
midlife, while E4's decline may begin earlier and accelerate continuously.

E4's trajectory appears to reflect **physical vitality decline** rather than
personality change in either the E or C domain. Multiple authors flag the measurement
confound: E4 items ("bursting with energy," "fast pace") may increasingly measure
physical capacity in older adults.

### Verdict: AMBIGUOUS
E4 is clearly NOT E-like developmentally (it's an outlier among E facets). But it's
also NOT C-like (no C facet shows its steep monotonic decline pattern). Its trajectory
may reflect a physical vitality dimension that doesn't map onto either domain's
personality-change patterns.

The test is ambiguous because E4 violates the E-domain prediction (it doesn't develop
like E facets) but doesn't clearly fulfill the C-zone prediction (it doesn't develop
like C facets either).

## Test Case 3: N2 Angry Hostility

Big Five domain: N → Bright Triad zone: ES x E (A zone)
Prediction: N2 trajectory should resemble A facets more than N facets.

### N2's trajectory
Robust decline across adulthood. Replicated in four studies: Terracciano (L), Schwaba (M),
Brandt (L), Ringwald (L). Curvilinear — late-life upturn in some studies.
Direction: decreasing anger/hostility across adulthood.

### Comparison to N domain-mates
| N facet | Direction | Shape | Match to N2? |
|---|---|---|---|
| N1 | Flat/increase | Non-declining | ✗ Opposite |
| N3 | Decline (unclear) | Mixed | Partial |
| N4 | Insufficient data | -- | Cannot assess |
| N5 | Monotonic decline | No upturn | Partial direction, different shape |
| N6 | Late-life increase | -- | ✗ Opposite late-life |

N2 shares the declining direction with N3 and N5 but is distinguished by its late-life
upturn and robust replication. N1 and N6 move in opposite directions.

### Comparison to BT zone-mates (ES x E = A facets)
| A facet | Direction | Shape | Match to N2? |
|---|---|---|---|
| A2 Straightfwd | Linear increase | -- | ✓ **CONVERGENT when reversed** |
| A4 Compliance | Linear increase | Steepest A increase | ✓ **CONVERGENT when reversed** |

N2 content is anger/hostility = reversed agreeableness. When reversed:
- N2 declining = increasing agreeableness
- A2, A4 increasing = increasing agreeableness
- **All three move in the same direction: toward greater interpersonal cooperativeness**

The cross-domain synthesis notes: "N2 robustly declines across adulthood. A-domain facets
broadly increase. This is directionally consistent with their genetic antagonism — they
move in opposite directions across the lifespan."

### Verdict: ✓ ZONE-MATES (with reversal)
When N2 is treated as reversed-A content (antagonism declining = agreeableness increasing),
its trajectory tracks A2 and A4's trajectory. This directional convergence is consistent
with the BT model's placement of N2 in the A zone.

N2 does NOT share trajectory shape with most N domain-mates: N1 is flat, N6 increases
late-life. N2's decline is the opposite of what N1 and N6 do.

Note: the convergence is directional (same direction when reversed), not shape-matched
(N2 is curvilinear while A2/A4 are linear). Shape-matching is a weaker test here because
declining hostility and increasing compliance may follow different nonlinear dynamics.

## Additional Test Case 4: A1 Trust

Big Five domain: A → Bright Triad zone: ES core
Prediction: A1 trajectory should resemble N facets more than A facets.

### A1's trajectory
Inverted-U with late-life decline. Unique among A facets — the only A facet that
decreases in old age. Terracciano (2005, L) provides the primary evidence.

### Comparison to A domain-mates
| A facet | Direction | Shape | Match to A1? |
|---|---|---|---|
| A2 | Linear increase | -- | ✗ |
| A3 | Flat | -- | ✗ |
| A4 | Linear increase | -- | ✗ |
| A5 | Flat | -- | ✗ |
| A6 | Increase (convex) | -- | ✗ |

A1 is a clear outlier among A facets. No other A facet shows late-life decline.

### Comparison to BT zone-mates (ES core = N facets)
| N facet | Direction | Shape | Match to A1? |
|---|---|---|---|
| N1 | Flat/increase | -- | ✗ |
| N3 | Decline (unclear) | -- | ✗ |
| N6 | Late-life increase | -- | ✗✗ Opposite late-life |
| N4 | Insufficient data | -- | -- |

A1's inverted-U trajectory doesn't match any N facet either. N6's late-life increase
is the OPPOSITE of A1's late-life decline.

If A1 is reversed (low Trust = high anxious distrust ≈ N-like content), then A1's
late-life decline (decreasing trust) could parallel N6's late-life increase (increasing
vulnerability). Both reflect increasing emotional insecurity in old age. This is a
conceptual convergence but not a trajectory-shape match.

### Verdict: AMBIGUOUS → WEAKLY SUPPORTED with reversal
A1 is clearly NOT A-like developmentally (unique late-life decline in a domain of
increases). It's also not straightforwardly N-like in trajectory shape. But when
reversed, its late-life decline (increasing distrust/insecurity) is conceptually
convergent with N6 Vulnerability's late-life increase (increasing fragility). This
is suggestive but not a clean match.

## Overall Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED (2 of 3 core test cases)

- **Core test cases (N5, E4, N2):** 2 of 3 favor zone-mates → Partially supported
- **Including A1:** 2 clear + 1 weak + 1 ambiguous out of 4 → Partially supported

The strongest result is N5 (mirror-image of C6 with three replications). The N2 result

rather than any personality domain's developmental dynamics.

## Key Pattern

The model's predictions work best where:
- The reassigned facet's content is clearly reversed content from a neighboring domain
  (N5 reversed = C content; N2 reversed = A content)
- The developmental comparison allows for reversal matching

The predictions work less well where:
- The reassigned facet may reflect a non-personality construct (E4 → physical vitality)
- The reassigned facet is idiosyncratic (A1 Trust — unique trajectory in both
  candidate zones)

## Caveats

1. **Qualitative comparison.** Trajectory similarity is judged by shape and direction,
   not by formal statistical comparison (e.g., correlated change correlations). Where
   specific values exist (e.g., C6 at +1.70 T/decade), they strengthen the comparison,
   but most facets are described only by direction and shape.

2. **Reversal asymmetry.** N2 and N5 are "reversed" content (N5 reversed = C6-like;
   N2 reversed = A-like). Allowing reversal makes the comparison work but adds a degree
   of freedom. The test is still valid because the DIRECTION of reversal is predicted
   by the model, not chosen post-hoc.

3. **E4's trajectory may not test the personality structure hypothesis.** If E4 items
   increasingly measure physical vitality rather than personality, then its trajectory
   reflects health aging, not personality structure. The developmental test is
   uninformative for E4's structural placement.

4. **A1 is weakly tested.** Only one study (Terracciano L) clearly shows the inverted-U
   trajectory. More developmental data would strengthen or weaken this test case.

5. **Missing data.** Developmental trajectories for N4 are insufficient. Cross-domain
   change correlations (e.g., r between N5 change and C6 change) are available only in
   limited form (Schwaba 2022 reports within-domain r_change but not cross-domain pairs
   for most facets).
