# Task H6: Developmental Rank-Order Stability by Bright Triad Zone

## Data Availability Assessment

Facet-level rank-order stability data in this corpus is LIMITED. The studies report:

| Study | Quality | What's reported | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Brandt et al. (2023) | L | Qualitative patterns from Figure 2 (4-year latent retest). Relative comparisons only (e.g., "N6 higher than N domain"). | No numerical per-facet stability values — reported via figures only |
| Terracciano et al. (2005) | L | Stable variance 69%-83% across 30 facets (M=76%) | Only the range, not individual facet values |
| Brandes et al. (2020) | L | Domain: A (.63) to E (.72). Facet: .57-.72 range | Childhood (ages 9-13), non-NEO instrument (ICID-S) |
| Schwaba et al. (2022) | M | Within-domain co-development correlations | Co-development, not rank-order stability |
| Bleidorn et al. (2009) | S | Level component heritability | Stability of genetic vs. environmental variance, not test-retest |

**No study in the corpus reports numerical rank-order stability estimates for all 30
individual NEO-PI-R facets.** Brandt (2023, L) is the closest but reports via figures
without tabulated per-facet values. Zone means CANNOT be computed.

- N1 Anxiety: declining stability in old age
- N4 Self-Consciousness: declining stability in old age
- N2, N5: cumulative continuity pattern (peak ~50, decline after)

**C facets:**
- C4 Achievement Striving: higher stability than C domain (consistently)
- C5 Self-Discipline: declining stability in old age (peak 45-49)
- C6 Deliberation, C1 Competence: increasing stability with age
- C2 Order: U-shaped (lowest 50-60, then rising)

**E, O, A facets:**
- No individual facet stability patterns reported in the extraction notes
- A facets described as having "most variable rank-order stability patterns" (text p.11)
- "Rank-order stability patterns are more diverse at the facet level than at the trait

## Domain-Level Analysis (fallback)

Since facet-level zone means are untestable, I check whether domain-level stability
is at least directionally consistent with the prediction.

Prediction: Primary domains (N, E) > Intersection domains (C, O, A)

### Brandes 2020 (L) — childhood domain stability:
| Domain | Stability (r) | BT type |
|---|---|---|
| E | .72 | Primary ✓ (highest) |
| C | .68 | Intersection |
| N | .66 | Primary |
| O | .65 | Intersection |

Intersection mean: (.68 + .65 + .63) / 3 = .65
Difference: .04 in predicted direction, but small and N is below C.

This does NOT support the prediction: E (primary) has the lowest co-development.

- A domain: "most variable rank-order stability patterns" → consistent with A being the

- N domain: "all facets r > .75" → consistent with N being a coherent primary ✓

## Facet-Level Observations (qualitative, not zone-testable)

From the available qualitative data, some individual patterns are suggestive:

**Consistent with prediction:**
- N6 Vulnerability (ES core, primary): "consistently higher stability than N domain"
- C4 Achievement Striving (I x ES, intersection): "higher stability than C domain" — but
  this facet is an intersection member, so high stability would VIOLATE the prediction
- All N facets > .75: the primary ES domain is uniformly stable ✓

**Inconsistent with prediction:**
- C4's high stability (intersection facet being most stable in its domain) ✗
- C6 and C1 show increasing stability with age — intersection facets gaining stability ✗
- E facets show within-domain differences (Brandes): Sociable (.72) vs. Positive Emotions
  (.62) — a .10 range within a primary domain

**Ambiguous:**
- Late-life stability declines (N1, N4, C5) affect facets in both primary and intersection
  zones, suggesting aging effects rather than structural differences

## Verdict: LARGELY UNTESTABLE

The corpus does not contain numerical rank-order stability estimates for individual
NEO-PI-R facets. Zone means cannot be computed. The hypothesis cannot be directly tested.

**Domain-level patterns are WEAKLY CONSISTENT** with the prediction:
- E (primary) has the highest domain stability in childhood (.72)
- A (intersection) has the lowest (.63)
- N (primary) is intermediate (.66), below C (intersection, .68) — a partial violation

**Qualitative facet patterns are MIXED:**
- N facets are uniformly stable (✓ for primary status)
- A facets have the most variable stability patterns (✓ for intersection instability)
- But some intersection facets (C4, C6) are highly stable, violating the prediction

## What Would Be Needed

To properly test H6, the corpus would need:
1. Numerical 4-year (or comparable) test-retest correlations for all 30 NEO-PI-R facets
   from a single large longitudinal study
2. Ideally, latent stability coefficients (correcting for measurement error)
3. Multiple studies for weighted averaging

Brandt et al. (2023, L) has this data in Figure 2 but it was extracted as qualitative
descriptions, not numerical values. Extracting the specific per-facet stability values
from that figure would make H6 testable.

## Caveats

1. **Co-development ≠ rank-order stability.** Schwaba's r_change measures whether facets
   within a domain change together, not whether individuals maintain their rank. The two
   can diverge: facets can show high rank-order stability (everyone maintains rank) but
   low co-development (they change in different directions).

2. **Childhood vs. adulthood stability.** The Brandes data (ages 9-13) may not generalize
   to adult stability. Personality stability increases dramatically from childhood to
   adulthood (Briley & Tucker-Drob 2014, XL).

3. **The prediction may be wrong at the facet level.** Rank-order stability reflects both
   genetic and environmental factors. Environmental stability increases with age (Briley
   & Tucker-Drob 2014). If intersection domains are more environmentally shaped, they
   might actually be MORE stable (because environmental commitments — jobs, relationships —
   lock in). This would reverse the prediction.

4. **Instrument-specific effects.** Non-NEO instruments (BFI-2, ICID-S, BFAS) have
   different facet compositions and may show different stability patterns.
