# Bright Triad Hypothesis Tests: Complete Report (H1–H10)

## Background: The Big Five and Why This Project Exists

The Big Five model of personality (also called the Five-Factor Model) is the dominant framework in personality psychology. It organizes individual differences into five broad domains, each measured by six facets in the NEO-PI-R (the standard 240-item personality inventory):

- **Neuroticism (N):** Anxiety, Angry Hostility, Depression, Self-Consciousness, Impulsiveness, Vulnerability
- **Extraversion (E):** Warmth, Gregariousness, Assertiveness, Activity, Excitement-Seeking, Positive Emotions
- **Openness to Experience (O):** Fantasy, Aesthetics, Feelings, Actions, Ideas, Values
- **Agreeableness (A):** Trust, Straightforwardness, Altruism, Compliance, Modesty, Tender-Mindedness
- **Conscientiousness (C):** Competence, Order, Dutifulness, Achievement Striving, Self-Discipline, Deliberation

An intermediate level — the **Big Five Aspect Scales (BFAS)** — subdivides each domain into two aspects (10 total), based on work by DeYoung, Quilty & Peterson (2007). This gives a three-tier hierarchy: 5 domains → 10 aspects → 30 facets.

The Big Five treats all five domains as structurally equivalent — five co-equal dimensions recovered from factor analysis. But behavioral genetics research over the past two decades has repeatedly found that the facets within each domain do not behave as a coherent unit. Some facets share more genetic variance with facets in OTHER domains than with their own domain-mates. The published two-aspect structure does not replicate at the genetic level in any domain. Several facets appear structurally misplaced.

This project began as a systematic paper-by-paper extraction of 36 studies to determine whether the published mappings of personality facets to aspects and domains hold up under scrutiny. Three evidence streams were used: (1) heritability and genetic architecture, (2) developmental trajectories across the lifespan, and (3) factor loadings and structural mappings. The resulting empirical base — the "conversation 7 verdict" — identified specific facets that are misplaced, domains that are genetically incoherent, and boundary regions where the Big Five rotation draws lines through the middle of what the genetic data says is a continuous space.

## Background: The Bright Triad Model

The Bright Triad is a theoretical model proposed by the author of this project. It claims the Big Five domains are not five equal primaries. The real primary structure of the mental phenotype is **three** factors, visualized as three overlapping circles in a Venn diagram:

1. **Intelligence** — the full cognitive stack (reinforcement learning, simulation/model-building, mentalizing, symbolic language, rationality). This is NOT the same as IQ; it is a broader construct encompassing all cognitive capacities.
2. **Emotional Stability** — reversed Neuroticism. The capacity for affective regulation and resilience.
3. **Extraversion** — the tendency toward social engagement, positive affect, and behavioral activation.

The other three Big Five domains are **pairwise intersections** of these primaries:

- **Conscientiousness** = Intelligence ∩ Emotional Stability — what you get when cognitive capacity meets emotional regulation (planfulness, self-discipline, goal pursuit)
- **Openness to Experience** = Intelligence ∩ Extraversion — what you get when cognitive capacity meets social engagement (curiosity, aesthetic sensitivity, imaginative exploration)
- **Agreeableness** = Emotional Stability ∩ Extraversion — what you get when emotional regulation meets social engagement (warmth, cooperation, trust)

The **triple intersection** — where all three primaries converge — is called **Brilliance**. This is proposed as a directly measurable construct, anchored by facets that load on multiple domains simultaneously (E3 Assertiveness, E4 Activity, E6 Positive Emotions, C3 Dutifulness, A3 Altruism, A6 Tender-Mindedness).

Four facets are placed **outside all circles** — they don't belong to any part of the core mental phenotype: C5 Self-Discipline, O6 Values, A5 Modesty, E5 Excitement-Seeking.

## What This Document Contains

This document presents ten pre-registered hypotheses (H1–H10) that test specific, falsifiable predictions of the Bright Triad model against the empirical base assembled from 36 studies. Each hypothesis targets a different aspect of the model:

- **H1–H2a:** Do zones differ in heritability and facet-specific genetic variance?
- **H3–H4:** Do genetic factor loadings show the predicted cross-domain patterns?
- **H5–H6:** Do developmental trajectories and rank-order stability follow zone boundaries?
- **H7–H8:** Does the Brilliance composite predict intelligence and life satisfaction better than individual domains?
- **H9:** Do "outside" facets show the weakest biological signatures?
- **H10:** Are primary-core facets the most cross-culturally stable genetically?

The hypotheses were written before the analyses were conducted. The evidence base was assembled independently in a prior phase of the project. The author's theoretical commitments are toward the Bright Triad model, but the extraction protocol explicitly guarded against confirmation bias (see the "avoiding over-compliance" rules in the extraction methodology).

## Evidence Base

The analyses draw on 36 studies extracted in a systematic paper-by-paper process, plus 3 additional studies used directly from their PDFs. Key study types:

- **Twin studies** (Jang 1996, 1998, 2001, 2002; Røysamb 2018; Yamagata 2006; Bleidorn 2009; and others): Provide heritability estimates, genetic correlations, and within-domain genetic factor structures.
- **Extended pedigree study** (Pilia 2006, N = 6,148): Provides the complete 30-facet × 5-factor genetic loading matrix and genetic cluster analysis. The single most important data source for H3, H4, and H9.
- **Longitudinal studies** (Terracciano 2005; Brandt 2023; Schwaba 2022; Ringwald 2024): Provide developmental trajectory data for facet-level mean change across the lifespan.
- **Meta-analyses** (Roberts 2006; Briley & Tucker-Drob 2014; Stanek & Ones 2023): Provide domain-level developmental and intelligence-correlation benchmarks.
- **Structural validation** (Soto & John 2017; DeYoung 2007; Zakrisson 2025; Haehner 2025): Provide facet-to-aspect mappings, BFI-2 inclusion/exclusion decisions, and aspect-level external validity data.
- **GWAS** (Schwaba 2025, N = 611K–1.14M): Provides domain-level molecular genetic architecture (SNP heritability, cross-geography genetic correlations, gene expression enrichment).

Study quality is rated using a T-shirt sizing system (XL = gold standard, L = strong, M = adequate, S = use with caution). The two highest-quality genetic studies — Pilia (2006, XL) and Yamagata (2006, XL) — are cited in 6 and 4 of the 10 hypothesis tests, respectively.

The complete study inventory with quality ratings and hypothesis usage counts appears in the Appendix.

## What the Bright Triad Gets Right

1. **The primary-core distinction is real.** Across H3, H9, and H10, facets in the ES and E cores consistently show the strongest, purest, most cross-culturally stable genetic signatures. N1 Anxiety is the most genetically pure facet in the NEO-PI-R (H3). Primary-core facets have the highest VCC (H10) and the fewest biological anomalies (H9).

2. **Center-triangle facets ARE more genetically diffuse.** H3 is the model's strongest result: center facets load on more genetic domains than any other zone (mean ratio 1.70 vs. 1.16 for primaries). E3 Assertiveness loads on four domains. This is exactly what a "triple intersection" should look like.

3. **ES and E function as genetic parents of intersection domains.** H4 shows that C facets cross-load toward low-N (ES parent, 75% hit rate) and O facets cross-load toward E (E parent, 100% hit rate). The direct parent predictions work.

4. **Outside-circle facets are biologically anomalous.** H9 confirms that O6 Values and A5 Modesty show the weakest biological signatures across multiple indicators. The "negative space" prediction holds.

## What the Bright Triad Gets Wrong

1. **Intelligence is not a detectable genetic parent.** H4's most informative result: C and O facets do NOT cross-load toward each other. C2 Order and C6 Deliberation load NEGATIVELY on O. The C↔O genetic relationship is antagonistic, not allied. The Intelligence factor, if it exists, leaves no trace in the genetic factor structure.

2. **The center triangle does not concentrate criterion variance.** Both H7 (intelligence) and H8 (life satisfaction) show that the Brilliance composite performs poorly as a predictor. The criterion-relevant variance sits in the intersection zones and primary cores, not in the triple intersection.

3. **Zone membership does not predict heritability.** H1 shows a negligible .02 spread across zones. Within-zone variability is 3–4× larger than between-zone differences.

4. **C5 Self-Discipline does not belong outside all circles.** H9 shows C5 has the highest genetic loading in its domain (.86), is well-integrated in its genetic cluster, and shows none of the predicted anomalies. This is the model's weakest zone assignment.

## Verdict: NOT SUPPORTED

The predicted ordering (Primary > Intersection > Center) is technically present but the effect size is negligible. A .02 spread across the three predicted zones, against within-zone SDs of .06-.08, means the zone assignment has no meaningful predictive power for heritability.

## Key Findings

1. **Heritability does not vary meaningfully by Bright Triad zone.** All zones cluster around h2 = .39-.41. Zone membership explains essentially no variance in heritability.

2. **Within-zone variability dominates.** The SD within each zone (.04-.15) is much larger than differences between zones (.01-.02). N4 Self-Consciousness (.27) and O2 Aesthetics (.57) are both in adjacent zones but differ by .30.

3. **The "Outside" facets are notable for instability, not for lower heritability.** A5 ranges .00-.46 across cultures; O6 ranges near-zero to .61; C5 ranges 0-.49 across studies. This instability is itself consistent with these facets being poorly captured by any structural model — but the mean h2 (.41) is no different from other zones.

4. **Sub-zone differences are more interesting than the main comparison.** The I×E zone (.47) is notably higher than ES×E (.35). If this replicates, it could mean the "Intelligence" parent circle is associated with somewhat higher heritability — but this is post-hoc and driven by small N.

5. **The hypothesis may target the wrong metric.** Total h2 is domain-general. The Bright Triad model makes predictions about *structural relationships* (which factors are primary, which are derived). Metrics that capture structure — genetic factor loadings, cross-domain genetic correlations, or facet-specific vs. common-factor heritability — might differentiate zones where total h2 does not.

## Caveats

1. **Uneven data quality.** Many facets rely on a single M-quality study. Pilia (XL) values are available for only a subset.

2. **Mixed h2 types.** Broad-sense (twin) and narrow-sense (pedigree) estimates are combined where necessary.

3. **Jang 1996 CE facets (h2=0) were excluded and replaced** with later estimates, introducing estimation uncertainty for C2, C5, C6, O3, A5.

4. **Small N per zone.** With 3-12 facets per zone, power to detect moderate effects is limited. The null result means "no evidence for" rather than "evidence against."

5. **Approximate values.** O3 (~.45), O4 (~.37) are estimated from domain averages, not directly reported.

## Interpretation

### Why intersection zones have the LOWEST specific genetic variance

The Bright Triad model posits intersection zones as pairwise overlaps of two primary factors.
The finding that intersection facets have the least facet-specific genetic variance is
actually consistent with an alternative reading of the model:

Intersection facets should be EXPLAINED by two parent factors, leaving little that's unique.
Their genetic variance should be captured by those parent factors' genetic architecture.
The data support this: after removing the five domain genetic factors, intersection facets
have the least remaining genetic variance. They ARE the domain factors, genetically.

This is most extreme for C4 and C6 (both zero), and C1 (.11 — barely above zero).
Three of five I x ES facets have negligible facet-specific genetics.

### Why center-triangle facets have MODERATE specific genetic variance

If center-triangle facets sit at the triple intersection (Brilliance), they should load on
three domain factors. The five-factor model should capture most of their variance through
those three loadings. But they might also carry unique "Brilliance" variance — a component
not captured by any single domain factor or pair. This would show up as residual genetic
variance above zero.

The data are consistent: center-triangle facets average .24 (full) or .28 (excluding A3),
suggesting they have moderate specific variance — more than intersection facets, less than
or equal to primary-core facets.

### The notable exception: A3 Altruism (CE in Center)

A3 is the one center-triangle facet with zero specific genetic variance. This means A3's
genetics are entirely captured by the five domain factors. Given that A3 is described in the
synthesis as "genetically diffuse across A, C, and E" with "no heritable specific variance,"
it is a facet whose multi-domain genetic loading accounts for ALL of its heritability — there
is nothing left over that would constitute a unique "Brilliance" component. A3 may be the
purest "triple-overlap" facet in the Bright Triad sense: it is nothing more than the
intersection of its parent factors.

### The I x E sub-zone is an outlier

The I x E (Openness) sub-zone has the highest mean specific genetic variance (.31) of any
sub-zone, driven by O2 Aesthetics (.37) and O5 Ideas (.33). These facets have substantial
genetic variance that is NOT captured by the five domain factors. This suggests that
"Openness" carries genuinely unique genetic content — perhaps related to the "Intelligence"
parent factor in the Bright Triad model — that the Big Five factor structure doesn't
fully capture.

### Key structural observation

N and E domains have heritable specificity for ALL facets (no CE residuals).
C and A domains have 2-3 CE facets each.
This N/E vs C/A split maps loosely onto the Bright Triad primary vs. intersection
distinction: ES and E are primaries; C and A are intersections. The primaries' facets
retain their genetic uniqueness; the intersections' facets are more likely to be
genetically captured by the domain structure.

## Caveats

1. **Single study.** All residual h² values come from Jang 1998 (L). No replication exists.

2. **CE vs. AE model selection.** CE models for C4, C6, A3, A5 rest on AIC model
   comparison. In a larger sample, small genetic effects might reach significance. The
   CE findings should be treated as "negligible genetic specificity" rather than
   "proven zero."

3. **C4 measurement discrepancy.** Jang 2002 (L) reports 41-43% within-C facet-specific
   genetic variance for C4. Jang 1998 (L) reports zero five-factor residual for C4.
   These are not contradictory: C4's within-C specific variance is shared with other
   domains (especially E4, rG=.775). When ALL five factors are removed, nothing remains.
   This is structurally informative, not a data conflict.

4. **Estimated values.** O3 (~.27) and O4 (~.25) residual h² are estimated from domain
   context, not directly reported. These affect the Center and I x E zone means.

5. **Zone sizes are unequal.** Primary cores (8), Intersection (12), Center (7),
   Outside (3). The Center and Outside means are less stable.

6. **The five-factor residual may not capture Brilliance variance.** If a "Brilliance"
   factor exists, it would load across multiple domains and might be partially captured
   by the five domain factors through Procrustes rotation. The residual h² would then
   underestimate the true Brilliance-specific component.

## Note on Jang 1998 vs Jang 2002 facet-specific measures

These studies measure different things:
- Jang 1998 residual h²: what's left after removing ALL FIVE Big Five domain factors
- Jang 2002 within-domain %: what's left after removing genetic factors WITHIN ONE domain

Example: C4 has 41-43% within-C specific variance (Jang 2002) but zero five-factor
residual (Jang 1998). This means C4's within-C unique variance is actually shared with
OTHER domains (specifically E4 at rG=.775). When all five factors are removed, nothing
is left.

For the Bright Triad hypothesis, the five-factor residual (Jang 1998) is the more
appropriate measure because it captures what's truly unique after all structural factors
are accounted for.

The results suggest that ES (Emotional Stability) and E (Extraversion) function as

- C's cross-domain loadings point toward low-N (ES parent ✓) and scattered E/A
- O's cross-domain loadings point toward E (E parent ✓) and NOT toward C
- C and O are genetically independent or mildly antagonistic

This is consistent with the Pilia S2 genetic cluster data: C facets cluster together
(Cluster 17), O facets cluster together (Cluster 15), and these two clusters do NOT
overlap. The only genetic bridge between C and O is through the E-N mega-cluster
(Cluster 18), not through any direct C↔O link.

## What Would Be Needed

To properly test H6, the corpus would need:
1. Numerical 4-year (or comparable) test-retest correlations for all 30 NEO-PI-R facets
   from a single large longitudinal study
2. Ideally, latent stability coefficients (correcting for measurement error)
3. Multiple studies for weighted averaging

Brandt et al. (2023, L) has this data in Figure 2 but it was extracted as qualitative

from that figure would make H6 testable.

## Caveats

1. **Co-development ≠ rank-order stability.** Schwaba's r_change measures whether facets
   within a domain change together, not whether individuals maintain their rank. The two
   can diverge: facets can show high rank-order stability (everyone maintains rank) but
   low co-development (they change in different directions).

2. **Childhood vs. adulthood stability.** The Brandes data (ages 9-13) may not generalize
   to adult stability. Personality stability increases dramatically from childhood to
   adulthood (Briley & Tucker-Drob 2014, XL).

3. **The prediction may be wrong at the facet level.** Rank-order stability reflects both
   genetic and environmental factors. Environmental stability increases with age (Briley
   & Tucker-Drob 2014). If intersection domains are more environmentally shaped, they
   might actually be MORE stable (because environmental commitments — jobs, relationships —
   lock in). This would reverse the prediction.

4. **Instrument-specific effects.** Non-NEO instruments (BFI-2, ICID-S, BFAS) have
   different facet compositions and may show different stability patterns.

## What the Data Reveal for the Bright Triad Model

### 1. The LS signal is concentrated in the ES core and I×ES zone

Withdrawal (-.51), Depression (-.52), and Industriousness (.44) dominate LS prediction. In the Bright Triad model, these map to the Emotional Stability primary and the Intelligence × Emotional Stability intersection — not to the center triangle.

### 2. The center triangle has ONE strong member (E6) and many weak ones

E6 Positive Emotions (.30-.40) is genuinely strong. E3 (.21) and E4 (.23) are moderate. The remaining center facets (A3, C3, A6, O4) have no individual LS data but their aspects (Compassion ~.30, Orderliness .14) suggest they are mixed.

### 3. The prediction fails because "Brilliance" doesn't integrate into a LS-predictive composite

The Bright Triad model predicts that the center triangle represents integrated mental phenotype functioning. If true, these facets should collectively predict LS better than any single zone. They don't — because the center-triangle facets are psychologically heterogeneous. E6 (cheerfulness) predicts LS for affect-related reasons. E3 (assertiveness) and E4 (activity) predict weakly. A3, A6, O4 likely predict weakly or not at all. There is no emergent "integration" signal.

### 4. The ES core dominance is partly artifactual

Withdrawal/Depression's strong LS correlation reflects partial content overlap: depressed people evaluate their lives negatively. This is almost definitional rather than structurally informative. The Industriousness signal (.44) is more structurally interesting — it suggests that goal-directed engagement (I×ES content) predicts satisfaction through achievement and mastery, not through affect.

### 5. An alternative reading that partially supports the model

If we exclude the ES core (on grounds of content-criterion overlap) and focus on the POSITIVE predictors:
- Center triangle: E6 (.40), E3 (.21), E4 (.23) → mean .28
- I×ES zone: C1 (.30), Industriousness (.44) → strong
- E core: E1 (.27), E2 (.26) → mean .27
- Edge/outside: E5 (.05) → weak

In this framing, the center and intersection zones are comparable, and both exceed the E core and Edge/outside. This is a weaker version of the prediction: center ≈ intersection > primary cores (E only) > outside. But it requires dropping the ES core entirely, which is a post-hoc move.

## Comparison with H7 (Brilliance × Intelligence)

H7 found that the intelligence signal is concentrated in the intersection zones (Intellect .40, Industriousness .32), not the center triangle (.08-.12 composite). H8 finds the same pattern for life satisfaction: the signal is concentrated in the ES core and I×ES zone, not the center triangle.

Both H7 and H8 converge on the same structural conclusion: **the "Brilliance" triple intersection does not concentrate criterion-relevant variance. The criterion variance sits in the intersection zones and primary cores.**

## Caveats

1. **Incomplete facet-level data.** Only 15 of 30 facets have specific LS values. The remaining 15 (all O facets, most A facets, most C facets) are estimated from domain/aspect data. The center triangle is particularly undersampled — only 3 of 7 facets have direct LS data.

2. **Aspect-to-zone mapping is approximate.** Each aspect contains facets from multiple BT zones (e.g., Assertiveness includes E3 center + E4 center + E5 outside). Aspect-level data cannot cleanly distinguish zone effects.

3. **Schimmack is small and unrated.** N = 136 students. Used as supplementary for facet-level coverage only.

4. **Haehner LS values are incomplete.** Only 4 of 10 aspects have specific LS correlations transcribed (Withdrawal, Volatility, Industriousness, Orderliness). The other 6 are described qualitatively. The full Haehner Table 9 would strengthen the analysis.

5. **Sun semipartials ≠ zero-order LS.** Sun's mean semipartial correlations (partialing complementary aspect, averaged across 13 WB dimensions) systematically differ from zero-order LS correlations. They were used only where Haehner values were unavailable.

6. **The ES core dominance may reflect measurement confounding.** Depression items and life satisfaction items share negative-evaluation content. The genetic evidence (Røysamb: personality-related genetics = 20% of LS variance) confirms there IS a genuine personality→LS pathway, but the phenotypic correlations likely overestimate it for Depression/Withdrawal specifically.

7. **N and E facets dominate the available data.** The facet-level analysis is heavily weighted toward N and E facets because these are the only domains with complete coverage. If O, A, and C facets showed different zone patterns, the conclusion could change.

## Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED

The prediction holds for **O6 Values** (strongest) and **A5 Modesty** (moderate-strong), partially holds for **E5 Excitement-Seeking** (moderate), and largely fails for **C5 Self-Discipline**.

### What the pattern reveals:

**1. O6 Values is the clearest "outside" facet in the NEO-PI-R.**
It is anomalous on 9 of 11 indicators. Its near-zero heritability, elevated DZ correlations, contradictory developmental trajectory, exclusion from its domain's genetic cluster despite adequate phenotypic loading, and social-attitudinal content collectively suggest O6 may not be a personality trait in the endogenous sense. It behaves more like a culturally transmitted attitude than a genetically grounded disposition. This converges with the conversation_7_verdict's characterization: "Social-attitudinal content qualitatively different from domain."

**2. A5 Modesty is anomalous but in a different way than predicted.**
The Bright Triad predicts high specific variance for outside facets (their genetics are "their own thing"). A5 shows the OPPOSITE — zero heritable specificity. A5's genetic variance comes entirely from the A domain factor; what makes A5 distinct from A is entirely environmental. Combined with reversed MZ/DZ correlations, cross-cultural h² instability (0–.46), and developmental flatness, A5's anomaly is not "doing its own genetic thing" but "barely having any genetic thing at all." This is a stronger anomaly than predicted — A5 may not be endogenously driven.

**3. C5 Self-Discipline is NOT an outside facet by biological criteria.**
C5 has the highest genetic loading on its domain (.86), is well-integrated in its domain's genetic cluster, has a clear developmental trajectory, is included in BFI-2, and predicts life satisfaction through its aspect. The only anomaly is h² instability and a phenotypic-genetic aspect mismatch (loads on Industriousness phenotypically but clusters with Orderliness genetically in Germany). The Bright Triad places C5 outside all circles, but by every biological indicator, C5 is a core C facet. Its placement outside all circles is the weakest zone assignment in the model.

**4. E5 Excitement-Seeking is genetically orphaned but phenotypically coherent.**
E5's 68–70% specific genetic variance and absence from any genetic cluster confirm it is doing its own thing genetically. But it has the highest VCC (.99) of any facet, meaning its phenotypic E placement is perfectly replicated at the genetic level. E5 is genetically within E but genetically alone within E — a peripheral but legitimate domain member.

**5. The comparison facets are NOT uniformly strong.**
E2 Gregariousness is also genetically orphaned (no cluster in Pilia S2). C1 Competence has a near-zero residual h² and boundary status. A2 lacks molecular associations. The difference between outside and comparison facets is not categorical — it's a matter of degree, concentrated on a few specific indicators (h² stability, BFI-2 exclusion, molecular associations).

## Caveats

1. **Indicator count is not a statistical test.** The 12 indicators are not independent — h² instability, cross-cultural instability, and anomalous MZ/DZ patterns are partially overlapping signals.

2. **Comparison set includes a boundary facet.** C1 Competence is itself anomalous (genetic N cross-loading, near-zero residual h²). A cleaner comparison facet (e.g., C4 Achievement Striving) would sharpen the contrast.

3. **Missing data.** E5 lacks cross-cultural genetic loading data. A5 and O6 lack LS correlation data at the facet level. Several molecular findings are S quality.

4. **The prediction is underspecified.** "Weakest biological signatures" conflates several distinct anomaly types: low heritability (A5), conflicting heritability (O6), high specificity (E5), and phenotypic-genetic mismatch (C5). These are different phenomena with different structural implications.

5. **N = 4 per group.** With only 4 outside facets and 4 comparison facets, any single exception (C5) has a large impact on the overall verdict. The test has low power for detecting a group-level effect.

## Verdict: PARTIALLY SUPPORTED

### What is supported:

**1. Primary-core facets ARE the most cross-culturally stable.**
By VCC (.958 mean), by count of stable facets (6 of 8 = 75%), and by count of unstable facets (1 of 8 = 13%), the ES core and E core facets consistently show the highest genetic stability across cultures. N1, N3, N4, E1, E2 are all among the most stable. This is the strongest signal in the analysis.

**2. Outside facets DO tend to be unstable.**
A5 Modesty is the single most unstable facet (loading range .37). O6 and C5 also show instability. By proportion, 2 of 3 outside facets are in the top-10 unstable list (67%). The "negative space" prediction works.

**3. Center-triangle facets are moderately unstable.**
3 of 7 center facets (A3, O4, A6) are among the most unstable. O4 Actions has the most unstable primary loading in the entire corpus. Center triangle has a higher instability rate (43%) than intersection zones (33%).

### What is NOT supported:

**1. Intersection zones are NOT intermediate — they are SPLIT.**
The I×ES sub-zone is highly unstable (C6 VCC .79, C2 h² range .08–.70, N5 VCC .86), while I×E (Openness facets) is quite stable, and ES×E has only one notably unstable facet (N2's cross-loading). The intersection zones don't form a coherent middle group.

**2. C (intersection) is at least as stable as E (primary) at the domain level.**
The domain-level congruence data from Yamagata shows C (.93–.97) ≥ E (.92–.96). This directly contradicts the prediction that primary domains should be more stable than intersection domains.

**3. The Outside zone mean VCC (.943) is higher than Center (.933) and Intersection (.905).**
This inverts the predicted ordering for outside facets. The paradox: outside facets are individually anomalous (A5 is the most unstable) but their VCC values are not uniformly low. C5 has VCC .95 and A5 has VCC .95 — both high. The instability of A5 and C5 shows up in h² variability and factor membership shifts, not in VCC.

### The structural picture:

The data suggest a revised ordering:

**Most stable:** N1, N3, N4 (ES core) + E1, E2 (E core) — the "distress" and "sociability" cores
**Moderately stable:** Most intersection facets + C3, E3, E4 (center with stable cross-loadings) + O facets
**Most unstable:** A5 (outside), O4 (center), A3 (center), C6 (intersection), C2 (intersection), N2 cross-loadings (intersection)

The instability concentrates in:
1. **A-domain facets** (A5, A3, A1 cross-loading, A6) — consistent with A being the least genetically coherent domain
2. **C-domain internal structure** (C6, C2, C5 factor membership) — consistent with C having no resolvable internal partition
3. **Cross-domain boundary facets** (N2's A loading, N5's C loading) — these are the facets the Big Five rotation handles worst

The Bright Triad's primary/intersection distinction partially captures this, but the center-triangle category does not predict a coherent instability pattern — some center facets are very stable (E3 primary loading range .10, C3 "clean"), while others are among the most unstable (O4 range .28, A3 drops to .32).

## Caveats

1. **Incomplete Yamagata data.** Only ~10 facets have explicit three-country primary loading values. The remaining ~20 are described qualitatively. A complete per-facet × per-country table would transform this analysis.

2. **VCC is not the same as cross-cultural stability.** VCC measures Sardinia-vs-USA genetic-phenotypic match, not three-way cross-cultural genetic consistency. A facet could have high VCC in Sardinia but variable loadings across Canada, Germany, and Japan.

3. **Different cross-cultural comparisons yield different orderings.** Pilia (Sardinia vs USA) shows C (.97) > A (.96) > N (.94) > E (.89) > O (.87). Yamagata (Canada/Germany/Japan) shows A (.86) as the lowest. The two studies disagree about the relative stability of A and O. This may reflect the Sardinian founder population's genetic homogeneity.

4. **Range is a crude measure.** The range (max − min) across three countries is heavily influenced by a single outlier. Japan consistently drives the largest deviations (A5 drops to .23, A3 drops to .32, N2's A loading reaches −.60). This may reflect a Japan-specific cultural effect rather than general cross-cultural instability.

5. **The Japanese sample differs from the Canadian and German samples.** Yamagata's Japanese sample is much younger and more restricted in age range. Some cross-cultural instability may reflect age differences rather than cultural differences.

6. **N = 3 countries.** Three-way comparisons have limited power to distinguish stable from unstable facets. A facet could appear stable across Canada, Germany, and Japan but be unstable in non-Western, non-industrialized populations.
