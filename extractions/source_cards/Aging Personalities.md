**BIO-VAR-AGP-02 `[MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** The post-50 decline in rank-order stability of personality traits is mediated by health-related changes (chronic conditions, physiological dysregulation, neuronal degeneration), not by age per se. When researchers condition on health — studying only healthy survivors — stability appears to persist. The instability becomes visible only when the full population, including those experiencing typical age-related health decline, is included. This means personality "change" in late life is largely the Go Stats giving out at different rates across individuals, which reshuffles rank ordering.

**Relevant to:** Go Stat erosion as the driver of late-life personality change (not Turn rerouting), distinction between mean-level change and rank-order stability, methodological caveat for any age-personality claim

**COMP-VAR-AGP-01 `[MECHANISM] [EMPIRICAL]`**

**Claim:** The positivity effect in aging is an active, resource-demanding emotion regulation strategy, not a passive byproduct of cognitive decline. It is most pronounced in older adults with the highest executive functioning, disappears under cognitive load, and is eliminated when experimental goals override free attention. fMRI data show older adults successfully recruiting lateral prefrontal regions (middle and inferior frontal gyri) for emotional interference suppression even as they show impaired performance on nonemotional control tasks. This is the same prefrontal network younger adults use — differentially deployed via motivational bias, not a separate system.

**Relevant to:** Go-Turn interaction (shrinking Go capacity routed by Turn priorities), computational resource allocation under constraint, predictive processing reweighting (Barrett), why emotional regulation persists while abstract reasoning declines

**Retrieval prompt:** "What do the fMRI and behavioral studies say about the specific prefrontal regions recruited during emotional vs. nonemotional interference tasks in older adults? Include the executive function requirement, the cognitive load elimination finding, and the goal-dependent activation pattern. Name the specific brain regions and task conditions."

**COMP-VAR-AGP-02 `[EMPIRICAL]`**

**Claim:** Diffusion model analyses reveal that the positivity effect operates at the level of memory structure, not just attentional preference. Older adults experience greater accessibility of positive information in long-term memory, producing increased familiarity for positive items in recognition tasks. This is a shift in what the memory system stores and retrieves, not merely what attention selects in the moment.

**Relevant to:** Predictive processing (prior recalibration over decades), Barrett's constructed emotion framework (priors shape experience), crystallized intelligence as accumulated prediction weights

**Retrieval prompt:** "What do the diffusion model analyses say about the distinction between response bias and memory bias in the positivity effect? Include the specific familiarity parameters and how they distinguish accessibility in long-term memory from attentional bias at encoding."

**COMP-VAR-AGP-03 `[EMPIRICAL] [FRAMING MOVE]`**

**Claim:** The maturity principle — the idea that personality develops adaptively across adulthood — requires refinement. While most facets develop in adaptive directions (increasing agreeableness, conscientiousness, emotional stability), several clearly functional facets decline: activity, openness to feelings, and social potency all decrease across adulthood in adults aged 30–70. These are not pathological losses. They represent the system shedding metabolically expensive agentic traits as the environment stops demanding or rewarding them, concentrating remaining resources on emotional stability and social harmony.

**Relevant to:** Turn routing narrowing with age, "adaptive retreat" vs. decline, niche constriction as metabolic survival strategy, facet-level vs. domain-level personality analysis, the "La Dolce Vita" effect

**Retrieval prompt:** "What does the maturity principle refinement study say about which specific facets decline vs. increase across adulthood? **COMP-BAL-SOC-01 `[MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** Goal disengagement in aging is triggered by a preconscious background monitoring system that tracks the accumulation of negative affect (or decline of positive affect) associated with specific goal pursuits. When this emotional signal clears a threshold, it produces an "action crisis" — a psychological state that breaks the positively biased implemental mindset required for goal commitment, forcing a critical review and potential disengagement. The specific emotion shifts with age: anger signals "try harder" when agency is still viable; sadness signals "let go" when control capacity has been exceeded.

**Relevant to:** Go Stat erosion → emotional signal switching, the floor where the substitution ratchet runs out, anger as metabolically expensive (presumes capacity to act), predictive processing (error accumulation → model update)

**COMP-BAL-SOC-02 `[MECHANISM] [FRAMING MOVE]`**

**Claim:** The positivity effect and the action crisis mechanism interact in a potentially maladaptive way. The positivity effect suppresses negative information processing, which may delay detection of the very negative-affect signals the action crisis system needs to trigger disengagement. The system resolves this tension through resource depletion: when sustained failure or biological decline consumes the executive resources required to maintain the positivity bias, the bias collapses, and the accumulated negative signals break through to trigger the crisis. In advanced old age, the system recalibrates — becoming more sensitive to sadness as a disengagement signal, allowing faster transition to goal release.

**Relevant to:** Go-preserving strategy delaying necessary Turn rerouting, internal conflict between regulation systems, the terminal cascade (positivity → depletion → disengagement → comfort), why the "good" strategy can become the obstacle

**Retrieval prompt:** "What do the sources say about the interaction between the positivity effect and goal disengagement processes? Include the resource-dependency of the positivity effect, what happens when resources are depleted, and any evidence about recalibration of emotional sensitivity in very old adults."

**COMP-BAL-SOC-03 `[MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** The terminal phase of aging adaptation follows a structured cascade: (1) the positivity effect maintains emotional well-being while Go capacity slowly erodes; (2) executive resource depletion eliminates the positivity effect; (3) goal disengagement replaces active regulation, with sadness functioning as the adaptive signal; (4) self-protective appraisals (downward comparison, external attribution) replace internal regulation; (5) the final transition moves from optimized functioning to palliative comfort. Failure to complete this cascade — particularly when environments block disengagement — results in aimlessness, hopelessness, or clinical depression.

**SOC-VAR-AGP-01 `[EMPIRICAL]`**

**Claim:** Retirement does not produce personality changes beyond age-graded normative trends. Work characteristics (autonomy, skill discretion, job demands) and subjective work effort are not significantly associated with personality change during the retirement transition. Retirees' personality trajectories do not diverge from those who continue working. Two theoretical explanations: (1) Identity Continuity Theory — retirees actively recreate environments matching former job characteristics, neutralizing the niche shift; (2) retirement is a "weak" environment lacking the behavioral demands that strong environments use to reshape personality.

**Relevant to:** Niche portability vs. niche repair, restoration framing (retirement doesn't test it because people port their niche), "weak environment" concept ↔ "easy-locked" environment concept, Turn routing crystallization by midlife

**Retrieval prompt:** "What does the MIDUS retirement study say about specific work characteristics tested, the comparison between retirees and non-retirees, and the theoretical interpretations offered? Include Identity Continuity Theory, the paradoxical theory of personality coherence, and the 'weak situation' concept."

**SOC-VAR-AGP-02 `[MECHANISM] [FRAMING MOVE]`**

**Claim:** Personality in late adulthood is robust against external niche changes (retirement) but vulnerable to internal biological changes (health decline). This asymmetry means the system has shifted its regulatory focus: it is no longer being socialized by external roles but is instead maintaining continuity through active agency while responding primarily to biological pressure. The environment can only destabilize personality indirectly — by causing or failing to prevent biological damage.

**Relevant to:** External niche vs. internal substrate as drivers of personality change, the asymmetry between social and biological influences in late life, why restoration requires more than removing the bad environment
