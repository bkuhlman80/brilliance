**COMP-KIR-SPE-01 `[MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** In Kirby's (2001) iterated learning model, compositionality emerges as the adaptive response to a transmission bottleneck — the fact that any learner is only exposed to a fragmentary sub-sample of the total language. Holistic languages (one arbitrary signal per meaning, learned by rote) lose unseen pairings at each generation and are inherently unstable across the bottleneck. Compositional languages survive because their systematicity — subparts of signals map to subparts of meaning — allows learners to reverse-engineer rules from incomplete data and generalize to unseen meanings. The bottleneck acts as an evolutionary filter: only languages that permit generalization from fragmentary exposure persist. The model does not predict convergence on any single compositional structure, but on *some* structure that satisfies transmissibility.

**Relevant to:** Domain-level architecture (why five or six rather than twenty), lexical hypothesis grounding, HPAM framing of Big Five as compression artifact rather than discovered essences

**COMP-KIR-CCE-01 `[EMPIRICAL]`**

**Claim:** Kirby, Cornish & Smith (2008) ran a diffusion-chain experiment with 27 visual scenes (3 shapes × 3 colors × 3 motions) and random initial "alien" languages. Under free evolution (Experiment 1), languages collapsed into systematic underspecification — single labels covering natural classes (e.g., "tuge" for all horizontally-moving objects) — and consistently collapsed color distinctions before shape or motion distinctions. This mirrors the "shape bias" in children's word learning. Under a filter for expressivity (Experiment 2), languages evolved compositional morphological structure, with distinct substrings mapping to individual features. Learnability was measured via normalized Levenshtein edit distance (transmission error); systematicity via correlation between signal distance and meaning distance (Hamming). Both measures improved over generations.

**Relevant to:** Why some behavioral dimensions survive cultural compression while others get absorbed, Go/Turn distinction (if capacity dimensions function like "shape" — functionally primary, preserved under compression), lexical hypothesis

**COMP-KEI-SCD-01 `[EMPIRICAL] [MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** Keil's (1979) M constraint states that for any two predicates, their sets of applicable terms must be disjoint, in a subset-superset relationship, or identical — they can never form an intersecting ("M" or "W") pattern. This forces ontological knowledge into rigid hierarchical predicability trees, where higher nodes represent more general categories and lower nodes more restricted ones. Children honor the M constraint from age five onward, starting with collapsed trees (\~3.9 terminal categories in kindergarten) and progressively differentiating to \~6.2 categories by sixth grade. Development follows a "beads-on-a-string" model: predicates move down the tree to narrower, adult-appropriate term sets, and new categories (e.g., "abstract objects") differentiate out of broader parent categories (e.g., "events"). Replicated with Spanish-speaking children in Puerto Rico, suggesting universality.

**Relevant to:** Domain-level architecture (developmental convergence on \~6 dimensions), why the Big Five number is not arbitrary, developmental trajectory of personality vocabulary, Abdi's open calibration windows

**Retrieval prompt:** "What does Keil (1979) say about the specific developmental sequence of ontological differentiation? Include the 3.9-to-6.2 category count, the beads-on-a-string model, the Puerto Rico replication, and the specific examples of which categories differentiate from which parent categories at which ages."

**COMP-JAC-SAC-01 `[MECHANISM] [FRAMING MOVE]`**

*Structural note: Jackendoff arrives at Bennett-compatible conclusions from the semantics side. His preference-rule machinery provides independent grounding for the claim that the brain's basic computational unit operates as weighted preference rather than logic gate — the same conclusion Bennett reaches from cortical-column architecture. The convergence matters more than either source alone.*

**Claim:** Jackendoff (1996) argues that categories with graded boundaries and cluster concepts (e.g., "climb," which satisfies some conditions but not all) are governed by preference rule systems: composite weighting of multiple conditions, where satisfaction of a subset is often sufficient, and central versus marginal category membership is a natural output. He explicitly grounds this in neural architecture, arguing that neuron firing is "a composite of many excitatory and inhibitory synapses of varying strengths" rather than a logic gate, making preference rule systems "quite natural in a psychological framework" even though they are "unusual in a logical framework." He generalizes this beyond language to visual perception (Gestalt principles), musical perception, phonetics, and pragmatics, framing it as a general property of mind design. He points to his 1983 *Semantics and Cognition* as the fuller development.

**Relevant to:** Go stat scaling of Turn mode output (weighted preference is how scaling works computationally), Bennett's computational generalism, the claim that the same computational logic operates across cognitive domains, Vitality as composite signal

**SOC-GOM-WTT-01 `[MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** Gopnik & Meltzoff (1997) describe child development as a cycle of theory formation and revision paralleling scientific discovery. Children begin with innate starting-state theories and pass through a specific sequence: (1) accumulation of counterevidence, (2) initial denial or misinterpretation of contradicting data (treating it as noise), (3) development of ad hoc auxiliary hypotheses that preserve the core theory but make it "ugly and messy," (4) a crisis point of disorganization, and (5) emergence of a new, stable, more coherent theory. This is a qualitative conceptual shift (discontinuous), not gradual accumulation. Children in the "grip of a theory" will misinterpret ambiguous data to preserve the current model. The authors apply this framework to the development of understanding actions, appearances, and kinds in infancy through age three — specifically tracking the shift from action-based to intention-based folk psychology (\~9 to \~18 months). They do not extend it to stable dispositions or personality traits; their "Theory of Mind" work addresses desires and intentions, not enduring character.

**Relevant to:** Calibration windows (Abdi), character framework-collapse (Sharon's Lottery View as auxiliary hypothesis), how counterevidence accumulates before a framework breaks, the distinction between understanding actions and understanding dispositions

**Retrieval prompt:** "What does Gopnik & Meltzoff (1997) say about the specific sequence of theory change — denial, auxiliary hypotheses, crisis, restructuring? Include the evidence they cite for discontinuous change versus gradual accumulation, the examples of children misinterpreting data to preserve a theory, and the specific developmental transitions in the 'Theory of Action' domain (9-month to 18-month shift from actions to intentions)."

**SOC-KEI-SCD-02 `[EMPIRICAL] [FRAMING MOVE]`**

**Claim:** Keil (1979) observed that young children initially restricted the predicate "sorry" to humans because they grounded it in visible behavior — the vocal act of saying "sorry." As children shifted to an internal-state theory (feeling sorry), this actually reduced their certainty about whether the predicate applied to non-human animals. The move from behavioral to dispositional understanding created instability rather than resolving it. This illustrates a general pattern: predicates grounded in observable actions are more stable classification anchors than predicates grounded in inferred mental states, and developmental progress toward mentalistic reasoning temporarily destabilizes category boundaries.

**Relevant to:** Why Turn modes (routing/identity — less visible) are harder to observe and name than Go stats (capacity/energy — more visible), the observability asymmetry in HPAM's measurement approach, Abdi's open calibration windows (shifting from behavioral to dispositional models of people)

## Symbolic Species

**MASK**  
**DEA-SYM-01 \[MECHANISM\]**  
**Bucket: MASK**  
Symbolic suppression as the foundational cognitive operation. Deacon argues that accessing symbolic reference requires actively inhibiting more direct, automatic indexical associations — the brain must suppress the “easy path” to discover higher-order sign-sign relationships. This is not a byproduct of symbolic thought; it is the core operation. The prefrontal cortex provides the selective inhibition needed to override prepotent associative responses. Acquisition requires a “restructuring event” where previously learned word-object correlations are demoted from primary focus to mere clues. The brain must use information “against itself,” generating an implicit “not” to exclude salient associations and shift attention to less obvious patterns.  
HPAM relevance: Masking is structurally identical to this operation — suppressing a routing pattern that works locally to serve a higher-order goal. The difference is that symbolic suppression is designed to resolve into graduation; masking is a suppression that never resolves.

**DEA-SYM-02 \[MECHANISM\]**  
**Bucket: MASK**  
The architectural cost of failed graduation. In Deacon’s model, healthy automation works by stripping symbolic operations of semantic content and converting them to indexical subroutines handled by subcortical systems (striatum, cerebellum). This frees prefrontal “voting reserve” for novel symbolic work. When a suppression pattern cannot automate — because the competing associative signal remains salient — the prefrontal cortex stays permanently deployed on routine management. The cost is architectural, not merely metabolic: the inferential engine is performing the work of a mechanical robot. Managerial overhead occupies capacity meant for divergent thinking and novel symbolic construction. Neural parcellation is zero-sum; resources consumed in chronic suppression are resources unavailable for everything else.  
HPAM relevance: This is the mechanism underlying chronic masking cost. The person isn’t just tired — they’re running their most expensive cognitive hardware on a task it was designed to delegate, starving the system of capacity for the novel work that defines human cognition.

**DEA-SYM-03 \[EMPIRICAL\]**  
**Bucket: MASK**  
The Sally Boysen experiment as animal model for masking. Chimps trained to pick a small candy pile (to receive the large one) could not automate the strategy because the physical presence of the large pile was too salient. The “emotional immediacy” of the competing response constantly undermined the ability to use information against itself. Performance never graduated to automatic; prefrontal-type inhibition had to remain engaged on every trial. The chimps understood the rule but couldn’t reliably execute it against a competing signal that never lost salience.  
HPAM relevance: Direct experimental analogue for Turn-preference masking. The person knows the “correct” behavior for their environment, but the underlying routing keeps generating a competing signal that prevents the suppression from ever becoming effortless. The prefrontal system has to keep winning the same election every day.

**DEA-SYM-04 \[MECHANISM\]**  
**Bucket: MASK**  
Masking as the “stuck middle” — neither graduation nor overload. Deacon’s framework produces three distinct states of indexical processing. Graduation: orderly top-down delegation where solved symbolic problems become efficient subroutines (prefrontal retired). Overload: involuntary bottom-up collapse where prefrontal system is defeated, person reverts to stimulus-bound concreteness (prefrontal overwhelmed). The stuck middle: chronic suppression that can’t delegate downward because competing signal never loses salience, but hasn’t collapsed because prefrontal system is still paying the bill. Under this analysis, “the mask drops” is not breakdown — it’s the prefrontal manager getting outvoted. The Turn routing that re-emerges was never damaged or overwritten, only actively suppressed by a system that ran out of budget.  
HPAM relevance: Core mechanism for the three masking timescales. Acute taxation \= daily prefrontal cost of the ongoing election. Chronic allostatic damage \= cumulative metabolic toll of a system that never stands down. Identity-level erosion \= loss of novel symbolic capacity (divergent thinking, self-construction) because the voting reserve is occupied.

**COMP**  
**DEA-SYM-05 \[MECHANISM\]**  
**Bucket: COMP**  
Graduation: symbolic-to-indexical conversion as the brain’s efficiency strategy. The brain’s “goal” is automated, unconscious, mechanical input-output matching. Practiced symbolic operations graduate by being stripped of semantic content and treated as indexical subroutines. Processing shifts from prefrontal cortex to subcortical systems (striatum, cerebellum) and posterior cortical regions. Three determinants of successful graduation: degree of invariance (predictable enough for cerebellar takeover), semantic weight (can be stripped to function as automatic switch), and mnemonic load (retrieval of habitual relationships vs. construction of novel ones). Function words like “the,” “if,” “was” are the canonical examples — automatic switches drained of semantic links, learned by rote. Metabolic cost genuinely decreases, not just redistributes.  
HPAM relevance: Maps onto how Go Stats operationalize capacity. Raw capacity (prefrontal-heavy, expensive) must convert into deployed ability (automated, efficient) through practice in consequential environments. The graduation mechanism explains how Go capacity becomes functional — and why environments that don’t demand deployment prevent this conversion.

**DEA-SYM-06 \[MECHANISM\]**  
**Bucket: COMP**  
The “voting reserve” model of prefrontal function. Prefrontal expansion in humans provides a “large voting contingency in reserve” that can be recruited to override competing associative responses. Novel symbolic tasks require mobilizing a majority of these votes to suppress bottom-up indexical patterns. Once a task is automated, it no longer requires this managerial overhead. The voting metaphor captures both the competitive nature of neural processing (signals literally compete for influence) and the resource cost of maintaining top-down control against salient bottom-up alternatives. Divergent thinking — sampling among alternatives rather than converging on invariant solutions — is the primary function this reserve is “overbuilt” to support.  
HPAM relevance: Go Stats as capacity variables map onto the size and availability of this voting reserve. Turn Stats as routing preferences map onto the default “candidates” that win elections when the prefrontal system isn’t actively intervening. Masking cost \= permanently deploying the reserve on routine elections it was designed to delegate.

**DEA-SYM-07 \[EMPIRICAL\] \[COUNTERPOINT\]**  
**Bucket: COMP**  
Symbolic threshold as discrete, but processing quality varies continuously. Deacon treats the transition from indexical to symbolic reference as a discrete threshold — chimpanzees either “got” the logic (shifting to categorical guesses with an all-or-none character) or remained stuck in rote paired-associative learning. Human evolution pushed the genetic basis for this threshold to fixation, making it species-universal. However, post-threshold processing quality varies significantly. Williams Syndrome: crosses threshold, produces precocious vocabulary, but processing is “shallow and vacuous” — relying on word-word logic without supportive indexical grounding. Aphasia and retardation: symbolic content grasped but combinatorial complexity degraded. PET studies: prefrontal activation intense for novel symbolic tasks, drops to background for practiced ones. Two people can cross the same threshold but have vastly different depths of understanding based on experiential grounding.  
HPAM relevance: Resolves apparent tension with Go Stats as continuous variables. The threshold is crossed species-wide; what varies is the depth, efficiency, and grounding of symbolic processing — which is what Go Stats actually measure. Williams Syndrome is a useful illustration of high-routing/low-capacity: the pattern looks right but the underlying machinery is thin.

**SOC**  
**DEA-SYM-08 \[MECHANISM\] \[EVOLUTIONARY\]**  
**Bucket: SOC**  
Ritual as external cognitive infrastructure for crossing the symbolic threshold. Deacon argues that brains not yet adapted for symbolic reference required ritual — highly redundant, repetitive, culturally structured performance — to force discovery of abstract sign-sign relationships. Ritual works by making individual details automatic through repetition, shifting attention from surface features to abstract logic. Functionally equivalent to the “reductio ad absurdum training ploy” used with chimps Sherman and Austin: explicitly training on what not to do to break rote indexical habits. The cultural environment literally performs part of the cognitive work of symbolic acquisition. Without this scaffolding, even biologically capable brains may not cross the threshold — chimpanzees don’t communicate symbolically in the wild not because they lack intelligence but because they lack the social-cultural scaffolding to push them over.  
HPAM relevance: Direct support for the claim that environment is cognitive infrastructure, not just opportunity. The cultural surround doesn’t merely permit development; it does computational work the brain can’t do alone. Extends to modern contexts: if the social environment doesn’t provide structured friction (consequence signals, feedback loops), Go capacity may remain latent even when biologically present.

**DEA-SYM-09 \[MECHANISM\]**  
**Bucket: SOC**  
“Less is more”: productive constraint through input filtering vs. easy-locked through output demand. Deacon (via Newport) argues that children’s immature brains — brief working memory, high distractibility, poor consolidation — act as a perceptual filter that prevents getting stuck in “learning potholes” of local word-object correlations. The global logic “pops out” because the child literally cannot track overwhelming surface detail. This is distinct from easy-locked, which operates on output: the environment doesn’t require the person to deploy capacity under real stakes. Immature brain \= blind to the wrong things (input filtering). Frictionless environment \= never required to do the hard thing (output demand). Deacon: human evolution aligned both — immaturity makes us blind to easy local details while social ritual/ecological stakes make the hard symbolic pattern the only viable path.  
HPAM relevance: Critical distinction for the book. Readers may conflate “constraint is productive” with “ease is destructive.” They’re different mechanisms on different systems. Input filtering (developmental) vs. output demand (environmental) need separate treatment. The productive case: you can’t see the distraction. The destructive case: nothing demands you look past it.

**DEA-SYM-10 \[EMPIRICAL\] \[EVOLUTIONARY\]**  
**Bucket: SOC**  
Co-evolutionary bootstrapping: the niche shapes the person, but ancestors authored the niche. Deacon’s answer to the person-shapes-niche vs. niche-shapes-person question: “an idea changed the brain.” Once ancestors stumbled onto rudimentary symbolic systems, these became indispensable behavioral prostheses. The cultural niche then “turned the tables,” creating selection pressure (Baldwinian evolution) for prefrontal expansion and extended developmental clock. Three feedback loops: ritual-social contract loop (representing abstract obligations required ritual scaffolding), prefrontal-symbolic loop (symbol-dependent niche selected for prefrontal expansion), language-brain adaptation loop (languages evolved outside the brain to be “child-friendly,” adapting to immature human neurology). Symbolic capacity is “interpreter-dependent” — exists only through the network of users. Early systems were fragile; if social structure collapsed, the idea of symbols could be lost even with biological capacity intact.  
HPAM relevance: Structural parallel to niche construction in personality development. The person’s early behavioral choices begin shaping the environment that then shapes them. Fragility point: if the social network supporting a particular routing pattern collapses, the pattern can be lost even when biological capacity remains — relevant to what happens when someone loses their niche (job loss, relocation, relationship dissolution).

**BIO**  
**DEA-SYM-11 \[MECHANISM\] \[EVOLUTIONARY\]**  
**Bucket: BIO**  
Metabolic cost and zero-sum trade-offs of cognitive capacity. Brain consumes up to 10x more glucose and oxygen per unit mass than any other organ at rest. Human brain is 3x the size predicted for an ape of our body size; prefrontal cortex is 2x predicted for a primate brain this size, 6x larger than a chimpanzee’s. Brain parcellation is zero-sum: prefrontal and cerebellar expansion came at expense of olfactory bulbs (divergently reduced) and primary visual cortex (unexpectedly small). As brain size increases, proportion of neurons any single neuron can link to drops rapidly, leading to increasing fragmentation of function and loss of speed. Larger brains devote higher proportion of capacity to “managerial” integration functions, becoming progressively less efficient in computations per functional output. The descended larynx enables speech but increases choking risk — a severe biological cost tolerated only because symbolic communication provides overwhelming advantage.  
HPAM relevance: Concrete biological evidence for “Go Stats are expensive.” Capacity isn’t free — it requires metabolic investment, imposes trade-offs against other systems, and scales with diminishing returns due to managerial overhead. The zero-sum framing supports the claim that deploying Go resources on masking literally reduces resources available for other functions.
