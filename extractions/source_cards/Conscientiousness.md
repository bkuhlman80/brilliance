## **SOC Cards: Social Investment and Crystallization**

*Structural note: Roberts' Social Investment Principle is the empirical backbone for HPAM's claim that Turn stats crystallize through career/life experience. It is the positive-direction counterpart to the substitution ratchet already in the SOC doc — Roberts explains why C rises when environments demand it; the substitution ratchet explains why C falls when environments stop demanding it. Together they form the corresponsive feedback loop that is the crystallization mechanism.*

**SOC-ROB-PDO-01 `[MECHANISM] [EMPIRICAL]`**

**Claim:** Roberts' Social Investment Principle (2006) proposes that conscientiousness increases in young adulthood (ages 20–40) because committing to adult social roles — career entry, marriage, parenthood, leadership — creates environmental "press" for C-related behaviors. The mechanism is not biological maturation alone but a demand-driven sequence: role expectations act as discriminative stimuli signaling which behaviors are rewarded → repeated behavioral compliance becomes habit → habitual behavior shifts role identity (the person begins to see themselves as a "hard worker") → identity generalizes into the broad dispositional trait. Young adulthood is the "fulcrum period" because identities are still provisional and role demands are highest. However, humans remain "open systems" — change is possible even in old age through sustained environmental investment. Clinical interventions have modified C facets in as little as six weeks. Rank-order consistency increases linearly from childhood, peaking after age 50, consistent with progressive crystallization through decades of role-consistent behavior.

**Relevant to:** Turn stat crystallization (this is the mechanism), corresponsive principle (already in SOC — Roberts provides the C-specific evidence), substitution ratchet (inverse of this process), Sutin et al. **SOC-ROB-PDO-02 `[MECHANISM] [EMPIRICAL]`**

**Claim:** Roberts proposes a corresponsive model of personality development in which selection and training operate synergistically. Selection: childhood temperament and early self-control predict who enters high-prestige, demanding careers — high-C individuals select into environments that reward their existing traits. Training: work experiences precede and cause trait changes — taking a leadership role or increasing homework effort produces measurable C increases. Crystallization through feedback: the environments people select into deepen and elaborate the traits that led them there, so by middle age the trait is highly stable because the individual has spent decades in a niche that consistently rewards C-related habits. The strongest evidence for the training (demand-driven) component comes from three lines: (1) longitudinal data showing work socialization effects precede trait changes, (2) honeybee colonies where previously "lazy" bees immediately become high-performing foragers when the high-performers are removed, demonstrating that C-like behavior is a functional response to environmental demand gaps, and (3) digital interventions shifting C facets in six weeks, contradicting a fixed biological set-point.

**Relevant to:** Corresponsive principle (this IS the C-specific evidence), niche repair framing (finding environments where natural feedback loops exist), substitution ratchet (the loop running in reverse), Hopf Cycle (intergenerational version), Kandler heritability finding in BIO (social role demands override individual genetic differences — converges with this)

## **COMP Cards: The Industriousness/Orderliness Bifurcation**

---

*Structural note: This card is the structural spine for all C content in HPAM. Every other C card in COMP depends on the claim that Industriousness and Orderliness are computationally distinct. The evidence is converging from five independent lines: human psychometrics (Roberts), animal taxonomy (Delgado & Sulloway), health outcomes (Bogg & Roberts, Stephan), personality pathology (Collison — Machiavellianism), and heritability (Luciano). The opposite correlations — primates positive on Achievement but negative on Orderliness; Machiavellians high on Agency but low on Planfulness — are the strongest evidence for dissociability.*

---

**COMP-ROB-WIC-01 `[MECHANISM] [EMPIRICAL]`**

**Claim:** Roberts et al. (2012) identify Industriousness and Orderliness as the two primary domains within conscientiousness, alongside subsidiary facets (self-control, responsibility, conventionality, decisiveness, formalness, punctuality). Industriousness involves working hard and persisting through challenges, primarily manifest in achievement settings (school/work). Orderliness involves being prepared, neat, and planful, primarily manifest in domestic or organizational environments. A general global dimension of C visible in childhood temperament differentiates into these specific facets through experience in adolescence and adulthood. **Relevant to:** Industriousness and Orderliness as distinct Turn stats (this is the psychometric grounding), mode system (high/low expressions of each stat are distinct modes with different pathological tails), Delgado & Sulloway's evolutionary pressures (different adaptive problems → different subsystems), Collison Machiavellianism finding (decoupling evidence — high Industriousness with low Orderliness is a real phenotype), Go/Turn distinction (both are Turn stats but with different Go draw profiles)

## **COMP Cards: Habit Formation vs. Effortful Control**

---

**COMP-WOO-HAD-01 `[MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** Wood & Rünger (2016) describe habit formation as model-free learning in which frequent repetition in a stable context "outsources" behavioral control to environmental cues, making the behavior automatic and goal-independent. The process is proceduralization: deliberate cognitive effort is replaced by direct cue → response triggering. Habit formation takes 18 to 254 days depending on behavioral complexity. Once formed, habits are energetically cheap to maintain because they bypass the effortful control systems (dlPFC, ACC) that sustained goal-pursuit requires. The Habit Discontinuity Hypothesis predicts that when environmental cues are removed — moving house, changing jobs, altering daily routine — habitual behavior declines or requires effortful re-engagement. This means habitual behaviors are cue-maintained (dependent on environmental stability) while effortful-control behaviors are goal-maintained (dependent on metabolic resources and goal representation).

**Relevant to:** Orderliness/Industriousness computational distinction (Orderliness exploits habit circuits — front-loads effort to build routines that then run cheap on cues; Industriousness runs on sustained effortful control — ongoing metabolic cost, goal-dependent), Go draw profiles (Orderliness costs Go up front then saves it; Industriousness costs Go continuously), Habit Discontinuity as Orderliness vulnerability (environmental disruption breaks Orderliness more than Industriousness because habits are cue-maintained), substitution ratchet Phase 2 in SOC (Go stat goes untrained because the substitute doesn't require deployment — this is the mechanism), Thorndike's impulse requirement in SOC (behavior executed without internal motor impulse \= habit without stamp-in)

### **SOC-DEV-DIF-01 `[MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** Conscientiousness develops through a four-stage differentiation process from childhood temperament to adult trait:

Stage 1 — Global effortful control in childhood splits along a social/task axis: self-control applied to interpersonal settings becomes Agreeableness; self-control applied to achievement/work settings becomes Conscientiousness. This explains why A and C share variance in the Stability metatrait — they originate from the same temperamental substrate.

Stage 2 — Within C, role-specific differentiation occurs during young adulthood (20–40) through the Social Investment Principle. Work contingencies reward Industriousness (persistence, effort, achievement). Domestic/organizational settings reward Orderliness (neatness, preparation, routine-following). The environment provides the "discriminative stimuli" that channel general C into specific aspect-level expression.

Stage 3 — Behavioral frequency leads to internalization: acting as a hard worker (role behavior) → habit formation → identity shift ("I am a hard worker") → generalization into dispositional trait of Industriousness. This is the crystallization mechanism: behavior → habit → identity → trait.

Stage 4 — Proceduralization: repeated self-regulatory strategies undergo automatization where perceptual cues trigger the response directly. This "takes the effort out of effortful control" — what started as costly Go deployment becomes cheap cue-triggered routine. The developmental endpoint is that well-crystallized C feels effortless because the effortful control has been compiled into automatic programs.

**Source:** Roberts et al. (2012), Roberts (2006), Annual Review of Personality Psychology (2022), Wood & Rünger (2016)

**Relevant to:** Turn stat crystallization (this is THE mechanism — HPAM asserts crystallization happens but this card provides the four-stage how), the A/C shared origin (explains Stability metatrait from the developmental side — complements the serotonin-Stability pharmacological evidence in BIO-DEY-NPE-01), Orderliness as habit-based vs. Industriousness as effort-based (Stage 2 differentiation maps directly onto the computational distinction in C4), proceduralization as Go → Turn conversion (what starts as effortful control becomes automatized routine — the Go stat trains the Turn stat until the Turn stat can run without Go support)

### **SOC-MIS-DEL-01 `[EMPIRICAL] [CONTROVERSIAL]`**

**Claim:** Mischel's marshmallow test originally showed strong longitudinal predictions: preschool delay time correlated r \= .42 with SAT verbal and r \= .57 with SAT math at 10–12 year follow-up. At 20–30 year follow-up (ages 27–32), longer delay predicted lower BMI, higher self-worth, and more adaptive stress coping. At 40+ years, high vs. low delayers showed different brain activation patterns in addiction-relevant regions.

However, the Watts, Duncan, & Quan (2018) conceptual replication (sample ten times larger and more diverse than original Stanford sample) found that the bivariate correlation (r \= .28) was reduced by two-thirds (to approximately β \= .08) after controlling for family background and early cognitive ability. Most of the predictive gain was achieved by children who could wait at least 20 seconds — beyond that threshold, additional waiting time added little predictive value.

The strategy finding remains robust: children who waited successfully used "cool" cognitive strategies — abstract re-representation (imagining the marshmallow as a cloud) and attention deployment (singing, looking away) — rather than raw willpower. When children who initially failed were taught these strategies, they were able to wait up to 18 minutes, equalizing their performance with natural high-delayers. This modifiability finding suggests self-control is a trainable metacognitive skill, not a fixed capacity.

**Source:** Mischel, Shoda, & Rodriguez (1989); Mischel (2014); Watts, Duncan, & Quan (2018)

**Relevant to:** Trainability of Turn stats (strategy acquisition changes the routing, not the capacity — consistent with Turn stats as learnable preferences), Go vs. Turn distinction (raw waiting time \= Go component; strategy use \= Turn component; the Watts replication shows that most of the predictive power comes from environmental factors that shape both), honest state of evidence (the classic narrative overstates the finding — after controls, the effect is real but small; the strategy/modifiability finding is the part that holds up best), environmental proxy concern (the marshmallow test partly measures whether a child has learned to trust that promised rewards will arrive — children in unstable environments discount rationally)
