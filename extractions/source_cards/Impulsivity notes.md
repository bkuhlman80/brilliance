Dickman's impulsivity inventory (Dickman, [1990](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4594014/#B11)) measures functional and dysfunctional impulsivity. 

* **Dysfunctional impulsivity** is defined as the tendency to act with less forethought than most people of same ability when this inclination is a source of difficulty.   
* In contrast, **functional impulsivity** is the tendency to act with relatively little forethought when such a style is optimal.

## Samo et al. (2026): Impulsivity as a Standalone Trait

Samo et al. applied Taxonomic Graph Analysis (TGA) — a 7-step network psychometrics framework — to the IPIP-NEO-300 (N \= 149,337) to derive personality structure bottom-up from items rather than imposing a top-down 5-factor solution.  
Key impulsivity finding: At the second level of their hierarchy, they identified six traits rather than five. The sixth is Impulsivity — emerging as a standalone trait alongside Neuroticism, Conscientiousness, Openness, Sociability, and Integrity. This is notable because in the NEO tradition, impulsivity content is split across N5 Impulsiveness and C6 Deliberation (and arguably C5 Self-Discipline). Samo et al.'s bottom-up analysis pulls it together into its own cluster.  
At the third (meta-trait) level, Impulsivity feeds into a novel meta-trait they call Disinhibition, alongside Stability and Plasticity.

## Everything on Luck (7 papers)

Your bibliography traces a conceptual progression:  
Early work — luck as unidimensional:

* Wiseman (2003) — popular article arguing luck is behavioral: lucky people are more extraverted (bigger networks), more open (receptive to novelty), and less neurotic (less attentional narrowing). His "luck school" intervention increased self-reported luck in 80% of participants.  
* Chotai & Wiseman (2005) — N \= 29,584. Summer-born people feel luckier (peak May, nadir November). Luck correlates with lower N, higher O, higher E — all showing the same seasonal birth pattern. Effect size \~3.4% attributable fraction.  
* Maltby et al. (2008) — Validated the Darke & Freedman Belief in Good Luck Scale as unidimensional. Luck belief correlates with irrational thinking and paranormal beliefs, but is distinct from locus of control.

Key reconceptualization — luck is bidimensional:

* Thompson & Prendergast (2013) — N \= 1,205 cross-cultural. Showed the field was conflating two uncorrelated constructs:  
  * Belief in Luck (BiL): maladaptive, correlates with Neuroticism and irrational beliefs  
  * Belief in Personal Luckiness (BPL): adaptive, correlates with positive affect and wellbeing  
* Thompson & Prendergast (2022) — N \= 844, Hong Kong. BiL is negatively associated with happiness, fully mediated by Neuroticism. BPL is positively associated with happiness and shares unique variance beyond the Big Five — suggesting luckiness may be a discrete trait.

Further extensions:

* Young, Chen, & Morris (2009) — N \= 78 \+ 74 (small). Another bidimensional split, but different: stable luck (like self-efficacy; correlates with E and achievement motivation) vs. fleeting luck (anxious, uncontrollable; correlates with N). After failure, stable-luck believers maintain effort; fleeting-luck believers give up.  
* Miwa, Yamashita, & Morita (2025) — N \= 516 \+ 300, Japanese. Extended to three components: perceived control over luck, positive cognitive bias (overestimating good luck), and affective experience of luck (savoring good luck). All three independently predict life satisfaction.

Consistent personality signature across studies: Luck beliefs correlate with lower N, higher E, higher O. Personal luckiness may capture variance beyond the Big Five.

## Everything on Impulsivity (7 papers \+ extensive extraction content)

### The structural question: what IS impulsivity?

Sharma et al. (2014) — landmark meta-analysis (58 self-report scales, 15 behavioral tasks). Three self-report factors emerged, each mapping to a Big Five domain:

1. Extraversion/Positive Emotionality (sensation seeking anchors here)  
2. Disinhibition vs. Constraint/Conscientiousness (lack of premeditation anchors here)  
3. Neuroticism/Negative Emotionality (urgency anchors here)

Behavioral tasks formed 4 separate factors (Inattention, Inhibition, Impulsive Decision-Making, Shifting). Self-report and task correlations were essentially zero (grand mean r \= .02).  
MacKillop et al. (2016) — N \= 1,252. Confirmed three uncorrelated latent factors: Impulsive Choice (delay discounting), Impulsive Action (inhibition), Impulsive Personality Traits (self-report). Sensation Seeking did not load on any factor (lambda \< .2).

### Is urgency one thing or two?

Billieux et al. (2021) — N \= 18,568. Network analysis of the s-UPPS-P found 4 clusters, not 5: positive and negative urgency cohere as a single "general urgency" cluster. The classical distinction may be artificial.

### Developmental trajectories

Harden & Tucker-Drob (2011) — N \= 7,640, ages 12–24. Impulsivity declines linearly; sensation seeking peaks \~age 16\. Correlation between their developmental changes is only r \= .21, confirming they're largely independent processes.

### Genetic architecture

Miller & Gizer (2024) — GenomicSEM of GWAS data. A sensation-seeking vs. self-control dual-systems model fit well (rg \= .60). Sensation seeking is positively genetically correlated with cortical surface area; lack of self-control is negatively correlated with cortical thickness (especially PFC, max rg \= \-.41).  
Harden et al. (2017) — N \= 810 adolescent twins. Found 4 biometric factors (h² \= .31–.66), including "Cognitive Dyscontrol" which correlated with IQ at rg \= \-.91. Key insight: "cognitive control" conflates a personality dimension (premeditation) with an ability dimension (cognitive dyscontrol).

### The cross-domain impulsivity signal in your extractions

Your own synthesis work identifies a strong N5–C6 impulsivity axis as the 3rd strongest quantified cross-domain signal in the entire corpus:

* N5 Impulsiveness genetically loads more on C than on N (loadings \-.33 to \-.53 across countries)  
* C6 Deliberation and N5 show converging developmental trajectories — both monotonically decline toward reduced impulsivity across the lifespan  
* C5 Self-Discipline joins them as a third facet sharing impulse-control content  
* N5 was excluded from the BFI-2 due to its interstitial placement

This converges with Samo et al.'s (2026) finding that impulsivity forms its own standalone trait when structure is derived bottom-up — it genuinely sits between N and C, not cleanly in either.

\#\# Samo et al. (2026): Impulsivity as Independent 6th Trait

Taxonomic Graph Analysis of the 300-item IPIP-NEO (N \= 149,337) derived a 3-level hierarchy with \*\*6 second-level traits\*\*, not 5:

| Meta-trait | Traits | Item count |  
|---|---|---|  
| Stability | Neuroticism \+ Conscientiousness | 48 \+ 34 |  
| Plasticity | Sociability (E+A blend) \+ Openness | 58 \+ 41 |  
| \*\*Disinhibition\*\* (novel) | Integrity (A+C blend) \+ \*\*Impulsivity\*\* | 14 \+ \*\*26\*\* |

The Impulsivity trait comprises 4 empirical facets drawn from 3 theoretical domains:  
\- \*\*Recklessness\*\* and \*\*Excitement-Seeking\*\* (from E)  
\- \*\*Cautiousness\*\* (from C, reversed)  
\- \*\*Immoderation\*\* (from N)

Disinhibition is nearly uncorrelated with Plasticity (r \= .056) and negatively correlated with Stability (r \= \-.365).

This is the structural headline: when you don't impose 5 factors, impulsivity separates out as its own trait, drawing content from E, C, and N simultaneously.

\#\# Narrative Assessment

\*\*Does impulsivity have the widest Big Five footprint of any personality construct?\*\*

\*\*Yes\*\*, with qualifications:

1\. \*\*Footprint \= 5/5 domains\*\* at the construct level — the theoretical maximum. No Big Five domain touches all 4 of its neighbors at |r| \> .20 in typical meta-analyses.

2\. \*\*Entropy \= 0.957 of maximum\*\* — more diffuse than any Big Five domain's cross-loading profile.

3\. \*\*Samo's bottom-up evidence\*\* confirms impulsivity refuses to be captured by any single Big Five domain, emerging as an independent 6th trait when structure is not imposed.

4\. \*\*But C is home base.\*\* Mean |r| with C (.330) is nearly double the next-highest (N at .194). Three of 5 UPPS-P facets anchor primarily on C. Impulsivity is diffuse but not symmetrically so.

5\. \*\*Samo's structural evidence narrows the footprint.\*\* Bottom-up item analysis draws Impulsivity content from only 3 domains (E, C, N). The A and O correlations are small (mean |r| \= .108 and .156) and may reflect indirect associations rather than shared content.

\#\# Caveats

1\. \*\*Composite correlation matrix.\*\* No single paper in the repo provides a complete UPPS-P x Big Five domain matrix. The table above combines two studies using different instruments (NEO-PI-R vs. NEO-FFI) and samples.

2\. \*\*Construction circularity.\*\* The UPPS was explicitly derived from NEO-PI-R facets (Whiteside & Lynam 2001), so the correlations partly reflect construction logic — the instrument was designed to capture impulsivity variance across Big Five domains.

3\. \*\*Sharma's meta-analysis\*\* found impulsivity maps onto only 3 of the Big Five personality factors (N, C, E). O and A do not emerge as distinct dimensions in the impulsivity scale space even though they show nonzero correlations.

4\. \*\*Entropy inflation.\*\* Entropy computed from raw |r| values (including near-zero ones like |r| \= .02) inflates apparent diffusion. A thresholded or squared-correlation approach would show more concentration around C.

5\. \*\*Missing comparison constructs.\*\* We haven't computed footprint entropy for other candidate "central" constructs (e.g., Assertiveness from BRILLIANCE I, or self-esteem, or emotional intelligence) to confirm that impulsivity's footprint is uniquely wide.
