**COMP-WAN-GBF-01 `[MECHANISM] [FRAMING MOVE]`**

**Claim:** Wang proposes that cortical areas share a canonical circuit architecture — the same basic excitatory cell types (intratelencephalic, pyramidal tract, corticothalamic) and the same inhibitory interneuron classes (parvalbumin, somatostatin, VIP) — but that biological properties vary systematically along macroscopic gradients aligned with cortical hierarchy. Spine density on layer 3 pyramidal neurons increases roughly threefold from V1 to prefrontal cortex in primates. NMDA receptor subunit composition shifts along the same gradient. PV-to-SST interneuron ratios change from sensory to association areas. Wang's key theoretical move: in nonlinear dynamical systems, quantitative parameter differences in a shared circuit produce qualitatively different computational behaviors through bifurcation. Persistent activity for working memory emerges in association cortex but not sensory cortex, even though both run the same basic circuit — the parameter difference crosses a dynamical threshold. This dissolves the apparent contradiction between architectural uniformity and functional diversity: the cortex is simultaneously uniform (same circuit) and diverse (different dynamics), and the paradox is resolved by nonlinear dynamics, not by declaring one side of the modularity debate right.

**Source:** Wang, X.-J. (2020). Macroscopic gradients of synaptic excitation and inhibition in the neocortex. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*; Wang, X.-J. (2022). Theory of the multiregional neocortex. *Annual Review of Neuroscience*.

**Relevant to:** Bennett bet / parameterized generalism (this IS the modern resolution — same architecture, graded parameters, qualitatively different dynamics via bifurcation), Go/Turn architecture (Go stats operate across a shared circuit whose parameters vary regionally; Turn mode diversity emerges from parametric variation, not module installation), gaming metaphor (same engine, different stat builds producing different playstyles — Wang gives the neural mechanism for why this works)

**COMP-YAO-ABC-01 `[EMPIRICAL]`**

**Claim:** The Allen Brain Cell Atlas whole-isocortex transcriptomic profiling (Yao et al. 2021, building on Tasic et al. 2018\) revealed that when all cortical areas are sampled — not just two extremes — most excitatory neuron types are shared across multiple areas and distributed in continuous gradients along the cortical sheet. Tasic et al. (2018) had profiled 23,822 cells from visual and motor cortex and found a striking dichotomy: nearly all GABAergic interneuron types were shared across areas, while most glutamatergic excitatory neuron types appeared area-specific. The expanded whole-isocortex atlas showed that the apparent sharp area-specificity of excitatory neurons was partly an artifact of sampling from opposite poles of a gradient. Cross-species comparisons (Bakken et al., *Nature*, 2021\) confirm broadly conserved cellular makeup across human, marmoset, and mouse, with similarities reflecting evolutionary distance — though few cell-type marker genes are actually conserved across species, revealing substantial divergence beneath surface-level conservation. One complication: agranular frontal cortex has genuine cytoarchitectonic differences from the canonical microcircuit model derived from granular sensory cortex, and motor cortex (long thought to lack layer 4\) contains excitatory neurons with connectivity profiles typical of layer 4 pyramidal cells — supporting deep conservation of circuit elements even when cytoarchitecture appears different.

**Source:** Yao, Z. et al. (2021). A taxonomy of transcriptomic cell types across the isocortex and hippocampal formation. *Cell*; Tasic, B. et al. (2018). Shared and distinct transcriptomic cell types across neocortical areas. *Nature*; Bakken, T.E. et al. (2021). Comparative cellular analysis of motor cortex in human, marmoset and mouse. *Nature*.

**Relevant to:** Bennett bet (gradient distribution of cell types supports parameterized generalism over discrete modules), Wang bifurcation framework (COMP-WAN-GBF-01 — the gradients Wang theorizes about are the ones Yao et al. measured), HPAM prediction that Turn stats have no general factor (if modes emerge from parametric variation along a gradient, no common factor is expected on the Turn side — each region of the gradient produces different routing characteristics)

**Retrieval prompt:** "How many cell types did Yao et al. identify across the whole isocortex, and what proportion of excitatory types were shared across multiple areas vs. restricted to one? What was the sampling design that corrected the Tasic 2018 finding — how many areas were sampled, and how did the gradient structure become visible? **COMP-CAD-ARE-01 `[EMPIRICAL] [MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** The protomap/protocortex debate has been resolved into a hybrid model of progressive specification. Cadwell et al. (2019) propose a "serial homology" framework: intrinsic genetic programs (morphogen gradients from signaling centers, transcription factor expression patterns including Pax6, Emx2, and COUP-TFI) establish broad proto-regions, while activity-dependent mechanisms — primarily thalamic input and subsequent neural activity — progressively refine boundaries. Single-cell transcriptomics from human fetal brain (Bhaduri et al., *Nature*, 2021\) showed that radial glia have detectable but weak areal differences early in the second trimester, organized along frontal-occipital gradients, and these differences are amplified progressively as cells differentiate from progenitors to intermediate progenitors to neurons. The protomap exists but is initially faint — experience turns up the volume. A complicating finding from Qian et al. (*Nature*, 2025\) using MERFISH spatial transcriptomics on over 18 million cells: while most cortical areas show continuous, gradual molecular transitions along the anterior-posterior axis (consistent with gradient-based specification), a sharp, discrete molecular boundary between V1 and V2 appears as early as gestational week 20, before cytoarchitectural differences are visible. This binary transition involves V1-specific layer 4 neuron subtypes and early synaptogenesis gene upregulation, challenging the assumption that all mid-gestation arealization is gradient-like. Some boundaries may be established by mechanisms more discrete than simple morphogen diffusion. One additional finding: inter-areal transcriptional states show robust divergence prenatally, converge during infancy and childhood, then re-diverge in adolescence — a non-monotonic trajectory suggesting postnatal experience re-establishes molecular differences initially set by intrinsic programs.

**Source:** Cadwell, C.R. et al. (2019). Development and arealization of the cerebral cortex. *Neuron*; Qian, X. et al. (2025). Spatial transcriptomics reveals human cortical layer and area specification. *Nature*; Bhaduri, A. et al. (2021). An atlas of cortical arealization identifies dynamic molecular signatures. *Nature*.

**Relevant to:** Bennett bet (the dominant pattern is graded, not modular — but the V1/V2 sharp boundary is the strongest counterpoint and should not be minimized), sensitive period logic (if intrinsic gradients set initial zones and experience refines, then the calibration window is for refinement, not installation), the non-monotonic transcriptional trajectory (prenatal divergence → childhood convergence → adolescent re-divergence) connects to adolescent calibration window material in BIO

**Retrieval prompt:** "What specific transcription factors and signaling centers does Cadwell et al. identify as establishing proto-regions, and what evidence do they give for activity-dependent refinement? In the Bhaduri fetal atlas, how weak are the initial areal differences in radial glia — is this quantified? For the Qian MERFISH finding: what specific molecular features define the V1/V2 boundary at gestational week 20, and what mechanisms do they propose for this discrete boundary that differs from the gradient pattern seen elsewhere? Describe the non-monotonic transcriptional trajectory — which study reports prenatal divergence → childhood convergence → adolescent re-divergence, and what do the authors make of it?"

**COMP-HEN-SPM-01 `[EMPIRICAL] [MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** Hensch's program has established the molecular logic of sensitive period regulation with remarkable precision. The core mechanism: parvalbumin-positive (PV) inhibitory interneuron maturation triggers sensitive period onset; perineuronal net (PNN) condensation around PV cells is a major brake on closure. BDNF signaling drives PV cell maturation (overexpression produces precocious critical periods; deficiency delays them). The homeoprotein Otx2 transfers into PV cells via PNN glycosaminoglycan binding to regulate both onset and closure timing. The strongest evidence for conservation across regions: Lee et al. (2017) showed that a point mutation in Otx2's GAG-recognition motif broadly delays PV interneuron maturation not only in visual cortex but also in auditory cortex and medial prefrontal cortex simultaneously — one genetic manipulation, three modality systems, same delayed critical period. The PNN mechanism also operates in the amygdala, where PNN formation coincides with the developmental switch from erasable to permanent fear memories (Gogolla et al., *Science*, 2009); degrading PNNs in adult amygdala re-enables fear memory erasure. However, circuit implementation differs by region: in visual cortex, PV interneurons in layer 4 and PNNs are the primary machinery; in auditory cortex, layer 1 interneurons (5-HT3aR-positive, non-VIP) serve as the critical hub, and closure is gated by Lynx1-mediated transitions in nicotinic acetylcholine receptor sensitivity (Takesian et al., *Nature Neuroscience*, 2018). PNNs in hippocampal CA2 surround excitatory pyramidal neurons rather than PV interneurons (Carstens et al., 2016), breaking the assumption that PNNs universally regulate inhibitory cells. Additional complication: ketamine reopens adult visual cortex plasticity by disrupting TrkB-PTPσ interaction without degrading PNNs (Cannarozzo et al., 2023), and PNN density can be dynamically regulated in adult cortex by transient changes in local network activity (Devienne et al., 2021\) — suggesting PNNs are not permanent structural barriers but modulable gates.

**Source:** Lee, H.H.C. et al. (2017). Genetic Otx2 mis-localization delays critical period plasticity across brain regions. *Molecular Psychiatry*; Gogolla, N. et al. (2009). Perineuronal nets protect fear memories from erasure. *Science*; Takesian, A.E. et al. (2018). Inhibitory circuit gating of auditory critical-period plasticity. *Nature Neuroscience*; Cannarozzo, C. et al. (2023). \[ketamine/TrkB-PTPσ study\]. *European Journal of Neuroscience*; Devienne, G. et al. (2021). \[PNN dynamic regulation study\]. *Journal of Neuroscience*. Review: Hensch, T.K. (multiple publications spanning 2004–2020).

**Relevant to:** Bennett bet (conserved molecular machinery across regions is strong generalism evidence; region-specific circuit implementation is the "parameterized" qualifier), masking model (if PNNs are modulable gates, not permanent barriers, then environmentally driven reopening of plasticity is biologically plausible — connects to timescale 3 reversibility question), stress acceleration (Sisk & Gee's E/I ratio marker connects to Hensch's PV maturation mechanism), V-acquisition period (BC-06: the E/I ratio reduction that closes the window is the Hensch mechanism operating on the association cortex timeline)

**Retrieval prompt:** "What is Hensch's complete molecular cascade for sensitive period onset and closure — include BDNF → PV maturation → PNN condensation → Otx2 transfer, with the specific experimental manipulations that established each link? In the Lee et al. 2017 Otx2 mutation study, what was the specific mutation, which brain regions were tested, and how was delayed PV maturation measured in each? **COMP-NAV-PTO-01 `[EMPIRICAL] [MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** Experience-dependent pruning tunes selectivity and efficiency without changing what a circuit computes. Navlakha et al. (2015, 2018\) measured synapse elimination rates in mouse barrel cortex and showed that biologically observed decreasing-rate pruning — aggressive early, slowing later — produces networks that route information more efficiently using 50–60% fewer connections than peak childhood levels while performing the same computational task. Scholl et al. (2021) modeled pruning in Restricted Boltzmann Machines trained for sensory encoding and found that Fisher-information-based pruning retains computationally relevant connections and eliminates redundant ones, yielding networks that perform the same encoding task with sharper, more selective receptive fields — cells surviving pruning are those with the most selective, information-rich responses. Faust et al. (*Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 2021\) reviewed six decades of pruning research and consistently found circuit refinement rather than computational transformation: in the visual thalamus, complement-mediated pruning segregates retinal axons into eye-specific layers, sharpening the retinotopic map; in barrel cortex, whisker-driven pruning refines the somatotopic map. The VTA still does reward learning; barrel cortex still represents whisker inputs. Calabro et al. (2025, longitudinal 7T fMRI, ages 10–31) found that adolescent pruning produces widespread decreases in regional homogeneity and higher intrinsic coding dimensionality — more orthogonal, independent neural activity patterns — predicting better working memory performance. Same regions, same tasks, higher-fidelity representations. The Lottery Ticket Hypothesis from machine learning (Frankle & Carlin, 2018\) provides an analogy: dense randomly-initialized networks contain sparse subnetworks that match full-network performance; pruning reveals pre-existing computational potential rather than creating new computations. One qualification: Navlakha's work on neuromuscular junction pruning (Dasgupta et al., *PNAS*, 2024\) shows pruning can perform discrete optimization — casting synapse elimination as a bipartite matching algorithm. And the fly olfactory circuit (Dasgupta et al., *Science*, 2017\) demonstrates that sparsity itself can be constitutive of computation: the circuit implements locality-sensitive hashing, and a dense version would compute something categorically different. The most defensible position: pruning primarily tunes, but resulting sparsity can cross thresholds where quantitative tuning enables qualitatively new computational capacities to emerge.

**Source:** Navlakha, S. et al. (2015). Decreasing-rate pruning optimizes the construction of efficient and robust distributed networks. *PLoS Computational Biology*; Navlakha, S. et al. (2018). Network design principles from pruning in biological systems. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*; Faust, T.E. et al. (2021). Mechanisms of synaptic pruning. *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*; Scholl, B. et al. (2021). Pruning during learning. *PLoS Computational Biology*; Calabro, F.J. et al. (2025). \[longitudinal pruning-decorrelation study\]. *Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience*.

**Relevant to:** Bennett bet (pruning tunes a general system rather than installing modules — but the sparsity-threshold qualification matters), Turn mode compilation (BC-03: production compilation at the neural level IS pruning — repeated deployment strengthens pathways, unpracticed strategies get pruned), Build type emergence (Specialist \= deeply pruned few pathways; Generalist \= moderately pruned many; Build type emerges from what got practiced, not what was chosen), masking model timescale 3 (if pruning produces structural configurations, identity-level erosion is degradation of a structural configuration — harder to reverse than acute parameter shifts)

**Retrieval prompt:** "What specific synapse elimination rates did Navlakha measure in barrel cortex, and how does the decreasing-rate profile compare to constant-rate or increasing-rate pruning in computational performance? In the Scholl RBM model, what metric did they use to decide which connections to prune (Fisher information), and what happened to receptive field selectivity after pruning? Describe the Faust review's key examples — complement-mediated pruning in visual thalamus, whisker-driven pruning in barrel cortex — and in each case, was the computational identity of the region preserved or altered? What is the Dasgupta bipartite matching finding and how does it differ from the 'pruning as tuning' story?"

**COMP-VTB-SAC-01 `[EMPIRICAL]`**

**Claim:** Stress-accelerated brain maturation is circuit-specific, not domain-general, and adversity type determines direction of effect. VanTieghem et al. (2021) compared 41 previously-institutionalized and 42 comparison early adolescents and found accelerated maturation specific to frontolimbic (amygdala-vmPFC) circuits, with "limited differences in more broadly distributed networks." The ABCD study (Beck et al., *Biological Psychiatry*, 2024; N \> 9,500) applied brain age models to structural, diffusion, and functional MRI data and found that different adversity types produce opposite effects: emotional neglect and deprivation were associated with younger-looking brains (delayed maturation), while caregiver psychopathology, trauma exposure, and family aggression were associated with older-looking brains (accelerated maturation). McLaughlin's dimensional model (formalized with Ellis, Sheridan, and Belsky, *Development and Psychopathology*, 2022\) provides the framework: threat accelerates maturation of threat-detection circuits; deprivation delays development of stimulation-dependent cortical areas. The Colich et al. meta-analysis (*Psychological Bulletin*, 2020; 54 studies, N \> 116,000) confirmed threat-related adversity associated with vmPFC thinning specifically, while deprivation was associated with more widespread thinning in frontoparietal, default mode, and visual networks. One complication: the Colich meta-analysis found no consistent association between early adversity and amygdala-PFC connectivity across all studies, despite Gee's influential original finding — suggesting the accelerated amygdala-PFC coupling observed in severely deprived institutionalized children may be a specific extreme-deprivation phenotype rather than a universal stress response. Additional finding: adversity effects on white matter are bidirectional and function-specific — Sisk et al. (2025) found adversity associated with higher connectivity in sensorimotor tracts but lower connectivity in cortico-cortical communication tracts.

**Source:** VanTieghem, M. et al. (2021). \[frontolimbic acceleration study\]. *Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience*; Beck, E.D. et al. (2024). \[ABCD brain age study\]. *Biological Psychiatry*; McLaughlin, K.A., Ellis, B.J., Sheridan, M.A., & Belsky, J. (2022). \[dimensional model\]. *Development and Psychopathology*; Colich, N.L. et al. (2020). \[adversity meta-analysis\]. *Psychological Bulletin*.

**Relevant to:** Bennett bet (not one thermostat and not fifty — a small number of broad signals differentially affecting a small number of system-level parameters), masking model (threat vs. deprivation producing opposite effects means the masking three-timescale model needs to specify which kind of adversity drives which timescale), Turn stat crystallization prediction (reward-related routing crystallizes before social-cognitive routing under threat-type adversity, because frontolimbic circuits accelerate while association cortex does not), Sharon and Maya character differences (different adversity profiles → different maturation effects)

**Retrieval prompt:** "In the VanTieghem study, what specific measures showed accelerated frontolimbic maturation, and what did 'limited differences in more broadly distributed networks' look like — were other networks unchanged, or just less affected? In the ABCD study, what specific adversity measures produced acceleration vs. delay, and how large were the brain-age effects (in years or standard deviations)? What is McLaughlin's formal distinction between threat and deprivation dimensions, and what specific neural predictions does each dimension generate? In the Colich meta-analysis, how many of the 54 studies found the Gee-type accelerated amygdala-PFC connectivity, and what do the authors attribute the inconsistency to?"

**COMP-CMP-HOP-01 `[EMPIRICAL]`**

**Claim:** In congenitally blind individuals, higher-order cortical areas preserve their computational identity when receiving input from a non-canonical modality. Area V5/MT — normally selective for visual motion — represents the physical motion properties of word referents (not abstract grammar) when processing language, preserving its motion-computation identity (Urbaniak et al., *Nature Communications*, 2025). Area V6 performs egocentric spatial navigation via auditory sensory substitution in blind individuals — the same spatial computation on different input (Aggius-Vella et al., *Current Biology*, 2023). The Visual Word Form Area responds to auditory reading after just 12 hours of sensory substitution training (Arbel et al., *Neuropsychologia*, 2023). In deaf individuals, the planum temporale — normally selective for auditory motion — shows enhanced responses to visual motion (Benetti et al., *NeuroImage*, 2021). Matuszewski et al. (*Journal of Neuroscience*, 2025\) demonstrated that computational hierarchies between early and higher-order occipital regions are retained in blind individuals for reading: early visual cortex processes low-level features while ventral occipitotemporal cortex performs higher-order lexical computation — same gradient regardless of input modality. Categorical coding in ventral occipitotemporal cortex even survives transient neonatal blindness from cataracts (Mattioni et al., *Nature Communications*, 2025). Park and Fine's unified model (*Frontiers in Neuroscience*, 2024\) proposes that cortical regions are general with respect to their computational style — hierarchical processing, anticipatory coding, categorical representation — but can be repurposed for novel functions when freed from canonical input during critical periods, constrained by long-range connectivity. Orsenigo et al. (2025) found that principal functional gradients (unimodal-to-transmodal) are preserved across blind, deaf, and typically developing individuals — deprivation reshapes modality-specific organization but within constraints of pre-existing hierarchical structure.

**Source:** Urbaniak, K. et al. (2025). \[V5/MT motion-language study\]. *Nature Communications*; Aggius-Vella, E. et al. (2023). \[V6 auditory navigation study\]. *Current Biology*; Matuszewski, J. et al. (2025). \[blind reading hierarchy study\]. *Journal of Neuroscience*; Mattioni, S. et al. (2025). \[cataract recovery study\]. *Nature Communications*; Park, W.J. & Fine, I. (2024). A unified model for cross-modal plasticity and skill acquisition. *Frontiers in Neuroscience*; Orsenigo, D. et al. (2025). \[functional gradient preservation study\]. Preprint.

**Relevant to:** Bennett bet (this is the strongest evidence for computational generalism — the computation is preserved, the input is swapped), Turn stat architecture (if higher-order cortical regions maintain computational identity across input modalities, then Turn modes are configurations of a general computational style, not products of dedicated modules), gaming metaphor (the same engine processes different inputs and produces the same type of output — motion area computes motion regardless of whether the signal came from eyes or ears)

**Retrieval prompt:** "In the Urbaniak V5/MT study, what specific control conditions established that the area was representing motion properties of word referents rather than abstract linguistic features? In Aggius-Vella, what task did blind participants perform, and how was egocentric spatial computation measured? What is the Park and Fine unified model's specific claim about what constrains cross-modal repurposing — is it connectivity, computational style, or both? In the Orsenigo gradient preservation study, what was the sample composition (how many blind, deaf, and typical participants), and which specific gradients were tested?"

**COMP-BED-CPL-01 `[COUNTERPOINT]`**

**Claim:** The evidence for computational generalism at higher cortical levels (COMP-CMP-HOP-01) does not extend cleanly to primary sensory cortex. Bedny (*Trends in Cognitive Sciences*, 2017\) and Saccone et al. (*Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience*, 2024\) have shown that V1 in congenitally blind individuals responds to language grammar, mathematical difficulty, and executive control — functions bearing no obvious resemblance to any visual computation. V1 becomes functionally coupled with fronto-parietal higher-cognitive networks, not sensory networks. Bedny proposes "cognitive pluripotency" — developing cortex can assume a wide range of cognitive functions far beyond its canonical role. Against this, Makin and Krakauer (*eLife*, 2023\) argue that all apparent reorganization is potentiation of pre-existing architecture with requisite computational capacity, not genuine functional transformation — they claim cortex at no point offers "structural opportunities for functional pluripotency." This is the field's central unresolved controversy. One finding that cuts against simple narratives in either direction: deaf auditory cortex preserves auditory responsiveness alongside visual reorganization (Kral et al., *Trends in Neurosciences*, 2023\) — cross-modal plasticity doesn't erase the original computation; the two coexist as separate, non-interacting populations within the same tissue. Saccone et al.'s "connectivity-constrained experience-dependent" account (2024) attempts a synthesis: repurposing is real but constrained by long-range connectivity patterns, not unbounded.

**Source:** Bedny, M. (2017). Evidence from blindness for a cognitively pluripotent cortex. *Trends in Cognitive Sciences*; Saccone, E.J. et al. (2024). \[V1 cognitive functions in blindness\]. *Developmental Cognitive Neuroscience*; Makin, T.R. & Krakauer, J.W. (2023). Against cortical reorganisation. *eLife*; Kral, A. et al. (2023). \[deaf auditory cortex preservation\]. *Trends in Neurosciences*.

**Relevant to:** Bennett bet (this is the one finding that could still break the parameterized generalism position — if V1 genuinely computes grammar, that's not parameter-tuning of a visual circuit, it's wholesale repurposing), modularity position "what would break HPAM" clause (evidence that different cortical regions use fundamentally different computational algorithms for personality-relevant information — the Bedny finding is the closest thing to this in the current literature, though it's at the bottom of the cortical hierarchy, not in association cortex where personality-relevant computation lives), hierarchy matters (even if V1 is pluripotent, association cortex — where Turn routing operates — preserves computational identity across modalities, so HPAM's architecture is not threatened by Bedny unless the finding extends up the hierarchy)

**Retrieval prompt:** "What specific tasks did Bedny and Saccone test in V1 of blind individuals — include the grammar task, the math task, and the executive control task? What was V1's connectivity profile in blind vs. sighted (which networks did it couple with)? What is Makin and Krakauer's specific mechanistic argument against reorganization — what do they mean by 'potentiation of pre-existing architecture,' and what evidence do they cite? In the Kral deaf auditory cortex finding, how was coexistence of auditory and visual responsiveness demonstrated — were the two response populations in the same tissue but different neurons?"

# PINKER, The Blank Slate

 Book abbreviation: BLS (Blank Slate). Author code: PIN. Here are seven cards — six COMP, one MASK.

Structural note: Pinker is HPAM’s strongest independent witness for parametric personality — he reaches the same conclusion as Bennett (individual differences are quantitative tuning within shared architecture) from opposite premises about what that architecture is. His explicit silence on calibration mechanisms marks the gap HPAM fills. Use him as corroboration, not foundation.

### COMP-PIN-BLS-01 \[FRAMING MOVE\]

Claim: Pinker argues that all healthy humans share a universal cognitive architecture — a “standard overall design” — and that individual differences represent quantitative variation within that design, not qualitative architectural differences. “People are qualitatively the same but may differ quantitatively.” He uses the analogy of parameters or switches (drawn from Universal Grammar) to explain how a shared architecture produces surface-level diversity: the same mental organs exist in everyone, but their settings differ. Einstein’s unusually shaped inferior parietal lobules are a “variation on the typical plan,” not a different blueprint. Nonstandard genes can produce nonstandard minds (genetic disorders), but these are deviations from the species design, not alternative designs.  
Relevant to: Go/Turn architecture (parametric variation as foundational assumption), HPAM theory metaphor (parameters within shared system), the Lottery View in Character Trap (Pinker’s framework supports “real differences” without fatalism)  
Retrieval prompt: “What does Pinker say in chapters 3 and 8 about quantitative versus qualitative variation in mental faculties? Include the Einstein brain example, the Universal Grammar parameter analogy, and his distinction between standard and nonstandard genes.”

### COMP-PIN-BLS-02 \[FRAMING MOVE\] \[HPAM SYNTHESIS\]

Claim: Pinker (strongest modularist) and Bennett (strongest generalist, via Mountcastle) disagree about what individual differences are parameterizing — Pinker says specialized modules with distinct computational procedures, Bennett says a general-purpose algorithm tuned by connectivity and input. But both converge on the claim that matters for HPAM: individual variation is parametric tuning within universal architecture, not qualitative architectural difference. This convergence means HPAM’s Go/Turn framework is robust to the modularity debate, because Go/Turn operates at the level of calibration, and both sides grant that calibration is where personality lives. The mapping to HPAM is Brian’s synthesis — neither author makes this convergence argument.  
Relevant to: Go/Turn architecture (robustness claim), HPAM modularity position (logged separately as Book Content), Bennett’s breakthroughs (calibration within stack)  
Retrieval prompt: “What does Pinker say about how genetic variation maps onto cognitive architecture — does he argue for parameter tuning within a shared design, or for qualitatively different architectures? Include the ‘parameters and switches’ language and any explicit statements about variation being quantitative.”

### COMP-PIN-BLS-03 \[MECHANISM\]

Claim: Even within his massive modularity framework, Pinker concedes that the mind contains domain-general “data-processing systems” that cut across content-specific modules: attention filtering, working memory, skill learning, rule storage and execution. He treats executive function (frontal lobes) as a supervisory attention system that receives information from the rest of the brain and executes decision rules to determine overall behavior, including overriding habits and primitive urges based on assessment of future consequences. He also accepts general intelligence (g factor) as a stable, partly heritable property linked to frontal-lobe gray matter, functioning alongside content-specific modules rather than replacing them. Pinker frames executive function not as a counterexample to modularity but as a specialized system for the specific job of internal struggle and behavioral control.  
Relevant to: Go stats (executive function as general-capacity system with energy costs), masking (override mechanism has a supervisor that spends energy to suppress default routing), the expensive-animal logic (supervisory override is metabolically expensive precisely because it fights calibrated defaults)  
Retrieval prompt: “What does Pinker say about domain-general cognitive processes — working memory, executive function, attention, and general intelligence? Include his description of the ‘supervisory attention system’ in the frontal lobes, his treatment of g factor as heritable, and how he reconciles these general systems with massive modularity.”

### COMP-PIN-BLS-04 \[EMPIRICAL\]

Claim: Pinker cites Mriganka Sur’s ferret rewiring experiments as evidence for intrinsic structural constraints on plasticity. Sur rerouted visual input to the auditory cortex of ferrets. The auditory cortex showed some capacity to process visual information — demonstrating plasticity — but could only do so within the limits of its existing intrinsic structure, producing far less precise visual processing than a normal visual cortex. Pinker uses this to argue against the “indefinitely malleable” view of the brain: the cortex takes advantage of its own activity to wire itself up, but the basic architecture and the toolbox of mechanisms to interconnect neurons are largely shaped by genes during prenatal development.  
Relevant to: Masking as metabolic cost (forced repurposing works but degrades — the auditory cortex can “do vision” badly, the way a masked introvert can “do extraversion” at a cost), Move 3 constraints (the organism you could become is bounded by the organism you already are), Go stats (degraded performance under forced repurposing implies extra energy spent for worse output)  
Retrieval prompt: “What does Pinker say about Mriganka Sur’s ferret rewiring experiments? Include the specific finding about auditory cortex processing visual input, Pinker’s interpretation regarding limits of plasticity, and his conclusion about genetic versus activity-dependent contributions to cortical organization.”

### COMP-PIN-BLS-05 \[MECHANISM\] \[FRAMING MOVE\]

Claim: Pinker argues that a blank-slate associationist architecture is computationally incapable of producing human behavior because “blank slates don’t do anything.” For a system to learn at all, it must possess complex pre-existing machinery to notice patterns and interpret information. He identifies specific computational problems generic networks cannot solve: generativity and recursion (infinite creative output from finite rules), compositionality (a complex thought is more than the sum of its parts — “cats chase mice” vs. “mice chase cats”), variable binding (applying rules to any member of a category, not just familiar instances), and the kind/individual distinction (ducks-in-general vs. a specific duck). Learnability theory proves that from a finite set of inputs, an infinite number of generalizations are possible — without innate constraints to restrict conclusions, a general-purpose system could never successfully acquire a culture or language.
