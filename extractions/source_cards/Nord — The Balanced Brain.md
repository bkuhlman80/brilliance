## CLUSTER 1: WANTING / LIKING ARCHITECTURE

*Structural note: Nord’s wanting/liking material is built on Berridge’s framework (hedonic hotspots, incentive salience) but Nord extends it into personality-level individual differences, not just pathological dissociation. The key HPAM contribution is threefold: (1) wanting and liking are neurochemically separable (dopamine vs. opioids/endocannabinoids), (2) they vary independently across healthy people as personality-level differences, and (3) their dissociation under pathological conditions maps onto Go stat architecture. BIO already has dopamine discussed extensively (Sapolsky, DeYoung, Nettle) but none of it formally separates wanting from liking at the neurochemical level. These cards fill that gap.*

---

**BIO-NOR-TBB-01 `[MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** “Wanting” (drive/motivation) and “liking” (hedonic capacity/pleasure) are dissociable brain systems with distinct neurochemical substrates. Wanting is primarily mediated by dopamine, which serves as a learning signal and motivational driver but is explicitly not a “pleasure chemical.” Liking is primarily mediated by the endogenous opioid system (endorphins) and endocannabinoids, which are responsible for subjective hedonic experience. If any chemical deserves the title of “pleasure chemical,” it is an opioid, not dopamine. The dissociation is demonstrated by cases where wanting operates without liking (rats self-stimulating to exhaustion without evidence of enjoyment; human patient B-19 compulsively pressing a stimulation button without reports of delight; mephedrone users continuing to take a drug that makes them sick) and liking operates without wanting (Parkinson’s patients who enjoy rewards presented to them but cannot generate the drive to seek rewards; depressed individuals with intact pleasure capacity but absent reward-seeking behavior).

**Relevant to:** Go stat architecture (wanting and liking as separable subcomponents of what HPAM treats as Enthusiasm), Vitality as dopamine gestalt (existing BIO Book Content — Nord’s data refines this: dopamine is Vitality’s *wanting* component; opioids are its *liking* component), BIO-SAP-ZEB-01 and ZEB-03 (Sapolsky’s dopamine depletion cards cover the wanting side; Nord adds the opioid liking side as independently variable), addiction architecture (late-stage addiction as wanting without liking), Flat mode phenomenology (drive present but hedonic capacity depleted \= compulsive narrowing; hedonic capacity present but drive depleted \= withdrawal and apathy)

**Retrieval prompt:** “What specific experiments does Nord cite for the wanting/liking dissociation? Include the Olds and Milner rat self-stimulation paradigm (lever press rates, neglect of food/water), patient B-19 details, the mephedrone undergraduates (specific side effects reported), and the Parkinson’s apathy data. Does he identify the specific opioid receptor subtypes involved in liking, and does he cite Berridge by name?”

**BIO-NOR-TBB-02 `[EMPIRICAL] [FRAMING MOVE]`**

**Claim:** Individual variation in the wanting/liking baseline ratio is a personality-level difference, not a pathological state. Because wanting and liking run on separate neurochemistry (dopamine vs. opioids/endocannabinoids), they can vary independently across healthy people. Nord describes a “wanting spectrum” controlled by dopamine differences: under normal circumstances, some people’s reward systems make them more prone to impulsively seek particular rewards. Similarly, having a negative interpretive bias (a tendency to interpret ambiguous information negatively) is not necessarily a disorder — Nord characterizes it as “just a different way of processing emotional information in the world, a bit like a personality trait,” noting that many people with such biases function without impairment. Genetic makeup influences receptor density and natural release levels of relevant neurochemicals — for example, the number of opioid receptors in the frontal cortex is directly associated with an individual’s potential for opioid-derived pleasure. Experiential history further edits these baselines through cumulative prediction-error learning.

**Relevant to:** Go stat baselines as population-level parameters (wanting/liking ratio varies across the healthy distribution, not just at pathological extremes), Enthusiasm stat definition (if wanting and liking are independent, Enthusiasm may need to specify which component it scales — or HPAM may need to acknowledge it blends both), SALMON instrument (does the Enthusiasm scale capture wanting, liking, or both?), existing BIO “Pharmacological Predictions” table (the wanting/liking dissociation predicts that amphetamines push wanting while opioids push liking — refines the “Vitality push” prediction for amphetamines)

## CLUSTER 2: PREDICTION ERRORS → ATTRACTOR STATES → V CALIBRATION

*Structural note: This cluster gives HPAM a learning mechanism for V calibration — how worldview solidifies from biological baselines through cumulative experience. BIO already has developmental calibration (ACM switch points, corresponsive principle, heritability trajectories) and pharmacological baselines (dopamine, serotonin). What’s missing is the learning algorithm itself: how does the system go from “initial sensitivity” to “entrenched worldview”? Nord’s prediction-error → attractor-state pathway fills that gap.*

---

**BIO-NOR-TBB-03 `[MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** Individual sensitivity to positive vs. negative prediction errors (the difference between expected and actual outcomes) shapes a person’s general model of the world as a good or bad place through a mechanism of cumulative learning. While prediction errors drive moment-by-moment fluctuations in happiness, cumulatively over many years of learning, small differences in how a brain responds to positive versus negative errors build an entrenched worldview. A “miscalibrated dopamine system” governing these prediction errors can originate from genetic differences, early-life stress, or biological changes like illness, creating a “skew” in the learning process. The accumulated worldview is learned, not innate — expectations are built from a multiplicity of experiences. The initial biological sensitivity acts as a filter on the learning process: a brain that under-responds to positive events will, over years, construct an entrenched worldview characterized by depression or anhedonia.

**Relevant to:** V calibration mechanism (the prediction-error → worldview pathway is the learning algorithm underneath V), BIO-ELL-ACM-01 through 03 (ACM provides the developmental windows; Nord provides the learning mechanism operating within those windows), BIO-NET-PER-06 (Nettle’s threshold model — prediction error sensitivity IS the threshold setting, and cumulative learning is how thresholds become stable worldviews), existing BIO “Corresponsive Principle” section (the prediction error skew creates experiences that confirm the skew — this IS the corresponsive feedback loop described at the neural level)

**Retrieval prompt:** “What specific evidence does Nord cite for the cumulative nature of prediction-error learning? Include any longitudinal data, the dopamine system miscalibration sources he identifies (genetic, early-life stress, illness), and whether he distinguishes between the speed at which positive vs. negative worldviews consolidate. Does he cite Rescorla-Wagner or any formal learning model, or is the mechanism described informally?”

---

**BIO-NOR-TBB-04 `[MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** Once a worldview is established, it functions as an “attractor state” — a stable, self-reinforcing system that pulls the person’s mental state back toward its baseline regardless of temporary changes. The gating mechanism operates through selective filtering: positive prediction errors (unexpectedly good outcomes) are discounted or contextualized (“I just got lucky”), while negative events are amplified and used as fuel to justify the existing model. This tilts the entire learning system, ensuring the initial bias is continuously reinforced rather than updated. The gating is implemented by a two-level neural hierarchy: the ventral striatum and lateral habenula generate raw prediction errors (bottom-up), while the medial prefrontal cortex hosts the world-model that decides which errors count (top-down). When the world-model is sufficiently “precise” (certain), it overrides the bottom-up signal — a person can have a good day but still believe life is miserable. The habenula specifically signals punishment prediction errors and suppresses reward-responsive dopamine cells; disruptions in this circuit produce a “catastrophic response to perceived failure.” Antidepressants work bottom-up by shifting perceptual bias in the amygdala; CBT works top-down by challenging the rules in the mPFC that gate incoming information.

**Relevant to:** V as evaluative gating layer (V hosts the world-model; Go stats produce the raw signal; V decides which signals update the model), the habenula as punishment-prediction-error circuit (new for BIO — not previously carded), the CBT vs. antidepressant distinction as top-down vs. bottom-up entry points into the same two-level system, BIO-DEY-NPE-01 (serotonin → Stability maps onto the mPFC gating layer; dopamine → Plasticity maps onto the prediction-error generation layer), why “just think positive” fails (the gating system is upstream of conscious evaluation — positive events get filtered before they can update the model)

**Retrieval prompt:** “What specific studies does Nord cite for the attractor state concept and the selective filtering of prediction errors? Include any experimental demonstrations of positive-event discounting in depressed individuals, the habenula’s role in punishment prediction errors (include the ‘catastrophic failure’ language), and the specific fMRI evidence for the mPFC hosting the world-model. Does he cite Carhart-Harris or Friston’s precision-weighting framework in the context of attractor states, or only in the psychedelics chapter?”

**BIO-NOR-TBB-05 `[MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** Hedonic adaptation — the reassertion of the happiness baseline after positive life changes — is driven by the same attractor-state logic that entraps negative worldviews. Mood functions as a self-reinforcing attractor state that pulls a person’s mental state back toward baseline regardless of fleeting external changes. When a new positive circumstance becomes the constant background of life, the brain’s prediction-error system stops signaling surprises, and the subjective sense of reward flattens. Interventions that genuinely shift the baseline include: regular exercise (associated with 43% fewer days of poor mental health per month, an effect larger than moving from low to middle income), psilocybin (sustained increases in wellbeing lasting at least 14 months in over half of participants), CBT (tools continue functioning after therapy ends because they change the world-model directly, unlike antidepressants which may only work while taken), deep brain stimulation (striking and sustained remission in half of patients at two-year follow-up), and sleep/diet (Mediterranean diet showed greater mental health improvement than social support groups).

**Relevant to:** V calibration runs in both directions (positive adaptation and negative entrenchment are the same process — the system stops signaling surprise once the new state becomes expected), the exercise number as concrete data for lifestyle-as-Go-input (43% fewer poor mental health days is a strong effect), CBT vs. antidepressant durability asymmetry (top-down changes to world-model persist; bottom-up perceptual shifts may not — this is a durability argument for V-level intervention over Go-level pharmacology), existing BIO “Metabolic Harshness” section (exercise and diet numbers add quantitative anchors)

## CLUSTER 3: PSYCHEDELICS / BELIEF DISRUPTION / REBUS

*Structural note: This cluster is entirely new territory for BIO. The REBUS model gives a specific mechanism for how rigid beliefs (high-precision attractor states) can be disrupted, which is the mirror image of the attractor-state entrenchment mechanism in Cluster 2\. For HPAM, the masking implications are the most architecturally important: if some belief rigidity is protective (masking that’s become structurally load-bearing), then disrupting it without providing replacement routing is dangerous. Nord’s own evidence for negative psychedelic outcomes supports this.*

---

**BIO-NOR-TBB-06 `[MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** Psilocybin disrupts rigid beliefs by relaxing the “precision” (confidence) the brain places in its existing models of the world, following the REBUS model (Relaxed Beliefs Under Psychedelics) proposed by Robin Carhart-Harris and Karl Friston. In depression, beliefs become mathematically “precise” and ingrained — the brain is so certain of its negative model that it discounts any positive information. Psilocybin induces a “sensory overload” of unpredictable incoming information while simultaneously relaxing the constraints on existing beliefs, weakening the brain’s confidence in its maladaptive models and making them susceptible to change. Psilocybin increases cognitive flexibility (the ability to adapt behavior to a changing environment) for at least four weeks after a single dose. A single high-dose psilocybin experience is rated among the top five “most personally meaningful” events of participants’ lives, and sustained increases in wellbeing and life satisfaction lasted at least 14 months in over half of study participants.

**Relevant to:** V rigidity as precision-weighting (Friston’s precision \= confidence in the world-model; high precision \+ wrong model \= bad V that resists updating), REBUS as V-disruption mechanism (psychedelics lower the model’s confidence, allowing prediction errors to update it), attractor-state escape (Cluster 2’s attractor states are what REBUS disrupts — the precision of the world-model is what maintains the attractor), masking implications (if psychedelics disrupt ALL high-precision beliefs including protective ones, then the mechanism is indiscriminate — see BC below), existing COMP “Reflective Mind” material (the Reflective Mind’s orientation is determined by V; if V precision is relaxed, Reflective Mind loses its anchor temporarily)

**Retrieval prompt:** “What specific evidence does Nord cite for the REBUS model? Include the fMRI vs. PET scan dissociation (decreased rest-state activity vs. globally increased activity), the cognitive flexibility duration data (is the 4-week finding from a specific RCT?), and the 14-month wellbeing finding (sample size, control condition, attrition). Does he distinguish between psilocybin’s acute pharmacological effects and the lasting cognitive changes?”

**BIO-NOR-TBB-07 `[COUNTERPOINT]`**

**Claim:** Psychedelics are not harmless. Patients in trials have experienced anxiety, headache, and nausea. Some have reported “long-lasting harms,” and clinical psychologists have treated individuals for distress or other mental health symptoms resulting from taking psilocybin in research studies. THC (a component of cannabis) can evoke a “psychosis-like state” including delusions and paranoia, and frequent consumption is associated with higher risk of developing psychosis. Whether disruption leads to positive recalibration or negative destabilization depends on several factors: the baseline state of the person’s brain circuits (pre-treatment activity levels determine who benefits and who does not), the availability of cognitive flexibility post-disruption, and the context of supportive therapy (most successful trials provide psychedelics within a therapeutic framework that helps challenge unhelpful beliefs while the drug makes the brain more sensitive to new information). If a person’s negative expectations are too “precise” (certain), they may discount positive surprises as random, preventing the model from recalibrating even after disruption.

**Relevant to:** Masking-as-protective-structure (if disrupting rigid beliefs can cause harm, this validates the argument that some rigidity is structurally load-bearing — you can’t strip masking away without providing replacement routing), the “supportive therapy” requirement as evidence for niche provision (the therapeutic context IS a niche that provides new routing options while old ones are destabilized), BIO-ELL-ACM-03 (ACM recalibration constraints — adult-stage profile reorganization is weak, and psychedelic disruption without developmental window support may explain negative outcomes), precision-too-high paradox (some people are so certain of their negative model that even disruption can’t update it — the gating survives the drug)

## CLUSTER 4: BODY-AS-INPUT (Interoception, Body-to-Brain, Chronic Pain)

---

**BIO-NOR-TBB-08 `[MECHANISM] [FRAMING MOVE]`**

**Claim:** Interoception — the sense of the body’s physiological condition — is not a direct readout of physical reality but an active construction where the brain uses past experiences and current context to infer the most likely cause of internal signals. Evidence for systematic misreading: (1) the “hanger” phenomenon, where the brain misattributes physiological hunger signals to emotional irritability based on external context; (2) the adrenaline “stooge” study, where participants injected with adrenaline misread their physical arousal as euphoria or rage depending on a confederate’s behavior; (3) chronic pain/hyperalgesia, where the brain sends pain signals after tissue has healed because pathways have become accustomed to expecting pain; (4) functional neurological disorder (FND), where the brain’s expectations generate real physical disabilities (paralysis, blindness) without structural cause — a “software problem”; (5) disgust avoidance linked to stomach rhythm, where pharmacologically restoring normal gastric rhythm eliminated automatic avoidance of disgusting images. The brain uses body signals to “gate” emotional processing, and those signals can be systematically wrong.

**Relevant to:** Go stat input accuracy (if the system that reads body state can be systematically biased, then Go stats are operating on potentially inaccurate self-monitoring — the organism’s fuel gauge can lie), Barrett’s constructed emotion framework (convergent — Nord arrives at construction from the body side, Barrett from the emotion side), interoception as trainable (mindfulness and CBT can recalibrate interoceptive processing — see TBB-09), BIO existing “Metabolic Substrate” section (allostatic allocation involves prediction about body state; if interoception is constructed, the predictions themselves become a variable)

**Retrieval prompt:** “What specific studies does Nord cite for each interoception example? Include the original Schachter-Singer adrenaline study details, the FND prevalence data, the domperidone-disgust study design (sample size, control condition), and whether he cites Lisa Feldman Barrett’s constructed emotion theory explicitly or arrives at the constructive account independently.”

---

**BIO-NOR-TBB-09 `[EMPIRICAL]`**

**Claim:** Individual differences in interoceptive accuracy are shaped by both physical and experiential factors, and accuracy is trainable rather than fixed. Physical differences: people with joint hypermobility are sixteen times more likely to suffer from panic disorders, because their connective tissue differences alter interoceptive signals and associated brain structures like the amygdala. In anorexia nervosa, the brain systematically misreads satiety as aversive rather than rewarding, reinforcing a cycle of starvation. Some people naturally experience more “noisy” or overlapping internal states, making them more likely to misattribute hunger to anger or eat in response to emotional upset. Trainability: mindfulness training tunes conscious responses to sensations, diminishing the salience of negative signals. CBT retrains the brain’s model of the body (e.g., recognizing a racing heart as a panic attack rather than a heart attack). Autobiographical memory specificity training helps patients retrieve more accurate past data to challenge misleading current body models. Because these expectations are learned, the brain’s “software” can be reconstructed through new experiences.

**Relevant to:** Go stat input variation (hypermobility → anxiety as a body-structure → brain-state → personality pathway — a purely physical individual difference producing a personality-relevant outcome), SALMON sensitivity (if interoceptive accuracy varies, self-report instruments are measuring a constructed signal, not raw capacity), masking (anorexia as interoceptive misreading that produces self-destructive masking — the person is masking hunger signals that are being read as threatening), BIO-ELL-DSE-01 (BSCT orchid/dandelion — interoceptive sensitivity may be a component of biological sensitivity to context)

**Retrieval prompt:** “What specific data does Nord cite for the hypermobility-panic link? Include the sample, the ‘sixteen times’ odds ratio, and the specific brain structure changes. What is the evidence base for interoceptive training effectiveness — include any RCT data for mindfulness, CBT, or memory specificity training on interoceptive accuracy outcomes.”

**BIO-NOR-TBB-10 `[MECHANISM] [EMPIRICAL]`**

**Claim:** Body-to-brain signaling operates through at least four specific causal pathways that directly alter brain function and mood: (1) **Cardiovascular (fear gate):** The timing of individual heartbeats controls emotion perception — participants detect fearful faces better and perceive them as more intense during cardiac systole (when the heart pumps blood) vs. diastole, with corresponding amygdala activation changes (Garfinkel & Critchley study). (2) **Gastric (disgust gate):** The stomach’s contraction rhythm causally drives disgust avoidance — pharmacologically restoring normal gastric rhythm with domperidone enabled participants to stop automatically avoiding disgusting images and to habituate to them. (3) **Immune (reward/punishment sensitivity shift):** Inflammatory stimuli (typhoid vaccine) causally shifted brain function — individuals became more sensitive to punishments and less sensitive to rewards, with changes in ventral striatum (reward processing) and insula (interoception). (4) **Gut microbiome:** In animal models, altering the microbiome causally changes anxiety-like behaviors; in humans, preliminary evidence shows prebiotics can alter cortisol responses and emotional bias. Nord treats these as roughly equivalent parallel channels that the brain integrates for homeostasis, with a “final common pathway” through the nervous system.

**Relevant to:** Go stats receive causal input from body systems (Go baselines are not sealed inside the skull — immune, cardiovascular, gut, and microbiome signals all shift Go stat operating parameters), existing BIO Immune System section (inflammation → reward/punishment sensitivity shift converges with cytokine-specific behavioral profiles already carded — Nord’s typhoid vaccine finding is a clean experimental demonstration of what the existing section describes mechanistically), new for BIO: cardiovascular gating, gastric rhythm, and gut microbiome as Go-input channels (zero existing coverage), the “parallel channels” framing (Nord doesn’t rank pathways — HPAM shouldn’t either, but should note that immune pathways have the strongest evidence base)

**BIO-NOR-TBB-11 `[MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** Chronic pain is maintained by a self-reinforcing loop where the brain’s predictive processing system generates pain signals that no longer require input from the body’s physical pain receptors (nociceptors). The mechanism involves neural circuitry shifts: brain regions involved in bodily awareness, attention, and emotion send descending signals to the spinal cord, generating pain that originates in the brain rather than in peripheral nerves. Over time, the brain “learns” to expect pain and becomes ultra-vigilant, monitoring for harm even at non-threatening levels. There is substantial anatomical overlap between brain regions disrupted in chronic pain and those disrupted in depression or anxiety, supported by shared physiological processes (particularly heightened inflammation). The relationship is bidirectional: chronic pain is a risk factor for depression, and depression significantly increases the likelihood of developing chronic pain. The loop can be broken through: expectation change (CBT helps patients re-appraise symptoms as less threatening), placebo-like remediative effects (altering high-level brain states like attention), short-term pharmacological resets (a localized steroid injection broke Nord’s personal chronic pain loop by stopping bottom-up signals long enough for the brain to stop monitoring), and exercise (triggers endogenous opioid release, increasing pain tolerance).

**Relevant to:** Withdrawal resource-tax architecture (chronic pain loop is structurally parallel — the brain monitors for threat, the monitoring itself is metabolically expensive, and the monitoring produces the signals that justify continued monitoring), predictive processing as a general mechanism for self-reinforcing loops (chronic pain, attractor states, hedonic adaptation, and masking all involve prediction → confirmation → strengthened prediction), shared circuitry with mood disorders (pain and depression aren’t comorbid by coincidence — they share hardware), the steroid-injection reset as analogous to niche repair (stopping the noxious input long enough lets the system recalibrate — same logic as removing someone from a mismatched niche)

**BIO-NOR-TBB-12 `[MECHANISM]`**

**Claim:** Antidepressants (specifically SSRIs) work through a “bottom-up” cognitive mechanism rather than correcting a chemical deficit. The timeline: SSRIs increase available serotonin after the very first dose, immediately altering how the brain processes emotional information — shifting the “emotional tipping point” so that even a single dose makes an individual more likely to recognize happiness in ambiguous faces and remember positive things, with immediate changes in amygdala response to emotional stimuli. These subtle perceptual shifts are not an immediate fix for mood; instead, the person begins interpreting daily social cues in a slightly more positive light, and these shifts must accumulate over several weeks into a fundamental change in the brain’s model of the world, experienced consciously as improved mood. SSRIs only work for approximately half of patients because depression can arise from many different biological systems; SSRIs specifically target emotion perception and bias (amygdala), so they are effective only for individuals whose depression is driven by those circuits. If a person shows a shift toward more positive cognitive bias after just one week of treatment, they are significantly more likely to show long-term mood recovery at eight weeks. Individual placebo sensitivity adds to the treatment response.
