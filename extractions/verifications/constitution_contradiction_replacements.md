## 1: Amygdala as Threat On/Off Switch

**Paper:** Li, H., et al. (2022). Neurotensin orchestrates valence assignment in the amygdala. *Nature*, 608, 586-592.

**Constitution claim:** "When the amygdala is active, the brain treats the arousal as danger; when it is quiet, the same arousal becomes fuel for growth." Load-bearing in both the appraisal claim (VIII) and the governance inversion narrative (XVII).

**Replacement proposed:** No.

The authors never name or critique a "threat-detector" model. They take the BLA's dual-valence nature as established background — the opening sentence presents the BLA as a *dual-valence associative learning structure*, not a threat detector:

> "During associative learning, the basolateral complex of the amygdala (BLA) forms associations with positive or negative outcomes." (Introduction, p. 1)

Their mechanistic answer to how the same neurons get routed toward reward vs. punishment: **neurotensin (NT) from the paraventricular thalamus (PVT) acts as a concentration-dependent gate** — high NT promotes reward encoding, low/decreased NT promotes punishment encoding. PVT-BLA:NT terminal calcium activity increases after reward learning and decreases after punishment learning.

Key implicit challenges to the "threat on/off switch" framing:

- **Same BLA neurons encode either valence** depending on neuropeptide context. Active amygdala does not equal danger.
- NT knockout doesn't just impair fear — it abolishes active behavioral strategies in *both* reward and punishment contexts, replacing them with passive responses.
- Dopamine "codes for 'absolute value'" (necessary for both valence directions but doesn't differentiate between them); NT is the signal that determines *which* valence.
- Fear conditioning appears as one of two symmetric assays throughout, not the amygdala's defining function.

From the Discussion:

> "NT neurons to the BLA provide a critical neuromodulatory signal modulating valence assignment in the BLA, which is distinct from other neuromodulators, such as dopamine." (Discussion, p. 6)

**Authors do not propose a replacement theory.** The empirical ammunition against "threat structure" is extremely strong, but the theoretical bridge from mechanism to framework is left for others. The paper's implicit position — that the amygdala is a flexible valence-assignment device, not a threat detector — is already the accepted view in affective neuroscience (citing Namburi 2015, Beyeler 2016), which makes the Constitution's on/off-switch framing outdated relative to the field it draws from.

## 2: "Zero Prediction Error = Zero Learning = No Escape"

**Paper:** Caradonna, S. G., et al. (2022). An allostatic epigenetic memory on chromatin footprints after double-hit acute stress. *Neurobiology of Stress*, 20, 100475.

**Constitution claim:** A perfectly predicted relief signal generates zero prediction error, therefore no update to the system. Computationally clean but biologically too clean.

**Replacement proposed:** No.

The authors never reference prediction error, Rescorla-Wagner, temporal difference learning, or any formal computational model. They operate entirely within a molecular/epigenetic and neuroendocrine vocabulary, using Bruce McEwen's "allostatic load / allostatic clock" metaphor as their conceptual scaffold.

**What they show:** Even a single acute stress event leaves persistent chromatin accessibility footprints — at steroid receptor binding sites (AR, GR, MR) in ventral hippocampus — that persist for at least 7 days, in the absence of observable behavioral change. These marks modify responses to future stressors.

Key quotes:

> "If the long-term effects of acute stress are weakly observable using behavioral measurements, where would the *memory* of the stress experience be located? We found that this *memory* was epigenetic." (Discussion, p. 8)

> "Acute stress leaves its hidden marks in the epigenome, only to be uncovered by a future stress exposure." (Conclusion, p. 9)

> "Yet epigenetic alterations left unchecked feed forward to impact neuronal architecture and circuits, leaving more lasting changes in our physiology and behavior." (Conclusion, p. 9)

The closest they come to a positive framework is the concept of **"allostatic epigenetic memory"** — acute stress leaves chromatin footprints that persist in the absence of behavioral change and "may manifest when a second challenge hits later in life."

On reversibility, they draw on McEwen: "While Bruce McEwen seemed to dispute *reversal*, he believed in *resilience*."

**Why this matters for the Constitution's claim:** The biological system registers and retains the event at an epigenetic level that is invisible to behavioral readout. A strict prediction-error framework that says "zero behavioral update = zero learning" would miss this entirely — the system has changed even when the output a PE model would track has not measurably shifted. **However, the authors themselves do not make this argument.** The inference from "chromatin marks persist regardless of predictability" to "therefore prediction-error accounts are incomplete" must be constructed externally. Caradonna et al. provide the biological existence proof but do not bridge to computational theory.

## 3: "Pruning Does Not Change What a Circuit Computes"

**Paper:** Bolton, J. L., et al. (2022). Early stress-induced impaired microglial pruning of excitatory synapses on immature CRH-expressing neurons provokes aberrant adult stress responses. *Cell Reports*, 38(13), 110600.

**Constitution claim:** Pruning does not change what a circuit computes — stated as an absolute.

**Replacement proposed:** No.

The authors never engage with any theory claiming computational invariance under pruning. They operate within a straightforward neurodevelopmental framework and treat the pruning-changes-function relationship as empirically established rather than theoretically contentious.

Their implicit model: **pruning sculpts circuit connectivity, and connectivity determines circuit function.** Pruning is constitutive of what the circuit becomes — not a cosmetic cleanup after computation is determined.

> "A key aspect of brain maturation involves the formation and sculpting of neuronal circuits that execute complex behaviors." (Introduction)

> "Aberrant microglial function is important because their selective pruning of synapses during development may be pivotal for the formation of refined, functional brain circuits." (Introduction)

**What they demonstrate:** ELS attenuates microglial engulfment of excitatory synapses on immature CRH-expressing hypothalamic neurons → excess excitatory synapses persist → prolonged ACTH release, aberrant behavioral threat responses in adulthood.

> "Microglia are critical for normal synapse pruning and the maturation of structural and functional connectivity of stress-regulating hypothalamic CRH+ neurons." (Discussion)

> "ELA provokes microglial dysfunction during development, which results in augmented excitatory synapses on PVN CRH+ neurons and consequent aberrant responses to future stress/threats." (Discussion)

The causal loop is closed by rescue experiments: selective chemogenetic activation of microglia during the ELA period normalized all indicators of aberrant circuit function — "providing a causal link between microglial function during development and lifelong stress reactivity."

**On whether normal pruning preserves computation:** The authors never frame normal pruning this way. Their language consistently treats pruning as *constitutive* of normal circuit function — circuits are formed *through* pruning, not preserved *despite* it. Notably, in control mice, artificially boosting microglial activity (increasing pruning beyond normal levels) also changed circuit function — producing delayed behavioral responses to threat. This suggests even in typical development, the *quantity* of pruning is tuning what the circuit does, not merely cleaning up after a computation that was already fixed.

**The Constitution's claim is partially defensible as a heuristic for normal-range pruning** (typical pruning may preserve the broad computational role of a circuit), but **fails as an absolute statement** — disrupted pruning demonstrably changes what circuits compute, and even normal pruning quantity appears to be a functional parameter, not a neutral operation.

## 4: MD Thalamus Cell Types for Signal Amplification vs. Noise Suppression (Conflicting Ratings)

**Papers:**
- Mukherjee, A., et al. (2021). Thalamic circuits for independent control of prefrontal signal and noise. *Nature*, 600, 100-104. (EF summary's "Paper 5.2" — rated green)
- Lam, N. H., et al. (2025). Prefrontal transthalamic uncertainty processing drives flexible switching. *Nature*, 637, 127-136. (Attention summary's "Paper 6" — rated red)

**Constitution claim:** MD thalamus works via "two cell-type channels — D2 for signal amplification, GRIK4 for noise filtering."

### Mukherjee et al. (2021) — the source of the two-cell-type model

**This paper *is* the framework; it does not replace anything.** It establishes:
- **MD_D2 neurons** target VIP+ interneurons in PFC → amplify prefrontal signals when task inputs are sparse (low signal).
- **MD_GRIK4 neurons** target PV+ interneurons in PFC → suppress prefrontal noise when inputs are dense but conflicting (high noise).

> "These different neural signals are carried by two genetically distinct thalamic projections." (Discussion, p. 103)

The cell types exist. The functional dissociation in their experimental context is well-supported. The EF summary's green rating is defensible as a report of what this paper shows.

### Lam et al. (2025) — implicit supersession

**Same lab (Halassa), overlapping authors, four years later.** Replacement is implicit, not explicit — they never say "the D2/GRIK4 model was wrong."

The reframing operates along three dimensions:

1. **From cell-type identity to functional selectivity:** Mukherjee defined MD populations by genetic markers (D2 vs. GRIK4). Lam defines them by what task variable they encode (conflict vs. context). The genetic labels disappear from the functional analysis entirely.

2. **From gain control to uncertainty decomposition:** Mukherjee framed MD as performing signal amplification and noise suppression. Lam frames MD as decomposing mixed cortical representations into separated uncertainty estimates (cueing uncertainty vs. rule uncertainty) — representations that are *mixed* in PFC but *separated* in MD.

3. **From single-area modulation to inter-areal routing:** Mukherjee studied MD's effect on a single cortical area (PL). Lam adds ACC and shows MD serves as a relay in an ACC → MD → PFC transthalamic pathway — "enabling cortico-cortical communication through a low-dimensional bottleneck."

> "A major conclusion from our work is that in contrast to frontal cortical areas, the MD thalamus shows a low-dimensional and demixed representation of task variables... the notion that the thalamus may demix cortical task variables is novel." (Discussion, p. 9)

### Resolving the conflicting ratings

The underlying biology likely stands — the D2 and GRIK4 cell types exist and have the connectivity Mukherjee describes. What has been superseded is the **functional labels**. The Constitution's characterization ("signal amplification" and "noise filtering") accurately represents Mukherjee 2021 but does not represent the Halassa lab's current thinking (Lam 2025), where the MD's role is **uncertainty decomposition** and **transthalamic routing** rather than two-channel gain control. The 2025 paper does not map its functional populations (MD_Conflict, MD_Context) onto the genetic cell types (D2, GRIK4).

The most defensible position: the cell types are real, but describing their *function* as "signal amplification vs. noise filtering" is the 2021 framing, not the 2025 framing. The Constitution should either cite the 2021 finding accurately as one level of description or update to the 2025 framing — not conflate them.

## 5: Alerting/Orienting/Executive as Separable Modules

**Papers:**
- Ghosh, S., & Maunsell, J. H. R. (2024). LC norepinephrine contributes to visual-spatial attention. *Neuron*, 112, 2231-2240.
- Grimm, C., et al. (2024). Tonic and burst-like LC stimulation distinctly shift network activity. *Nature Neuroscience*, 27, 2167-2177.
- Breton-Provencher, V., et al. (2022). Spatiotemporal dynamics of noradrenaline during learned behaviour. *Nature*, 606, 732-738.
- Laszlovszky, T., et al. (2020). Distinct synchronization, cortical coupling and behavioral function of two basal forebrain cholinergic neuron types. *Nature Neuroscience*, 23, 992-1003.

**Constitution claim:** Alerting, orienting, and executive attention are separable functions that can "operate simultaneously without competition" given sufficient capacity. Each maps onto a separable neuromodulatory system.

**This is the most architecturally consequential contradiction.** Four papers collectively dismantle the clean one-neuromodulator-per-function mapping from both the NE and ACh sides.

### Ghosh & Maunsell (2024) — NE does spatial selective attention, not just alerting

**Partial replacement.** LC-NE selectively enhances perceptual sensitivity to attended stimuli in a hemisphere-specific way. Optogenetic LC activation improves perceptual d' only contralaterally — not broadcast arousal.

> "Collectively, the results identify a distinct causal contribution of NE activity in mediating task-relevant selective sensory processing distinct from attention orienting and nonselective arousal." (Discussion)

They invoke the existing **GANE model** (Glutamate Amplifies Noradrenergic Effects): high glutamate release at attended stimuli creates localized NE hotspots. Alerting is acknowledged as real but shown to be *separable from* a spatially selective function. Intertrial LC spike rates did not differ by attentional hemifield — "contrary to what would have been predicted by hemisphere-specific arousal."

### Grimm et al. (2024) — NE is a dual-mode gain controller, not a single "alerting" dial

**Explicit replacement.** LC-NA as a **"dynamic gain controller along a cortical hierarchy"**: same nucleus, same stimulation intensity, but tonic vs. burst firing produce qualitatively different brain-wide maps.

> "Burst-like firing — typically related to salient stimuli that require sensory reorientation — preferentially activates 'lower-level' sensory processing, whereas tonic firing — related to sustained attention and task engagement — activates 'higher regions' tasked with more complex and abstract information processing." (Discussion)

Alerting is reframed as *one mode* within a dual-mode system. The system is a **mode switch** reconfiguring which cortical layers are activated, not a volume knob.

### Breton-Provencher et al. (2022) — NE has two concurrent functions with modular outputs

**Explicit replacement.** LC-NA performs **two concurrently encoded functions** — task execution (spatially targeted to motor cortex) and reinforcement encoding (broadcast after outcomes). Single LC neurons sort into functionally distinct clusters with different cortical targets.

> "LC-NA neurons form distinct groups with respect to encoding of action execution and positive reinforcement, whereas the negative reinforcement signal is globally encoded in LC-NA neurons." (Discussion)

> "Modular targeting of LC outputs thus enables diverse functions, whereby some noradrenaline signals are segregated among targets, whereas others are broadly distributed." (Discussion)

Arousal explicitly dismissed: "we measured the effect on behavioural performance as a function of pupil constriction and found no clear relationship, suggesting that the effects of LC-NA activity on task execution are independent of changes in arousal levels."

### Laszlovszky et al. (2020) — ACh is internally heterogeneous, not unitary "orienting"

**Explicit replacement — Model C.** They test three competing models of cholinergic heterogeneity and propose a hybrid:

- **BFCN_BURST** predicts response occurrence (arousal/engagement — closer to "alerting").
- **BFCN_REG** predicts response accuracy (selective detection — overlapping with "orienting" or "executive").

> "Our result suggests a third, more complex scenario... We propose that there are two basal forebrain cholinergic cell types... While BFCN_BURST and BFCN_REG are two separate cell types, the firing mode seems crucial to regulating slow and fast cholinergic modulation." (Discussion, p. 9)

The two types are anatomically segregated along the anterior-posterior basal forebrain axis, meaning different cortical regions receive different cholinergic "messages." Even within the bursting type, neurons switch between burst and Poisson-like modes depending on state.

### Collective implications for the Constitution

The clean mapping of alerting = NE, orienting = ACh, executive = DA is attacked from both directions simultaneously:

**Bottom-up (neuroscience):** Each neuromodulatory system is internally heterogeneous. NE does alerting AND selective spatial attention AND task execution AND reinforcement encoding, depending on firing mode, spatial targeting, and temporal context. ACh contains at least two functionally distinct cell types whose behavioral correlates cut across the ANT categories.

**The separability claim fails not because the functions don't exist, but because the neuromodulator-to-function mapping is many-to-many, not one-to-one.** Each neuromodulatory system simultaneously participates in operations assigned to multiple ANT categories. Describing them as separable modules that "operate without competition" obscures the internal heterogeneity that determines what each system actually does in a given context.

## 6: Stress Acceleration as Tidy Two-Channel System

**Papers:**
- Santiago, A. N., et al. (2018). Early-life stress reduces PV synapses and PNNs in the basolateral amygdala. *Journal of Comparative Neurology*, 526, 1647-1672.
- Gildawie, K. R., et al. (2020). Sex-specific effects of maternal separation on PV interneurons and PNNs in PFC and amygdala. *Neuroscience*, 428, 23-37.

**Constitution claim:** Threat-acceleration / deprivation-delay as a clean dichotomy for how early stress reshapes PV/PNN maturation.

**Replacement proposed:** Neither paper proposes a replacement framework. Neither cites or engages with the threat-acceleration / deprivation-delay dichotomy.

### Santiago et al. (2018)

**Stressor:** Scarcity-Adversity Model (SAM) — maternal maltreatment via insufficient bedding, PN8-12. This is a **threat/maltreatment** model.

**Key finding that violates the dichotomy:** A threat stressor *reduced* PNN intensity and PV-to-pyramidal synapses in BLA and *delayed* the ontogenetic rise of PV density. A "threat accelerates" model predicts the opposite.

The authors attribute the direction to **developmental timing relative to PV/PNN onset**:

> "SAM treatment ends just as BLA cells are beginning to express PV and PNN in normal ontogeny (PN12). This indicates that reductions... do not result simply from suppression of PV- or PNN-protein expression during the SAM treatment. Rather, the SAM effects... are the delayed manifestation of SAM, presumably resulting from perturbations in BLA circuitry that exist during SAM, which alter the developmental trajectory of the activity-dependent emergence of PV-synapses and PNNs." (Conclusions, p. 19)

The mechanism is activity-dependent maturation disrupted during a sensitive window — not a stressor-type-specific directional rule. Some effects were transient (PNN density normalized by weaning; PV intensity in posterior BLA normalized within 3 days), but synaptic reductions persisted.

No sex differences were found (sexes combined after ANOVA showed no main effect of sex).

### Gildawie et al. (2020)

**Stressor:** Maternal separation (MS) — 4 hours/day, PN2-20. Typically classified as a **deprivation/neglect** model.

**Key findings that violate the dichotomy:** The effects of a single stressor type go in *opposite directions* depending on region, sex, and age:
- **PFC:** MS *decreased* PNN density at P20 (both sexes) — consistent with "deprivation delays"
- **BLA:** MS had *no effect* on PNN density but *increased* PNN intensity in adult females — opposite direction from PFC
- **BLA:** MS *increased* PV density in adolescent males only — opposite to what "deprivation delays" would predict
- **PFC:** MS *increased* PV+PNN intensity in adult males only

> "Taken together, the present findings reveal sex-, age-, and region-specific effects of early life adversity on PNN and PV maturation." (Discussion, p. 31)

The same deprivation stressor produces delay-like effects in one region and acceleration-like effects in another, with different trajectories in males vs. females.

### Cross-paper synthesis

The two papers collectively demonstrate that the direction of PV/PNN change following early stress is determined by a complex interaction of at least:

1. **Brain region** (PFC and BLA respond in opposite directions to the same stressor)
2. **Sex** (males and females show different patterns, sometimes opposite)
3. **Developmental age at measurement** (effects at P20 may normalize or reverse by P40/P70)
4. **Developmental stage of PV/PNN in that region when stress occurs** (activity-dependent emergence can be disrupted only during sensitive windows)
5. **Measure type** (density vs. intensity can dissociate — structural integrity can be compromised without reducing number)

Santiago 2018 is particularly damaging: a *threat* stressor produces *delay* of PV/PNN maturation in BLA — the exact opposite of what threat-acceleration predicts. Gildawie 2020 adds that even within a single stressor type, regions and sexes diverge. No two-channel typology captures this.

Neither paper proposes what should replace the dichotomy. Their contribution is documenting the complexity that any replacement framework must accommodate.

## 7: Astrocyte Omission

**Paper:** Mederos, S., et al. (2021). GABAergic signaling to astrocytes in the prefrontal cortex sustains goal-directed behaviors. *Nature Neuroscience*, 24, 82-92.

**Constitution claim:** The thalamus "determines which cortical configurations sustain themselves" — with no role for glia in the hardware layer of executive function.

**Replacement proposed:** No — causal necessity demonstrated, but theoretical integration left to others.

The specific pathway: PV interneuron GABA → astrocyte GABA_B receptor → Ca²⁺ → glutamate release → mGluR1 activation at GABAergic terminals → enhanced IPSCs onto pyramidal cells.

> "While neurons have been associated with encoding task-specific responses, astrocytes have been largely ignored for high-demand computation tasks." (Discussion)

Three findings that challenge the neuron-only view:

1. **Removing GABA_B receptors from astrocytes alone** — leaving all neurons genetically unaltered — causes "widespread collapse in the neural coding critical for working memory and decision-making."

2. **Boosting PV interneuron activity directly (via ChR2) could not rescue the cognitive deficits** caused by astrocyte GABA_B receptor ablation. The neuronal pathway alone is insufficient.

3. Astrocytes function as necessary intermediaries in the inhibitory microcircuit that generates **gamma oscillations** — a core neural correlate of working memory.

The authors describe astrocytes as "cellular checkpoints for tuning the signal-to-noise ratio and help optimize relevant information in cortical networks" and as "a refined unit of communication in cortical networks that underlie the information coding that regulate decision-making actions."

The "contradicted by omission" label is apt. This is not a minor modulatory influence — it is a cell type whose absence causes collapse of the very computations the Constitution claims to explain. A document claiming to describe what sustains cortical computation while ignoring astrocytes is incomplete in a way that matters functionally.

## Cross-Set Comparison with Brilliance Contradiction Set 1

Several papers appear in both contradiction sets. Where the same paper is relevant to both a BRILLIANCE and a Constitution claim, the pattern holds: authors provide strong disconfirming evidence but rarely build replacement theories.

| Paper | Set 1 contradiction | Set 2 contradiction | Replacement status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Li et al. 2022 | Amygdala = threat (4) | Amygdala on/off switch (1) | No — same finding, same absence of replacement |
| Lam 2025 / Mukherjee 2021 | MD thalamus labels (3) | MD thalamus cell types (4) | Implicit supersession — same analysis applies |
| Ghosh, Grimm, Breton-Provencher | NE monolithic alerting (2) | Separable modules (5) | Yes — replacement models proposed for NE |
| Laszlovszky 2020 | ANT premise (10) | Separable modules (5) | Yes — Model C proposed for ACh |
| Mederos 2021 | Astrocyte omission (9) | Astrocyte omission (7) | No — same finding, same absence of replacement |

The papers that *do* propose replacements (NE and ACh heterogeneity) are the same papers providing replacements in both sets, suggesting these are the most theoretically mature lines of research. The amygdala, stress, and pruning literatures provide strong empirical correctives but remain framework-free — the theoretical architecture must be sourced elsewhere or constructed de novo.
