# Targeted Extraction: ACh → Orienting → Social Attention → Affiliative Extraversion

**Date:** 2026-03-27
**Purpose:** Extract specific evidence from 15 repo papers relevant to the five-step bridge argument

---

## Paper-by-Paper Findings

### 1 — Ariss et al. (2023)

**Question:** Did alcohol change any measure of visual attention to social partners?
**Found:** Yes. Alcohol increased gaze to self-view and decreased gaze to partner-view on video calls. "Relative to sober participants, those consuming alcohol spent more time looking at themselves and less at their conversation partners in these virtual interactions" (p. 11).
**Quote-worthy line:** None ideal — the finding goes the wrong direction for a simple "alcohol enhances social attention" story.
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Complicates — alcohol shifted overt gaze *away* from partners in this virtual context. However, the paradigm had a persistent self-view feed (unique to video chat), and the authors note overt gaze ≠ covert attention.

**Question:** Social context?
**Found:** Internet-based video chat (Zoom-style dyadic interaction). Crossed Alcohol × Friends vs. Strangers.

**Question:** Covert attention or only overt gaze?
**Found:** Only overt gaze (Pupil Labs head-mounted eye-tracking). Authors explicitly acknowledge the limitation: "self-focused attention might nonetheless manifest in a divergent manner across individuals and contexts."
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps — the overt/covert distinction leaves room for arguing covert orienting is the real mechanism.

**Question:** Did they test extraversion as a moderator?
**Found:** NOT FOUND. No personality variables examined.

**Question:** Limitations re: attention vs. gaze?
**Found:** Authors flag that gaze is not equivalent to attention and recommend "employing a multidimensional approach to capturing self-focused attention" in future work.

### 2 — Fairbairn & Sayette (2015)

**Question:** What measure of extraversion?
**Found:** Domain-level only — NEO-FFI (60-item short form, yields domains but not facets). Mean E = 32.11 (SD = 6.52).
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps — because only domain E was measured, the question of which facets drive the effect is entirely open.

**Question:** Mediation pathway?
**Found:** Alcohol → increases group-mates' Duchenne smiling (unmoderated by E) → Extraverts derive more reward from that smiling → enhanced positive mood and social bonding. E moderates the b-path (mediator → outcome), not the a-path (alcohol → mediator). Once social mediators are accounted for, the direct E × Alcohol interaction on mood is no longer significant.
**Quote-worthy line:** "Individuals high in extraversion appeared to associate greater reward with the Duchenne smiles that were displayed by their group-mates, while, in contrast, the relationship between group-mates' smiling and reported mood and social outcomes was not significant among individuals low in extraversion."
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps strongly — the mediation is about differential sensitivity to partners' social signals, which is exactly what an attention/detection mechanism could underlie.

**Question:** Affiliative vs. agentic E facets discussed?
**Found:** NOT FOUND. But they cite Ashton, Lee, & Paunonen (2002) — "What is the central feature of extraversion?: Social attention versus reward sensitivity" — in the references without discussing it in text.
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps — citing that title without resolving the question creates the exact gap the bridge fills.

**Question:** Attention, orienting, or detection as mechanism?
**Found:** Footnote 4 offers: "One potential explanation for increased associations between group-mates' smiling and self-reported mood among individuals high in extraversion is that group-mates' smiles are viewed as carrying more self-relevant information to these individuals." The discussion uses "sensitivity" without specifying whether this is perceptual, attentional, or purely evaluative.
**Quote-worthy line:** "Individuals high in extraversion were selectively sensitive to pleasurable social stimuli (partner Duchenne smile) and not to negative or neutral social signals."
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps strongly — "selectively sensitive to pleasurable social stimuli" is essentially a description of preferential orienting. The mechanism is left vague — the bridge argument fills it.

### 3 — Fairbairn & Kang (2025)

**Question:** What are the two pathways?
**Found:** (1) Immediate pleasurable stimuli pathway — alcohol narrows attention to immediate environmental cues, so contexts featuring immediate pleasurable stimuli generate potent reward. (2) Higher-order cognitive dampening pathway — alcohol mitigates negative emotions linked to self-awareness, future planning, divided attention.
**Quote-worthy line:** "Alcohol narrows attention to immediate stimuli, generating positive emotion in situations featuring salient pleasurable cues."
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps — Pathway 1 explicitly invokes attention narrowing, compatible with enhanced detection of social cues.

**Question:** Attention, orienting, or perceptual sensitivity invoked?
**Found:** Yes — Pathway 1: "alcohol has the tendency to impair attention, diminishing capacity for divided focus and thus narrowing attention to immediate environmental cues." But framed as general cognitive impairment, not enhanced orienting.
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps — they invoke attention narrowing but don't make the step to social cue detection. The bridge makes that step explicit.

**Question:** Affiliative vs. agentic E?
**Found:** NOT FOUND. Paper is about social context, not individual differences.

**Question:** ACh, nicotine, or cholinergic system?
**Found:** NOT FOUND.

**Question:** Neuromodulators discussed?
**Found:** Essentially none. The model is entirely cognitive/behavioral. No DA, 5-HT, GABA, or ACh discussed mechanistically.
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps — the leading social-cognitive model of alcohol reward has no neuromodulatory specification. The bridge fills this.

### 4 — Fairbairn & Sayette (2013)

**Question:** What is "emotional inertia"?
**Found:** High autocorrelation in affective states over time — the degree to which emotion at time t is predicted by emotion at t−1. Operationalized as first-order autoregressive coefficient of Duchenne smiling duration in successive 10-second intervals.

**Question:** Specific to positive or negative emotions?
**Found:** Both. Alcohol reduced inertia of Duchenne smiling, mediating effects on positive mood (z = 4.46, p < .0001), negative mood (z = −3.01, p = .002), and social bonding (z = 2.58, p = .009).

**Question:** Connected to alcohol myopia / attention narrowing?
**Found:** Yes — this is the central argument. "Alcohol may enhance mood by allowing individuals to freely enjoy the present and lose awareness of the past."
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps — temporal-attentional narrowing is the same family of processes as social-attentional narrowing. If alcohol narrows temporal attention, it plausibly narrows social attention toward the most salient immediate cue (the partner's face).

**Question:** Orienting, attentional capture, or sustained attention to partners?
**Found:** Not directly. Attention discussed only in AM theory context. Social interaction is the context but attention to partners is not measured or theorized.

### 5 — Goodwin & Sayette (2024)

**Question:** Overall effect size?
**Found:** Hedges' g = 0.164, 95% CI [0.027, 0.301], p = .019, k = 16 studies. Minimal heterogeneity (I² = 17.5%). Trim-and-fill estimate: g = 0.082 [−0.079, 0.243].
**Quote-worthy line:** "The weak magnitude of this effect, although surprising in light of considerable theoretical and anecdotal evidence, may thus be partially explained by the substantial noise in available data."
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps — there IS a significant effect, even if small. The small size is attributed to methodological noise, especially confederate paradigms.

**Question:** Definition of "affiliative verbal behavior"?
**Found:** Verbal content reflecting social approach tendency — self-disclosure, responsiveness to partner, MICS positivity, MICS problem-solving.

**Question:** Pharmacological vs. expectancy effects?
**Found:** "Minimal evidence of a difference" — most placebo vs. control comparisons showed no differences. Dosage set effects unlikely.

**Question:** Why smaller than mood effect?
**Found:** Heterogeneous methods, small samples, decades-old studies, conservative imputation. Critically: "Little support for an effect of alcohol in one-sided confederate interactions" — effects appear mainly in unscripted, reciprocal paradigms.
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps — the finding that effects depend on *reciprocal* social cues (not one-sided confederate scripts) is consistent with an attention/detection mechanism that requires dynamic social input.

**Question:** Moderators tested?
**Found:** Study quality tested (NS). Familiarity discussed narratively but not tested. Sex and personality NOT tested.
**Quote-worthy line:** "A major limitation of the extant literature is the inability to quantify when and for whom alcohol's effects on affiliative verbal behavior are most evident."

### 6 — Moore & Depue (2016)

**Question:** Full combinatorial formula?
**Found:** Page 138, Equation 1:

`[(DA + OP + OXT) : CRH × 1/(5-HT + NE + GABA)]`

Where: DA + OP + OXT = incentive-affiliative-positive emotionality; CRH = uncertainty-negative emotionality; 5-HT + NE + GABA = neural constraint.
**Quote-worthy line:** "the combination of these components would be represented by the threshold sensitivity values of the following components: (a) VTA DA + u-OP + OXT as an incentive-affiliative-positive emotionality component, (b) CRH as an uncertainty-negative emotionality component, and (c) 5-HT + NE + GABA as a neural constraint component."
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps strongly — the formula explicitly separates affiliative (OXT, OP) from agentic (DA) but the absence of ACh is conspicuous.

**Question:** Is acetylcholine mentioned ANYWHERE?
**Found:** NOT FOUND. Entire 58-page paper including both appendices and glossary — ACh, acetylcholine, and cholinergic do not appear.
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps strongly — confirms the Depue framework has a complete blind spot for cholinergic systems. This is the gap the bridge fills.

**Question:** Role of each neuromodulator?
**Found:** DA: incentive motivation, positive emotionality. Opiates (mu-opioid): consummatory reward, social bonding. OXT: social bonding alongside opiates. CRH: anxiety, negative emotionality. 5-HT: tonic inhibition, threshold modulation. NE: cue discrimination, attention/focus maintenance. GABA: emotional memory, network oscillations.

**Question:** Affiliative vs. agentic E?
**Found:** Model links E broadly to DA-mediated incentive motivation. Social Closeness and Agreeableness linked to OP + OXT bonding system. But affiliative and agentic E are collapsed into a single additive term (DA + OP + OXT), not formally separated.

**Question:** Attentional mechanisms?
**Found:** Yes, but exclusively through NE/LC. Entire section on tonic vs. phasic LC activity, Adaptive Gain theory, ventral/dorsal attention network shifting. "LC NE activity plays a dynamic role, enhancing the shift between the ventral fronto-parietal attention system that detects changes in salient information, and the dorsal fronto-parietal attention pathways."
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps — attention is engaged but assigned entirely to NE/LC. Cholinergic sustained selective orienting is completely missing.

**Question:** OXT's role?
**Found:** OXT appears only in the combinatorial formula and in reference to Depue & Morrone-Strupinsky (2005). No extended discussion of OXT mechanisms, sensory processing, or attention interaction. Treated as purely a reward/bonding molecule.

### 7 — Depue & Morrone-Strupinsky (2005) — BBS Target Article

**Question:** Is acetylcholine mentioned?
**Found:** YES — precisely once, section 6.3.3 (p. 340): "in the human brain OTRs are densely expressed by the basal forebrain acetylcholine (ACh) neurons in the nucleus basalis of Meynert and the diagonal band of Broca... Taken together, these ACh afferents are known to modulate memory systems in the amygdala, hippocampus, and neocortex, indicating that OT may modulate memory formation or consolidation through its influence on ACh function."
**Quote-worthy line:** "in the human brain OTRs are densely expressed by the basal forebrain acetylcholine (ACh) neurons in the nucleus basalis of Meynert and the diagonal band of Broca"
**Implication for the bridge argument:** HELPS ENORMOUSLY — Depue himself identified the OXT-ACh anatomical connection but interpreted it solely as a memory mechanism. He did not take the next step to recognize that the same NBM cholinergic projections mediate attentional orienting. This is precisely the undrawn line the bridge completes.

**Question:** Posner's orienting network or ANT mentioned?
**Found:** NOT FOUND. No formalized attentional network model appears anywhere.

**Question:** TPJ or STS mentioned?
**Found:** NOT FOUND in the target article or commentaries in connection with affiliative behavior.

**Question:** How are affiliative stimuli DETECTED (not just rewarded)?
**Found:** Two mechanisms, neither involving attentional orienting: (1) Medial extended amygdala (MXA) integrates affiliation-relevant sensory information. (2) Gonadal steroids play "permissive and/or facilitatory roles in sensitizing sensory, perceptual, and attentional processes involved in detecting and transmitting external affiliative stimuli." Detection is essentially treated as a given — stimuli arrive at the MXA and are then subject to reward processing.
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps enormously — detection is the biggest gap in the model. The front-end question of how the organism preferentially orients to affiliative stimuli is handled only by a hand-wave to the MXA and gonadal steroids.

**Question:** Do any commentaries raise attention/orienting as a missing piece?
**Found:** YES — two commentaries:
- **Behrendt** (pp. 350–351): "autistic individuals may lack adequate responsiveness to affiliative stimuli, and hence subsequent affiliative reward, because of deficits in (a) orienting visually to caregiver social cues, such as smiles and faces"
- **Levine** (pp. 356–357): "vasopressin is more related to the part of the process that focuses attention on relevant stimuli — for example, the opposite-sex vole with which the animal is forming a pair bond"
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps strongly — two commentators independently identified attention/orienting as relevant. Depue did not incorporate their observations.

**Question:** Perceptual component of affiliation?
**Found:** Depue acknowledges perception repeatedly but never develops it: "the influence of permissive and/or facilitatory factors, such as gonadal steroids, OT, and VP, on (1) sensory, perceptual, and attentional processing of affiliative stimuli and (2) formation of social memories." In the Authors' Response, "the core capacity to experience affiliative reward" is the "sine qua non of affiliative bonding" — centering the model on reward rather than perception.
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps — perception/attention is acknowledged as relevant but is an untheorized placeholder. The bridge theorizes it.

### 8 — Savidge & Bales (2023)

**Question:** Shared DA circuits between bonding and cognition?
**Found:** VTA-to-NAc (pair bond formation/maintenance + reward learning); VTA-to-PFC (bond-related behavioral flexibility + executive cognitive functions). Bonding alters D1/D2 receptor expression in NAc and PFC, affecting cognitive flexibility and working memory.

**Question:** NBM, basal forebrain, or cholinergic systems?
**Found:** NOT FOUND. Paper focuses exclusively on dopaminergic circuits.
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps — even a paper explicitly bridging bonding and cognition ignores the cholinergic attentional system, showing how deep this blind spot extends.

**Question:** Affiliative vs. agentic distinction?
**Found:** NOT FOUND.

**Question:** Main argument direction?
**Found:** Bonding-induced neuroplasticity in DA circuits produces cognitive changes as a consequence — "bonding modifies cognitive circuits."

**Question:** Attentional mechanisms in bonding?
**Found:** Attention mentioned briefly as one cognitive domain affected by bonding-related DA changes, but no formal attentional mechanism or network discussed.

### 9 — Slessor, Phillips, & Bull (2008)

**Question:** What social perception tasks decline?
**Found:** Both gaze detection (Study 1: detecting subtle eye-gaze aversion) and joint attention (Study 2: following gaze cues) decline with age. First study to investigate these basic social perceptual skills in aging.
**Quote-worthy line:** "The present study is the first to investigate age-related changes in the basic social perceptual skills involved in the detection of eye-gaze direction and the ability to follow the gaze of others in order to establish joint attention with them."

**Question:** Neural basis discussed?
**Found:** Yes. STS identified as key region: "between the ages of 40 and 87 a 24% decline in gray matter density of the STS has been reported" (citing Sowell et al., 2003). Also ventromedial PFC and superior frontal sulcus. TPJ not explicitly named.
**Quote-worthy line:** "Age-related impairments in eye-gaze perception and joint attention may be due to the beginnings of age-related deterioration in the STS and ventromedial prefrontal areas, key regions in the neural network responsible for gaze processing."
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps — directly links age-related STS decline to degraded social attention.

**Question:** Cholinergic decline connection?
**Found:** NOT FOUND. No neuromodulatory mechanisms discussed.

**Question:** Personality measured?
**Found:** NOT FOUND.

### 10 — Miller et al. (2022)

**Question:** Cholinergic system in LI circuit?
**Found:** NOT FOUND. Neurocircuitry discussion limited to NAc, hippocampus, amygdala, vmPFC. Pharmacology limited to DA, GABA, NMDA, antipsychotics.

**Question:** Neuromodulators in LI?
**Found:** Dopamine (primary — blockade in NAc shell restores LI), GABA, NMDA, L-type calcium channels, beta-catenin, endocannabinoids.

**Question:** Social learning connection?
**Found:** NOT FOUND. Exclusively fear conditioning paradigms.

**Question:** NAc overlap with social reward circuit?
**Found:** NAc (shell and core) central to LI. Same regions known to be central to social reward in other literatures, but this paper does not make the connection.
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Neutral — shared substrate is real but must be argued from other sources.

### 11 — Waters et al. (2003)

**Question:** Attentional bias as orienting?
**Found:** Not in Posner terms. Interpreted through incentive salience (Robinson & Berridge). They note the acute Stroop effect may represent "a sort of shock response that habituates with exposure" — closer to an orienting-like interpretation without using that language.

**Question:** Cholinergic/nicotinic mechanism?
**Found:** NOT FOUND. Despite using nicotine patches as a manipulation, no mechanistic discussion of cholinergic modulation of attention. Nicotine patch treated as motivational state manipulation. And: "patch treatment had no effect on RT Stroop effects."
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Hurts slightly — a paper that manipulates nicotine and measures attentional bias but doesn't discuss cholinergic attention represents a missed connection.

**Question:** Attention measure?
**Found:** Modified emotional Stroop task (color-naming latency for smoking vs. neutral words). Two measures: standard Stroop (mean RT difference) and acute Stroop (first subblock difference).
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Neutral — Stroop measures interference/distraction, not spatial orienting.

**Question:** Posner cited?
**Found:** NOT FOUND.

### 12 — Dolcos et al. (2020)

**Question:** Individual differences section?
**Found:** Yes — Section 4 (p. 573+) covers developmental differences, sex differences, and personality/clinical differences. Substantial treatment.
**Quote-worthy line:** "In the field of emotion processing, individual differences are the norm rather than the exception."

**Question:** Extraversion discussed?
**Found:** Once, in passing: "Other personality traits (extraversion, neuroticism) can also influence emotion processing and its interactions with executive functions (Canli, 2004; Forster et al., 2015; Rohr et al., 2015), but are not the focus of the present discussion" (p. 576). Section 4.3 covers only trait anxiety and trait depression.
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Hurts slightly — extraversion acknowledged as relevant but deliberately excluded, suggesting thin evidence base for E-attention links.

**Question:** Cholinergic system?
**Found:** NOT FOUND. No mention of ACh, cholinergic, or nicotinic anywhere in 43 pages.
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Hurts — a comprehensive emotion-attention review ignoring the cholinergic system suggests it's not integrated into mainstream models.

**Question:** TPJ, STS, or social attention?
**Found:** Yes. pSTS discussed in social cognition/body perception (Section 2.4). TPJ mentioned: "Emotional bodies recruit action processing areas including the premotor cortex and emotion-related areas including the temporo-parietal junction." Figure 1 lists "Attention to social information [pSTS/EBA, mPFC, AMY, N170/400]."
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps — confirms pSTS and TPJ recognized as components of social emotion-attention network.

**Question:** Attentional bias toward positive social stimuli?
**Found:** Discussed in aging (positivity effect: reduced processing of negative, increased engagement with positive), reward-attention capture (striatal), and depression (attend away from positive). No dedicated treatment of positive social attentional bias as a trait-level phenomenon.
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps modestly — positive attentional bias exists in the literature but is framed as age-related or clinical, not as a personality trait.

### 13 — Westbrook & Braver (2016)

**Question:** Two duties?
**Found:** (1) Functional modulation of working memory circuits (maintenance stability + specific flexibility for updating); (2) Value-learning and decision-making about effortful cognitive action.
**Quote-worthy line:** "DA does double duty in that it: (1) influences WM contents by functional modulation of WM circuits, and (2) supports value-learning and decision-making about effortful cognitive actions."

**Question:** DA and extraversion/personality?
**Found:** NOT FOUND. No personality discussion.

**Question:** PFC-specific or broader?
**Found:** Extends well beyond PFC — involves striatum (ventral and dorsal), ACC, OFC, midbrain DA system (VTA, SN).

**Question:** ACh, cholinergic, or orienting?
**Found:** NOT FOUND. NE mentioned briefly; ACh absent.

**Question:** What DA does vs. doesn't?
**Found:** DA does: translate incentive information into cognitive effort via both WM modulation and value-based action policies. DA does not: simply encode reward — phasic DA encodes effort-discounted pseudo-RPEs reflecting *net* incentive value (reward minus effort cost).
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps — "DA encodes net incentive value" rather than raw reward is a more sophisticated view that could refine DA-extraversion mapping to specifically agentic/executive constructs.

### 14 — Sayette et al. (2012)

**Question:** What were the 34.9 million frames coded for?
**Found:** FACS-coded facial expressions: AUs 6+12 for Duchenne smiles; AU 12 + AUs 14, 15, 23, or 24 for "smile controls"; AUs 9, 14, 15, 20 for negative affect. Also speech behavior: vocalizations, pauses, switching pauses, turns. 30 fps during drink-administration session (Minutes 13–36).

**Question:** Duchenne smiling as social attention or emotion?
**Found:** Purely emotional and social-bonding. "We assumed that co-occurring, mutually responsive smiling reflects shared affiliative intentions and positive affect among group members." No attentional mechanism discussed.

**Question:** What participants were attending to?
**Found:** NOT FOUND. Free-form social interaction; no structured stimuli, no gaze/attention measurement.

**Question:** Gaze, orienting, or attentional mechanisms?
**Found:** NOT FOUND.

**Question:** Personality measured?
**Found:** Personality measured at initial assessment but not analyzed. "Our work sets the stage for further research to evaluate potential associations between socioemotional responses to alcohol and individual differences in personality."
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps slightly — personality data exist for this sample but were never analyzed, representing a concrete opportunity.

### 15 — Westlin et al. (2023)

**Question:** Main argument against one-to-one mapping?
**Found:** Three challenged assumptions: (1) Localization — mental events arise from whole-brain distributed activity, not dedicated neural ensembles. (2) One-to-one mapping — mappings are many-to-one (degenerate). (3) Independence — a neuron's function depends on its neural context, not intrinsic properties.
**Quote-worthy line:** "Brain and behavior are linked by degenerate (i.e., many-to-one) mappings."

**Question:** Posner attention networks?
**Found:** Posner et al. (1988) cited as reference [2] — example of the localization assumption being challenged. Not discussed in detail. Attention mentioned as one phenomenon "better modeled as whole-brain events."

**Question:** Degeneracy discussed?
**Found:** Yes — central concept. Entire major section devoted to it. Defined as "the ability for many independent mechanisms to produce the same functional outcome."
**Quote-worthy line:** "Degeneracy of causal mechanisms is an organizing principle of virtually all biological domains."

**Question:** Could this explain the Schindler (2024) mismatch?
**Found:** Yes, directly. Their framework predicts exactly Schindler's pattern. "The relevance of any neuron's action potentials to a given psychological process is dependent on the other neurons it is interacting with" — nicotinic receptors could modulate orienting through *functional connectivity* rather than spatial co-localization. The ACC example: "a similar pattern of BOLD activity contributed to either an attentional function or a memory function, depending on the regions to which it was functionally connected."
**Implication for the bridge argument:** Helps strongly — provides the theoretical scaffolding for why robust functional pharmacology (nicotine → orienting) can coexist with no spatial receptor-network correspondence. One-to-one spatial mapping was the wrong expectation.

## Summary: What We Found

### Helps the bridge argument:

1. **Depue (2005) identified the OXT-ACh-NBM connection himself** — but interpreted it only as memory, not attention. The undrawn line from NBM cholinergic projections to attentional orienting is the core of the bridge. (Paper 7)

2. **Fairbairn & Sayette (2015) show extraverts are "selectively sensitive to pleasurable social stimuli"** — exactly the process the bridge argument specifies — but leave the mechanism vague. Only domain-level E measured; facet question entirely open. (Paper 2)

3. **Moore & Depue (2016) contains zero mentions of ACh in 58 pages.** The entire Depue framework assigns attention to NE/LC and treats OXT as purely a bonding molecule. The cholinergic attentional system is a complete blind spot. (Paper 6)

4. **Two BBS commentators (Behrendt, Levine) independently flagged attention/orienting as missing from Depue's model in 2005.** Depue did not incorporate their observations. (Paper 7)

5. **Westlin et al. (2023) degeneracy framework explains the Schindler (2024) spatial mismatch.** Nicotinic receptors modulate orienting through network-level interactions, not local concentration. One-to-one spatial mapping was the wrong expectation. (Paper 15)

6. **The social-cognitive alcohol model (Fairbairn & Kang 2025) invokes attention narrowing but has no neuromodulatory specification.** The bridge fills both the social-specificity gap and the neuromodulatory gap. (Paper 3)

7. **STS gray matter declines 24% between ages 40–87** (Slessor 2008), directly linking structural degradation to impaired gaze detection and joint attention. (Paper 9)

8. **Goodwin & Sayette (2024) find alcohol effects on affiliative behavior depend on reciprocal social interaction**, not one-sided confederate paradigms — consistent with a dynamic detection/orienting mechanism. (Paper 5)

### Hurts the bridge argument:

1. **Ariss et al. (2023): alcohol shifted overt gaze AWAY from partners.** The video-chat self-view is a confound, but the finding is not what a simple "alcohol enhances social attention" story predicts. (Paper 1)

2. **Dolcos et al. (2020): 43-page emotion-attention review with zero mention of cholinergic systems.** The cholinergic system is not integrated into mainstream emotion-attention models. (Paper 12)

3. **Waters et al. (2003): nicotine patch had no effect on attentional bias (RT Stroop) in smokers.** A paper manipulating nicotine and measuring attentional bias that finds no nicotinic modulation of bias. (Paper 11)

4. **Dolcos et al. (2020) explicitly excluded extraversion** from their individual-differences section, noting it "can also influence emotion processing" but is "not the focus." The evidence base for E-attention links is thinner than for anxiety-attention links. (Paper 12)

### Reveals a gap we didn't know about:

1. **Detection is the biggest gap in the Depue model.** The front-end question — how does the organism preferentially detect, orient toward, and attend to affiliative stimuli? — is acknowledged ("sensory, perceptual, and attentional processing") but never theorized. It is a placeholder in the most influential model of affiliative personality neuroscience. (Papers 6, 7)

2. **Every paper that could have discussed cholinergic mechanisms didn't** — including those that manipulate nicotine (Waters), study social attention (Ariss, Slessor), bridge bonding and cognition (Savidge & Bales), or review emotion-attention interactions (Dolcos). The cholinergic social-attention link is not just unconfirmed; it is invisible to the fields that should be testing it.

3. **The Fairbairn/Sayette group has personality data from their alcohol-social-interaction paradigms but has never analyzed facet-level E.** Sayette et al. (2012) collected personality but didn't analyze it. Fairbairn & Sayette (2015) used domain-level only. The data to test whether affiliative E facets drive the social-sensitivity effect may already exist.

4. **Westbrook & Braver (2016) refine DA as "effort-discounted net incentive value"** — not raw reward. This is more consistent with mapping DA to agentic/executive extraversion (effortful pursuit of goals) than to affiliative extraversion (detection/response to social cues). (Paper 13)

### Key quotes for the manuscript:

1. **Depue & Morrone-Strupinsky (2005), p. 340:** "in the human brain OTRs are densely expressed by the basal forebrain acetylcholine (ACh) neurons in the nucleus basalis of Meynert and the diagonal band of Broca"
*— Depue himself identified the OXT-ACh anatomical link but did not connect it to attention.*

2. **Fairbairn & Sayette (2015):** "Individuals high in extraversion were selectively sensitive to pleasurable social stimuli (partner Duchenne smile) and not to negative or neutral social signals."
*— The behavioral phenomenon the bridge argument explains.*

3. **Behrendt commentary in Depue (2005), pp. 350–351:** "autistic individuals may lack adequate responsiveness to affiliative stimuli, and hence subsequent affiliative reward, because of deficits in (a) orienting visually to caregiver social cues, such as smiles and faces"
*— Orienting deficit as upstream cause of affiliative reward failure, identified by a commentator but not incorporated into the model.*

4. **Westlin et al. (2023), p. 251:** "Degeneracy of causal mechanisms is an organizing principle of virtually all biological domains."
*— Theoretical foundation for why functional pharmacology (nicotine → orienting) coexists with no spatial receptor-network correspondence.*

5. **Slessor et al. (2008), p. 820:** "Age-related impairments in eye-gaze perception and joint attention may be due to the beginnings of age-related deterioration in the STS and ventromedial prefrontal areas, key regions in the neural network responsible for gaze processing."
*— Structural evidence linking social attention to specific neural substrates that degrade with age.*
