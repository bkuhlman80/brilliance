# Q3 Verification: Should We Flip the E/ES Mapping? (The Eysenck Provocation)

**Date:** 2026-03-27
**Status:** No flip warranted, but the reasoning is more nuanced than expected
**Verdict:** The current mapping (affiliative E → orienting, ES → alerting) holds, but the empirical basis is thinner than it should be. The critical test — facet-level extraversion × ANT alerting/orienting — has never been run.

---

## The Provocation

Eysenck argued extraverts have low tonic cortical arousal (ARAS), which sounds like an alerting story. CBT/anxiety research shows neuroticism as biased orienting toward threat, which sounds like an orienting story. This would reverse the current BRILLIANCE mapping (E = orienting, ES = alerting) to E = alerting, ES = orienting.

## The Resolution: Why We Decided Not to Flip

The conversation decided NOT to flip, for four reasons:
1. Eysenck's ARAS mechanism didn't replicate cleanly
2. His N was about limbic reactivity, not attention — the attentional-bias finding came from other researchers later
3. The behavioral case for affiliative E as social orienting is strong
4. The Freeman NBM anatomy supports E-as-orienting (OXT → ACh → orienting)

This extraction verifies each of those reasons against the primary literature and identifies what's actually known vs. assumed.

---

## Empirical Answers to the Three Direct Questions

### Q3a. Is there evidence comparing alerting vs. orienting efficiency with extraversion at the FACET level?

**No. This study does not exist.**

The closest study is Matthews & Zeidner (2012, *Personality and Individual Differences*, 53(5), 574-579; N=99), which used domain-level Big Five (Saucier Mini-Markers) with the ANT. Finding: superior executive control associated with extraversion. Alerting and orienting had weaker, non-significant predictor sets. But crucially, they used **domain-level E only** — cannot distinguish warmth/gregariousness from assertiveness/excitement-seeking.

### Q3b. Does the ANT literature break down Extraversion into facets?

**No. Every ANT-personality study uses domain-level measures only.**

Matthews & Zeidner (2012) describe their study as "the first to test for associations between the ANT and the Five Factor Model of personality." No subsequent study has used facet-level instruments (NEO-PI-R, BFI-2, BFAS) with the ANT. The ANT-personality intersection is surprisingly under-researched given both tools have been available for 20+ years.

### Q3c. Is there any study that simultaneously measures all three Posner networks and all Big Five facets?

**No. This gold-standard study does not exist.**

The question of whether E-facets differentially map onto alerting vs. orienting vs. executive attention **cannot be answered from existing data**. Any claims about which extraversion facet maps to which attention network are currently theoretical inferences, not empirical findings.

## Verification of the Four Reasons Not to Flip

### Reason 1: Eysenck's ARAS Didn't Replicate Cleanly

**Status: Confirmed — the theory was incrementally replaced, not cleanly disconfirmed.**

| Study | Finding | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Kumari et al. (2004, *J Neuroscience*) | Higher E predicted greater fMRI signal change from rest to demanding n-back in DLPFC/ACC; E negatively associated with resting thalamic signal | Partially supports: introverts show higher resting cortical activity |
| Wacker et al. (2010, *JRP*) | Frontal alpha asymmetry–E link "considerably weaker and less consistent than initially hoped"; posterior-frontal theta distribution was consistent | Supports arousal-related mechanism but not the simple alpha story |
| Kussner (2017, *Frontiers in Psychology*) | Review found "as much evidence in favor as against Eysenck's theory" | Inconclusive |

**Current consensus:** The original ARAS mechanism (simple tonic cortical arousal via reticular formation) is too crude. Some arousal-related effects exist but are regionally specific and frequency-band specific. The field has moved to reward-sensitivity/dopaminergic accounts of extraversion (Gray/RST, Smillie 2013, DeYoung 2013, Wacker & Smillie 2015) rather than the Eysenckian ARAS model. Nobody has cleanly disconfirmed the arousal hypothesis — it has been incrementally replaced by more specific models.

**Implication for the flip:** The Eysenck provocation (E = low alerting tone = alerting story) rests on a mechanism that the field no longer considers primary. It hasn't been disproven, but it's not the leading account. This weakens but does not eliminate the case for flipping.

### Reason 2: Eysenck's N Was Limbic Reactivity, Not Attention

**Status: Confirmed and strengthened — the threat-bias finding is NOT about orienting.**

This is one of the most important findings in this verification:

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| Fox, Russo, & Dutton (2002, *Cognition and Emotion*) | High trait-anxiety shows **difficulty disengaging** from threat, not facilitated orienting toward it |
| Cisler & Koster (2010, *Clinical Psychology Review*) | Three distinct mechanisms: (1) facilitated attention (automatic, amygdala-mediated, <100ms — may involve orienting), (2) **difficulty disengaging** (most consistently observed; executive control failure), (3) attentional avoidance (strategic, PFC-mediated, >1250ms) |
| Derryberry & Reed (2002, *J Abnormal Psychology*) | Anxiety-related threat bias moderated by attentional control: anxious people with good executive control shift away from threat; those with poor control cannot |

**The attentional bias to threat in neuroticism/anxiety is primarily about disengagement failure (executive control), not about the orienting network.** The facilitated-capture component (which could be orienting) is brief, automatic, and amygdala-mediated — it's the least consistently measured and least dominant of the three mechanisms.

**Implication for the flip:** The Eysenck provocation assumed N-threat-bias = orienting. It doesn't. The dominant mechanism is executive control failure. This removes the strongest motivation for mapping ES (emotional stability / neuroticism) to orienting. The current mapping (ES → alerting) is not threatened by the threat-bias literature.

### Reason 3: The Behavioral Case for Affiliative E as Social Orienting Is Strong

**Status: Conceptually strong, empirically untested at the attention-network level.**

The Corbetta (2008) "circuit breaker" framework and Decety & Lamm (2007) "building block" framework both support the theoretical claim that social cognition recruits orienting machinery. The Krall (2015) ALE meta-analysis confirms anterior rTPJ overlap between attention reorienting and false belief tasks. This is all documented in the Q2 extraction.

However: **no study directly tests whether affiliative extraversion (warmth, gregariousness) is associated with orienting-network efficiency on the ANT or Posner cueing tasks.** The behavioral case is built on the plausibility chain: affiliative E → social attention → rTPJ → orienting network. Each link has support, but the chain has never been tested end-to-end.

### Reason 4: The Freeman NBM Anatomy Supports E-as-Orienting

**Status: Confirmed via Q1, with the critical nuance that ACh → orienting is pharmacologically supported.**

The Posner framework assigns ACh to the orienting network. Pharmacological evidence is strong and consistent:

| Study | Finding |
|---|---|
| Thiel, Zilles, & Fink (2005, *Neuropsychopharmacology*) | Nicotine sped RTs on invalidly cued trials (reorienting) but "alerting was not affected by nicotine" |
| Vossel, Thiel, & Fink (2008, *Neuropsychopharmacology*) | Nicotine reduced validity effect in 90% cue validity condition |
| Vossel, Thiel, & Fink (2008, *Neuroscience*) | Nicotine reduced validity effect and reorienting-related activity in right inferior parietal cortex |
| Giessing et al. (2006, *IJNP*) | Mecamylamine (nicotinic antagonist) down-regulated brain activation associated with orienting |
| Raz et al. (2005/2008) | Alpha-4 nicotinic receptor polymorphism associated with attentional scaling; no association for dopamine beta hydroxylase gene |
| Petersen & Posner (2012, *Annual Review of Neuroscience*) | Review states: "drugs influencing the neuromodulator acetylcholine affect orienting but not alerting" |

**However, a recent challenge:** Schindler et al. (2024, PMC10893969) used molecular imaging (PET receptor maps from 1,239 participants + Allen Brain Atlas) and found **no evidence for the suggested neuromodulatory separation for any of the three attention networks.** Nicotinic receptor (α4β2) availability did NOT correlate with orienting network activation spatially. The behavioral effects may operate through indirect modulation rather than direct receptor expression in orienting nodes.

**Implication:** The behavioral pharmacology (nicotine → orienting) is solid and consistent. The molecular imaging challenges whether this reflects tight spatial correspondence between cholinergic receptors and orienting structures. But for the BRILLIANCE architecture, what matters is the functional effect: ACh functionally modulates orienting, regardless of whether the spatial correspondence is perfect. The Freeman NBM finding (OXT → ACh neurons → cortex/amygdala) still supports the chain OXT → ACh → orienting → affiliative behavior.

## The Neuromodulator-Network Mapping: Current Status

| Network | Classical Modulator | Behavioral Evidence | Molecular Challenge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Alerting** | Norepinephrine (NE) | Moderate (LC stimulation increases alerting) | Schindler 2024: NE transporter did not correlate with alerting network spatially |
| **Orienting** | Acetylcholine (ACh) | **Strong** (nicotine consistently improves reorienting; mecamylamine impairs it) | Schindler 2024: nicotinic receptor availability did not correlate with orienting network spatially |
| **Executive** | Dopamine (DA) | Moderate (dopaminergic effects on conflict resolution) | Schindler 2024: DA receptors did not correlate with executive network spatially |

The behavioral pharmacology dissociations are much cleaner than the molecular imaging suggests. This may mean the neuromodulators act on network hubs (like NBM for ACh) rather than being uniformly distributed across network nodes — which is actually consistent with the Freeman NBM story.

## Risk Assessment

| Element | Evidence | Risk to current mapping |
|---|---|---|
| Eysenck ARAS → E = alerting | Theory incrementally replaced by DA/reward accounts | **Low** — the provocation rests on a superseded model |
| N-threat-bias → ES = orienting | Dominant mechanism is disengagement failure (executive), not orienting | **Low** — removes the main motivation for flipping ES to orienting |
| ACh → orienting (functional) | Strong, consistent behavioral pharmacology | **Low** — supports E-as-orienting via the OXT→ACh chain |
| ACh → orienting (anatomical) | Challenged by Schindler et al. molecular imaging | **Medium** — but NBM hub story is actually consistent |
| Facet-level E × attention networks | **No data exists** | **HIGH** — the entire mapping is theoretical at this level |
| Behavioral case (affiliative E → social orienting) | Plausible but never tested end-to-end | **Medium** — each link supported, chain untested |

## Summary Judgment

**The current mapping (E → orienting, ES → alerting) should be retained**, for the reasons identified in the original conversation. The verification strengthens the case in two ways:

1. **The N-threat-bias is NOT about orienting.** It's primarily about disengagement failure (executive control). This eliminates the strongest empirical motivation for mapping ES to orienting.

2. **The ACh → orienting pharmacology is robust.** Five independent pharmacological studies consistently show nicotine modulates orienting but not alerting. This supports the OXT → ACh → orienting → affiliative E chain.

But the verification also reveals a critical vulnerability:

3. **No facet-level ANT study exists.** The mapping of specific E-facets (warmth, gregariousness vs. assertiveness, excitement-seeking) to specific attention networks is entirely theoretical. Matthews & Zeidner (2012) is the only ANT-personality study, and it used domain-level E only. This is a genuinely empty empirical space.

**For the BRILLIANCE architecture:** The mapping holds as the best-supported theoretical framework, but it should be acknowledged as a prediction rather than an established finding. The critical test — ANT × BFI-2 facets or ANT × BFAS aspects — has never been run. This represents both a vulnerability and an opportunity: it could be proposed as a direct empirical test of the architecture.
