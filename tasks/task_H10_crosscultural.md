# Task H10: Cross-Cultural Genetic Congruence Is Highest for Primary-Core Facets

## Reference
See `bright_triad_reference.md` for the model description and zone assignments.

## Hypothesis
Facets in the primary cores (ES and E circles, no overlap) should have the most stable genetic factor loadings across countries/samples. Intersection-zone, center-triangle, and peripheral facets should show more cross-cultural variability. The logic: biological primaries should be more genetically universal, while intersection constructs may be more environmentally/culturally shaped.

## Prediction
Cross-cultural genetic loading stability: Primary cores > Intersection zones > Center triangle / Edge / Outside

## Data needed
Genetic factor loadings for NEO-PI-R facets reported across multiple countries or samples within the same study. Look for:
- Studies that ran genetic Big Five factor analyses in two or more countries and report per-facet loadings in each
- Domain-level cross-national genetic congruence coefficients
- Any facet where the genetic loading shifts substantially across samples (e.g., a facet that loads .65 on its domain in one country but .37 in another)

The key data is VARIABILITY of loadings across samples, not the loadings themselves.

## Analysis
1. For each facet where cross-cultural genetic loading data is available, compute the range (max loading − min loading across countries) or note the degree of variability.
2. Assign each facet to its Bright Triad zone.
3. Compare: which zones have the most stable loadings? Which have the most variable?
4. Also check domain-level congruence coefficients: are N and E (the two primary domains) more cross-culturally stable than C, O, and A (the three intersection domains)?

## Output
- Per-facet table: facet, zone, loading in each country/sample, range or variability index
- Identification of the most cross-culturally unstable facets and their zone assignments
- Zone-level summary: which zones are most/least stable?
- Domain-level congruence check (if available)
- Verdict: Supported / Partially supported / Not supported / Untestable
- Caveats (number of countries available, sample differences between countries)
