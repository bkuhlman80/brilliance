# Task H2a: Facet-Specific Genetic Variance by Bright Triad Zone

## Reference
See `bright_triad_reference.md` for the model description and zone assignments.

## Hypothesis
Center-triangle facets (Brilliance zone) have the LEAST facet-specific genetic variance of any zone, because their genetic variance is distributed across multiple domain genetic factors rather than concentrated in one domain.

## Prediction
Mean facet-specific genetic variance: Center triangle < Intersection zones < Primary cores

The logic: primary-core facets (e.g., N1, N3, E2) should have genetic variance concentrated within their home domain. Center-triangle facets (e.g., E3, A3) should have their variance spread across multiple domains, leaving less that's specific to any one.

## Data needed
Facet-specific genetic variance estimates from multivariate genetic analyses of NEO-PI-R facets. This is the proportion of a facet's genetic variance that is NOT shared with other facets in its domain — it's what's left after extracting common within-domain genetic factors.

Look for:
- Multivariate within-domain genetic factor analyses that report facet-specific (residual) genetic variance
- Any study reporting what percentage of a facet's heritability is shared vs. specific

This is distinct from univariate heritability (H1). A facet can be highly heritable but have low specific variance if most of its heritability is shared with domain-mates.

## Analysis
1. Build a table: one row per facet (for all facets where this data is available), columns for facet-specific genetic variance estimate and Bright Triad zone.
2. Compute zone means.
3. Compare: does the predicted ordering hold?
4. Note which facets have data vs. which are missing.

## Output
- Data table with all extracted facet-specific variance values and zone assignments
- Zone means
- Data coverage: how many of the 30 facets have this estimate?
- Verdict: Supported / Partially supported / Not supported / Untestable
