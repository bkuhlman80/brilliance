# Task H8: Center-Triangle Facets Predict Life Satisfaction More Strongly Than Peripheral Facets

## Reference
See `bright_triad_reference.md` for the model description and zone assignments.

## Hypothesis
Center-triangle facets and aspects (Brilliance zone) should be among the top predictors of life satisfaction. Edge and outside-circle facets should predict less strongly. The logic: Brilliance represents having the full mental phenotype architecture online and integrated, which should be the strongest predictor of well-being.

## Prediction
Mean life-satisfaction correlation: Center triangle ≥ Intersection zones > Primary cores > Edge/outside

## Data needed
Correlations between life satisfaction (or subjective well-being) and personality at the facet or aspect level. Look for:
- Facet-level life satisfaction correlations from twin studies or large surveys
- Aspect-level life satisfaction correlations (e.g., from BFAS studies)
- Any facet-level well-being prediction data

Note: life satisfaction data at the facet level is relatively rare. Aspect-level data (10 BFAS aspects) is more common. Use whatever granularity is available.

## Analysis
1. Collect all available facet- or aspect-level life satisfaction correlations.
2. Map them to Bright Triad zones (the mapping from BFAS aspects to zones: Enthusiasm → E core area; Assertiveness → Center triangle; Compassion → Center triangle area; Politeness → ES∩E zone; Industriousness → I∩ES zone; Orderliness → I∩ES zone; Intellect → I edge; Openness → I∩E zone; Volatility → ES core; Withdrawal → ES core).
3. Compute zone means.
4. Compare against prediction.

## Output
- Table of all available life satisfaction correlations with zone assignments
- Zone means
- Verdict: Supported / Partially supported / Not supported / Untestable
- Note that aspect-level data (if that's what's available) provides only an approximate zone mapping
