# A Brief History of Personality — the object, the record, the silence

*Plan v1.1. A sequence, not a schedule. The only true deadline is that everything in
Phases 1–3 must be finished and dormant before the novel is announced.*

*Changes from v1.0: the Phase 0 note on the text is stale and corrected — the apparatus is
stripped and the manuscript is assembled. The terminal edge is corrected: v1.0 described it as
the framework's forward pointer to a second volume on disorder, which was the plan and is not
what was written. Typesetting added to the physical spec as an explicit gap and to open items.
The stylometry audit added as a pre-print task, inherited from the retired companion brief.
The open item on where the text stops is closed.*

---

## The three standing rules

Everything below is negotiable except these.

1. **Never assert a falsehood about the object.** Not to a journalist, not to a reader, not
   as a joke, not to a bookseller. Silence is unlimited and free. One lie converts a
   literary posture into a deception, and if it surfaces the story becomes the deception.
2. **Invent no person.** No pen name, no fabricated author, no sock accounts, no
   in-character emails, no puzzle apparatus. You are declining to sign a book. You are not
   running a game.
3. **The novel never points at the object.** No QR code, no URL, no author's note, no back
   matter. The novel describes a book precisely enough to be recognized, and stops.

---

## Phase 0 — Decide, before spending a dollar

Two rulings needed up front, because they change the front matter and the paperwork and
cannot be retrofitted cleanly.

**Is the anonymity permanent, or a held card?** If held, the copyright registration is set
up now to allow a later claim, and you should be at peace with a reveal that may never
come. If permanent, the theory's entire public life is downstream of the novel's success.

**Are you at peace with being found out early?** A bookseller could say publicly in 2031
that you handed him copies in 2027. If that outcome is survivable — and it is, because
nothing false was ever said — proceed. If it isn't, don't start.

**On the text.** *Corrected from v1.0, which said the text was not ready and still full of
project apparatus.* The apparatus is stripped and the manuscript is assembled at roughly
83,342 words. What remains is the voice pass — two hundred pages rewritten in the author's
voice — and that is tracked in the voice-pass kickoff, not here. The prose may stay strange and
forbidding. It may not look like something nobody edited for a reader.

## Phase 1 — Make the object

**The text.** *Corrected from v1.0.* The book stops where the thinking stopped. It ends in the
**tax** material, on a complete sentence, with a difficulty left unresolved. It does **not**
end on the framework's old forward pointer to a second volume on disorder — that was the plan
of record in v1.0 and it is not what was written. Do not restore it, and do not manufacture a
break or paper one over. The edge is real and it is already on the page.

**Keep the epistemic notes.** They are the best thing in the document and they are what makes
an unsigned book unclassifiable rather than merely crankish. *Their voice and their properties
are governed by the Smirker document; which of the fifty-eight survive is a structural decision
and is not yet recovered.*

**The degradation, done by you before any vendor sees it.** Print, scan, print, scan. Two
or three generations until the artifacts are inseparable from the page. The coffee ring
goes in at generation one so it survives into the grain.

**Physical spec — hold every line of this:**

| | |
|---|---|
| Trim | 5.5 × 8.5 (half-letter). **Not** 6 × 9 — that is the modern self-pub tell. |
| Stock | Uncoated offset, 50–60#, cream or natural. Never bright white. |
| Cover | Plain card, one ink, **no lamination**. Lamination is the loudest modern tell. |
| Ink | Black only, throughout. |
| Binding | Perfect bound (glued spine). |
| Run | ~40. The printer's note says the number; the number is then true forever. |
| Front matter | No author, no bio, no acknowledgments. A printer's note, a date, a place. |
| Type size, leading, margins | **Not specified. Genuinely undecided.** See open items. |

**The typesetting gap is real and it is the reason page count has been unanswerable.** The spec
above fixes six things and is silent on the three that determine how long the book is. At
83,342 words the difference between plausible settings is roughly 200 pages and 280 — which
changes spine width, which changes the cover, which changes the bindery quote. Two figures have
already been in circulation from a single assembly report. Decide it with the vendors; do not
decide it in the manuscript.

**Split the work between two vendors.** A copy shop runs the text block; a bindery does the
binding and cover. This is how a cheap private edition actually got made, and the
small misregistrations it produces cannot be faked by a POD file.

**Vendors (SLC):**

- **Perfect Register** — 1850 S 900 W. Bindery; took over Schaffer Bindery. A customer
  review describes short-run binding for a private publication. Call first.
- **Original Copy And Print** — 2815 Highland Dr. Independent job shop; runs technical
  manuals and user guides. The text-block half.
- **Scrub Oak Bindery** — by appointment. Not for the run. A working conservator and a real
  node in the SLC book world. Worth having met before you need anything.
- **Avoid:** Firewire, Book Printers of Utah (self-publishing service companies — they will
  do everything well that you need done badly). Avoid FedEx/Office Depot.

**Get "print exactly as supplied, no correction" onto the written quote.** A good shop's
instinct is to fix what you spent weeks degrading. **Name the double spaces after periods
explicitly on the quote.** They are a 1951 typewriter habit and they run through the whole
manuscript; they are also the single most likely thing to be silently cleaned up by any shop,
any word processor, and any typesetter between here and the press.

**What you tell the vendors:** the truth, in its plainest form. A small private edition of
an anonymous text, forty copies, cheap, exactly as supplied. Nobody will blink. Inventing a
story here is the only move that could hurt you.

**Before the degradation pass — two audits.**

1. **The citation ceiling.** Nothing published after 2017 anywhere in the manuscript. This is
   the only date evidence in the object and the only place it can contradict its own record.
2. **The stylometry audit, against Brian's natural prose.** *Inherited from the retired
   companion brief, which is the only place it lived.* A fully built character is the natural
   defense and the fingerprint should diverge on its own — run it once anyway. Two things
   specifically: the manuscript against Brian's own writing, and the manuscript against the
   four letters from life, no sentence of which may have been lifted.

## Phase 2 — Establish the record

Four records. None of them made by you. All dated by someone else.

1. **Copyright registration, filed anonymously.** Strongest of the four, and it doubles as
   the claim-later mechanism: the term runs 95 years from publication or 120 from creation,
   and the statute contemplates identity being revealed later in the registration records,
   which switches the term to life-plus-seventy. The Office will not remove a name once
   issued — so file without naming yourself and the record stays clean permanently.
   *Worth an hour with an actual IP attorney. I am not one.*
2. **ISBN, registered to an imprint.** Dated agency record, and it is what a reader types
   in when they find a copy. **Name the imprint carefully** — it is the one piece of lore
   you are allowed to plant, and it should be a phrase from inside the fiction.
3. **A library catalog record.** The one people forget and the hardest to fake. Donate to
   collections that will actually catalog it — a Utah special collection, a university that
   collects local imprints. The record propagates to WorldCat with a date, held by a third
   party with no stake in your novel.
4. **A dealer listing, made by a dealer.** Not by you. A bookseller taking a copy into
   inventory creates a timestamp in someone else's system.

---

## Phase 3 — Place it, slowly

**Not one drop.** Forty copies entering the world in one week, in one city, is the most
suspicious possible pattern. Place a few at a time over a year or more: thrift shelves,
used shops in several cities as travel allows, little free libraries, and — best — into the
hands of collectors who will do whatever collectors do.

Do not track them. Do not photograph them. Do not place a copy anywhere you would be
unhappy to have it sit for four years.

---

## Phase 4 — The hard stop

**When the novel is announced, placement ends forever.** Not because it becomes wrong, but
because after that date every placement is marketing, and one copy placed post-announcement
retroactively contaminates all thirty-nine placed before it.

---

## Phase 5 — Publish the novel

Named author. Market it hard. No artifact exists in this phase because nobody has raised
one.

**What you can say freely, all of it true:**
- There is a book inside the novel that the characters treat as scripture.
- It has no author. It is incomplete. Marge thinks it never defines its own central term.
- The framework underneath the novel is a real framework, worked out in full, hundreds of
  pages, built over years.

**What you never do:** claim to have found anything. You are a novelist; a novelist is not
asked how they know about a fictional object. There is nothing to dodge, which is why the
silence in Phase 7 will not read as dodging.

**Do not let the publisher run any of this.** If marketing learns of the object, the correct
framing is: a personal art project from years ago, not a marketing asset, leave it alone.
A scavenger hunt, an influencer mailer, or an unboxing converts the object into swag inside
a week.

---

## Phase 6 — Wait

Nothing happens. This phase may last years and may never end. Budget for silence as the
most likely outcome: forty copies in thrift stores may simply never be found. The cost of
that outcome is a few hundred dollars and a weekend of hand-annotation, which is why it is
worth doing regardless.

---

## Phase 7 — If a reader finds one

The question arrives from the other direction, from someone you never prompted.

**One sentence, prepared, and never improved:** *I'm not going to answer that.* Warm, flat,
unembarrassed, identical every time, for years. Not a claim, so not a lie. A consistent
non-answer generates more lore than any invented story, and costs nothing.

**The failure mode to watch:** coy hints, escalating teases, *well, who's to say.* That
reads as manipulation the instant anyone smells it, and teenagers are the most sensitive
audience alive to being played. The whole thing lives or dies on your ability to be boring
about it in public for two years.

---

## Scaling, when it works

Demand will exceed forty copies by orders of magnitude and you will want a second printing.
**Don't** — the printer's note says forty, and breaking that breaks the object.

The answer is already in the lore: in the novel, the book exists because a reader who
believed it paid to have copies made. So release a print-ready file, quietly, late, and let
readers print their own. Fans running off spiral-bound copies at a campus print shop and
annotating them is not a compromise of the fiction. It is the fiction, happening.
