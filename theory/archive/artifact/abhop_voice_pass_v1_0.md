## The protocol

**5–15 pages per session**, source and output side by side. Prose is the deliverable. Hand over
pages.

**Not in one pass.** A single sweep across two hundred pages produces uniform mannerism —
restatement on a metronome, *you* every third paragraph, an admission of ignorance at fixed
intervals. That is worse than flat prose, because flat prose can be rewritten and a costume has
to be stripped first.

### The gradient, which is fixed at both ends

The **terminal pole is written**. The ending is short, fast, dense; the manners are gone; one
joke sits on each of the worst sentences; a numbered list stops at 2 with no item 3; and a
difficulty is left unresolved on a complete sentence.

**Set the opening deliberately at the other pole** — unhurried, restating, working the example
all the way through, assuming nothing, giving an idea room before stating it. Once both poles
exist, every chunk between them has a defined gradient to sit on, and the escalation stops
being something to remember and becomes something the two endpoints enforce.

### Where to start

**Groundwork, and the thermostat-and-bacterium argument that runs it.** It is the first thing a
stranger reads cold, it is currently the most compressed part of the book, and it needs to
*grow*, which is voice work. Ten pages of Night One.
