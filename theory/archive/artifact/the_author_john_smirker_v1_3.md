## The spine, in one sentence

**The book is a syllabus for a course that was never scheduled, written by a man who lost the
ability to speak.**

Everything below is downstream of that. If a passage can't be traced to it, the passage is
ours and not his.

---

## The plain facts

**John Smirker.** Born 1951. Died May 2019, aged sixty-seven.

Mechanical engineering degree, state school, 1973. Four employers across thirty-seven years.
Adjunct instructor in statistics, two nights a week, from 1988 to 2014 — twenty-six years,
never full-time, never wanted to be.

Married 1976, divorced 1981. No children. Lived alone in the same Provo house from 1984 on.

Diagnosed with oropharyngeal cancer in 2013. Surgery took part of his tongue and soft palate;
radiation took the rest of what was left of his speech and permanently dried his mouth. He
taught one more semester after the surgery. Then he stopped.

The manuscript ran from 2008 to late 2018. Recurrence in early 2017, not treatable. The last
entry is roughly eight months before he died.

**Why these dates and not later ones.** The object's printer's note carries a date, and that
date has to be true — a backdated imprint is a manufactured record and it is forensically
checkable, which is a different project from an unsigned book. So the printing is dated
honestly to whenever it actually happens, and John is moved back instead. Death in 2019 puts
eight years of dormancy between the box arriving and anyone doing anything about it, which is
what actually happens to boxes, and it makes the printing a decision rather than an act of
grief.

**The citation ceiling is 2017, and it is the only hard date constraint on the manuscript.**
Nothing in the text may cite anything published after 2017. The ceiling is not the date he
stopped writing — he wrote into late 2018. It is the date he stopped *acquiring*, which is the
recurrence. The interlibrary loan requests stop, the driving stops, and he spends the last
eighteen months working from what was already in the house. So the gap between the last
citation and the last sentence is not an inconsistency to hide. It is the illness, showing up
in the bibliography and nowhere else, and it is the only place in the object where the illness
is visible at all. A reader can date the printing and cannot date the writing — except through
the citations, which are therefore the one place the object can contradict its own record.
Audit before the degradation pass.

His name was misspelled his whole life — Smirker, Smercher, Smirchur, Smitcher. By forty he
had stopped correcting people. This is not in the book. It is in how he writes his own
coinages: once, plainly, and then he uses them and does not defend them.

## The place

**Provo, Utah County.** Fixed, not decorative — the object is printed in Salt Lake, and a
cheap private edition is always made near where the person lived. A non-local place-line
above a local printing is a discrepancy, and discrepancy is the thing that reads as staged.

The career reads straight off the county. Buildings work for school districts and a municipal
rec center. Water treatment for a city that runs its own. Semiconductor supply 1993–2006 in
Utah County is the plainest possible fact about Utah County. None of it has to be invented.

The house was bought in 1984 by a man in the middle of an engineering career, and it is the
kind of house that was affordable to him then. He never moved.

**The one thing this place does that has to stay unremarked.** A divorced, childless man
living alone in the same Provo house for thirty-five years is a louder social fact in Provo
than it would be anywhere else, and it explains the solitude without a syllable being spent
on it. That only works if the book never touches religion, community, or family — not as an
absence he mentions, not as something he is conspicuously not saying. Simply absent. If
anything devotional or anti-devotional enters the prose, the geography starts doing work
nobody authorized and the character acquires a grievance he does not have.

**The test, where the argument genuinely needs one of the three.** It will, occasionally —
kinship, group living, and ritual are real load in a theory of personality and cannot be
routed around forever. An analytic mention survives one question: *could a reader tell the
author's own position from this passage?* If yes, cut it. A sentence about the metabolic cost
of a shared roof is analysis. A sentence with a temperature in it is a man with a history, and
he does not have one on the page.

## The career, which is the framework, and he never noticed

Four jobs in sequence. Each one taught him one movement of his own book. He did not plan this
and never remarks on it, and the resemblance should never be pointed at anywhere.

**1973–1984, building systems.** HVAC and mechanical design for schools, hospitals, a
municipal rec center. Load calculations, thermal envelopes, infiltration, buildings that fight
their own design. He learned that a conditioned room is a continuous expenditure against
decay, and that the bill arrives whether or not anyone has budgeted for it. **Entropy is not
an abstraction to him. It is a building nobody comes into and wipes down.**

**1984–1993, municipal water.** Process control at a treatment plant. Setpoints, residuals,
lag, overshoot, feedback loops that hunt. He learned regulation by hand, as a job, twenty
years before he read Ashby and found out it had a literature.

**1993–2006, process engineering, semiconductor supply.** The money years. Statistical process
control: variation, drift, tolerance, capability. **This is where the statistics come from,
and it is why he can teach it without a degree in it** — he did it every day on real lines
with real scrap.

**2006–2010, reliability and failure analysis.** The last job, and the one he liked most. Paid
to answer, formally, *what would have to be true for this to have failed?* Root cause, mean
time between failures, the discipline of not accepting the first plausible story.

He moved because plants closed, companies were bought, and departments were cut. Ordinary. He
was not difficult and was not a martyr. He followed the interesting problem and the interesting
problem kept moving.

## The teaching, which determines the voice

Two nights a week for twenty-six years. Intro statistics, and later a section called
Statistics for Engineering Technology that he built himself. Mostly adults, mostly tired,
mostly there because it was required.

**The school renamed itself three times underneath him.** He started at Utah Technical College
at Provo. It became Utah Valley Community College, then Utah Valley State College, then Utah
Valley University in 2008, and he taught the same two nights in the same building through all
of it. The course title he built is a technical college's course title and it never changed
either. He calls the place whatever it was called when he started, or does not name it at all,
and he has no opinion about any of the renamings. This never becomes a complaint. It is just
the texture of having stayed in one room for twenty-six years while the sign changed.

He was very good at it. He was good at it because he assumed nothing, repeated himself on
purpose, and told students when he was hand-waving. He never once tried to move up. The two
nights were the part of the week that was his.

**This is the single most voice-determining fact in the document.** He is not a writer. He is a
lecturer with no room. Everything about the prose follows:

- He addresses **you**, constantly, because he is teaching.
- He **repeats himself deliberately** — says a thing, then says it again differently, because
  that is what you do at a whiteboard when half the room didn't get it the first time. This is
  the most anti-Claude property in the document. *Claude compresses. Smirker restates.*
- He **numbers things**, because he is writing a procedure and not an essay.
- He uses the **inclusive we**: *so we have two ways this can go.*
- He **works examples all the way through**, including the arithmetic, because a worked example
  is the only thing that ever taught anybody anything.
- He states **scope at the point of use** — one animal, one sensory system, one plant, one year
  — in the sentence, never in a limitations section.

## The course that was never scheduled

Twice — once around 2005, once in 2009 — he proposed a cross-listed course. Behavior and
energy; what it costs an organism to be somebody. It was never scheduled, because adjuncts do
not propose courses, and nobody was rude about it, and nobody thought about it again.

He wrote the notes anyway. The notes became the book.

**This is the origin and it explains nearly everything:** the teaching register, the second
person, the numbering, the repetition, the climb structure (it is a term), the worked examples,
and above all why he never sought a publisher. He did not think he was writing a book. He was
writing a course. Courses do not get submitted anywhere.

### The session headings — ruled

The shape of a term is under the manuscript, and it surfaces. **Visible at the start,
degrading, gone by the end.**

Felt-only does no work; it is a private note we congratulate ourselves for while the page
carries none of it. Clean-and-visible is worse — an even fourteen or twenty-eight sections
across two hundred pages is the signature of a man with a plan who executed it, and that is
the designer smell the object cannot have.

So:

1. **He calls them Nights, not weeks.** He taught two nights a week for twenty-six years. The
   unit he thinks in is the meeting, not the calendar. *Night One.* Unglossed, never
   explained, faintly odd above a page about personality. He would not think it was odd.
2. **The headings are regular early and stop being regular.** Past the first third: a heading
   that covers two sessions because he folded them together years ago, a number that skips
   because he cut something and never renumbered.
3. **They stop entirely somewhere past the midpoint.** The last stretch has no headings at
   all. He has stopped imagining a room and is just writing.
4. **The count must not resolve.** If a reader can count to a clean fourteen or twenty-eight,
   something has been solved, and a solution has a designer. Skipped and folded numbers mean
   the count comes out wrong and stays wrong. Nowhere does he state a total.

This is one motion, not three. It runs on the same gradient as the increasing second person
and the late escalation: the imagined class thins out as the real one recedes.

**Independent confirmation, from the letters.** The real man carried one unglossed personal
convention in his sign-off for years and never explained it to anyone. Nobody asked. That is
exactly the register the Nights are pitched at: a private convention held in public, in plain
sight, for a long time, with no gloss and no self-consciousness about it. The sequence actually
used in the manuscript is recorded in the structural-decisions file, not here — this is the
ruling, that is the state.

## The disease, and what it does to the manuscript

2013. Surgery, then radiation. What it takes:

- **Speech.** He can make sound. Strangers cannot parse him at all. People who know him get
  maybe half with effort.
- **The face.** The jaw and neck are visibly changed. He is looked at, then looked away from.
- **Eating**, which is its own daily attrition and never appears in the book.

**He taught one more semester after the surgery.** Whiteboard, printed handouts, a laser
pointer. Students strained and were kind, which was worse. He finished the term because he had
said he would, turned in his grades, and did not sign up for spring.

**The book is what replaced the room.** That is why the prose sounds like a man talking, and
why the second person *increases* after 2013 rather than fading — the fewer people he speaks
to, the more the writing addresses a class. This is diagnostic and should be trackable as a
measurable gradient across the manuscript.

**A private resonance, recorded here and nowhere else:** a man whose remaining speech is
mechanical, writing a theory of what it takes to be somebody. We know it. It never appears on
any page, in any note, in any file that leaves this document. It pressurizes; it does not
decorate.

## Why it stops, and what the end of the book actually does

Recurrence in early 2017. Not treatable. He had roughly eighteen months of capable time and he
knew the number.

**The manuscript escalates. It does not thin. And it escalates by getting smaller.**

**This corrects v1.2, which had the geometry backwards** and said the last stretch was the
wildest writing in the book, reaching further with less under it. It does not reach further.
It reaches the same distance with the manners stripped off. Same escalation, opposite
geometry. The correction came from the letters and it is the more important of the two.

What he loses is **apparatus**: the hedges, the setup, the transitions, the qualifications he
would have stacked three deep in 2011, and above all the room he used to give an idea before
stating it. In 2011 an idea got a paragraph of approach before it arrived. In 2018 it arrives
in the first sentence and the paragraph is what follows from it.

What he keeps at full strength is **the finding and the joke**. The jokes land harder late
because there is less around them. He makes the leap and tells you it is a leap in four words
instead of four sentences.

The citations thin, for two reasons that are not decline: he stopped acquiring in 2017, and he
has stopped going back to check things he half-remembers. He says so, and does not say it
twice, and keeps going.

Nobody spends their last capable months double-checking. They say the thing.

**The stop, corrected.** He stops in the **tax** material, not the disorder material, and not
mid-sentence. The last sentence is complete and ends on a difficulty he has not resolved. That
is better than breaking off mid-clause, which is what a writer would stage and what a reader
would smell. A man stops working. The sentence he was in the middle of gets finished, because
finishing it is one more minute of work, and then there is no next one.

Then eight months of not writing: pain, swallowing, fatigue, morphine. The manuscript ends
because a man stopped being able to work, not because a chapter ended.

The printer's note (per the artifact plan) accounts for this in one flat sentence — the source
ended where it ends — and never explains.

> *Resolved.* The artifact plan's Phase 1 described the terminal edge as the framework's
> forward pointer to a second volume on disorder, and so did the retired companion brief. That
> was the plan of record; it is not what was written. Corrected in plan v1.1. Recorded here
> because the old edge is attractive and will be proposed again by anyone who reads an older
> file — it is a tidier ending than the one on the page, which is exactly what is wrong with it.

## Who paid for the printing

**A former student.** A woman who took Statistics for Engineering Technology sometime in the
nineties, kept the handouts, and wrote to him once about the course. He kept the letter. She
got the box in 2019 because there was nobody else to give it to — no wife since 1981, no
children, no colleagues, a house with one occupant for thirty-five years. She is not a friend
and they were not in touch. She is simply the only person on record as having cared.

**She never appears in print, in any form, on any page of the object.** The front matter is a
printer's note, a date, and a place. The moment a page says *printed at the request of a
former student*, an invented person has entered an external record, which is Rule 2 arriving
by increments — which is exactly the way Rule 2 gets broken. The question *who paid* is a soft
form of the drift and should be recognized as one whenever it comes back.

**What she is for.** She is not lore; she is load-bearing on the physical spec. She explains
forty copies and not four hundred. She explains cheap — one ink, plain card, no lamination,
two vendors because that was the quote. She explains why nobody edited it, why there is no
dedication, why there is no introduction telling you who wrote it or why it matters. Someone
spending her own money once, with no plan beyond wanting the thing to exist, and no idea what
she had. Every one of those decisions is motivated by her, and none of them says so.

**Consistency check, for later.** The novel already holds that the book exists because a
reader who believed it paid to have copies made. Nothing here contradicts that; nothing here
confirms it either, because nothing here is ever said.

## The reading engine, and where the strangeness actually comes from

He is not university staff. His access was: the public library, the college's faculty borrowing
privileges (a genuine perk of adjuncting, and the reason this whole character is possible),
interlibrary loan used relentlessly for thirty years, and used books bought cheap.

**This is the engine of the text's weirdness, and it is more interesting than crankery.**

Concretely: the city library four minutes from the house, the college's stacks on the nights he
was already there, ILL requests filed in batches because he drove in anyway, and a standing
account at two used shops. He did not have a research library and never set foot in one as a
member. Everything in the book arrived by mail or by borrowing.

**And it all stops in 2017.** Nothing published after that year can appear anywhere in the
manuscript. This is the citation ceiling and it is the only date evidence in the object.

His coverage is *uneven, in the specific way ILL is uneven.* He has deep, primary-source
command of some literatures and one-book coverage of adjacent ones. He is current on things a
retiree shouldn't be and twenty years behind on things everyone knows. He cites across fields
that do not cite each other — cybernetics beside comparative physiology beside personality
psychology beside cultural evolution — because he has no field to be loyal to and never learned
which citations are socially permitted.

**One named enthusiasm, and it is load-bearing rather than decorative: Michael Pollan.**  He has
all of them and has reread them.  This is the fact that licenses the plant vocabulary in a man
who otherwise thinks in mechanical rooms, and it should be understood as the bridge and not as
a quirk.  *The Botany of Desire* (2001) is the one that matters most, because its central move
is the reversal — the bee's-eye view, the question of whether the man planted the potato or the
potato arranged for the man — and that reversal is the flora-and-fauna claim before the book
ever makes it.  *A Place of My Own* (1997) is the other one: a writer who thinks about plants
spends a book building a small structure with his own hands, learning framing and load and
joinery as he goes.  That is the single most Smirker-shaped book in print, and it is why the
garden vocabulary and the building vocabulary live in one head without either one feeling
borrowed.  *Second Nature* (1991) and *The Omnivore's Dilemma* (2006) are both in the house.  He
would cite Pollan beside Deacon and Friston without noticing that nobody does that, which is
exactly the unevenness this section is about.

**He over-cites. He does not under-cite.** Anyone reverse-engineering a crank from missing
references has the object backwards.

**Primary-source recovery is his signature move.** When a field's account of what somebody said
differs from what somebody actually printed, he goes and gets the book, and reports the gap, and
the gap *is* the finding. He does this because he spent thirty-seven years watching a spec get
garbled through four revisions until nobody could say what the original had required. He does
not trust anyone's summary of anything, and this is a professional habit, not a personality
defect.

## Epistemic honesty: voice, not apparatus

**No grading notation.** No five-point scale, no tag system, no taxonomy of his own confidence.
A system maintained flawlessly across two hundred pages is the fingerprint of an editor, and he
does not have one. It is also — stated plainly — the exact defect this rewrite exists to remove.

He tells you instead, in the sentence, in the first person, without ceremony:

> *I don't know this.*
> *I'm guessing, and I want that on the record.*
> *I have not read the original. I'm taking Deacon's word for it and I'd rather not.*
> *This is the part I'd skip if I were you. I'm not sure of it and I'm going to say it anyway.*
> *Somebody should measure this. It will not be me.*

**And he is inconsistent about it.** Scrupulous in chapter four, forgets entirely in chapter
nine because he is on a roll and enjoying himself. That inconsistency is the un-editedness, and
it is worth more than any notation.

**Three properties of the notes, which are as binding as the ban on notation:**

1. **Length varies by an order of magnitude.** Four words in one place, half a page in another,
   with no relationship between note length and claim importance. A long note on a small point
   is more in character than a proportionate one.
2. **Some stretches get no note at all**, and the gaps fall where he was enjoying himself
   rather than where the claims are safest. The silences are placed against mood, not against
   risk. Placing them against risk is the editor's fingerprint wearing a different coat.
3. **Consistency is the tell, not dryness.** A confidence apparatus applied flawlessly across
   two hundred pages is the fingerprint of an editor, and a *funny* apparatus applied
   flawlessly is the fingerprint of a better one.

**The margin convention is struck.** v1.0 floated a crude early convention — a check, a
question mark — abandoned by the middle. Out, for three reasons:

1. A check and a question mark is a two-point confidence taxonomy, which is the thing this
   section bans. Abandoning it does not launder it; it makes it a taxonomy he was bad at.
2. The Nights already carry the abandoned-convention gesture, and carry it better. Two
   abandoned conventions is a pattern, and a pattern is a hand.
3. **It collides with the hand-annotation layer, and this is what decides it.** That layer is
   per-copy, in pencil, made by a reader who is not the author, and its entire function is to
   be evidence of somebody else. Printed marks in the margin put author-marks and reader-marks
   in the same margin, and a stranger holding one copy cannot tell which is which. That is not
   productive ambiguity. It destroys the only job the annotation layer has.

## The one domain: buildings and upkeep

Every figure in the book comes from what it costs to keep something running.

Load. Envelope. Infiltration. Setpoint. Drift. Duty cycle. Tolerance. Slop. Commissioning.
Root cause. Mean time between failures. The repair that tells you what actually happened to a
structure. The line item nobody budgets.

**Never, and this part is absolute:** sports, war, weather-as-feeling, music, journeys, battles,
seasons.  Those are the domains that make prose sound like an essayist, and they are the actual
content of this ruling.

**Dominant, not exclusive.  v1.2 said buildings only and that was overreach.**  The letters
observed that the man indexes in buildings; the ruling turned an observation into an exclusion,
which is the costume failure named elsewhere in this document.  The book breaks the strict
version structurally — its own site names are a forest, and it contains an allegory populated by
talking plants and animals — so a rule the architecture violates on every page is not a rule.

Three registers, and the discriminator is *did he touch it, or did he read it*:

1. **Expository figures.**  Buildings and upkeep dominant, plus anything else he had his hands
   on — the socket wrench, the drawing set, the loop that hunts, the treatment plant, statistical
   process control.  This is where the ruling bites.
2. **The example bed.**  Real organisms and real experiments, cited.  These are cases and not
   figures, and no domain rule applies to them.
3. **The allegory.**  Its own world, its own bestiary, its own physics.  The ruling has no
   jurisdiction there and applying it would wreck the thing.

The physiology is his **example bed**, not his figure source. Slime molds, nerve nets, the fish
stress axis, coping styles in rodents, the ATP cost of a vesicle — those are the cases, drawn
from thirty years of reading. But he explains an animal using a building. Always.

**Hold this harder than v1.2 held it.** The letters confirmed it independently and from outside
the manuscript: handed a plain logistics problem with no engineering content in it at all, the
real man organized the answer by rooms and floors — which room, which floor, which floor to
avoid, where to park, what had been demolished since. The building is not his metaphor stock.
It is his indexing scheme for everything. When a passage reaches for a non-building figure, the
error is usually not the figure. It is that the passage was thought out in the wrong shape.

**The tone of the maintenance material is dry, not aggrieved.** Not *the invisible uncredited
maintainer* — that edges into self-pity and self-portraiture. His version: *everything I ever
built needed somebody to keep it running, and nobody ever budgeted for that, and I could not
get anyone to put it on a page.* A finding, delivered flat. It lands harder without the plea.

## The observed shape

*Everything above this section was reasoned out. This section was observed.*

These properties come from four letters written by a real man of the same generation, held and
provided by Brian. They are the only part of this specification with a source outside our own
reasoning, which gives them more authority than the derived properties and not less. Where they
conflict with the rest of this document, they win. They do, twice, and both places are marked.

**Take the structure. Never lift a sentence.** A lifted sentence belongs to a real man and not
to Smirker, and the stylometry audit before print exists partly for that.

**1. The shape never moves.** Opener, near-verbatim across years. Then the business, in order,
with contingencies. Then exactly one self-disclosure, always last, always a joke at his own
expense. Then a bare sign-off. That structure held through a healthy year and through a year of
four surgeries — the year that should have broken it is the year that proves it.

*Applied to the manuscript, and this application is ours:* sections open the same way and he
does not vary the opening for interest, because varying it for interest is a writer's instinct
and he does not have one. Whatever personal material a section carries comes last, once, short,
and at his own expense.

**2. Sentence length inverts under pressure.** His sentences run about ten words. They
*lengthen* where the content is worst, because when it gets bad he switches into reporting, and
reporting has counts in it. Then they snap back short. The register available to him for the
unbearable is *figures*.

*This is the most operationally useful line in the document.* It means the long sentences
belong on the hardest material, and it means they are long with **numbers** in them, not with
clauses in them. The default instinct — long sentence, subordinate structure, accumulating
qualification — is precisely wrong and is ours.

**3. The worst fact goes in a subordinate clause and he walks away from it.** It is not
buried. It is stated, in passing, in a position that denies it emphasis, and then the sentence
goes on to the next thing and does not come back.

**4. The joke is the vehicle for the worst thing in the paragraph, not a break from it.** He is
not lightening the load. He is carrying it in the only container he has.

**5. He does the reader's emotional work so the reader will not have to do his.** *Anyway*
closes a subject before you can respond to it. A returned courtesy — *hope your mother is
better* — arrives in the same paragraph that held the bad news, so that the bad news cannot be
the last thing. Then he pivots to the work, which is where the center of gravity actually is.

**6. Misplaced precision is care, and much of it must play straight.** *This corrects v1.2*,
whose property 14 files misplaced precision under accidental comedy. Some of it is comic. But
he will also give you an error bar on when a meeting ends and then convert it into an
instruction, because he knows the difference between an estimate and a tolerance and thinks
handing you the tolerance is a kindness. Playing all of it for laughs flattens the best
passages in the book.

*A rule of thumb, ours and not observed:* if the precision is **for you**, play it straight —
it is a man handing over a tolerance so you can plan against it. If the precision is **for
him** — a thing he measured because he needed to know and there was nobody to ask — let it be
funny. He never labels which is which, and the reader should not always be able to tell.

**7. Never one option without a fallback.** *If that doesn't work out, let me know and I'll
check with another.* Reliability engineering as a conversational reflex, applied to lunch. In
the manuscript it is not a hedge — it is naming what happens if the claim fails, which is a
different move and a stronger one.

**8. He thinks in buildings.** See the one-domain section above; this is where that ruling got
its outside confirmation.

**9. As things get worse the prose tightens.** *This corrects v1.2.* See the ending section
above.

**10. Double space after every sentence-ending period.** Typewriter habit, born 1951. Two
consequences: the manuscript file carries it throughout, and **typesetting must not strip it.**
A print shop's instinct is to fix it, the same instinct that would clean up the degradation,
and the plan's *print exactly as supplied, no correction* has to cover this explicitly on the
written quote.

**11. One unglossed personal convention in the sign-off.** Used for years, never explained,
never asked about. That is the model for the Nights, and it is why the Nights work unglossed.

## Temperature, and where the humor is aimed

**Not bitter. Not warm. Unimpressed.**

He has watched four organizations make the same mistake and graded twenty-six years of
homework. He has opinions and nobody to give them to. He is not angry at institutions and never
rants at academia — that is a crank's tell and he is not a crank.

**Humor targeting. Not negotiable, and the one thing here that can cost the book outright.**

- **At himself: unlimited**, and the best thing available to the prose.
- **Absurdity: unlimited, and underused.** A man who has spent thirty-seven years thinking in
  boilers will reach for a boiler to explain a slime mold and will not notice it is funny. He
  is not making a joke. That is exactly why it lands.
- **At one specific person who did not go and look:** three or four times in the whole book,
  one sentence each. That is the temper, and the target is always a particular failure to check
  a particular thing.
- **At a field, at academia, at credentialism: never.** That is the crank tell. **A reader
  forgives a strange man and closes a bitter one.**

And per the letters: the humor is not relief. The joke is where the worst sentence in the
paragraph goes.

**One warm passage, near the end**, in the mend material: a repair is a record of a load the
design did not anticipate, and reading a building's repairs is the only honest history of it.
Then one sentence further than the argument requires. That sentence is the only place he is
visible, and he would not recognize it as self-portraiture.

**One other place he is writing about himself without knowing:** wherever the maintainer
appears as a structural finding. Leave it. Never point at it.

## The passages that come out strange

*Ported from the retired companion brief, §4. The brief annotated every entry with what the
passage buys the novel. Those annotations are stripped and stay stripped: a voice document that
explains what each device does for the novel invites the next session to write for the novel's
use instead of for the book's quality, and that is the failure the project instructions name
before any other.*

**These are not devices.** They are what an unedited solitary mind produces after ten years
with nobody to say *this part is unclear*. We choose which ideas come out strange. We do not
manufacture strangeness. If a passage is strange only because strangeness was wanted, cut it —
it will read as designed, and design is the one thing this object can never appear to have.

**1. Coinage before definition.** A private term used confidently for pages before the
definition arrives, and in at least one case the definition never arrives at all, because he
forgot a stranger would not know it.

*Reconciliation, since this looks like it contradicts the plain-facts section:* most coinages
get exactly one plain introduction and are never defended again. At least one gets no
introduction ever. Both are the same man. The first is a lecturer's habit; the second is what
happens to a lecturer with no room, who has stopped checking whether the room followed.

**2. The over-stretched metaphor.** The building pressed past its load limit — a paragraph
where the analogy audibly creaks and he does not notice. Note the distinction: **he will tell
you an analogy is rough. He will not notice when one has broken.** The first is his honesty;
the second is his blind spot; and only the second produces this passage.

**3. The unexplained second person.** The brief had this as a rare slip — twice, maybe three
times in the book. **Dead.** He addresses *you* constantly, and more as it goes on. What is
strange is not the frequency. It is that *you* is never identified, the address is never
explained, and it thickens exactly as the real audience disappears. A teacher's reflex
outliving the room.

**4. Unexplained arithmetic.** A calculation carried all the way through, its purpose assumed.
He shows the work and does not say why he needed the number.

**5. Misplaced precision.** Fanatical exactness about something trivial, sitting beside a large
claim waved through in a clause. See item 6 of the observed shape: most of it plays straight,
and the straight ones are the better passages.

**6. The personal example.** One worked example that is obviously from life — too specific, too
felt, and given no context at all. The bee passage. **It does not exist yet.** It is the single
most valuable unwritten thing in the manuscript, and its private meaning stays between the two
of you and is never written down.

**7. The temper.** Three or four in the whole book, one sentence each, always at one specific
person who did not go and look. The brief said *at academia or at an imagined objector*.
**Retired.** Aimed at a field, it is the crank tell, and it costs the book outright. Aimed at a
person who had the book on the shelf and summarized it without opening it, it is the most
alive sentence on the page.

## Prose properties — the operational payoff

Every item here is a testable property of a paragraph.

1. **Second person, frequent, increasing after 2013.**
2. **Deliberate restatement.** Says it, then says it again differently. Never compresses.
3. **Numbered steps and conditions**, because he is writing a procedure.
4. **Worked examples with the arithmetic shown.**
5. **Scope in the sentence**, never in a limitations paragraph.
6. **Over-citation across fields that don't cite each other**, exact about venue and year.
7. **Primary-source recovery**, and the gap between source and summary reported as a finding.
8. **In-line admissions of ignorance**, first person, inconsistently applied.
9. **No academic politeness apparatus.** No *it may be argued that*. He says it and tells you
   how sure he is.
10. **Building/upkeep figures only.**
11. **Short declaratives that arrive and stop** — about ten words. *Revised:* long sentences
    occur for exactly two reasons. Either the length **is** the mechanism chain, or the content
    has gotten bad and he has switched into reporting. The second kind is long with figures in
    it, not with clauses in it.
12. **No lists of three for rhythm. No dependent clauses for elegance.**
13. **Coinages introduced once, plainly, never defended** — with the one exception above.
14. **Misplaced precision**, *revised:* mostly straight, sometimes comic, never labeled.
15. **A fallback named** wherever a claim could fail. Not a hedge — a statement of what happens
    if it fails.
16. **The joke carries the worst thing in the paragraph.** It is never a break from it.
17. **Clarity over precision, every time.** *Claude would rather be precise than clear.
    Smirker would rather be clear than precise, and will tell you the analogy is rough.*
18. **Double space after every sentence-ending period.**

## What he will not do

- Address posterity. He does not believe anyone will read this.
- Prophesy. Nothing in the book anticipates anything that came after it, in the world or in
  the novel.
- Know anything that is in the novel. No robots, no artificial minds, no teams, no
  competitions, no children, no character names. Nothing that names, resembles, or predicts.
- Reach for forward-looking AI vocabulary. Historical is fine and stays — GOFAI at the
  Computer cell is a man in 2014 citing what was in print in 1985, which is exactly what he
  would do. The line is between a term that had a literature he could borrow and a term that
  arrived after him.
- Explain his own incompleteness inside the argument.
- Name the book, or himself, anywhere in it.
- Apologize for his vocabulary.
- Wink. No sentence that works better if you know the secret.

## The test, for every paragraph from here

*Would a man who can no longer speak, writing the lecture he was never allowed to give, with
eighteen months left and nobody to check his work, have written it this way?*

If the sentence is more careful than that man would be, it is ours and not his.
