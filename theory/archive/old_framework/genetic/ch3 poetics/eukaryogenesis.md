# The Origin of the Mitochondrion: Predation or Syntrophy? A Theory-Neutral Survey of Eukaryogenesis

## TL;DR
- **The FACT that mitochondria descend from an endosymbiotic alphaproteobacterium captured by an archaeal-lineage host is settled; the MECHANISM, DRIVER, HOST COMPLEXITY, and TIMING of that capture are genuinely unsettled.** The field is in transition, not consensus, on how the bacterium got inside.
- **"Predation gone symbiotic" (phagocytosis-first) is one of several live options, not the established account, and its standing has weakened over the past decade** relative to syntrophy/metabolic-partnership models — largely because the closest cultured archaeal relatives (Asgard archaea) are small, slow-growing, syntrophic cells that show no phagocytosis, and because ancestral-state reconstructions do not robustly place phagocytosis in the last eukaryotic common ancestor (LECA).
- **The two framings are not strictly mutually exclusive: they often address different questions** — syntrophy speaks to the initial *driver/selective force* of the association, phagocytosis to a possible *enclosure mechanism*. The honest current read: a metabolic-partnership origin is now the more widely favored framing among specialists working on archaeal hosts, but a phagocytosis/predation route remains defended and cannot be excluded; decisive evidence is still missing.

## Established Facts vs. Leading Hypotheses vs. Open/Disputed

**Established (broad, independent support):**
- Mitochondria evolved once, from within Alphaproteobacteria, and all known eukaryotes descend from a mitochondrion-bearing LECA (mitochondrion-free eukaryotes such as *Monocercomonoides exilis* lost the organelle secondarily). (Sagan/Margulis 1967; Roger, Muñoz-Gómez & Kamikawa 2017; Karnkowska et al. 2016, 2019.)
- The host lineage is archaeal, related to (or within) the Asgard archaea, which carry hundreds of "eukaryotic signature proteins" (ESPs) including actins, ESCRT, ubiquitin and small-GTPase systems. (Spang et al. 2015; Zaremba-Niedzwiedzka et al. 2017, *Nature* 541:353, which catalogued hundreds of ESPs; a 2024 structure-based pangenome analysis added 908 "isomorphic" ESPs, more than tripling the overall number of reported Asgard ESPs.)
- LECA was already complex (nucleus, endomembrane system, cytoskeleton, mitochondria, sex/meiosis machinery). The hard problem is the FECA→LECA "dark ages," for which there are no surviving intermediates.

**Leading hypotheses (contested):**
- Syntrophy/metabolic-interdependence models (hydrogen hypothesis; syntrophy hypothesis; reverse flow; Entangle-Engulf-Endogenize) as the *driver*.
- Phagocytosis-first/mitochondria-late models (Cavalier-Smith; de Duve) in which an already-complex proto-eukaryote engulfed the bacterium.
- Topological/hybrid models (inside-out; outside-in).

**Open/disputed:**
- Whether mitochondria came "early" (triggering complexity) or "late" (after substantial host complexity, including phagocytosis) evolved.
- Whether the archaeal host could phagocytose at all.
- Whether mitochondrial bioenergetics was the *prime mover* of genome/cell complexity.
- The origin of the nucleus (a separate, less-resolved question).

## Per-Model Assessment

### Model 1 — Phagocytosis-first / mitochondria-late ("predation gone symbiotic")

**The strongest case.** Engulfment by phagocytosis is the *only* mechanism actually observed today by which one cell routinely takes an intact bacterium into its cytoplasm; it is therefore the most parsimonious route for getting a bacterium inside another cell. Under this view, an already-elaborated proto-eukaryote (with a dynamic actin cytoskeleton, membrane trafficking, and possibly a nucleus) engulfed a free-living alphaproteobacterium and retained it. The discovery that Asgard archaea encode actins, ESCRT and membrane-remodeling machinery was read by some as bringing a "phagocytosing archaeon" into reach.

**Named proponents:** Thomas Cavalier-Smith (phagotrophy/"neomuran" origin of eukaryotes, e.g., 2002 *Int. J. Syst. Evol. Microbiol.* 52:297-354; 2009 *Int. J. Biochem. Cell Biol.*); Christian de Duve (phagocytosis-first); and, in nuanced form, Anthony Poole and colleagues who argue phagocytosis is not tied to any one tree topology (Poole & Penny; Poole & Neumann). Pittis & Gabaldón (2016, *Nature* 531:101) provided phylogenomic support for a "host with chimaeric prokaryotic ancestry" that acquired mitochondria comparatively late.

**Named critics:** William Martin and colleagues (Martin, Tielens, Mentel, Garg & Gould 2017, "The Physiology of Phagocytosis in the Context of Mitochondrial Origin," *Microbiol. Mol. Biol. Rev.*) argue phagocytosis is bioenergetically expensive and physiologically a *feeding* mechanism that itself requires mitochondrial ATP and abundant oxygen — so it is unlikely to predate mitochondria. Lane & Martin (2010) argue an amitochondriate phagocyte could not have afforded the genomic/energetic cost of becoming complex.

**Key empirical evidence:** Phagocytosis is widespread among extant eukaryotes; the host clade encodes ESPs consistent with membrane dynamics. **What would falsify it:** Demonstration that the archaeal host lineage was small, wall-bound, and energetically incapable of phagocytosis (trending this way with cultured Asgard data); robust ancestral-state reconstruction placing phagocytosis *after* mitochondria.

**Shift over ~10 years:** Weakened as a *standalone, mitochondria-late* account. A 2022 ancestral-state reconstruction (the Gould group, *Genome Biology and Evolution*) traced mitochondria — but **not** phagocytosis — to LECA. The cultured Asgard archaeon shows no phagocytosis. However, the *mechanism* of engulfment remains defensible because nothing yet rules out a non-phagocytotic or primitive-phagocytotic enclosure.

### Model 2 — Syntrophy-first / mitochondria-early (metabolic partnership as driver)

**The strongest case.** Metabolic symbioses (syntrophy) between archaea and bacteria — especially based on interspecies hydrogen/formate transfer — are pervasive and well-characterized in nature. Under this view, a metabolic dependency between an archaeal host and a bacterial partner *preceded* and *drove* the physical association; enclosure followed as the partnership tightened, without requiring classical phagocytosis. Mitochondria (or their precursor) would then be "early," potentially enabling subsequent complexity. The single cultured Asgard archaeon fits this picture strikingly: it is an obligate syntroph that grows by degrading amino acids/peptides and depends on H₂/formate-scavenging partners.

**Named proponents:** William Martin & Miklós Müller (hydrogen hypothesis, 1998 *Nature* 392:37-41); David Moreira & Purificación López-García (syntrophy hypothesis, 1998 *J. Mol. Evol.* 47:517-530; updated "HS-Syntrophy" 2020 *Nature Microbiology* 5:655-667); Spang et al. (reverse-flow model, 2019 *Nature Microbiology*); Imachi et al. (Entangle-Engulf-Endogenize, 2020 *Nature*); Nick Lane & William Martin (energetics, 2010).

**Named critics:** Phagotrophy proponents (Cavalier-Smith) argue syntrophy models struggle to explain the *autogenous* origin of phagocytosis, the cytoskeleton and the nucleus, and that nothing distinguishes a phagotrophic archaeon from a protoeukaryote. Critics also note the original hydrogen/syntrophy models assumed a methanogen host, which the Asgard phylogeny and metabolic reconstructions do not support (Asgard hosts are not methanogens).

**Key empirical evidence:** Ubiquity of syntrophy in anaerobic ecosystems; the cultured Asgard archaeon's obligate syntrophic physiology and lack of organelles; the chimeric (archaeal + bacterial) nature of eukaryotic genomes. **What would falsify it:** Evidence that mitochondria entered an *already-complex* phagocytosing host (mitochondria-late); demonstration that no plausible metabolic hand-off existed between host and the alphaproteobacterial ancestor (which, unlike the H₂-producing partner in the original models, may have been a facultative aerobe).

**Shift over ~10 years:** Strengthened. The discovery and especially the *cultivation* of Asgard archaea moved "long-held views… in favour of symbiogenetic models based on metabolic interactions between archaea and bacteria" (López-García & Moreira 2020). This is the clearest directional shift in the field.

### Model 3 — Topological / hybrid models

**Inside-out (Baum & Baum 2014, *BMC Biology* 12:76):** Proposes the proto-eukaryote was an archaeal cell that extruded membrane-bound "blebs" (protrusions) outward through its cell wall; these blebs engulfed ectosymbiotic proto-mitochondria from the *outside in*, and the spaces between blebs became the endomembrane system/ER, with the original archaeal cell body becoming the nucleus. Adds a concrete cell-biological route to eukaryotic topology that requires *no* phagocytosis and is compatible with a syntrophic driver. **Proponents:** David Baum, Buzz Baum. **Critics/standing:** Influential as a framework (and notably resonant with the protrusions seen in cultured Asgard archaea), but largely untested directly; treated as a hypothesis-generating topology rather than an established sequence.

**Entangle-Engulf-Endogenize (E³) (Imachi et al. 2020):** Based on the cultured Asgard archaeon's morphology (protrusions/blebs), proposes the H₂-producing archaeon physically entangled and gradually internalized the alphaproteobacterial partner via its protrusions. Explicitly a syntrophy-driven, non-phagocytotic enclosure model.

**Reverse flow (Spang et al. 2019):** Metabolic model in which the archaeal host transferred reduced compounds/electrons to the bacterial partner; refines the *direction* of metabolic exchange relative to the hydrogen hypothesis.

These hybrid models matter because they dissolve the false binary: they pair a **syntrophic driver** with a **non-phagocytotic enclosure mechanism**, showing that "how the bug got in" need not be predation.

## Sub-Question A: Host identity and complexity (metagenomic vs. cultured)

Phylogenomics robustly places the eukaryotic host within or sister to the **Asgard archaea**. Spang et al. (2015, *Nature* 521:173) described Lokiarchaeota from Loki's Castle vent metagenomes as the closest archaeal relative of eukaryotes, with a eukaryotic-feature "starter kit." Zaremba-Niedzwiedzka et al. (2017, *Nature* 541:353) expanded this to the Asgard superphylum. Subsequent work (e.g., 2023, *Nature*) refined the placement of eukaryotes near the Heimdallarchaeia/Hodarchaeales within Asgard.

**The single most important caveat: most of this rests on metagenome-assembled genomes (MAGs), not cultured organisms.** This drew an explicit dissent: **Da Cunha, Gaia, Forterre et al. (2017, *PLoS Genetics* 13:e1006810)** argued the eukaryote–Loki affiliation was an artifact of chimeric sequences and unequal evolutionary rates, and that Asgard are sister to Euryarchaeota, not eukaryotes — claiming that removing a single protein (EF2) broke the affiliation. That specific claim was directly contested by Spang et al. (2018, *PLoS Genetics* 13:e1007080), who reported that "the claim 'removal of the EF-2 is sufficient to break the Eukaryotes-Lokiarchaea affiliation' has been disproven by phylogenetic analyses performed on three different concatenated datasets… none of which included elongation factor EF-2." The eocyte/two-domain (eukaryotes within archaea) topology is now the majority view but is not unanimous.

**The cultured organism is decisive for physiology.** Imachi et al. (2020, *Nature* 577:519) reported the first cultured Asgard archaeon, obtained as a pure co-culture with *Methanogenium* sp. strain MK-MG after a 12-year study — "from bioreactor-based pre-enrichment of deep-sea sediments to a final 7 years of in vitro enrichment" — from Nankai Trough methane-seep sediment at 2,533 m depth. It was initially named "*Candidatus* Prometheoarchaeum syntrophicum" strain MK-D1. The 2024 formal description (Imachi et al., *Int. J. Syst. Evol. Microbiol.* 74:006435) corrected the spelling to ***Promethearchaeum syntrophicum*** gen. nov., sp. nov. (dropping the "o" per nomenclatural rules) and proposed the family **Promethearchaeaceae**, order **Promethearchaeales**, class **Promethearchaeia**, phylum **Promethearchaeota** and kingdom **Promethearchaeati**. Observed physiology/morphology:
- Small cocci, ~300–750 nm diameter (~550 nm typical); anaerobic; mesophilic (optimum ~20 °C); extremely slow-growing — with a 30–60-day lag phase, requiring >3 months to reach full growth, and an estimated doubling time of ~14–25 days (Imachi et al. 2020).
- Obligately **syntrophic** — degrades amino acids/peptides with obligate dependence on H₂-/formate-scavenging partners (a methanogen and/or a sulfate-reducing bacterium); high H₂ inhibits it.
- Produces membrane vesicles, chains of blebs, and membrane-based protrusions.
- **No visible organelle-like internal structures; no observed phagocytosis.**

A second cultured Asgard, "*Candidatus* Lokiarchaeum ossiferum" (Rodrigues-Oliveira et al. 2023, *Nature* 613:332), grows faster, has a larger genome, ESPs at ~5% of coding genes including four actin homologs, and a visualized actin cytoskeleton with extensive tentacle-like protrusions imaged by cryo-electron tomography.

**Interpretation:** The cultured data show host cells that are *structurally more complex than typical prokaryotes* (cytoskeleton, elaborate protrusions) but are *small, slow, wall-bearing, anaerobic syntrophs with no phagocytic apparatus and no organelles.* This cuts against the "already-complex phagocytosing proto-eukaryote" required by strong mitochondria-late models, and supports a host that acquired its partner via metabolic interdependence and protrusion-mediated contact. The morphology (protrusions/blebs) is strikingly consistent with both inside-out and E³ enclosure mechanisms.

## Sub-Question B: Timing (mitochondria-early vs. mitochondria-late)

The field frames this as an explicit axis (Roger, Susko & Leger 2021, *Current Biology*, "Reconstructing the Timeline of Eukaryogenesis," depict mitochondria-early, -intermediate, and -late scenarios). All current models agree mitochondria were present in LECA and that endosymbiosis occurred pre-LECA. The dispute is whether the bacterium arrived in a *simple* host and triggered complexity (early) or a *complex* host that had already evolved phagocytosis and endomembranes (late).

- **Mitochondria-late** received notable phylogenomic support from Pittis & Gabaldón (2016, *Nature* 531:101): eukaryotic genes of mitochondrial/alphaproteobacterial origin had *shorter* "stem lengths" than many other bacterially-derived host genes, implying the host had already accreted diverse bacterial genes (was already chimeric/complex) before mitochondrial acquisition. This was **contested**: Martin et al. argued "late mitochondrial origin is pure artefact" (bioRxiv/critique) and Degli Esposti (2016, *Genome Biol. Evol.*) argued many "early" bacterial assignments were misinferred.
- **Mitochondria-early** has gained ground with Asgard cultivation: a simple syntrophic host fits the early-acquisition picture, and energetic/selective-pressure arguments (below) posit the organelle as a trigger.

**Current read:** opinion is genuinely split but has *shifted toward "early"/symbiosis-driven* among researchers working on archaeal hosts, while phylogenomic timing analyses remain methodologically contested. This is a "view in transition," not a settled consensus.

## Sub-Question C: Driver / mechanism — relative support

- **Syntrophy/metabolic-interdependence as driver** currently enjoys the broadest momentum, anchored by (i) the ubiquity of syntrophy in nature, (ii) the obligately syntrophic physiology of the only cultured host, and (iii) the chimeric genome signal. Its main weakness: the original models posited a methanogen host and an H₂-producing partner, whereas the actual host is a non-methanogenic organoheterotroph and the mitochondrial ancestor's original metabolic gift to the host is not pinned down (updated models invoke sulfur/electron cycling or facultative aerobic metabolism).
- **Predation/phagotrophy as mechanism** retains the advantage that engulfment is the only *observed* route for cell-in-cell entry, but faces the objection that phagocytosis is energetically costly, is a feeding strategy dependent on pre-existing mitochondrial ATP, and is not robustly reconstructed in LECA's pre-mitochondrial host.

**Are the framings mutually exclusive?** Largely **no** — and this is a key finding. Syntrophy is primarily a claim about the *selective driver* and ecological context; phagocytosis is primarily a claim about the *physical enclosure mechanism*. One can have a syntrophy-driven association enclosed by a non-phagocytotic mechanism (inside-out, E³), or a predation event that becomes metabolically symbiotic. They become genuinely competing only in their strong forms: "complex phagocytosing proto-eukaryote engulfs prey for food" (strong predation) versus "simple archaeal syntroph engulfs partner without phagocytosis" (strong syntrophy).

**Is "predation gone symbiotic" defensible today?** It is **one of several live options, and a weakened one** — not the established account and not fully outdated. The strong version (a complex amitochondriate phagocyte hunting bacteria) is increasingly hard to reconcile with the cultured-host data; the weak version (engulfment as enclosure step) survives but is no longer the default framing among host-focused specialists.

## Sub-Question D: Energetics of complexity — the Lane–Martin vs. Lynch–Marinov exchange

This is the most quantitatively contested thread, and verifying the actual analyses (not abstracts) matters.

**Lane & Martin (2010, *Nature* 467:929):** Claimed that relocating bioenergetic membranes internally (mitochondria, with local mtDNA for redox control) permitted a "200,000-fold expansion in the number of genes expressed" and gave eukaryotes orders of magnitude more "energy per gene" (they later restated this as 10⁵–10⁶ times more power per gene), making this the *prerequisite* (prime mover) for eukaryotic complexity. This is an explicitly singular historical argument, not a statistical law.

**Lynch & Marinov (2015, *PNAS* 112:15690, "The bioenergetic costs of a gene"; and 2017, *eLife* 6:e20437, "Membranes, energetics…"):** Using cellular energetics/genomics data across cell sizes, they found that the cost of a gene *as a fraction of a cell's lifetime ATP budget* declines with cell volume in both bacteria and eukaryotes, with **continuous, near-isometric scaling and no discontinuity at the prokaryote–eukaryote divide** (scaling exponents ~0.88 and ~0.97). Their stated conclusion: "an energetic boost associated with the emergence of the mitochondrion was not a precondition for eukaryotic genome-size expansion," and bioenergetics played no "direct, causal role" in the transition; in fact they concluded a shift of bioenergetics to mitochondria "conferred no directly favorable energetic effects. In fact, the effect appears to be negative." Their alternative driver is population-genetic: eukaryotes' smaller effective population sizes (Nₑ) weaken selection, letting genomes expand passively below the drift barrier.

**The exchange (2016, *PNAS* 113):** Lane & Martin (E666, "evolutionary deficit spending") replied that Lynch & Marinov measured *demand* (cost) while the 2010 argument was about *supply* (energy availability per gene), that ATP synthesis is constrained by membrane surface area while protein-synthesis cost scales with volume, and that giant polyploid bacteria pay an ignored cost of extreme polyploidy ("In giant prokaryotes, the cell interior is not complex, it is inert"). Lynch & Marinov (E667) replied that the literal "200,000-fold" figure is untenable (real eukaryotic gene counts are ~6,000–40,000, a 1.2–8× expansion, not the ~10⁹ genes implied), that "energy per gene" is "an ecologically and evolutionarily meaningless definition," and that membrane surface area does not in fact constrain ATP production.

**Data-quality dispute (2018, *Biology Direct* 13:21):** Gerlitz, Knopp, Kapust, Xavier & Martin (from Martin's lab) audited the table underlying Lynch & Marinov's 2017 Figure 2 and reported that, of 80 audited values, 48 (60%) "do not exist in the cited papers," were incorrect, or were not reproducible — for example, a *Tetrahymena* "ribosome count" of 7,490,000 traced back to an optical-density (OD₂₅₉) measurement, not a ribosome number, and a HeLa cell volume reported to 1/1000 µm³ (2798.668) against a source value of 2,600 µm³. (Lynch & Marinov's 2017 paper had already required a formal eLife correction, 2018;7:e35006, for errors in that same table.) Lynch & Marinov (2018, *Biology Direct* 13:26) responded that they had already re-annotated the data with no change in conclusions, and that the audit attacked a distributional claim distinct from their scaling claim.

**Current standing:** The **strong "energetic prime mover / energy per gene"** argument is **contested and not accepted as a quantitative law**; independent reassessments (Chiyomaru & Takemoto 2020, *R. Soc. Open Sci.* 7:191859, which found "a limited contribution of power per genome and power per gene to the prokaryote–eukaryote dichotomy"; Schavemaker & Muñoz-Gómez 2022, *Nature Ecology & Evolution* 6:1307) tend to find the strong energetic-barrier version weak or unsupported while not fully vindicating the pure drift model. The "mitochondria mattered" intuition survives in a **reframed** form — Raval, Garg & Gould (2022, *eLife* 11:e81033) shift the emphasis from raw bioenergetics to *endosymbiotic selective pressures* (e.g., intron/Muller's-ratchet dynamics driving the nucleus). The existence of fully complex but mitochondrion-free *Monocercomonoides exilis* (Karnkowska et al. 2016 *Curr. Biol.*; 2019 *MBE*) shows eukaryotic complexity can be *maintained* without any bioenergetic organelle — a secondary loss that does not bear on the origin but does undercut the strongest "strictly dependent on mitochondrial power" claim.

**Epistemic flag:** This entire debate is **dominated by two labs** (Lynch/Marinov vs. Lane/Martin/Gould/Gerlitz) that generate both the data and the critiques; few fully independent datasets exist (Chiyomaru & Takemoto being a notable exception). Treat both the "200,000-fold" figure and the "perfect continuity" claim as contested, single-lab-anchored assertions.

## Sub-Question E (SEPARATE, LESS-RESOLVED): Origin of the nucleus

Mark clearly: **this is a distinct and substantially less-resolved question than A–D, and it does not determine the predation-vs-syntrophy question.** Three families of proposals:
- **Symbiosis side-effect / intron defense (Martin & Koonin 2006, *Nature* 440:41):** The nuclear envelope arose to spatially separate slow mRNA splicing from fast translation, after group II introns from the mitochondrial endosymbiont invaded the host genome and decayed into spliceosomal introns. This ties nucleus origin causally to mitochondrial acquisition (and to mitochondria-early framing).
- **Autogenous / topological:** Inside-out (Baum & Baum 2014) makes the nucleus the original archaeal cell body; other autogenous models derive it from invaginated membranes. Cavalier-Smith treats the nucleus as autogenous and coincident with phagotrophy.
- **Viral eukaryogenesis (Bell 2001, *J. Mol. Evol.*; Takemura 2001, *J. Mol. Evol.*):** The nucleus descends from a large DNA virus (poxvirus-like/NCLDV) that established a persistent "viral factory" in an archaeal host. Revived by giant-virus discoveries (medusavirus, and the recently described "ushikuvirus" — Bae et al. 2025, *Journal of Virology* 99:12, isolated from Lake Ushiku, Japan; it infects the amoeba *Vermamoeba vermiformis*, carries a >660,000-bp genome encoding 784 genes, and is most closely related to clandestinovirus near family *Mamonoviridae*), and by parallels between NCLDV membrane-bound viral factories and the nucleus. **Standing:** explicitly "the most controversial" hypothesis; intriguing but unproven, awaiting demonstration of an archaeal virus that builds a nucleus-like membrane compartment. Notably, even proponents caution that ushikuvirus does not by itself strengthen the viral-eukaryogenesis hypothesis — and unlike medusavirus/clandestinovirus, which replicate inside an intact nucleus, ushikuvirus disrupts the nuclear membrane. Much of the recent coverage is in press/popular outlets and should be flagged as such.

## Consensus Assessment

- **Dominant view:** Eukaryotes arose from a symbiosis between an Asgard-related archaeal host and an alphaproteobacterium; the genomes-and-cell-biology evidence for this is strong and broadly replicated.
- **Where the center of gravity has moved:** Among specialists working on the host lineage, toward **symbiogenic/metabolic-partnership (syntrophy) framings**, and (more weakly) toward **mitochondria-early**, driven by Asgard discovery and cultivation. López-García & Moreira (2020) describe "long-held views… metamorphosing in favour of symbiogenetic models based on metabolic interactions."
- **Genuine, active dissents:** (i) phagotrophy-first/mitochondria-late (Cavalier-Smith legacy; Pittis & Gabaldón timing signal); (ii) the phylogenetic placement itself (Forterre group); (iii) the energetic prime-mover thesis (Lane & Martin) vs. its rejection (Lynch & Marinov).
- **Confidence:** High that endosymbiosis happened and the host was archaeal; **low-to-moderate** on mechanism, driver, timing, and host complexity. The accurate characterization is **"a dominant symbiotic framework with the mechanism/driver/timing in transition and unresolved,"** not a settled consensus for either predation or syntrophy.

## What Would Change the Assessment (decisive evidence still outstanding)
- **A cultured Asgard archaeon observed to internalize a bacterial partner** (by any mechanism) — would directly test enclosure routes; protrusion-mediated (E³/inside-out) vs. phagocytotic.
- **Direct evidence on the original metabolic hand-off** between host and the alphaproteobacterial ancestor (what the bacterium gave the host) — would adjudicate which syntrophy model, if any.
- **Robust, artifact-resistant ancestral-state reconstruction** of phagocytosis relative to mitochondria in the FECA→LECA interval.
- **Resolution of the host's phylogenetic position** with cultured-genome-quality data (settling the eocyte vs. three-domain dispute and the Forterre critique).
- **An archaeal virus shown to build a nucleus-like membrane compartment** — would materially raise the viral-nucleus hypothesis (currently the field calls this the would-be "game over" experiment).
- **Independent (non-partisan-lab) energetics datasets** — to settle whether any bioenergetic discontinuity exists at the divide.

## Epistemic Caveats
- **Metagenomic vs. cultured:** The Asgard-host phylogeny rests heavily on MAGs; only two Asgard lineages are cultured, and the physiological inferences that most constrain the mechanism question come from those two organisms (one lab each: Imachi/JAMSTEC; Schleper-Pilhofer/Vienna-ETH). Strong physiological generalizations from n≈2 cultured strains should be held loosely.
- **Single-lab vs. broadly replicated:** The energetics debate is essentially two labs; the late-acquisition timing signal is one group (Gabaldón) contested by another (Martin); the phylogenetic dissent is largely one group (Forterre). The *fact* of archaeal-host + alphaproteobacterial-symbiont origin, by contrast, is broadly replicated.
- **Popular vs. peer-reviewed:** Claims about "tentacled ancestors," the viral-nucleus revival, and "missing link" framing are amplified in press outlets (Science news, Quanta, Live Science, university press releases). The peer-reviewed cores (Imachi 2020/2024; Rodrigues-Oliveira 2023; Bell/Takemura; Bae et al. 2025) are sound, but the sweeping evolutionary interpretations in popular coverage outrun the data.
- **Hypothesis ≠ result:** Inside-out, E³, reverse-flow, and viral-nucleus are *models/hypotheses*, not demonstrated sequences. Naming a model is not evidence for it.
- **Nucleus origin** is flagged as a separate, less-resolved question and should not be read as bearing on the predation-vs-syntrophy decision.
