# Development, Phylogeny, and Executive Function: A State-of-Evidence Report

## 1. What is well established (strongest defensible form)

1. In human children, prefrontal-dependent abilities are measurable well before adult levels: rudimentary working memory and response inhibition are detectable in the first year (roughly 5–12 months), and performance improves along a protracted, non-linear trajectory extending into at least the early twenties. **Established.**
2. Behavioral performance on inhibitory-control tasks varies across non-human species, and in large comparative datasets absolute brain size (and, in an alternative account, absolute pallial/cortical neuron number) predicts performance better than relative brain size. **Established (as a statistical regularity); contested (as to mechanism).**
3. Humans have an unusually protracted postnatal course of prefrontal synaptic development relative to macaques, with chimpanzees intermediate/human-like; peak PFC synaptic markers shift from <1 year in macaques to roughly mid-childhood in humans. **Established at the level of tissue/gene-expression; the cognitive consequences are inference.**
4. Whether "executive function" is one ability or several is not settled and appears to change with age; measurement (test-retest reliability, factor structure, cross-species/cross-age invariance) is weak enough that many ordering claims cannot currently be distinguished from orderings of task difficulty. **Established that measurement is the binding constraint.**
5. A direct, quantitative test mapping the ontogenetic emergence order of executive functions onto a cross-species (phylogenetic) performance ordering **does not exist in the literature.** The recapitulation-style quantitative method has been executed for primate manipulative motor skills and for digital organisms, but not for executive function. **Established as an empty cell.**

## 2. Question 1: Developmental evidence

### 2.1 Ages of first above-chance appearance

**Working memory.** Observation: infants maintain a representation across a short delay from ~5–8 months (looking-based and reaching paradigms). Inference: this indexes a precursor of working memory. Scope: high-income Western samples, small-N lab tasks. **Contested→established** that *some* maintenance exists in the first year; the construct label "working memory" at this age is an inference, not a direct observation.

**Inhibitory / response inhibition.** The A-not-B / delayed-response task (Piaget; operationalized by Diamond) shows a graded developmental signature: at 7.5–9 months infants tolerate only 2–5 s delays before perseverating; by 12 months they tolerate >10 s (Diamond & Goldman-Rakic, 1989, *Exp Brain Res*), with delay tolerance rising ~2 s per month. Rhesus monkeys with dorsolateral PFC lesions reproduce the 7.5–9-month human error pattern (errors at 2–5 s, chance at 10 s). Observation: the graded delay-tolerance function and the lesion-monkey parallel. Inference: DLPFC maturation drives the improvement. This is the single most-cited brain-behavior anchor in the developmental literature and rests substantially on one small lesion-plus-longitudinal study from 1989. **Contested** as to whether A-not-B is specifically a PFC/inhibition measure (see Thelen dynamic-field reinterpretation, §7).

**Response inhibition in infancy (modern).** Preregistered longitudinal fNIRS work (Fiske, Holmboe and colleagues; Early Childhood Inhibitory Touchscreen Task, cohort N>100 tested at 10 months, 16 months, 3.5 years) reports right-lateralized DLPFC/parietal activation during inhibition at 10 months, but behavioral inhibition did *not* improve from 10 to 16 months, then improved by 3.5 years. Observation: a dissociation between early neural recruitment and behavioral gain. **Established (single cohort, preregistered).**

**Task switching / cognitive flexibility.** Above-chance flexible switching is typically first robust on the Dimensional Change Card Sort around 3–5 years; 3-year-olds perseverate on the post-switch rule, 4–5-year-olds switch. **Established** for the DCCS trajectory in preschoolers.

**Goal maintenance / proactive control.** Children rely on reactive control and shift toward proactive (anticipatory) control across middle childhood and adolescence; adolescents still show incomplete proactive control and continued reliance on reactive control. **Contested→established** as a qualitative shift rather than a simple quantitative gain.

**Delay of gratification.** Preschoolers can delay for progressively longer intervals from ~3–5 years. The predictive-validity claim (that early delay forecasts adult outcomes) has substantially collapsed under preregistered replication. Watts, Duncan & Quan (2018, *Psychological Science*) reported that "an additional minute waited at age 4 predicted a gain of approximately 1/10th of a SD in age-15 achievement. But this bivariate correlation was only half the size of those reported in the original studies and was reduced by two thirds in the presence of controls." Sperber et al. (2024, *Child Development*, N=702, 83% White, 46% male) extended this to adulthood and concluded Marshmallow performance "does not reliably predict adult functioning," detecting only "modest bivariate associations … with educational attainment (r = .17) and body mass index (r = −.17), [with] almost all regression-adjusted coefficients … nonsignificant." **Established** that the strong long-run predictive claim is not supported.

### 2.2 Shape of trajectory and end of maturation

The trajectory is non-linear and protracted, not a single smooth curve, and components dissociate. A lifespan battery (N=350, ages 10–86) found working memory and planning improving into young adulthood, inhibitory control peaking in early adulthood, and declines in working memory and inhibition beginning as early as 30–40 years; cognitive flexibility dissociated (switch costs fell while mixing costs rose across the lifespan). **Established** that components have different peaks and different aging profiles.

**Evidentiary basis for "maturation ends at ~25."** This claim is weakly grounded. It traces to 1990s–2000s structural MRI. Giedd et al. (1999, *Nature Neuroscience*) analyzed 243 scans from 89 males and 56 females aged 4 to 22 years, with frontal/parietal gray matter peaking "at about age 12" — meaning age 25 lies well beyond the observed range and was an extrapolated, not observed, endpoint; Gogtay et al. (2004) scanned participants only into their early twenties. Multiple recent analyses characterize "the brain matures at 25" as a myth: there is no dataset showing a sharp cutoff at 25, and newer network/connectivity work suggests changes continue into the 30s. **The specific "25" endpoint is a claim in circulation not supported by primary evidence (see §8).**

### 2.3 Non-WEIRD and cross-population evidence

EF trajectories have been documented outside high-income Western settings (Ecuadorian preschoolers, N=982; a rural Pakistan cohort with six adapted EF tasks; three LMIC birth cohorts in Guatemala/Philippines/South Africa; an India scoping review). Two findings matter for ordering claims: (a) in LMIC adult cohorts EF often loads onto a *single* factor associated with schooling, using NIH Toolbox subtests; (b) a schooling-vs-non-schooling comparison (UK vs Kunene region, Namibia/Angola; *PNAS* 2025) reported "profound, sometimes qualitative" differences on standard EF tasks, arguing typical EF measures capture "culturally specific" skills tied to schooling rather than only universal capacities. **Established** that standard EF tasks are not culture-neutral; this threatens any universal developmental ordering.

## 3. Question 2: Comparative and evolutionary evidence

### 3.1 Behavioral batteries with matched procedures

**Primate Cognition Test Battery (PCTB; Herrmann et al. 2007, *Science*).** Samples: 105 human children (52 males, 53 females; 2.5 years ±2 months) from a medium-sized city in Germany, 106 chimpanzees (Ngamba Island and Tchimpounga sanctuaries), and 32 orangutans (Orangutan Care Center and Quarantine, Pasir Panjang, Kalimantan, Indonesia). Result: children and apes similar in the *physical* domain; children superior in the *social* domain (basis of the "cultural intelligence hypothesis"). Extensions: Old World monkeys (long-tailed macaques, olive baboons) performed comparably to apes on most scales (Schmitt et al.); lemurs ("lemur baseline") broadly comparable in the physical domain; four parrot species performed at chance throughout, raising doubt about whether the PCTB travels beyond primates. **Established** as a data pattern; **contested** because tasks were designed around human-relevant skills and may underestimate non-human competencies.

**MacLean et al. (2014, *PNAS*) "The evolution of self-control."** 567 individuals, 36 species, two tasks (cylinder detour, A-not-B). Result: absolute brain volume best predicted performance and accounted for more variance than body-mass-corrected volume; within primates, dietary breadth (not social group size) predicted performance. **Established** as reported; **contested** on measurement grounds (see §5).

**Corvids and beyond.** Ravens, New Caledonian crows, and jackdaws matched great apes on the cylinder task despite far smaller absolute brains (Kabadayi et al. 2016; ravens reportedly reaching 100% accuracy), directly challenging the absolute-brain-size account. Domestic cats, goats, and dogs also perform substantially; parrots underperform relative to their high neuron counts. **Established** that cylinder performance is not monotonic in brain size across clades.

**ManyPrimates (2019 pilot; 2022).** Large collaborative, preregistered, matched-procedure delayed-response (short-term memory) study: pilot 176 individuals/12 species/11 sites; full study 421 individuals/41 species/29 sites. Result: shorter delays → better performance; a strong phylogenetic signal for short-term memory (validity limited by species number). **Established** as the strongest infrastructure for matched cross-species cognition; explicitly self-limiting on inference.

### 3.2 Comparative neuroanatomy and scaling

The observation/inference chain differs sharply by lab:
- **Smaers / Passingham & Wise lineage:** granular PFC areas are evolutionarily recent, appearing in early primates/strepsirrhines and elaborating in simians; PFC shows "exceptional" expansion in great apes and humans across specific evolutionary windows (hominoid ~30–19 mya, hominid ~19–15 mya, human-chimpanzee ~8–6 mya, human ~6–0 mya) (Smaers et al. 2017, *Current Biology*). **Contested.**
- **Herculano-Houzel / Gabi et al. (2016, *PNAS*):** the prefrontal region holds ~8% of cortical neurons in humans *and* other primates, with no disproportionate human expansion — prefrontal expansion followed the same allometric trajectory as other primates. **Contested; directly opposes the "exceptional expansion" claim.**

This is a genuine, unresolved taxonomic/quantitative disagreement about whether the human PFC is "special," turning on measurement choices (cytoarchitecture vs volume vs neuron count; 13 vs 19 vs larger species samples).

### 3.3 Developmental-timing (heterochrony) differences across species

**Synaptic/gene-expression timing.** Liu et al. (2012, *Genome Research*): peak synaptic-gene expression in PFC shifts from <1 year in chimpanzees and macaques to ~5 years in humans, corroborated in the Results by synaptic-marker protein profiles (Western blot and proteomics peaking at 4–8 yr in human PFC vs first year in chimpanzee/macaque) and electron microscopy. Bianchi et al. (2013, *PNAS*): chimpanzees, like humans, show delayed PFC synaptic peak (~3–5 yr) and prolonged pruning, contrasting macaques (peak ~3 months) — i.e., the "protracted" pattern is partly shared with chimpanzees, not uniquely human. **Established at tissue level; the two papers conflict on whether the delay is human-specific.**

**Finlay & Darlington "translating time."** Across 18+ mammalian species and 271 developmental events, the *order* of neurodevelopmental events is highly conserved and predictable from a single model (modeled-to-empirical correlation ≈0.99), with a systematic "primate/limbic" delay in corticogenesis associated with larger cortices. Observation: conserved sequence + heterochronic shifts. Inference: cross-species maturational states can be aligned. **Established** as a modeling result; it is about neural events, not executive-function abilities — a level-of-analysis gap relevant to Question 3.

### 3.4 Paleoanthropology / cognitive archaeology

Inference chain (observation → cognitive claim, weakest link named): stone-tool morphology and behaviors (Oldowan flakes ~2.6–3.3 mya; Acheulean handaxes ~1.75 mya; hafted points ~100 kya) → inferred executive/working-memory demands. Stout, Hecht and colleagues used fMRI/EEG in modern knappers: Acheulean (not Oldowan) toolmaking recruited dorsal PFC and predicted strategic-judgment success, "corroborating" central-executive demands (verified against Results of the *PLOS ONE* study; effect magnitude correlated with correct strategic judgments). Coolidge & Wynn propose an "enhanced working memory" event within the last ~200 kyr. **Theoretical inference / speculative.** Weakest link: the analogy that modern brains performing a reconstructed task reveal the cognition of extinct hominins, who may have produced identical objects by different cognitive routes. The imported claim of ~100% heritability of an EF factor (from twin studies) does not establish a datable genetic "EF enhancement" event; it is a within-population variance statistic, not evidence of a phylogenetic step.

## 4. Question 3: The relationship between the sequences

**Bottom line: For executive function specifically, the question is not currently answerable with available evidence. The strongest honest classification is "no established correspondence," because the direct test has never been run and measurement cannot yet support the ability-ordering on either side.**

Evidence bearing on this:

1. **No direct quantitative test exists.** No peer-reviewed study cross-tabulates the developmental emergence order of inhibitory control, working memory, switching, and delay of gratification in children against a cross-species EF performance ranking with statistics. This is an empty research cell, not a mixed literature. Adjacent comparative EF work — the lemur cognitive-control battery (De Petrillo & Rosati, N=39, 4 species; ecological rather than social predictors), the macaque inhibitory-control battery (Loyant et al. 2022), and Rosati's (2025) narrative review "Cognitive adaptations in humans and other great apes" — treats EF comparatively but performs no such ontogeny-vs-phylogeny cross-tabulation.

2. **The closest existing quantitative "ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny" test is about motor skills, not EF.** Heldstab et al. (2020, *Science Advances*), "When ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny: Fixed neurodevelopmental sequence of manipulative skills among primates" (36 species, N=128), found the within-individual developmental order of food-manipulation motor skills matched the adult cross-species complexity ordering almost exactly: 97% of species and 82% of individuals strictly followed the ontogenetic sequence. This is a strong demonstration of the *method* and a real correspondence — but for manipulative foraging skills, explicitly not for executive function. A comparable quantitative correspondence exists only elsewhere for digital organisms (Clune et al. 2012, *American Naturalist*, "ontogeny tends to recapitulate phylogeny in digital organisms"). Generalizing either to EF is unwarranted.

3. **Descriptive parallels exist but are confounded.** Diamond's A-not-B work is a genuine ontogeny/phylogeny parallel (7.5–9-month infants ≈ DLPFC-lesioned monkeys), and corvid detour-task development is reported to follow the same sequence as human/macaque infants but at a faster rate (Kabadayi thesis). These are descriptive correspondences at best, with no established shared mechanism, and each is vulnerable to the task-difficulty/affordance confound (§5).

4. **The neural-timing correspondence is real but at the wrong level.** Finlay's conserved neurodevelopmental sequence and the human PFC synaptic delay (Liu 2012; Bianchi 2013) establish heterochrony in *tissue* development. Mapping tissue heterochrony onto an ordered sequence of *cognitive abilities* is an inference no one has closed empirically.

Against the framework's menu of outcomes: (a) "correspond + mechanism identified" — **not supported**; (b) "correspond descriptively, no mechanism" — **partially supported for motor skills (Heldstab), not for EF**; (c) "partly correspond with specified exceptions" — **the honest state for scattered descriptive parallels (A-not-B, corvid detour)**; (d) "apparent correspondence is a measurement artifact" — **live and not excludable**, given §5; (e) "not currently answerable" — **the best-supported verdict for EF specifically.** These outcomes are not mutually exclusive across constructs: motor sequences lean toward (b), executive function toward (e)/(d).

## 5. Measurement validity (treated as primary, not caveat)

**Is EF one ability or several?** The unity/diversity model (three correlated factors: inhibition, updating, shifting) is the field's load-bearing framework but is age-dependent and disputed. Karr et al. (2018, *Psychological Bulletin*; systematic review and reanalysis of 46 samples, N=9,756) found child/adolescent samples most often best fit a *unidimensional* model, adults showed both unity and diversity, and — critically — low model acceptance/selection rates suggested "possible bias toward the publication of well-fitting but potentially nonreplicable models with underpowered samples." Some newer preschool CFA work (Veraksa et al. 2025, N=500, NEPSY-II + DCCS) argues a three-factor structure appears as early as age 5, contradicting the one-factor-in-early-childhood view. **Contested; the factor structure itself changes with age**, which undermines cross-age ordering.

**Test-retest reliability and the impurity problem.** Standard EF reaction-time tasks (flanker, Simon, antisaccade, switching) have acceptable reliability for raw mean RTs but poor reliability for the *difference scores* meant to isolate the executive component; Paap and colleagues (2016, *J Neurosci Methods*) report that the "impurity problem is worse than typically assumed" and that purer difference-score measures have low convergent validity partly caused by deficient test-retest reliability (interference scores reliably lower than switching costs). In young children (5–6 years, with and without ADHD), few individual EF tasks reached adequate reliability, though CFA-based latent working-memory and inhibition factors reached test-retest correlations approaching 1.0. **Established** that pure-process EF measurement is unreliable at the task level and only rescued at the latent-variable level.

**Comparative-task reliability.** The cylinder/A-not-B tasks have poor or rarely assessed repeatability. Loyant et al. (2025, *Biological Reviews*, DOI 10.1111/brv.70055) conclude motor-inhibition detour tasks suffer ceiling effects and weak cross-task validity. Concrete numbers: rhesus macaque touchscreen inhibitory-control tasks were significantly but weakly repeatable over two weeks (Loyant et al. 2022, *PeerJ*, N=21; adjusted repeatability R≈0.13, 95% CI ≈0.04–0.24, p<0.001 — note these were Distraction/Go-No-go/Reversal touchscreen tasks, not the physical cylinder); a companion-dog battery including the cylinder task showed age-adjusted ICC≈0.45, with raw cylinder scores rising ~9 points at retest (a practice effect; CANID 2025 preprint — provisional); robins' cylinder performance was not repeatable over a year (Shaw 2017), while Australian magpies' cylinder performance was repeatable juvenile→adult (Ashton et al. 2018). Pheasant studies found cylinder and detour tasks *uncorrelated* and contaminated by response learning (van Horik et al. 2018, *Proc R Soc B*). A reanalysis of the MacLean individual-level data (Völter et al. 2018) found no significant correlations among the inhibitory-control tasks. **Established** that comparative inhibitory-control tasks lack demonstrated convergent validity and stable individual-difference reliability.

**Measurement invariance.** Cross-age invariance of EF tasks is rarely tested despite being mandatory for legitimate age comparisons (Meredith's requirement); several factor-structure studies never tested it and merged age groups. Cross-species invariance (does the cylinder task measure the same construct in a corvid and a macaque?) has essentially never been formally established. **Absent, not mixed** — this is itself a substantive finding.

**Difficulty/motor/verbal confounds.** Cats' cylinder performance depended on apparatus size and material, not just inhibition (Bobrowicz & Osvath 2018); ape hand-tracking tasks may favor manual foragers over beak/mouth foragers; children's EF tasks confound verbal-instruction and decontextualization demands (the schooling effect, §2.3). An apparent developmental or evolutionary *ability* ordering could substantially be a *task-difficulty/affordance* ordering. **Established as a live threat** that directly feeds outcome (d) in Question 3.

## 6. Terminology crosswalk

| Term | Home literature | Maps to / conflict |
|---|---|---|
| Executive function(s) (EF) | Developmental & cognitive psych | Umbrella: inhibition + working memory/updating + shifting |
| Cognitive control | Cognitive neuroscience | Near-synonym for EF; used interchangeably by comparative labs |
| Self-control | Comparative cognition | Ambiguous: MacLean uses it for cylinder/A-not-B (motor), but self-control classically requires a value/temporal decision — Beran and others argue MacLean's tasks measure *motor self-regulation*, not self-control |
| Motor self-regulation / response inhibition | Comparative & developmental | Inhibiting a prepotent motor response (cylinder, detour); a subset of "inhibition" |
| Inhibitory control | Both | Diamond splits into interference control (attentional/cognitive) vs response inhibition; comparative work often collapses these |
| Delay of gratification | Developmental | A "hot"/value-based self-control; distinct from motor inhibition though often lumped |
| Effortful control | Temperament literature | Overlaps EF but measured via parent report/temperament, not identical |
| Working memory vs short-term memory | Developmental vs comparative | ManyPrimates "short-term memory" (delayed response) ≠ the manipulation-heavy "working memory" of human EF batteries; same word, different measured thing |
| Central executive | Baddeley WM model / cognitive archaeology | Imported into paleoanthropology as the faculty "enhanced" in *Homo sapiens* |

Two silent-merge hazards: (1) "self-control" used for both motor detour tasks and value-based delay tasks; (2) "working memory" used for both simple maintenance (infant/animal delayed response) and manipulation/updating (adult EF). Do not treat these as the same measured construct — much apparent cross-literature "agreement" is terminological coincidence.

## 7. Live disagreements (each in strongest form)

**A. Is EF unitary or fractionated in childhood?**
- *Unidimensional-in-childhood:* Best evidence — Karr et al. (2018) reanalysis, unidimensional fits most accepted in child/adolescent samples. Weakest point — could reflect underpowered designs / too-few-indicators, i.e., failure to *detect* diversity rather than its absence.
- *Early-differentiation:* Best evidence — Veraksa et al. (2025) three-factor fit at age 5. Weakest point — single battery, cultural/sampling specificity, no invariance test.

**B. What does absolute brain size explain?**
- *Absolute brain volume:* Best evidence — MacLean 2014, 36 species, robust phylogenetic signal. Weakest — corvids/cats/parrots break monotonicity; individual-level correlations vanish (Völter 2018).
- *Absolute pallial/cortical neuron number:* Best evidence — Herculano-Houzel; corvids have ape-comparable forebrain neuron counts (Olkowicz et al. 2016). Weakest — parrots with high neuron counts underperform on the cylinder task; neuron counts predict "flexibility/innovation," not specific task scores.

**C. Is the human PFC exceptionally expanded?**
- *Yes (Smaers 2017):* cytoarchitectonic granular-PFC hyperscaling, lateralized. Weakest — depends on a volume proxy and species sample.
- *No (Gabi/Herculano-Houzel 2016):* ~8% of cortical neurons in humans and other primates alike; same allometry. Weakest — "prefrontal region" defined coarsely (all cortex anterior to the corpus callosum).

**D. What does A-not-B measure?**
- *PFC/inhibition maturation (Diamond):* the DLPFC-lesion-monkey parallel. Weakest — one small foundational study; construct specificity unproven.
- *Sensorimotor dynamics (Thelen et al. 2001):* the error is competition between current and remembered reaches; changing infant posture between trials abolishes it, with no appeal to object permanence or PFC maturation. Weakest — does not by itself explain the monkey-lesion data.

**E. Are standard EF tasks universal or culturally specific?**
- *Universal capacity:* cross-country batteries load onto coherent EF factors (LMIC cohorts). Weakest — invariance rarely tested.
- *Culturally specific (Munakata, Doebel; PNAS 2025):* schooled vs non-schooled samples differ qualitatively, so tasks measure schooled cognition. Weakest — hard to separate "no capacity" from "no task familiarity."

## 8. Claims in circulation not supported by current evidence

1. **"The brain (PFC) fully matures at 25."** Source: extrapolation from 1990s–2000s structural MRI (Giedd et al. 1999, sample ages 4–22 with gray matter peaking ~age 12; Gogtay et al. 2004), whose samples stopped near age 20–22. No primary dataset shows a sharp 25 cutoff; recent connectivity work extends change into the 30s. **Not supported.**
2. **"The Marshmallow test predicts adult success."** Source: Shoda/Mischel/Peake (1990) with small, homogeneous samples. Attenuated by Watts et al. (2018) (age-15 effect half the original size, two-thirds reduced by controls) and overturned for adulthood by Sperber et al. (2024). **Not supported (strong long-run form).**
3. **"Ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny" as a general biological law (applied to cognition).** Source: Haeckel (1866); discarded in embryology since the mid-20th century (von Baer's objections; Gould 1977, *Ontogeny and Phylogeny*). Any cognitive-recapitulation claim inherits this baggage; only von Baer-style conservation of *sequence* (not ancestral-adult restaging) survives. **Not supported as a law.**
4. **"Human PFC underwent unique disproportionate neuronal expansion."** Contradicted by Gabi et al. (2016). **Contested→not supported in the strong form.**
5. **"MacLean's cylinder/A-not-B tasks measure self-control."** Terminologically disputed; on standard definitions they measure motor self-regulation (Beran 2015), which lacks the value/temporal decision that defines self-control. **Contested.**
6. **"Delayed PFC synaptogenesis is uniquely human."** Bianchi et al. (2013) show chimpanzees share the prolonged synaptic peak and pruning. **Not supported in the uniqueness form.**

## 9. Gaps and study designs that would settle them

1. **The core link (empty cell).** No study aligns a child EF emergence sequence with a cross-species EF ordering. *Design:* a preregistered, matched-battery study administering the *same* inhibition/WM/switching/delay tasks to a developmental human cohort and to multiple species, with formal measurement-invariance testing across age and species, followed by a statistical correspondence test (as Heldstab 2020 did for motor skills). Until invariance holds, correspondence tests are uninterpretable.
2. **Cross-species measurement invariance.** Never established for cylinder/A-not-B. *Design:* multi-species item-response/CFA with invariance testing; report per-species N and psychometrics (the ManyPrimates consortium is the natural vehicle).
3. **Cylinder-task reliability with tight CIs.** No clean species-general ICC exists. *Design:* multi-site test-retest with age controls and ceiling-effect mitigation (larger apparatus, graded difficulty).
4. **End of human EF maturation.** *Design:* accelerated longitudinal cohorts spanning ~15–40 years with harmonized tasks and change-point modeling; component-specific endpoints remain open.
5. **Non-WEIRD developmental trajectories.** *Design:* longitudinal EF in non-schooled and schooled majority-world samples with culturally adapted, invariance-tested tasks, to separate universal capacity from schooled performance.
6. **Datable cognitive-evolution events.** Paleoanthropological EF "enhancement" claims are untestable as stated; no design settles them from material culture alone. The weakest link (reverse-inferring extinct cognition from modern task performance) is intrinsic and should be flagged wherever such claims appear.
