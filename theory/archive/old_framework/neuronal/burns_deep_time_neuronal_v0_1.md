## Purpose

Reference material for the *Environment and tendencies* segment of the clade block (Book_Frame.md, Clade block §2). Burns presents the deep-time and environmental context for each clade as Lisa presents the clade's tendencies. This doc gives him the substrate to draw from: where the clade most likely emerged, when, under what climate and atmospheric conditions, against what evidentiary record. The doc is neutral reference. Burns transforms it into his own voice at draft time.

The biological clades (Protocell through Primate, Ch 1–8) use one category set. The symbolic-line clades (Band-Human through Empire-Human, Ch 9–12) use a category set adapted to a timescale where plate tectonics is essentially static and where geography, climate stability, and ecology carry more weight than tectonic and atmospheric variables.

Dates carry implicit uncertainty bars; "approximately" and "contested" are used where the science is actively debated.

## Biological clades (Ch 5–8)

Category set:

- **Time period.** When the clade's defining transition occurred.
- **Environment of emergence.** The most likely setting for the clade's origin.
- **Climate.** Surface temperature regime and major climatic variability.
- **Plate tectonics.** Continental configuration and tectonic regime.
- **Geological events.** Discrete events that shape the clade's window.
- **Atmosphere.** Atmospheric composition during the clade window.
- **Ocean and surface chemistry.** Marine and surface chemistry relevant to the clade.
- **Biotic context.** What else was alive, what the clade competed with or depended on.
- **Key fossil and evidence sites.** Where Burns can anchor specifics.
- **Signature evidentiary signal.** The marker that anchors the transition in the rock record.

### Bilaterian (Ch 5)

**Time period.** Late Ediacaran through Cambrian explosion (~580–520 Ma) for body-plan origins.

**Environment of emergence.** Shallow marine, soft sediment bottoms, with active swimmers entering the water column. Burrowing into sediment a major new mode.

**Climate.** Warm, equable, ice-free across the Cambrian. No major glaciations within the core window.

**Plate tectonics.** Gondwana assembling (~550 Ma onward). Laurentia, Baltica, and Siberia as separate continents distributed across low latitudes.

**Geological events.** The Cambrian explosion itself — appearance of nearly all modern phyla in the body-fossil record. Small Shelly Fauna preceding the explosion proper.

**Atmosphere.** Oxygen at perhaps 50–60% of modern levels (contested). CO2 still high; warm greenhouse.

**Ocean and surface chemistry.** Oxygenated shallow seas; deeper anoxia patchy. High phosphate availability assisting skeletal biomineralization. Calcium-carbonate seas.

**Biotic context.** Trilobites, early arthropods, mollusks, echinoderms, brachiopods, and primitive chordates. Predation pressure rising; first arms races between predators and prey.

**Key fossil and evidence sites.** Chengjiang (~518 Ma); Burgess Shale (~508 Ma); Sirius Passet (Greenland); Emu Bay Shale (S. Australia). Pikaia from the Burgess Shale as an early chordate.

**Signature evidentiary signal.** Head-tail organization; through-gut anatomy; bilaterian trace fossils (directional burrows); soft-tissue Lagerstätten preserving complete body plans.

### Vertebrate (Ch 6)

**Time period.** Earliest vertebrates approximately 520 Ma (Chengjiang's Haikouichthys, Myllokunmingia). Jaws by approximately 430 Ma (Silurian). Tetrapods by approximately 375 Ma (Devonian — Burns' research sweet spot).

**Environment of emergence.** Cambrian seas for vertebrate origins. Devonian shallow seas, freshwater swamps, and tidal-floodplain transitions for the tetrapod move onto land.

**Climate.** Warm Cambrian and Ordovician. Late Ordovician glaciation (~445 Ma) triggers the first major mass extinction. Warm Silurian and Devonian; the Devonian itself runs hot and humid.

**Plate tectonics.** Gondwana, Laurentia, Baltica converging. Caledonian and Acadian orogenies building mountains. Iapetus Ocean closing.

**Geological events.** Late Ordovician mass extinction (~445 Ma). Plants colonize land in the Silurian; arthropods follow. Late Devonian extinctions — Kellwasser (~372 Ma) and Hangenberg (~359 Ma) — both significant.

**Atmosphere.** Oxygen rising. Devonian CO2 drawn down as land plants spread and forests appear.

**Ocean and surface chemistry.** Well-oxygenated marine waters across most of the period. Freshwater habitats expanding; estuarine and floodplain habitats central to the tetrapod transition.

**Biotic context.** Jawless fish → jawed fish → lobe-finned fish → tetrapods. On land: vascular plants by mid-Silurian, forests by late Devonian (Gilboa). Arthropods on land; insects evolving.

**Key fossil and evidence sites.** Chengjiang (earliest vertebrates); Ellesmere Island (Tiktaalik, ~375 Ma); East Greenland (Ichthyostega, Acanthostega); the Old Red Sandstone; Gilboa fossil forest (NY state). Burns' tiktaalik work places him directly on this last set.

**Signature evidentiary signal.** Vertebral column with cranium; jaws (gnathostome transition); bony skeletons; the fin-to-limb transition recorded in Tiktaalik's wrist; first digits.

### Mammal (Ch 7)

**Time period.** Stem mammals from Late Triassic, approximately 225 Ma. Crown mammals across the Mesozoic in small-bodied nocturnal form. Major adaptive radiation after the K-Pg extinction at 66 Ma.

**Environment of emergence.** Small, nocturnal, insectivorous, in the shadow of dinosaurs throughout the Mesozoic. Forest-floor and arboreal niches. Explosive ecological diversification after K-Pg into every available niche.

**Climate.** Warm Mesozoic greenhouse. Cooling through the Cenozoic, with the Eocene-Oligocene transition (~34 Ma) marking a significant step down. Pleistocene glaciations from approximately 2.6 Ma.

**Plate tectonics.** Pangaea breaking up from the Triassic onward. Atlantic opening through the Jurassic and Cretaceous. India colliding with Asia in the Cenozoic, raising the Himalayas. Drake Passage opening triggers Antarctic glaciation.

**Geological events.** End-Triassic extinction (~201 Ma). K-Pg extinction (66 Ma) — Chicxulub asteroid impact plus Deccan Traps volcanism. Eocene-Oligocene transition. Pleistocene glacial cycles.

**Atmosphere.** High CO2 through the Mesozoic. Declining CO2 through the Cenozoic. Oxygen at modern levels for most of the clade window.

**Ocean and surface chemistry.** Mesozoic ocean anoxic events (e.g., OAE2 at the Cenomanian-Turonian boundary, ~94 Ma). Modern carbonate-chemistry regime through the Cenozoic.

**Biotic context.** Dinosaurs dominant in the Mesozoic; mammals small and nocturnal. Flowering plants radiate mid-Cretaceous. After K-Pg, mammals fill nearly every available niche. Grasslands expand in the Miocene, supporting a new ungulate-and-grazer ecosystem.

**Key fossil and evidence sites.** Karoo Basin (S. Africa) for synapsid ancestors. Solnhofen and Jehol biotas for early mammals alongside feathered dinosaurs. Messel (Germany, Eocene mammals). La Brea tar pits (Pleistocene Los Angeles).

**Signature evidentiary signal.** Hair, lactation, three middle-ear bones (the jawbone-to-ear-bone transition itself preserved in the fossil record), neocortex, endothermy, parental care.

### Primate (Ch 8)

**Time period.** Stem primates from approximately 65 Ma. Great apes from approximately 25 Ma. Hominin lineage splits from chimpanzee lineage approximately 6–7 Ma. *Homo sapiens* from approximately 300 ka.

**Environment of emergence.** Arboreal forest for stem primates. Forest-to-savanna mosaic in East Africa for hominins. The East African Rift Valley is the pivotal hominin landscape.

**Climate.** Cenozoic cooling continues. Miocene drying expands savannas at the expense of forests. Pleistocene glacial cycles (~2.6 Ma onward) drive repeated habitat reshuffling.

**Plate tectonics.** East African Rift opening from approximately 25 Ma onward — creates the rift-valley landscape where hominin evolution plays out. Himalayan uplift drives the Asian monsoon. Isthmus of Panama closes approximately 3 Ma, restructuring ocean circulation.

**Geological events.** Eocene Thermal Maximum (early primate window). Mid-Miocene Climate Optimum followed by cooling. Plio-Pleistocene rift volcanism in East Africa, preserving hominin fossils in ashfall sequences.

**Atmosphere.** CO2 declining through the Cenozoic; oscillating with glacial-interglacial cycles in the Pleistocene. Approaching modern levels.

**Ocean and surface chemistry.** Modern.

**Biotic context.** Canopy forest ecology for stem primates. Savanna mosaic with C4-grass expansion for hominins. Pleistocene megafauna present — proboscideans, large carnivores, ungulates. Other hominin species coexisting through most of *Homo*'s history (Neanderthals, Denisovans, *H. floresiensis*, *H. naledi*).

**Key fossil and evidence sites.** Fayum (Egypt, early primates). Hadar (Lucy, *Australopithecus afarensis*). Olduvai Gorge. Sterkfontein, Laetoli, Koobi Fora. Zhoukoudian (*H. erectus*, China). Atapuerca (Spain). Omo Kibish and Jebel Irhoud (early *H. sapiens*).

**Signature evidentiary signal.** Grasping hands and feet, forward-facing eyes, expanded visual cortex, brain enlargement, bipedalism, stone tools (Lomekwian ~3.3 Ma, then Oldowan ~2.6 Ma).
