# What a Learner Brings to First-Language Acquisition: Output Constraints and Installed Priors — A Literature Review (2020–present)

## TL;DR
- **Output/articulatory constraint:** Computational models robustly show that constraining a learner to a vocal-tract output space lets curiosity/reward-driven agents *self-organize* infant-like vocal developmental sequences (Moulin-Frier, Oudeyer; Warlaumont; Guenther's DIVA/GODIVA), but there is **almost no controlled experiment that holds the learner fixed and varies only output-space dimensionality**, and the field cannot yet isolate *why* constraint helps (hypothesis-space reduction vs. shared perception-production coordinate system vs. transfer to higher levels). On developmentally plausible data, speech/phoneme-based learners generally **underperform** text-based ones on grammar benchmarks — output realism is currently a cost, not a benefit, for higher linguistic levels.
- **Installed priors:** Many *structural/formal* priors have been installed before language training (formal-language pre-pretraining, meta-learned Bayesian priors, architectural/recursion biases). The strongest controlled finding is that **the TYPE of structure matters**: hierarchical and (mildly) context-sensitive structures transfer best, while flat/random structures and even some context-sensitive copy languages fail or hurt. Effects are **strongly scale- and architecture-dependent**.
- **Social-cognitive prior:** Installing a *social-cognitive* prior (belief/false-belief tracking, joint attention, intention reading) into a first-language learner is **largely a null** for naturalistic neural acquisition models, with two important exceptions: intention/social-cue priors are standard in classic *Bayesian word-learning* models (Frank/Goodman/Tenenbaum; Yu & Ballard), and a belief-tracking listener prior has been installed in *referential-game* "language acquisition" agents (Liu et al., ICLR 2023). Most theory-of-mind-in-LLM work is **emergent probing**, not installed priors. The false-belief / dominance / reputation prior installed into a naturalistic first-language learner appears genuinely undone.

---

## Key Findings

**Consensus claims (well-supported across labs):**
1. An articulatory/output space plus an intrinsic-motivation or reward signal is *sufficient* to reproduce qualitative milestones of infant vocal development (unarticulated → vocalic → canonical/syllabic babbling) without hard-coding the developmental schedule. (Moulin-Frier, Nguyen & Oudeyer 2014; Warlaumont & Finnegan 2016; Guenther DIVA lineage.)
2. Pretraining on structured non-linguistic or formal data installs an inductive bias that transfers to natural language, and the *type* of structure matters — hierarchical/nested structure transfers better than flat or random. (Papadimitriou & Jurafsky 2020, 2023; Hu, Petty, Shi, Merrill & Linzen 2025.)
3. Under developmentally plausible data budgets (~100M words or less), neural LMs are far less data-efficient than children, and most "innate-structure" interventions yield only modest gains; many curriculum interventions yield **no** improvement. (BabyLM Findings 2023, 2024.)

**Contested claims:**
- *What meta-learning installs*: McCoy & Griffiths frame it as distilling a Bayesian **simplicity prior**; Linzen-group critics (Meta-Learning Neural Mechanisms, 2025 preprint) argue it installs reusable **neural mechanisms** (e.g., counters), not a simplicity prior.
- *Whether transformer architecture carries a hierarchical/syntactic bias intrinsically* (largely "no, it comes from data" — Petty/Frank; Mueller & Linzen) vs. data+depth interacting to produce it.

**Individual assertions (single-study, await replication):** specific rankings of which formal language transfers best (k-Shuffle Dyck); the claim that "seeing [u] aids vocal learning"; that non-context-free relationships form the best inductive bias.

## Details

### QUESTION 1 — Output/articulatory constraint and phonological learning

**Foundational tools and models.** The articulatory-synthesis modeling tradition supplies the "output system" whose constraints are at issue. VocalTractLab (Birkholz) is a 3-D vocal-tract synthesizer controlled by ~20 articulatory parameters; Maeda's model supplies the synthesizer used inside Guenther's DIVA. DIVA (Directions Into Velocities of Articulators) and its sequencing extension GODIVA (Bohland, Bullock & Guenther 2010) are neural models in which auditory-motor and somatosensory-motor mappings are *learned during a babbling phase* (mathematically, an approximation of the Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse of the Jacobian relating auditory/somatosensory and articulator spaces). A key DIVA property: after learning, the model can produce speech sounds *even under articulatory constraints/perturbations*, because targets are specified in auditory/sensory space rather than as fixed articulator positions — a direct demonstration that the structure of the output space shapes (and provides robustness to) acquisition.

**Intrinsic-motivation / curiosity models.** Moulin-Frier, Nguyen & Oudeyer (2014, *Frontiers in Psychology*) is the central reference: using the SAGG-RIAC curiosity architecture to control a vocal-tract synthesizer, they show that **intrinsically motivated exploration of the output space self-organizes** a developmental sequence (no external speech → exploration around a neutral/schwa configuration → increasing articulatory contrasts), and that adding social/vocal peers changes which sounds are explored. This is a **single-architecture demonstration**, not a controlled manipulation of output dimensionality. Howard & Messum's Elija model and Warlaumont's spiking-network models (Warlaumont & Finnegan 2016, *PLOS ONE*) show that reward — whether from intrinsic auditory salience or simulated caregiver responses — drives the emergence of canonical (consonant-vowel) babbling via reward-modulated plasticity, consistent with deaf/cochlear-implant data showing audition is critical to babbling onset.

**Frame/Content vs. Articulatory Phonology.** MacNeilage & Davis's frame/content theory attributes CV preferences to jaw-cycle (mandibular oscillation) biomechanics. The directly relevant modeling result (Articulatory Phonology account, using articulatory synthesis) finds **limited support for frame/content and more for Articulatory Phonology**, and notably finds **no developmental trend** in CV preferences and **no perceptual bias** toward preferred combinations — a disconfirming/mixed finding for the strongest biomechanical-frame account.

**Does constraining the output space help, hurt, or neither?** The honest answer from the literature: constraint is **enabling** (it makes the babbling-to-speech problem tractable and reproduces milestones), but the field **lacks controlled comparisons that vary output-space dimensionality while holding the learner fixed**. The van Niekerk/colleagues work ("Artificial vocal learning guided by speech recognition," *Journal of Phonetics* 2024) is among the few to manipulate output realism: using a high-dimensional 3-D vocal tract with explicit coarticulatory dynamics and adult vs. child vocal-tract configurations, they find highly intelligible English CVC words are learnable under a deep-learning speech-recognizer ("phonological perception") guidance, but **only moderate intelligibility** under raw acoustic-imitation ("phonetic perception") guidance — isolating the *perceptual objective* rather than the *output dimensionality* as the key variable.

**Speech/phoneme- vs. text-based learners on developmentally plausible data.** This is where output realism currently **imposes a cost** at higher linguistic levels. Lavechin et al. (BabySLM, Interspeech 2023) built the benchmark; Lavechin et al. (*Cognition* 2024, "Modeling early phonetic acquisition from child-centered audio data") is the pivotal disconfirming result: a prediction-based statistical learner shows infant-like **perceptual attunement on clean audiobook speech but fails on ecological child-centered audio**, and attunement only re-emerges when the model is given **inductive biases** forcing focus on speech segments and speaker/pitch/room-invariant representations. Goriely et al. ("From Babble to Words," 2024 arXiv) and Bunzeck et al. ("Graphemes vs. phonemes," CoNLL-BabyLM 2024) both find **grapheme/text models outperform phoneme models** on grammar benchmarks, though phoneme models "demonstrate promising grammatical learning" and excel at sound-based tasks. STELA (predictive coding on raw speech) shows linguistic categories can **emerge as an end product** rather than being prerequisites — a finding that must be classified as *emergent capacity*, not installed prior.

**Can the literature isolate the source of any benefit?** Largely **no**. The three candidate mechanisms — (i) hypothesis/search-space reduction, (ii) a shared producer-perceiver coordinate system (perception-production link), (iii) transfer of structure to higher linguistic levels — are usually confounded. DIVA's design *implements* (ii) by construction (auditory-space targets), and van Niekerk et al. isolate the perceptual objective, but no study cleanly dissociates (i) from (ii) from (iii).

### QUESTION 2 — Priors installed into language learners

**A catalog of installed priors, by TYPE:**

*Structural / formal / syntactic (the dominant category):*
- **Pretraining on non-linguistic structured data.** Papadimitriou & Jurafsky (2020, EMNLP, "Learning Music Helps You Read," aclanthology 2020.emnlp-main.554) trained LSTMs on MIDI music, Java code, and nested-parenthesis data, then fine-tuned only embeddings on Spanish: "training on non-linguistic data with latent structure (MIDI music or Java code) improves test performance on natural language, despite no overlap in surface form or vocabulary." Notably, both recursive and non-recursive ("paired tokens but no recursion") parentheses "leads to the same substantial gains" — an early signal that *recursion specifically* may not be the active ingredient. Replicated/extended by Chiang & Lee (2022, Shuffle languages), Ri & Tsuruoka (2022, transformers), Chiang & Lee (2020, amino-acid sequences).
- **Pre-pretraining on formal languages.** Hu, Petty, Shi, Merrill & Linzen (2025, "Between Circuits and Chomsky," ACL Outstanding Paper, aclanthology 2025.acl-long.478): "For a 1B-parameter language model trained on roughly 1.6B tokens of natural language, pre-pretraining achieves the same loss and better linguistic generalization with a 33% smaller token budget," using **k-Shuffle Dyck** — *outperforming pre-pretraining on a matched amount of natural language*. Crucially, the **copy language ww (cross-serial dependencies, no hierarchy) HURTS** natural-language performance — a clean directional/disconfirming result that **context-sensitivity alone is insufficient; hierarchy + transformer-learnability (C-RASP definability) is what matters.** Mechanistic evidence: attention heads acquired in pre-pretraining remain important afterward.
- **Injecting structural hints.** Papadimitriou & Jurafsky (2023, Findings of EMNLP): comparing recursive/nested (NEST), crossing/context-sensitive (CROSS), random (RAND), repeat (REP) pretraining languages, fine-tuned on English, Japanese, Basque — **non-context-free (crossing) relationships form the best inductive bias**, and breaking NEST's recursive constituency structure *improved* downstream learning. A counterintuitive result against naive "recursion is best."
- **Counterexamples/nulls within formal-structure transfer.** Chiang & Lee (2021, "On the Transferability... Artificial Datasets"): RoBERTa pretrained on *Nesting* vs. *Flat* parentheses had **similar downstream performance** — i.e., modeling hierarchy was **not critical** to transferability — a direct tension with the Hu et al. hierarchy claim, plausibly explained by scale/architecture/objective differences.

*Meta-learned / Bayesian priors:*
- McCoy, Grant, Smolensky, Griffiths & Linzen (2020, CogSci, "Universal linguistic inductive biases via meta-learning") use MAML to encode inductive biases in a network's initial weights, separating universal (initial weights) from language-specific (learned) factors.
- McCoy & Griffiths (2025, *Nature Communications* 16:4676; arXiv 2023) "distill" a Bayesian model's prior into a neural network via meta-learning; prior-trained networks learn formal languages rapidly *and* learn English faster. **Contested interpretation:** the 2025 preprint "Meta-Learning Neural Mechanisms rather than Bayesian Priors" argues, with converging tests, that meta-training installs **reusable neural mechanisms (e.g., counters)**, not a Bayesian simplicity prior — because the same behavioral outcomes arise from meta-training sets that violate the simplicity prior.
- Marinescu, McCoy & Griffiths (2024, "Distilling Symbolic Priors for Concept Learning") extend the distillation approach to concept learning (R²≈0.95 to humans), outside language proper.

*Architectural / working-memory / recency:*
- Mueller & Linzen (2023, ACL, "How to Plant Trees"): **model depth matters more than width or parameter count** for hierarchical generalization; pretraining on *child-directed speech* induces a hierarchical bias with an order of magnitude less data than web text/Wikipedia. Mueller et al. (2022) "Coloring the Blank Slate" — pretraining imparts hierarchical bias.
- Disconfirming the "transformer has intrinsic hierarchy bias" view: Petty & Frank ("Transformers Generalize Linearly," 2021) find **no evidence** of an architectural hierarchical bias — transformers prefer linear generalization; their syntactic successes are attributed to large training sets. "Sometimes I am a Tree" (2024) shows hierarchical generalization is **unstable and data-driven**.
- Warstadt & Bowman (2020, CogSci): BERT acquires a structural generalization in **3 of 4** phenomena but a **linear** generalization for NPI licensing — a mixed result, classified as **emergent** (from raw-data pretraining), not an installed prior.

*Statistical/distributional:* Zipfian vocabulary distribution (one of Papadimitriou & Jurafsky's 2023 three injected biases) — found **less effective** than crossing-dependency structure.

**Data-scale dependence.** This is pervasive: Mueller & Linzen's hierarchical bias emerges with far less data from child-directed speech; Hu et al.'s gains are demonstrated at 1B parameters / ~1.6B tokens; Papadimitriou & Jurafsky's original effect was in small LSTMs (≤30M tokens); the Nesting-vs-Flat parentheses null was in RoBERTa. Several BabyLM submissions report that benefits visible at scale **shrink or vanish** under 10M–100M-word budgets, and curriculum-learning submissions "reported few improvements over non-curriculum baselines" (Martinez et al. 2023; BabyLM Findings 2023). Steuer et al. (2023) found benchmark performance is **not correlated** with ability to predict human psycholinguistic data.

**Has a SOCIAL-COGNITIVE prior been installed? (The key requested null check.)**
- **Emergent vs. installed must be kept separate.** The large theory-of-mind-in-LLMs literature is **emergent probing of already-trained models**, NOT installed priors, and its headline numbers are fragile under controls. Kosinski's arXiv 2023 version claimed davinci-003 "solved 90% of false-belief tasks, at the level of seven-year-olds," but the peer-reviewed version (Kosinski, *PNAS* 2024, 121(45):e2405460121) reports performance dropped to **60% after updating the items and to 20% after including true-belief controls** — strengthening the "emergent, not robust" framing. Strachan et al. (2024, *Nature Human Behaviour* 8(7):1285–1295), testing against 1,907 humans, found "GPT-4 models performed at, or even sometimes above, human levels at identifying indirect requests, false beliefs and misdirection, but struggled with detecting faux pas," and argued the faux-pas failure reflected "a hyperconservative approach towards committing to conclusions rather than... a genuine failure of inference." Ullman (trivial-alteration failures) similarly cautions against over-reading. All of this is emergent.
- **Installed social priors DO exist, but mostly outside naturalistic first-language acquisition.** (a) In *referential-game / emergent-communication* learners framed as "language acquisition": **Liu, Zhu, Liu, Bisk & Neubig (2023, ICLR, "Computational Language Acquisition with Theory of Mind," arXiv 2303.01502)** install ToM "by giving the speaker agent an internal listener model that is trained alongside the speaker and used to rerank potential utterances"; "training speakers with a highly weighted ToM listener component leads to performance gains in the authors' image referential game setting." This builds on Rabinowitz et al.'s Machine Theory of Mind (2018) and Zhu, Neubig & Bisk's ICML 2021 "Few-shot Language Coordination by Modeling Theory of Mind." These are **signaling games, not naturalistic acquisition from child-directed input** — the "acquisition" label is the authors' framing. (b) In the *classic Bayesian word-learning* tradition: **Frank, Goodman & Tenenbaum (2007 NIPS / 2009 *Psychological Science*)** install a speaker **referential-intention** latent variable and learn which social cues (gaze, pointing) matter, tested on CHILDES; **Yu & Ballard (2007, *Neurocomputing*)** install joint-attention/prosodic social cues as a "spotlight" on the intended referent. Both are first-language word-learning models and both **outperform** purely associative baselines — but they are probabilistic cognitive models, not neural LMs.
- **The genuine null:** No located work installs a **false-belief-tracking**, **social-hierarchy/dominance/kin/reputation**, or **cooperative-vs-deceptive** prior into a **naturalistic neural first-language-acquisition** learner. False-belief priors appear only in non-linguistic RL agents (Rabinowitz et al.'s ToMnet passes Sally-Anne; Jaques et al. 2019 social-influence intrinsic motivation in multi-agent RL) or as emergent probing of trained LLMs. **This sub-cell is, on a genuine search, empty.**

**Does the TYPE of prior change acquisition relative to another prior or to none?** Yes, where controlled: Papadimitriou & Jurafsky 2023 (crossing > nesting > flat/random); Hu et al. 2025 (hierarchical+C-RASP > other formal > matched natural language; copy-language ww < baseline). But the Nesting-vs-Flat null (Chiang & Lee 2021), and the 2020 finding that recursive and non-recursive parentheses gave *the same* gains, plus the meta-learning interpretation dispute, show the **mechanism** behind type-effects is not settled.

## Recommendations

**For a researcher deciding what to build/test next, staged by evidence thresholds:**

1. **First, run the missing controlled comparison for output constraint.** Hold the learner (architecture, objective, data) fixed and vary *only* output-space dimensionality (degrees of freedom of the vocal-tract model) — this is the experiment the field is missing. Benchmark on phonological acquisition metrics (BabySLM lexical/syntactic; canonical-babbling ratio). *Threshold to change conclusions:* if reducing DOF improves phonological acquisition holding everything else fixed, that would convert the current "enabling but unisolated" status into an established hypothesis-space-reduction effect.

2. **To claim an output-constraint benefit is ESTABLISHED**, a study must (a) be a controlled comparison varying only the constraint, (b) dissociate the three candidate mechanisms (search-space reduction vs. shared perception-production coordinate system vs. higher-level transfer), and (c) replicate across at least two synthesizers/architectures and at least two data scales. No current study meets all three.

3. **For installed structural priors, prioritize developmentally plausible scales.** Given pervasive scale-dependence, report results at 10M and 100M words, not just ≥1B-token regimes; treat any benefit shown only at scale as scale-contingent. *Threshold:* a prior that helps at 1B tokens but not at 10M words should not be described as relevant to human acquisition.

4. **If pursuing social-cognitive priors, the cleanest open contribution is to install a belief/intention/joint-attention prior into a naturalistic (CHILDES-trained, BabyLM-style) neural learner** and compare against a matched no-prior and a structural-prior baseline — bridging the Frank/Goodman/Tenenbaum Bayesian tradition and the Liu et al. referential-game tradition into naturalistic acquisition. This cell is currently empty and is the highest-value novel target.

5. **Resolve the meta-learning interpretation dispute** with pre-registered tests proposed by both camps (simplicity-prior predictions vs. neural-mechanism predictions), since it determines whether "installing a prior" means installing a probability distribution or installing a circuit.

## Caveats and Clearest Current Limits

- **Preprint flags:** Hu, Petty, Shi, Merrill & Linzen ("Between Circuits and Chomsky") originated as an arXiv preprint and is now an ACL 2025 Outstanding Paper; "Meta-Learning Neural Mechanisms rather than Bayesian Priors" (2025), Goriely et al. "From Babble to Words" (2024), and "Language Acquisition Device in LLMs" remain **arXiv preprints** at the time of review — treat their specific numbers as provisional. Peer-reviewed anchors: McCoy & Griffiths (*Nature Communications* 2025), Lavechin et al. (*Cognition* 2024), Mueller & Linzen (ACL 2023), Papadimitriou & Jurafsky (EMNLP 2020 / Findings EMNLP 2023), Warlaumont & Finnegan (*PLOS ONE* 2016), Moulin-Frier et al. (*Frontiers* 2014), Liu et al. (ICLR 2023), Kosinski (*PNAS* 2024), Strachan et al. (*Nature Human Behaviour* 2024), Frank/Goodman/Tenenbaum (*Psych Science* 2009).
- **Installed vs. emergent confusions are the biggest risk in this literature.** Theory-of-mind-in-LLMs, structural generalization in BERT (Warstadt & Bowman), and STELA's emergent phoneme categories are all **emergent capacities measured after training**, not installed priors — they must not be cited as evidence that a prior was "put in." The Kosinski 90%→20%-under-controls trajectory is a cautionary case of how fragile emergent-probing numbers can be.
- **What NO system has yet shown — Question 1:** No model has demonstrated, via controlled comparison, that output-space constraint *causally* improves *higher-level* (syntactic/semantic) acquisition; nor has any study isolated which of the three candidate mechanisms drives any phonological benefit. On developmentally realistic audio, speech-based learners still struggle and require added inductive biases to attune.
- **What NO system has yet shown — Question 2:** No system has installed a genuinely *social-cognitive* prior (false belief, joint attention, intention reading, reputation/dominance) into a *naturalistic neural first-language learner* and shown it changes acquisition relative to controls. No consensus exists on whether type-of-structure effects reflect distilled priors or reusable mechanisms. And no installed prior has closed the human–model data-efficiency gap (3–4 orders of magnitude) at developmentally plausible scales.
- **Live vs. recurring debates:** The output-constraint/embodiment debate is an *older* developmental-robotics program (Oudeyer, Guenther, Warlaumont, 2010s) recurring with new synthesizers and RL. The *formal-language-transfer and BabyLM* debates are **genuinely live** (2020–present), with active disagreement (hierarchy-matters vs. nesting-doesn't-matter; architecture-bias vs. data-bias; simplicity-prior vs. neural-mechanism). The installed-social-prior question is not a live debate so much as an **unopened one**.
