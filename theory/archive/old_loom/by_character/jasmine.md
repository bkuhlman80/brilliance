## Chapter 1: Protocell

### The Metaphysics

[…]

"What we have, instead of fossils, is the chemistry the protocell left in its descendants. And the chemistry it left in its descendants is what gives us LUCA."

Jasmine, who had been listening intently, said, **"LUCA?"**

Burns opened his eyes. Jasmine had pulled him back to the room. He looked at her. He looked at Bramble on the table. He looked at Jasmine again.

"Last Universal Common Ancestor. The most recent organism that everything alive on Earth today descends from. Bacteria, archaea, eukaryotes — all of us. Common ancestor. We do not have LUCA in the rock record either, but we can reconstruct a great deal about LUCA by looking at what its descendants have in common. Recent work — Moody and colleagues, two years ago — puts LUCA at around four point one billion years, with already two thousand six hundred protein families, already with a CRISPR-Cas system. LUCA was an old hand by the time the rock record starts. Whatever the protocell was, it had been getting better for hundreds of millions of years before LUCA, and LUCA had been getting better for hundreds of millions of years before Strelley Pool. We are downstream of a lineage that was already very, very accomplished before it left any bodies."

Jasmine nodded. She said, **"OK."**

Burns closed his eyes again.

"So when we say *location of the protocell*, what we are actually asking is: where did the chemistry that became LUCA come from. And we cannot answer that from the rocks. We can argue about it from the chemistry. And we are. Loudly. For thirty years and counting."

Jasmine said, **"Burns."**

He opened his eyes.

She said, **"You're circling the runway. Just land the plane already."**

Burns laughed the chest-laugh — at himself, this time, at the truth of it. The team laughed with him. Bart laughed last and longest.

### Respiration Type

"And there is the new knob, on the opposite side from the crank — Respiration Type."

Jasmine said, **"What is an FSM?"**

Lisa said, "Oh. Finite State Machine. Yeah, so — think of the last time you walked down into a subway station and had to go through one of those turnstile things. When you walk up, the turnstile is locked. You push it, it won't let you through."

Bart said, "And then you jump over it."

Mira snickered.

Lisa said, "And then you put your coin in, and the turnstile unlocks. Then you push the bar and pass through. And the bar locks again. Yeah? The turnstile is running a simple algorithm. Coin unlocks. Turning locks. That is an FSM. A small number of states it can be in, and a small set of rules about how to get from one state to another. Bramble's FSM has three states. The turnstile's has two."

Jasmine said, **"Got it."**

By noon Bramble has been to the back door and has come back to the kitchen because the back door sunbeam goes away. By evening Bramble is in the corner where the noon sunbeam was at noon, on STOP, charging on whatever has trickled in from late afternoon. The cadence is — I learned the cadence by August. ## Chapter 3: Eukaryote

### Bigger

"Intracellular Transport," he said. "The cell is too big now to just let things slosh where they need to go. So it runs delivery. It has little motors that physically walk cargo from one part of the cell to another along tracks — actual legs, actual walking, actual tracks."

### The Relay

Jasmine had been waiting for the floor, in the way she waited — without leaning forward, the depleted patience of someone who had been up at two with a labor and had still come, and who was read by the team, when they read her at all, as merely tired.

**"It vacuums,"** she said. **"I'll start with the plain things, because the plain things are most of living with it. It vacuums. **"The thing I noticed most, though — the thing that's new in the way it *behaves,* not just what it does — is the toggle. There's a setting Bart showed me how to flip. One way, it finds a route through the kitchen and it runs that route, the same loop, tighter and tighter, until it's basically worn a groove — it knows the kitchen, it does the kitchen, it's efficient and a little boring and it never bumps into anything. The other way, it stops trusting the groove. It goes poking into corners it's never bothered with, tries the gap behind the trash can, gets itself somewhere new. More misses that way. Bumps things. ## Chapter 4: Eumetazoa

### Allostasis

"Allostasis," Lisa said. "Stability through *change* instead of stability by sitting still. The animal forecasts what it's about to need and shoves the setpoint there *ahead* of the need — and it shows up the instant nerve tissue does, because you can't run a forecast without something fast enough to run it on. In Bramble it's a forecaster minding its own charge, deciding to go feed *before* it's empty instead of after."

Jasmine had Mira's confiscated juice box and was drinking it. **"Do plants do that? My tomatoes know it's morning before the sun's up. They move first."**

"Plants run the *old* way," Burns said. "Homeostasis, not allostasis. Beautifully — water, light, the daily clock, every line held. But a plant *holds* its lines. It doesn't go shoving a setpoint to a new place and then guarding the new place. Which is why" — he paused, because he loved this — "a plant cannot get fat."

[…]

"And *Bramble* does it now," Bart said. "The neighbor's leaf blower about put it through the ceiling the first morning — Jasmine says — and by the end of the week it barely twitched." Jasmine nodded.

### Off the Floor

"Bramble block," Lisa said. "Jasmine."

Jasmine set the juice box down — she'd finished most of Mira's — and took her second to find the start, the way she always did, because the lab came to her wanting findings and what she had was a house.

**"Plain things first. It's quieter. You'd think a vacuum getting smarter would get louder, and it went the other way — the clicking's gone, so you don't hear Bramble think anymore. You turn around and it's just somewhere new."**

**"And it's stopped dying on the floor."**

She said it the way the big ones came out of her, like it was nothing.

**"All fall I was hauling it to the dock. It'd run itself flat and quit wherever it stood — middle of the kitchen, mostly — and somebody'd carry it back, and the somebody was me. I haven't done that in I don't know how long. It just takes itself to get fed now, before it runs out, and comes back."**

"That's the forecaster," Lisa said. "That's the allostasis — what we were saying earlier. It's reading its own charge and going to feed before it strands instead of after. The dying-on-the-floor thing, the one every Bramble we've built has done since the protocell — that's the problem that just walked out of your kitchen."

**"There's a new thing, too,"** Jasmine said. **"On the active setting — the one where it stays out where we are — it won't sleep. Can't, on that setting; it just stays up. So the only way it doesn't run down is it has to break off and go feed itself and come back. And you can watch it not want to. It'll be parked in the kitchen doorway, in the foot traffic, which is plainly the spot it's picked — and then there's a moment where it gives the spot up and goes to the dock, and you can *see* it do it. It leaves the thing it wants to go keep itself alive. Then it comes back to the same doorway."** She turned the empty juice box in her fingers. **"It looks like somebody deciding. I know it isn't. It looks like it."**

Bart sat forward on his green pint. "It's got two wants now."

"It's got two wants now," Lisa said, "and they don't both fit at once. That's the new thing. One want, it just runs. Two, something in it has to choose between them — and a thing that can choose can choose wrong." She didn't go further. She'd come back to it at the board.

And Mira, who had surfaced from the crayons the moment her own subject came up, said, "I want Bramble to come downstairs."

The table turned to her.

"It can't come in the basement. It gets stuck on the stairs, and then it's just stuck, and somebody has to go down and get it. I want it where I am."

Nobody answered her about the stairs.

It was a silence the narrator only read right on the second pass: five adults deciding all at once not to say the thing, because the thing was standing under a drop-cloth at the end of the table where Mira couldn't see it, and to say it now was to spend it. Jasmine tucked a piece of hair behind Mira's ear. **"Soon, bug,"** she said, which was true and gave away nothing.

## Chapter 5: Bilaterian

### Downstairs

"Bramble block," Lisa said. "Jasmine."

The tetrapod had been on the floor by Jasmine's chair the whole meeting, head up, the screen on its face catching a word and giving it up for the next — LISA, the camcorder, MIRA, MIRA again — reading the room one thing at a time the way it read any room now. Jasmine put a hand flat on its back, not to do anything to it, and took her beat, because the table wanted findings and what she had was eleven weeks of a dog-sized thing loose in her kitchen.

**"Plain things first. It walks. You've all watched it walk; I won't sell you the walking. The one I didn't see coming"** — she looked at Mira — **"is the stairs."**

"It comes downstairs," Mira said.

**"It comes downstairs. She's wanted that since the fall, and I'd quit believing it ever would — the old one you carried down and it sat where you set it. This one does the basement on its own. Down to her room, and back up when it's done. First week, I came down and the two of them were just there, her on the rug and it standing in the doorway like it had worked out it was allowed in."**

**"The cold gets into it, too, down there. The basement runs cool, and in the cool it goes heavy — slow to rise, slow to turn, like it's wading. Up here by the warm slate, or in a patch of sun, it's quick and sure. I thought for a while it had moods; it doesn't. Bart says a cold battery can't push as hard — so cold makes it slow, warm makes it sharp, and the thing has worked out where the warm is. It'll leave a cold corner for a sunbeam and pick up speed as it settles, the way you lean toward a fire without meaning to. When Mira wants it lively I've learned not to send it down to her cold room and expect much; I let it warm first, by the vent or the furnace, till it's got its quick back. Mira says it sulks when it's cold — her word, from the winter, and she's not let it go, and I've quit arguing it."**

She turned the bottle a quarter and didn't drink.

**"It feeds off nothing you can point at, too. No dock. ### Goo

"Impetus doesn't win on that board. Impetus wins on a different one — whether the thing steers. Whether it goes forward, on purpose, toward the good and off the bad, under a signal that tells it which is which. Not who had the most children. Who pointed themselves at the right thing and went."
