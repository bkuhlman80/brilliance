## LEAK check

Searched deliberately and hard for any presupposition of a clade-11/12 Breakthrough or Capacity (Codes, Trust, Distributing, Crime, Farming[11], Standardizing[11], Stories, Extracting, Conquering, Standardizing[12]) per the spec's instruction to report every one, however small, at STRONG severity. Three candidates surfaced and were run down individually; none held up as a genuine leak.

1. **Mira's "trial" of the disc** (charge of theft, sworn witness, verdict of "Guilty"). This borrows the vocabulary of Crime (clade 11, forbidden — property offense, adjudication). On close reading, though, the scene never depicts a functioning legal/property system operating on the settlement's own rules; it is explicitly a six-year-old's private, invented game, staged entirely inside her own head as a way of processing jealousy over a machine's attention. The narrator frames it as *her* fiction throughout ("She held a trial," "she'd worked the charge up careful") — the household possesses no institution of ownership, adjudication, or punishment that the game is drawing on; it's borrowed from the child's general cultural vocabulary (television, playground), not from the settlement's own machinery. **Not a leak** — no Capacity is being presupposed as functional within the story world, only referenced as a child's play-frame.

2. **The "Save"/"stored procedure" language.** Lisa's line "stored regulation... is just the technical name for a custom" and the child's sticker-word "SAVE" both use vocabulary adjacent to notation/writing systems (Codes, clade 11). But nothing in the chapter depicts an actual writing system, external symbolic storage, or durable inscription being invented or used — "SAVE" is a spoken word taught to a machine, and the "stored procedure" language is Lisa's own metaphor, borrowed from her professional vocabulary (software), not a dramatization of settlement-level Codes. **Not a leak** — figurative language, not a presupposed Capacity.

3. **Marge's records-removal / "the flat language of a state office."** A modern bureaucratic institution (postal system, government forms, a large religious organization's membership rolls) is real-world backdrop for a contemporary-set scene, exactly the way "Westminster post," "the Games," and "commission" funding are backdrop elsewhere in the book. The chapter is not dramatizing this institution's functioning as an on-topic Capacity — it never lingers on record-keeping mechanics, writing systems, or large-scale anonymous trust as the *subject* of a scene; it is scenery for a personal, Self-Construal-anchored story (already credited in Pass A). **Not a leak** — incidental modern-world realism, not content the chapter is asking to be credited with.

**Conclusion: no genuine LEAK found in this chapter.**
