## Skeptic-step notes (sampled DRIFT and UNANCHORED findings, read in full)

**DRIFT #1 — Water Witch romantic frame (l.28–58), read in full.** This is the chapter's second-longest scene and its most polished prose. Lisa's departure reasoning is legitimately on-ledger (Self-Construal, credited in Pass A) — but it is delivered inside a much longer bar scene that spends at least half its length on Brian's own romantic reckoning with Marge: the Sausalito kitchen in 2002, "the wanting in me had run out the way the fish ran out... and that this year... it had come back up off the bottom." This is unmistakably pair-bond/attachment material — the register of desire, not of institutional standing among strangers. It is beautifully written and it is off-ledger. The toast itself ("To blackberries") tries to braid the two women's situations together as "the same move whatever the country," which is a clever narrative gesture, but the underlying content (romantic longing, a decades-old failure to act on a feeling) belongs to a lower clade's Motivation set (something like Band-Human bonding/commitment-to-a-person), not to City-Human's institutional-standing material. Flagged as DRIFT, not recommended for cutting — it's clearly load-bearing for the series' character arcs — but it should not be counted as clade-11 coverage.

**DRIFT #2 — Mira and the new neighbor boy (l.185–187), read in full.** After a long, meticulously on-ledger passage of Bramble sorting the new family's boxes (Standardizing, credited in Pass A), the scene pivots hard: "Mira watched him carry the lamp in and did not offer to help... and then went and asked the only question that actually mattered to her, which was whether they had a kid." The passage explicitly ranks this above "anything happening in a couple of months down at the mountain" (the Olympics, i.e., the chapter's Codes-adjacent city-spectacle backdrop). This is a clean, small, real DRIFT: a six-year-old's peer-friendship priority, textbook Band-Human affiliative material, surfacing in a scene otherwise built to carry City-Human content. Not a defect — it's good child characterization — but it is off this clade's ledger.

**DRIFT #3 — Burns and the four-legged robot (l.334, l.338), read in full.** Two short, quiet beats bookend the retrospective's "off the board" sequence: Burns crosses to the oldest (four-legged, mammal-line) bot and "stood over it a while with his hands in his pockets, not touching it," saying nothing; later Bramble crosses to stand in front of Burns the same way, also saying nothing, and Burns "did not send it off." Both beats are wordless companionable presence — the shape of mammal-clade attachment (proximity-seeking, silent co-regulation) rather than city-clade institutional standing. The second half of the pairing (Bramble-to-Burns) trades partly on Representation (Bramble "stopped in front of him... the way it stopped in front of anyone the law had a claim on"), which is why it's credited partially in Pass A — but the Burns-to-quadruped half has no City-Human content at all. Genuine, minor DRIFT.

**UNANCHORED #1 — "Worm to Imperial City" Planner survey (l.340–362), read in full.** Seven slides, each a different animal or human wearing a cartoon Lisa's face, illustrating the Modeler/"Planner" function from flatworm to imperial city. This is clearly deliberate, well-built material — but examined against the clade-11 ledger specifically, it carries none of City-Human's own Motivations, Tendencies, Signatures, or Capacities. It's a parallel framework (the cross-clade Function axis) riding alongside the clade content, not part of it. Candidate for nothing — it's plainly intentional structural work — but it does not count toward clade-11 coverage, and its "imperial" framing was the one passage in the chapter that needed a deliberate LEAK check (see above; cleared).

**UNANCHORED #2 — "Nobody's Hand" (l.318–332), read in full.** The retrospective's board-mechanics scene: the last hand-set knob comes off, Mira relabels it "Respect/Savage" and re-seats it on the shelf, Lisa narrates the board going "down to the symbolic four." This is rich material for the series' overall machine-building plot (the Evo-Algo completing) but contains no City-Human Motivation/Tendency/Signature/Capacity language at all — it's pure mechanism-of-the-story, not clade content. Correctly left uncounted.

**UNANCHORED #3 — "Four to Nothing" (l.404–416), read in full.** The standing sentience vote, with each character's threshold explained (Burns at the bilaterian, Bart at the mammal, Marge at the band, Lisa refusing a threshold at all).
