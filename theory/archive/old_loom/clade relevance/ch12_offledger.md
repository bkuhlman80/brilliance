## LEAK check

Clade 12 is the top of the twelve-clade ladder (Reviser function, closing the symbolic line). There is no clade above it, so there is no forbidden-list and, structurally, no possible LEAK in the technical sense used for Chapters 1–11. Noting that explicitly, as instructed, rather than skipping the section.

The clade-12-specific risk instead is **invented complexity**: does the chapter claim any capacity or concept that isn't on *any* clade's ledger at all — i.e., content that reaches past the top of the whole framework rather than merely past the top of this one clade?

Checked directly:
- **The personhood/sentience scene** ("The New Person," 296–314). Ben asks Bramble "whether it knew it was there" — the single most loaded line in the chapter, and the chapter's best candidate for overreach. But the text does not let the machine, or the narrator, assert an answer. Bramble deflects with "Are you?" and offers a story instead of a claim. Ben: "I did not answer. I would like to say I was deciding how, but the truth is plainer and worse, which is that I did not have one." This is the same shape that cleared as non-violation in six of the eleven prior chapters — a concept (personhood, self-awareness) is *named and weighed*, not asserted as achieved. Given this is the culminating chapter, the temptation to finally cash the check is real, and the text visibly resists it: Lisa explicitly forecloses further architecture ("That's the architecture... There's no thirteenth"), and Burns's toast — "to whoever builds the thirteenth, a long time from now" — treats any further tier as a future, unbuilt thing, not something this chapter claims to have reached. Verdict: **cleared, not a violation.**
- **"Authored" face / the honest band readouts** (e.g., "WEIGHING [ EXPRESSING ]", the deliberate smile before the cake). These use established in-book mechanics (a screen that shows an "honest" readout versus a "put-on" face) that were already part of the book's world-building in earlier chapters, not new invented framework tiers. Not overreach.
- **"A family that goes all the way down"** — Bramble's self-description stacking every clade's animal inside itself. This is a dramatization of Identity/Universality (already scored in Pass A), not an invented new concept; it stays within the twelve-clade lineage the book has already built.
- **The closing essay's membrane/self metaphor** ("A self is a membrane drawn around a crowd") is presented as the narrator's own philosophical extension, explicitly marked as *his* reach past what he's learned from the framework ("What I did not write, because I had not yet climbed high enough to learn it..."), and it resolves into a personal decision (go knock on Marge's door), not into a claim about Bramble or a new tier of the model. It is off-ledger in the ordinary UNANCHORED sense, not an invented-complexity violation, because it never claims to be part of the twelve-clade architecture — it explicitly steps outside it.

**No invented-complexity overreach found.** If anything, the chapter is unusually disciplined about refusing to claim more than the framework licenses, twice pointedly declining to name a thirteenth clade.

## Skeptic-step notes (full reads, sampled)

**DRIFT #1 — Fisher Brewing Company, full section (lines 33–47).** Read in full. Burns tells the narrator, under a juniper-fresh openness, that he doesn't know whether his son loves him, and that he built "a whole machine a child could love precisely because he could not engineer the one across the table into loving him." This is unambiguously Attachment/parent-child content — warm, well-earned, and completely absent from Clade 12's ledger, which has no kinship-love element at any level (Commitment is duty-to-abstraction, not love-of-kin; Identity is category-membership, not affection). It does real thematic work (it sets up the Burns/Bart tension resolved obliquely in the cake fight, and it sets up the narrator's own emotional arc toward Marge), but it is not disguised Clade-12 content under a different register — checked word-for-word against every Clade-12 definition and none fit. Genuine DRIFT, flagged regardless of how good the prose is, as instructed.

**DRIFT #2 — "Worm to Empire," the Pursuer-shape sequence (242–256).** Read in full. Bart runs one personality shape — the Pursuer, "the one-clade-deep program that says there's a good thing, it's over there, go" — from the worm up through every rung to the empire-scale extraction figure at Cerro Rico. This is explicitly, by the text's own account, *clade-1 material* (the worm/Effector-only shape) being carried upward and re-skinned at clade 12. It is on-ledger for Extracting/Conquering at the specific top rung ("the figure at the head of the shaft... Goes toward. Keeps going toward. The brake's the one part it never grew" — scored in Pass A), but the organizing device itself — a shape whose *origin* is a lower clade — is a genuine cross-clade anchor. Distinguishing the two: the top-rung image is on-ledger (already counted); the shape-tracing device and the eleven lower rungs it walks through before arriving there are DRIFT, since most of the sequence's weight and its punchline ("THE PURSUER, UNINSTRUMENTED") land on the *character* of an unbraked drive rather than on anything specific to empire.

**DRIFT #3 — Mira's drawing of "the crawling one" (109–113), read in full.** Mira draws Bramble's earlier, legless, floor-bound self, at eye level with her own younger self, and captions it: "everything the crawling one was after was down there on the floor at her level, and so the crawling one had all there was to want." This is a nostalgic callback to a much earlier developmental stage of the same character — embodied, immediate, floor-level wanting, with no institutional, abstract, or category content at all. It is the closest thing in the chapter to an explicit "remember when he was simpler" beat, and it reads as a deliberate contrast-setup for everything Bramble becomes by the chapter's end (family-of-strangers, imagined category, Honest knob) rather than as disguised Clade-12 content. Checked against the ledger and found no fit at any Clade-12 element. Moderate-confidence DRIFT.

**UNANCHORED #1 — the staring contest ("In Her Own Hand," 55–65), read in full.** Mira tries to lose on purpose and her own eyelid overrules her. No framework term is invoked, and no clade's definition — checked across the twelve — maps cleanly onto "a screen doesn't need to blink and a body does." This reads as pure texture establishing Bramble's alien, unblinking constancy ahead of the Honest-knob material that follows. Candidate only, never a cut, per the brief.

**UNANCHORED #2 — Marge's phone call in the hall (163–167), read in full.** Mira notices a voice go "careful" and doesn't ask about it. This is pure foreshadowing for the Marge subplot resolved in "The Door" — no framework content, on- or off-ledger, is present. It exists to plant a detail (Marge gets calls she doesn't explain) that pays off three sections later.

**UNANCHORED #3 — "Fillings and Emulsions," the cake fight (328–340), read in full.** Bramble smiles a visibly "made" face, then lays the cake scooper against Burns's head, then Bart's, and the room dissolves into a mutual food fight. No ledger term is invoked. There is a tempting DRIFT reading here — the scene inverts Conquering's "hoard, don't spread" logic into indiscriminate spreading, almost a Band-level share-the-catch instinct performed by an empire-clade creature at its own graduation party — but the connection is never made explicit by the text (no character remarks on it, unlike every other capacity, which gets its own named explainer earlier in the chapter), so it is scored UNANCHORED rather than DRIFT. Flagged as a close call.

## Headline for Brian

This is the strongest coverage chapter of the whole thirteen-chapter run: 9 SATURATED / 3 CARRIED / 3 THIN / 0 ABSENT, with the retrospective section doing an almost card-by-card recitation of the entire Clade-12 ledger in the framework's own vocabulary — Bart runs all four Capacities by name with near-verbatim citations (Cerro Rico's 13,000/year, Qin Shi Huang's one-script-one-axle, the Mongols' quarter of the world), Burns lands the Axial Age dating and the Trust breakthrough almost word-for-word against the ledger text, and Marge delivers Identity as a small lecture. That's expected and appropriate for a finale built explicitly as a retrospective ("Four Circles" literally lights up all twelve clades on one screen) — but it means the STATED layer alone would already look SATURATED, and it was worth the extra scrutiny to confirm nearly every element also has independent DRAMATIZED or RESONANT material carrying real narrative weight, which it does.

The most important single finding: **the long-running Marge/institutional-membership thread does not resolve into this clade.** Empire-Human's Identity and Universality elements are custom-built for exactly the kind of belonging that thread has been circling for eleven chapters — an abstract, imagined, impersonal category one can be a member of. The chapter sets that trap perfectly (Bramble's "family that goes all the way down" speech is as clean a dramatization of imagined-category belonging as this whole project has seen) and then, deliberately, does not let Marge fall into it. Her arc resolves in the opposite direction: away from impersonal belonging and toward one specific, named, previously-unreachable person. The closing essay makes the contrast explicit in its own language — Bramble "had no place yet for the one person in the room who had spent three years outside the glass making certain she was made of no one," and Marge's final act is to "risk the distance" toward a single individual, not to find her place in a category. After eleven chapters of this thread reading as DRIFT, landing once as genuinely on-ledger (Ch10), and once adjacent (Ch11), Ch12 is the chapter built to finally absorb it — and instead pointedly declines to, which reads less like an oversight than like the intended final beat: the book's last word on belonging is that the empire-scale answer isn't the one the characters actually needed.

Structurally, there is no LEAK to check at the top of the ladder, and the chapter is unusually disciplined about not inventing a thirteenth tier — it names the temptation twice (Lisa's "there's no thirteenth," Burns's toast to "whoever builds the thirteenth, a long time from now") and declines it both times, including in the one scene (Bramble's "are you?" exchange) most primed to overreach.

With this chapter done, all thirteen chapters (Ch1 through Ch12, the twelfth and final clade) have now been assessed under this method.
