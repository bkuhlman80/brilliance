## LEAK check

This chapter is the cleanest non-presupposition case found across all five chapters run so far, and it's worth naming directly rather than just noting "zero LEAKs" again. At 4.4.c, Lisa states outright and at length everything the unit *cannot* do: "it can't remember, can't model, can't model itself, can't decide, can't regulate. No battery, no sensors, no central anything, no map of where it is or what it just did." That's five clades' worth of capacities explicitly and simultaneously disclaimed in one passage, by the character who built the framework's own vocabulary for talking about them. It's about as far from a LEAK as a text can get.

The "motivation" debate itself (4.9.b) is the chapter's second-cleanest instance: Marge objects directly — "A protocell does not have a motivation... There is no intent in this object" — and the team explicitly agrees to use the word only as "a placeholder for a function," never importing the metaphysics. This is the scene that establishes the book's whole methodological caution about anthropomorphizing, on the page, at the founding chapter. Worth flagging as the clearest possible example of the discipline every later chapter has been observed keeping.

Found **zero** LEAKs.
