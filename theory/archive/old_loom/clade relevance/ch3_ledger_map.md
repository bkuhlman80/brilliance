### Self-Production (Reviser)
**Verdict: ABSENT**

Checked hard for this one, since the ledger's autopoiesis language ("rebuilds its own membranes, organelles, machinery continuously") is easy to confuse with Interiority's boundary-maintenance language. Two near-miss candidates, both of which the text itself explicitly assigns elsewhere:
- *"the membrane is not a wall that happened to be there but a refusal renewed every second against a world that is only doing what water does"* ("What is Kept?", line 554) — reads like continuous self-rebuilding, but this passage is glossing the *boundary*, which the chapter has already named Interiority's turf (Lisa: "a boundary the unit spends itself to maintain... with the maintaining as a continuous cost," line 326).
- Burns, "May We Hold": *"It was continuous work. It was bailing against a world that kept coming in."* (line 466) — again explicitly framed by Burns as elaborating Interiority ("An inside was a thing you kept"), not as a separate rebuilding-of-parts motivation.

No organelle-rebuilding, no membrane-remaking-as-machinery-turnover content distinct from the boundary-maintenance material already claimed by Interiority. Tally: 0/0/0.

**Pattern:** the chapter is extremely strong on the two elements it foregrounds by design — Interiority (the new Motivation) and Intracellular Transport (the "click" as a running motif) — plus the Host signature, which gets an entire named-in-text scene devoted to it. It is comparably strong on Phagocytosis/Acquisition Type because Bart's lecture delivers the ledger definition almost verbatim and the household dramatizes it daily. The three ABSENT elements (Self-Production, Respiration Type, Foraging Type) share a pattern: they are the three elements whose defining content is *mechanistic/metabolic* rather than *behavioral/representational* — the chapter's household-drama register is well suited to dramatizing behavior (foraging-adjacent wandering, memory, engulfing) but never finds a scene for cellular respiration chemistry or organelle turnover specifically. Taxis and Persistence are both THIN in the same way: each gets exactly one line, delivered almost as a checklist tick, with no scene built around it — worth flagging to the author as candidates for a beefed-up beat if fuller-clade coverage is wanted, though per the brief these are not cuts, just gaps.
