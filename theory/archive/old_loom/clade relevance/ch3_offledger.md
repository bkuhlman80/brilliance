## LEAK check

**Corrected on review.** The first pass through this chapter flagged the Sentience vote as two STRONG-severity LEAKs — self-model/Standing vocabulary (Bart, Burns) and symbolic-reference vocabulary (Marge, citing Deacon by name). Rereading the actual scene against the spec's own LEAK definition and against precedent from two prior chapters overturns that call.

The LEAK definition is precise: a beat **presupposes** a Breakthrough or Capacity from a clade above the current one — treats it as functioning in the specimen. It is not a rule against characters *discussing* a higher-clade concept by name. This distinction is exactly what earlier chapters established and rewarded: Ch5's structurally identical sentience vote was checked hard and ruled zero LEAK, on the ground that "nobody at the table asserts Bramble has crossed into a higher clade's capacity." Ch8 went further and explicitly praised a scene where Bart names Speaking (Band-Human's Breakthrough, clade 9, forbidden at Primate) and states outright that Bramble lacks it — "it can read the kid now and it can't say a word to her... Not this build's to fix" — calling it "the cleanest possible non-presupposition... a model instance, not a violation."

Ch3's Sentience vote is the same shape, done at greater length and with three independent voices instead of one: Bart votes no because "Nothing this far down has that. A cell doesn't know it's a cell" (self-model, explicitly denied). Burns votes no because "there is no one home in him... [that] turns up a long, long way up from here, in animals" (same). Marge votes no because "this is a very good domino" — a system with reference but not symbolic reference, the Rubicon named and then explicitly ruled uncrossed. All three name a higher-clade concept in order to deny it applies. That is not a leak of the concept into the specimen; it is the concept being used, correctly, as the yardstick that shows the specimen falls short of it — three separate, careful demonstrations of exactly the deferral discipline the LEAK check exists to reward, not punish.

**Verdict: NONE.** Corrected from LEAK. If anything, this is the strongest instance of clean non-presupposition found across all eight chapters assessed so far — not because the vocabulary is restrained (it isn't; Marge cites a real book by title), but because the scene stages the reasoning in full and lets three characters independently arrive at "not yet," on three different grounds, rather than asserting it in one line.

One softer candidate, reconsidered on the same grounds: Mira's Signature scene (line 480–500), where written self-naming licenses Marge's coinage of "Signature" as a taxonomic term. This was correctly judged not a LEAK on first pass — Mira's own literacy is not attributed to Bramble — and holds on recheck for the same reason as above: nothing here presupposes Bramble himself does this.

Zero LEAKs, consistent with seven of the eight chapters assessed. (Ch3 was, briefly, going to be the exception; it isn't.)

## Skeptic-step notes (full rereads)

**DRIFT #1 — Coffee Garden envelope (lines 36–53).** Reread in full. This is scene-setting for Marge as a character and, on a first pass, could be mistaken for pure texture. But the envelope beat is specific and freighted: *"a corner of letterhead I knew from a hundred steeples, a line of form-text about the removal of records. She pushed it back down without a word."* This is the same Marge-thread the task brief flagged in advance (resignation form / institutional membership, previously found to be genuinely on-ledger in Ch10 as Self-Construal). I checked hard for a Clade-3 mapping and found none — Eukaryote's ledger has no belonging, membership, or social-standing content at all, exactly as the brief predicted. This is DRIFT, confirmed, not a false positive.

**DRIFT #2 — The Noble Savage fable (lines 414–440).** Reread in full. This is the chapter's longest single off-ledger-looking passage, so it deserved the most scrutiny. On close reading it is NOT pure drift — the mechanism Marge invokes to defuse the fable ("We gave it an inside... The model made the fit it's in, and it'll make the next one... it would wear the same groove into the new floor by spring") is a direct, accurate restatement of the board/groove mechanism Lisa built in "Memory," i.e., genuinely on-ledger Interiority content, delivered in the register of a folk tale and a therapist's confession instead of Bart's engineering-lecture register. I credited that portion to Interiority in Pass A (RESONANT). What remains after subtracting that portion — the Iron John retelling itself, the "caged person," "forty years of guarding a corner," the therapist's own unspoken history with the envelope from Coffee Garden — is genuinely off-ledger human psychological material with no Clade-3 mapping. Verdict stands: DRIFT for the fable/psychology frame, RESTATEMENT (already credited) for the mechanism buried inside it.

**DRIFT #3 — Burns's "you get to be the room for a little while" (line 468).** Reread in context of the full "May We Hold" speech. The speech as a whole is legitimately on-ledger (Interiority/Host, credited in Pass A: "An inside was a thing you kept... it was continuous work"). The specific clause aimed at Mira — inheritance, generational succession, "before they build their own" — is doing parenting-and-legacy work that doesn't map to any Clade-3 element; Self-Production was checked as a candidate (an organism "handing forward" its own building materials) and rejected because the chapter's own Self-Production ledger entry (organelle/membrane rebuilding) has no textual presence here — this is about a child's future, not cellular machinery. Judged: DRIFT, but a small, single-clause one embedded in an otherwise on-ledger speech, not worth a cut, worth noting.

It carries real narrative weight but no framework content. UNANCHORED, correctly a candidate only, not a cut — cutting it would gut the chapter's best scene.

**UNANCHORED #2 — Notes on the Third Retrospective arrival scene (lines 113–121).** Reread in full. Weather, fog, character blocking (who hands out beer, who takes Mira's coat, Marge's tea-grip).
