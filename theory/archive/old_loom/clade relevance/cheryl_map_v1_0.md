## What this changes about the fix list

**Ch1 is the only real construction job**, and it's now more urgent than "elevate its priority" — it's the *sole* gap in an otherwise-complete twelve-chapter device. Every other chapter already has the mechanism running, several of them (5, 8, 10, 12) at a level I wouldn't touch without a specific note from you about what's wrong with them. Ch1 needs a scene: Cheryl, alone or with someone, surrendering a piece of clade 12's material — Universality/Identity, "who I represent myself as," the most flattering, most rehearsed, most public account she has of herself — at the *lowest cost in the whole ladder*, since nothing has been refused yet this early. My first instinct: something small and almost funny, the "philanthropist-naturalist" self-myth she'd tell at a dinner party, undercut by one detail she includes for no reason the record needs. But this is a first instinct, not a draft — flag if you want me to take a real pass at it.

**Ch2 and Ch11 are sharpens, not builds.** Both have real material already pointing the right direction; neither lands with the precision of Ch8's chocolate or Ch10's "leaning toward her." Small, targeted work — a line or two each, not new scenes.

**Ch7 needs your ruling, not mine, per your own instruction.** I read it before calling anything. There is no Marge scene in Ch7 at all — confirmed, the only chapter in the set where the Cheryl/Marge line goes fully dark. But Cheryl's own reverse-ladder confession (clade 6, Mastery/Temperament) *is* present and well-executed, delivered instead through Jasmine — "keeping every living thing at exactly the distance where it can't get a hand on you" is a genuinely strong Temperament beat. So the chapter isn't empty; it's just carrying Cheryl's ladder through a different partner than usual, in the one chapter where Bramble's own clade (Autonomy — running on your own model instead of the room) is the most loaded possible mirror for what Cheryl does structurally all book. I can't tell you from the prose alone whether the Marge-silence is the joke or the hole — that's a call about intent, not a location call — but I can tell you the ladder itself doesn't break here. It just changes partners.

**The escalation-by-refused-drafts logic holds up against the actual text.** Cost visibly climbs: Ch4's confession is spoken and complete in one breath; by Ch9 the reflex "comes up" and gets *visibly suppressed* rather than spoken; by Ch12 it's involuntary and physical and she can't control it at all. That's not a flat sequence of equally-costly admissions — it reads like a woman who's been getting harder and harder to let off the hook, exactly as your memo describes.
