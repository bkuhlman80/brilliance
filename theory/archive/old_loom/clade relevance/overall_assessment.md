# Clade-Relevance Pass — Overall Assessment and Ranked Findings

*Synthesis across all twelve chapters' Pass A (ledger map) and Pass B (off-ledger sweep) results. Written for adjudication — every item below is a candidate. Nothing here is a verdict.*

---

## Headline

Twelve chapters, zero LEAKs. Every chapter that came anywhere near a higher-clade concept — the recurring sentience votes (Ch3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 11, 12), the Self-Model boundary in Ch7, the "Worm to Imperial City" slide in Ch11 — deferred it cleanly: named, weighed, and explicitly not claimed for Bramble. That discipline is the load-bearing achievement of the whole draft and it held for thirteen installments without cracking once. Whatever else this pass surfaces, it isn't a laddering problem. The ladder holds.

The real findings cluster into three kinds: one structural gap that's louder than everything else combined (Ch9's Motivations), one recurring pattern that's either a deliberate second clock or an unmanaged drift (the lunch-scene material), and a long tail of smaller, mostly-forgivable coverage gaps and restatements that are craft notes, not defects.

## Ranked findings

### 1. Chapter 9 — three of four Motivations are absent

Mastery, Autonomy, and Affiliation — three of the four base drives on the Band-Human ledger — never surface anywhere in the chapter, named or dramatized. Only Commitment, the new one, gets developed. This isn't a Tendency miss or a Signature miss; Motivations are the floor the rest of the board sits on, so this is structurally the most significant gap in the entire twelve-chapter run, and every pass-1 reader flagged it as the chapter's headline finding independent of anything else found. Worth deciding deliberately: either the chapter is intentionally narrowing to Commitment because that's the clade's whole point (defensible — Speaking is also doing enormous work that chapter and something had to give), or three carried drives quietly went dark and are worth a beat each. My read: probably intentional given how much chapter 9 is carrying already, but it's the single item on this list most worth a conscious yes/no rather than a shrug.

### 2. The lunch-scene device consistently drifts several rungs up, chapter after chapter

Every "Cheryl-and-someone" two-hander scene — the Fisher Brewing lunch (Ch2), the Roof lunch (Ch6), Chuck-A-Rama (Ch9), Water Witch (Ch11), Fisher Brewing again (Ch12), Coffee Garden (Ch3) — lands on Primate/City/Band-register material (standing, self-evaluation, institutional identity, romantic attachment) regardless of what clade the chapter is actually on. It's the single most frequent recurring DRIFT finding in the whole dataset, more frequent than any individual coverage gap. Two readings are both live: this is a second clock, deliberately running at adult-relational pace independent of Bramble's climb (which is how it eventually resolves — Ch10's Self-Construal payoff and Ch12's pointed non-resolution both suggest real authorial intent), or it's an unmanaged habit that happens to always reach for the same emotional register no matter the week. The fact that it resolves meaningfully argues for the former. Worth naming explicitly rather than leaving as background noise, because it's cited as DRIFT in more chapters than any other single thing.

### 3. Orienting Style goes missing two chapters running (Ch6, Ch7), and Ch7 skips the entire model-free side of cognition

Ch7 (Mammal) is otherwise one of the tightest-covered chapters in the run, but both of its genuine gaps — Orienting Style and Temporal Credit — are the model-*free* elements on that clade's ledger. The chapter's whole dramatic interest is model-based cognition (Simulating, Self-Model, Autonomy), which is a coherent authorial choice, but it means the "dumb," non-modeled side of mammalian learning never gets a scene. Combined with Ch6 also missing Orienting Style (a different citation, same functional slot), that's a Tendency the book has dramatized cleanly exactly once (Ch5) and then skipped twice in a row. If there's ever a pass for added material, this is the cleanest single candidate: a scene showing bald reward-timing variance with no model attached.

### 4. Affiliation gets reduced to a single deferred name-check in Chapter 11

Same failure shape as finding #1, one order of magnitude smaller: a base-drive Motivation (Affiliation, carried from Primate) appears exactly once, in a line that explicitly declines to develop it ("the last neuronal thing standing... not tonight"). It's a clean RESTATEMENT by the letter of the method, and it's the kind of thing that's easy to miss because the chapter is otherwise saturated everywhere else. Low stakes on its own; worth noting because it's the same pattern as #1 recurring at a smaller scale, which suggests "carried Motivations get shorted when a chapter has a lot of new material to cover" might be a general tendency worth watching for in revision, not a one-off.

### 5. Coping Style and Experiencing Style are both absent in Chapter 8 (Primate)

Two Tendencies from the same neighborhood of the board, both missing in the same chapter. Individually unremarkable (both are absent elsewhere too), but two adjacent gaps landing together in one chapter is worth a second look — it's the kind of thing that happens when a chapter's household-demo budget gets spent entirely on the chapter's marquee mechanism (here, Agreeableness) and nothing's left over for the quieter ones.

### 6. Two beautiful, substantial DRIFT passages are worth a conscious ruling, not a reflexive keep

Ch11's "Water Witch" bar scene (Brian and Lisa, the Sausalito backstory, the wanting come back up off the bottom) and Ch12's Fisher Brewing confession (Burns not knowing if his son loves him) are both flagged by name in their pass-1 docs as some of the strongest prose in their chapters — and both are attachment/pair-bond material with zero anchor on their chapter's actual ledger (City-Human and Empire-Human respectively, neither of which has a kinship-love element at all). The spec is explicit that quality is not a defense, so these are real findings, not false positives. They're also almost certainly staying in the book. Naming them here isn't a cut recommendation — it's flagging that if a future pass or a beta reader ever asks "why does this scene feel like it's from an earlier chapter," these two are the answer, and it's worth having a ready reason on hand (they're doing character-arc work the ledger was never going to cover) rather than being surprised by the question.

### 7. The long tail of expected, forgivable coverage gaps

Enclosure Type, Acquisition Type, Precision Machinery, Reproduction (Ch1); Life-History Type (Ch2); Self-Production, Respiration Type, Foraging Type (Ch3); Foraging Type, Home Type (Ch4, corrected to present in Ch6); Gut Sense (Ch6); Neuroticism (Ch11, clean absence). Every one of these was checked directly and the pass-1 docs agree the biological referent simply doesn't map onto a household robot or the chapter's chosen register (metabolic chemistry, organelle turnover, literal foraging-ambush behavior). These are not neglect; they're the correct move over faking an anchor. No action needed, but they're worth having on record as "checked and declined" rather than "missed," in case anyone re-derives this list from scratch later.

## Two things worth knowing about the pass itself, not the book

**A recognizable authorial move kept fooling the evaluator, and it's worth naming as a real craft signature.** A large share of the "corrected on recheck" entries across the docs — Ch1's Ghost-in-the-Machine/Operational Closure, Ch2's Blank Slate/State Gating, Ch5's Interiority/Foraging Type, Ch6's Interiority, Ch7's kangaroo essay/Autonomy, Ch8's bakery-lunch/Affiliation — share one shape: on-ledger content delivered through a displaced specimen (a human case study, a different animal, a folk tale) instead of the ledger's own citation. The evaluator's first pass kept filing these as off-ledger because the *specimen* was wrong even though the *mechanism* was exact. This is a genuine stylistic signature of the book — it explains why a naive read of individual chapters would under-credit them — and it's worth knowing about as a pattern rather than rediscovering it chapter by chapter.

**Confidence is uneven across the twelve write-ups.** The earlier chapters in run-order (by the docs' own account, run "out of sequence") needed a second, corrective pass before they stabilized; the later ones (Ch10–12) applied the corrected method from the start and read as more reliable on a single pass. If any of these findings get contested, the Ch1/Ch2/Ch5/Ch6/Ch8 corrections are the ones to reread most skeptically — not because they're wrong, but because they're the ones that already got overturned once.

## What this pass is not saying

No chapter needs a structural rewrite. No LEAK needs fixing. Nothing here says a scene is bad — several of the flagged passages are explicitly the strongest prose in their chapters. This is a location instrument: it says where the clade's own vocabulary is thin, missing, or borrowed from elsewhere, and leaves the cutting decisions where they belong.



# APPENDIX: Clade-Relevance Findings → Fix Directive. Loom → Loom. 

The summary above is a good location instrument and a bad set of recommendations. It ends with "no chapter needs a structural rewrite" and "this is a location instrument," which is what the spec told it to say. Brian has overruled that. We fix every item on the list. This document says how.
Read it before you touch a chapter, because the first two sections change what most of the findings even mean.

PART ONE — The technique everything else depends on
The lunches run the same engine as the floor
The pass flagged the Cheryl two-handers as the single most frequent DRIFT in the dataset: every lunch lands on adult-register material — standing, self-evaluation, institutional identity, attachment — no matter what clade the chapter is on. Six chapters, same finding.
It read the mechanism as a defect. Here is the mechanism.
The clade ladder is Bramble's ladder. The humans do not climb it. They were born at the top of it. Two people in their forties talking about their standing and their wanting are running at full modern register because that is what a human being is — and every one of those old engines is still down there, running, underneath.
Which is the book's entire thesis. The new layer never replaces the old one. The scallop drops back onto the four-billion-year-old way when the new gift strains. So:
Down on the floor, a machine climbing the ladder rung by rung. Up at the table, six people who already have every knob on the board — and one of them cannot mail a form.
Twelve lunches, each one a human being driven by that chapter's oldest engine, at full human complexity. That is the carry-forward, demonstrated twelve times on the people. The floor climbs the ladder; the table proves nobody ever got off it.
DO: key each lunch to its chapter's clade, and go all the way in
Ch 5 is Bilaterian. Impetus, Orienting, Affect. Marge with the form filled in down to the date, who has not mailed it in weeks, most of the way out of a door and not all the way. That is Stern's roundworm — the shy one that will not cross the barrier toward the tempting smell, trial after trial — running inside a forty-four-year-old with a self-model and language and shame and twenty years of church stacked on top of it.
Same drive. Same ancient engine. A vastly more complicated animal.
That is not the lunch reduced to the clade. It is the displaced specimen — the mechanism exact, the specimen borrowed, and the borrowed specimen is a woman in a restaurant.
DO NOT hedge this for taste
The instinct to keep it "subtle" so the seams don't show is the tasteful-literary reflex, and it is the same prior that produced render shallow and you know what I mean. Fusion is not concealment. Brian's discriminator — the reader cannot tell whether they are watching the people or the framework — does not say hide the framework. It says you cannot tell which, because they are the same substance.
So the Impetus lunch is maximally Impetus. All the way down. This is a curriculum, and two worked examples of one mechanism in different substrates is how transfer is taught. A reader who sees Impetus in the worm and in the woman has learned Impetus. A reader who sees it only in the worm has learned a fact about worms.
The visibility is load-bearing. If the reader cannot tell the lunch is running the same engine as the floor, the teaching fails.
The one real division of labor
The retrospective owns the vocabulary. The lunch owns the experience. Lisa names Impetus at the table; the reader carries the word next door and finds Marge sitting in it. Not because the lunch is hiding anything — because a woman in a restaurant does not narrate her own drive-state, and the reader crossing that gap is the act of learning.
Cheryl is the exception, and this is where the book turns. She has been in both rooms. She has the word. See Part Two.
DO: check before you build
Ch 9's Chuck-A-Rama was flagged as drifting to Band-register material — in the Band-Human chapter. That is not drift. That is on-ledger, and the evaluator marked it a defect because it was pattern-matching lunch = adult = drift without checking the clade.
Read every lunch against its own clade before deciding anything is broken. The book may already be doing this by instinct in several places. Where it is, the job is sharpen, not build.
The naturalist turns the camera around
This is the narrative technique the whole back half of the book runs on, and it is the answer to "what is Cheryl's arc." Get this right and half the findings below fix themselves.
Cheryl never breaks register. Not once. Not at the end.
She is Ricketts on page one and Ricketts on the last page. No interior granted, to anyone, ever. She does not go inside Marge, or Jasmine, or Bramble. The temperature never moves.
What moves is where she points the camera.
Two Cheryls, and the seam between them is the book
Cheryl-then — living it, in 2031, with imperfect access to her own reasons. She logs the line of Marge's temple garment under her collar. She does not know why. She cannot name it because she cannot see it.
Cheryl-now — decades later, at the desk, assembling the account. And the assembling is where the access comes from. Not therapy. Not epiphany. You lay the tapes out, you see the same detail three hundred times, and you go looking for why.
So the narration says both, in one breath, and neither half is coy:
I logged the line under her collar. I would have told you it was habit — I logged everything. I can tell you now that I logged nothing else that could not have gone into the report.
That is not a withhold. That is the actual epistemology of a person reconstructing her own life — which is what this book already is: an account built from records, by someone who was not fully present to what the records were recording.
DO: name the thing. Always. In the correct technical terms.
If Cheryl does a striptease, she calls it a striptease.
No more there was a word for it and I was not going to say it. No more I did not reach in after it, on purpose. She has the whole board. She sat in that room for three years while Lisa put the words on the wall. A woman with a complete diagnostic vocabulary for her own condition does not get to pretend she lacks one.
She writes: I logged it. It was of no use to the account. I logged it anyway. And then she names the mechanism, plainly, with the correct word.
That is not a caption. A caption explains what a gesture meant. This is a woman convicting herself with the correct technical term — and then continuing to do it for four hundred pages, in full knowledge, in an account she is editing, and leaving every instance in.
The version where she cannot see it is a woman longing — soft, forgivable, faintly charming. The version where she sees it exactly, names it precisely, and cannot stop is a woman with every knob on the board who cannot mail the form either.
That is so much worse for her. That is the point.
The escalation is drafts refused, not disclosure metered
Cheryl has a taste-prior, and it is the deepest thing about her: never say what you want. She organized an entire life around anticipating what other people need so that she would never have to name a want of her own. That is the water-burp at the scale of a person, and it will bend every sentence she writes.
Bramble's entire job is catching it.
Future-Bramble edits the account. It sends the drafts back. Not with kindness, not with an explanation — just: not yet. And she writes it again, a little more expensive each time.
So the confessions do not escalate by cost, and they do not escalate by dawning awareness. They escalate because the previous draft was refused. The worst one comes last because it took six refusals to get there.
And Bramble never warms. It stays what it is: the one reader who will not look away and will not let her lie. Its refusal to accept her evasions is the most caring act in the book, and it is completely indistinguishable from a machine executing a quality standard. She cannot tell which. She says so. And she keeps writing anyway.
The editing never appears on the page. Not a preface, not a bracket, not a line. It exists only as the pressure that explains why this woman is so pitiless about herself.
The access curve IS the arc
The reader's access to Cheryl's reasons rises across twelve chapters — not because she warms, not because she breaks register, but because the writing is happening across those twelve chapters and she is getting better at seeing what she did.
Bramble climbs the ladder up. Cheryl climbs the same ladder down.
Early, she surrenders the oldest and dumbest things about herself — persistence, hunger, the body that keeps going after the mind has quit. Late, she surrenders the symbolic ones — commitment, what she represents, what she has told herself she is.
The machine assembles a self, cell by cell. The woman disassembles one, in the same order, in reverse. They pass each other.
This is how you key Cheryl's confessions. Not by a mood curve. By the clade.

PART TWO — The findings, and how to fix each one
FIX 1 — Chapter 7 is arguing against the book (highest priority; this is not a coverage gap)
What the pass found: Ch 7 (Mammal) covers Simulating, Self-Model, Autonomy — the model-based mind — and both of its gaps, Orienting Style and Temporal Credit, are the model-free elements. The summary calls this "a coherent authorial choice." It is not.
Why it is a thesis-level defect: the book's central claim is that the new layer never replaces the old one. A Mammal chapter that shows only the model-based mind, with no dumb model-free residue anywhere in it, demonstrates clean replacement — the new layer arriving and the old one going quiet. That is the one thing this book exists to deny, and it happens in the chapter where the reader is most primed to over-generalize, because Simulating is the flashiest breakthrough on the ladder.
DO: write the model-free system as comedy, on the floor
The gift here is that the fix is funny. The model-free system is stupid and does not know it.
Write the beat where Bramble can now run the future forward — genuinely, impressively, the whole new gift on display — and still does the idiot thing, because the reward timing trained it and the model does not get a vote. The model watches the body do the wrong thing and cannot stop it.
That is the funniest beat available in Ch 7 and it is also the thesis. Same substance. Write it on the floor, in a C&H episode, where the table's camera cannot reach.
DO: bring Temporal Credit with it
Bald reward-timing variance, no model attached. The two gaps are the same gap and one scene pays both. Do not write two scenes.
FIX 2 — Chapter 9's three dark Motivations (cash them; do not add them)
What the pass found: Mastery, Autonomy, and Affiliation — three of four base drives on the Band-Human ledger — never surface. Only Commitment develops. Motivations are the floor the board sits on. Every reader flagged it independently.
DO NOT write three beats to fill three cells
Write a Mastery beat, an Autonomy beat, and an Affiliation beat and you will produce three scenes that exist to fill cells, and the reader will smell every one. That is checkbox writing in the bad sense: scenes generated by the gap rather than by the material.
DO: ring the bell on what the chapter is already standing on
Ch 9 has the material and never cashed it. Speech arrives.
* Speech arriving IS a Mastery event. A skill acquired badly, in public, with visible incompetence — and Mastery is the drive to get good at a thing. Bramble getting words wrong, in front of people, and going back at it.
* Speech arriving IS an Autonomy event. Bramble can now refuse in words. Not walk away — say no. The whole meaning of the drive changes the moment the refusal is sayable.
* Speech arriving IS an Affiliation event. Bramble can be talked to rather than at — and can say a thing again because Mira laughed the first time.
Three carried drives. One floor cluster. Zero added scenes. The chapter was standing on all three and never rang the bell.
The general law this teaches
Fix by cashing, never by adding.
A coverage gap is almost never a missing scene. It is a scene that already contains the element and never names or lands it. Look for the bell before you build the tower.
FIX 3 — The lunch "drift" (reclassify, then fix the real defect underneath)
The clade-drift finding is not a defect. See Part One. Do not realign the lunches to the clade by making them smaller or more childlike. Key them to the clade at full adult complexity.
But the instrument found something real and mislabeled it
Six lunches, and every one reaches for the same key — standing, self-evaluation, wanting. That is not clade drift. That is monotony, and it is a genuine defect.
DO: fix it with the ladder, not with realignment
The lunches are where the confessions live. Confessions climb the clade ladder downward (Part One). If all six lunches run the same emotional register, the ladder is not climbing — and the fix is the escalation, not the clade.
Route to `cheryl_map_v1_0.md`. Fill the map before writing.

Orienting Style is latent inhibition — the cue seen so often it has gone to background. This is extremely writable and it is already how households work. The vacuum has been making that noise for six weeks and nobody hears it anymore. Mira stopped seeing the thing on the floor as a thing.
One beat. Household. Behavioral. On the floor. And it pairs naturally with FIX 1's model-free comedy in Ch 7 — the same scene can carry both if you are careful, though do not force it; two small beats beat one overloaded one.
FIX 5 — Ch 11's Affiliation deferral (CHECK BEFORE FIXING — this may be a plant, not a defect)
