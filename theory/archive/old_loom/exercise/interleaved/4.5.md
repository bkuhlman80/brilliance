## The Bramblogues

Bart reached under the cards and came up with the page he'd filed in February, under the five units. He didn't look glad to have it back.

"The bodies went five for five," he said. "Every unit did the thing I built it to do. That's the part I don't miss on. The calls about the *house* are a different matter." He set a finger on the page. "Mira judges. Yes or no."

He found the first line and read it off. "*If the kid picks a favorite, my money's on 5.*" He looked at her. "Who's your favorite."

Mira held up Bramble.

"That's a three. Five's the show-off — the pretty one."

"It's Bramble," Mira said, like he'd asked something with an obvious answer.

"First hour," Jasmine said. "Never came loose."

"No," Bart said, and didn't argue it. Down the page. "*Nobody's going to look twice at 3.*" He nodded at the bot in Mira's hand — the one this whole meeting had been named for. "We've looked at three all afternoon. No." He had the last line without reading it. "*4 ends up in a drawer by July.*"

"Four has a bed," Mira said.

"It has a—"

"I made it. It goes under the toaster."

He set the page down. "A bed. Not a drawer. No." He looked into the camcorder, at Cheryl behind it. "For the record: I went the whole house column and lost it, and signed it swearing I hadn't." He folded the page once. "Big O would frame this."

Mira, already onto the next thing: "Bramble has favorite spots."

"Tell us about the spots."

She held Bramble up, turned it to face the room. "Bramble likes the heating vent. Bramble likes the morning sunbeam. Bramble likes my hand. Bramble doesn't like the back door. Bramble doesn't like the rug in the hallway. Bramble doesn't like the bathroom tile." She turned it back around and set it on the table. "Bramble," she said again, by way of conclusion.

The team sat with it a moment. Then Lisa reached for the marker. "Framework," she said.

## Won't Fix

"The design, Big O can have," Bart said. "The calls, I have to answer for." He took his page out of the box, laid it face-up, and set his beer on it like a paperweight on a confession. "And this quarter I'm reading the whole thing — the parts I'd rather skip included."

Mira looked at her mother before she answered.

"No," she said. "Mama carried him."

"Mama carried him." He let it sit on the tape. Then the last call, and he read it to Jasmine, because she was the one who'd taken it off him. "*Nobody in that house can tell Explore volume up from Explore volume down by looking. Tear the page up if I'm wrong.*"

"The wandering one bumps into more," Jasmine said, not making it worse than it was. "You can hear it from the next room."

"By *feel.* Lisa put a volume knob on it — Explore, up for the improvising and down for the worn-in habit — and you had the two ends sorted by feel before I'd finished being smug, Explore volume up against Explore volume down, and then you went and found the thing I didn't know was in there — the bright cold mornings, the worn groove stuck in the shade, the wanderer that drifts until it finds the sun." He picked the page up. He'd written *tear it up* on it himself, and he did not tear it up; he folded it once and put it in his shirt pocket. "I'm keeping it. You keep the receipt on a bet you lose that badly. It teaches you which sense you bet against."

Mira wasn't looking at the page. She was looking at the new body, at the four old units turning slow at its corners.

"That's One," she said, and put a finger on a wheel. "That's Two. That's Four. That's Five."

"Those are wheels now," Bart said.

"That's One, Two, Four, and Five," Mira said — not arguing, just naming them — "and now they go in a circle."

Bart's mouth opened and didn't find the line. There was nothing on the page about the four. He'd filed a sentence on them in the log — *she'll work that out on her own time, not my department* — and it had read fine on the page and would not survive being said out loud at this table, so he didn't say it.

"They're the wheels," he said, and had nothing to put behind it. "Jasmine. The house. Go."

## Two Wants

Then December's page. He read it quiet, without the strut, because the call that had mattered most all winter was the dull one — the one that meant nothing went flat on the tile and stayed there — and the best call on the page wasn't even his.

He read the dull one first, to Mira, because she was the one underfoot when it used to happen. "*The gauge holds. Bramble stops dying, and Jasmine doesn't carry him off the floor again, not once.*" He looked up. "Did Mama pick him up dead this winter? Even once?"

"You can watch him not want to," Bart said, and didn't take that one for himself. He looked down the table at Lisa, whose board it was, and gave her the win with his chin. "That one's hers. I built the body; she built the wanting." He folded the page. "The dare rode on the gauge — tear it up if it died on the floor even once. It didn't. It stays pinned."
