## Prediction for the Next Installment

**Confidence: Medium**

**Duration**
Medium-length; similar density to Ch 1, roughly 300-400 words. The voice dwells at length on mechanism and metaphor but moves through ideas with steady momentum rather than circling back. Likely structures material in logical sequence rather than doubling back.

**Register**
Clipped and technical overlaid with colloquial ease—trades between jargon ("escapement," "Going knob," "volume") and casual speech ("call it," "is the point"). Flat in affect; no colored language or flourish. Declarative sentences dominate. The tone is expository but not distant: explanatory to an understood audience.

**Concreteness**
Intensely object-level. The voice stays rooted in physical action—winding, kicking, draining, flinching. Abstract concepts are introduced only through their mechanical correlates (personality as "volume," personality as something that "drains"). Refuses abstraction without grounding it in the kinetic.

**Interiority**
Minimal. The speaker names confusion ("I still haven't worked out") but does not name feelings or inner states. Confession comes through technical uncertainty, not emotional revelation. The voice observes its own thinking process ("I keep wanting to treat it") but remains focused outward on what the mechanism does, not what the speaker feels about it.

**Attention**
Lingers on the relationship between components—how the crank and heat work together, how volume drains over time, how either missing piece breaks the whole. Passes quickly over context (who Lisa is, the larger contract, the work setting). Slows down on constraint and process; rushes past names and people.

**Silence**
Notably quiet on: why these robots exist, what they're for, emotional stakes, the speaker's own investment or frustration. Does not say whether the mystery of the personality knob bothers the speaker, whether failure is rare or routine, whether the work is chosen or assigned. The silence is absence of judgment.

**Signature**
- Heavy use of participial chains ("wind it," "wind it and it drains," "leave it slack and it sits dead")—action threaded into explanation
- Repetition as insistence: "Going knob — Lisa's word," "Lose either and it's a paperweight," "That's a knob, same as any other's going to be"
- The tic of parenthetical definition or naming ("call it the part")—the speaker narrating its own language choices in real time
- Short declarative sentences punctuating longer chains: "This is the Going knob." "Takes both, is the point." "That's a knob." The period lands hard.
- Casual self-address: "I will" (finding the name), "I keep wanting"—admission of ongoing work

### Predicted Opening
