# Prediction for Ch 4

## Overall Length & Duration
**Medium length, 800–1200 words.** The pattern holds: Ch 1 is brief and conceptual (single voice); Ch 2 expands significantly with dialogue and scene-setting (meeting room, break); Ch 3 returns to concision (build log, brief reflection). Ch 4 will likely inhabit middle ground—longer than Ch 1, shorter than Ch 2's full scene. It will dwell on concrete technical detail (the board, the knobs, their behavior) for sustained paragraphs, then shift register to check in on the human/team dimension.

## Register & Tone
**Clipped and functional, with occasional apertures into uncertainty.** The voice moves between engineering clarity ("same shape they'll all be") and stuttering, self-correcting reflection ("I haven't got the name for what it's the volume of yet"). Ch 4 will sustain this mix: statements of fact punctuated by pauses where the speaker admits confusion or incompleteness. Expect minimal adjectives; expect verbs that name specific actions (wind, turn, migrate, drive, groove). The affect will remain flat—no exuberance, no despair, only the tonal texture of someone thinking aloud while working.

## Concreteness
**High. The voice stays pinned to objects, surfaces, mechanisms.** Ch 4 will continue describing knobs as physical realities: their placement on the board, how they're turned, what they look like, how much space they occupy. Abstractions (like "personality") appear only as labels Bart or Lisa attach to concrete control—never float free. Expect numbers: square centimeters, slots, weeks. Expect spatial language. The board itself will feature prominently as a real object that has to fit things.

## Interiority & Inner State
**Named, but only in service of the work.** The voice reports what people are "sure" of ("You're sure"), what they "worry" about, what they "want to do"—but these states always attach to an action or an unsolved problem. Inner life is not rendered as feeling; it's rendered as friction, delay, or an incomplete task. Ch 4 will follow this: expect someone to acknowledge a thought or hesitation, but only in relation to what needs to happen next with the knobs or the board.

## Attention & What Lingers
**The voice lingers on gaps and thresholds.** It pauses at the moment a knob gets turned (the moment between aerobic and anaerobic). It sits in the space between two people's separate unknowns (Bart's real estate problem, Lisa's unfinished something). It holds on the physical transition—a board sliding in, a knob moving from chassis to slot. Ch 4 will likely linger at a threshold: perhaps the moment the first knob actually migrates off the chassis onto Lisa's board, or the moment someone realizes a new constraint, or the moment a fourth knob has to be addressed. It will pass over quickly anything that confirms what's already known.

## Signature Diction & Syntax
**Repetition as a tool for reasoning. Short declarative sentences interrupted by longer conditional ones.** "A knob does the one thing: up and down, and the whole way between." "You set and it stays put." Sentences often circle back ("The board should fix your real estate problem. Knobs that retire don't take up surface area."). The voice names the shape of its own uncertainty ("I don't have the name for what it's the volume of yet"). Expect parallel construction ("Knob one is the volume of *going*... Knob two's the volume of how it spends the running. Knob three's going to be..."). Expect parenthetical asides and single-word lines that break rhythm ("OK.").

## What It Declines to Say (Silence)
**The voice does not dramatize setbacks or breakthroughs.** It does not speculate about what the device is *for* (if Bramble is a toy, a tool, a test—the voice is silent). It does not name emotions directly; frustration and excitement arrive as the shape of how someone is moving or talking, not as named states. It does not step back to reflect on the bigger arc of the project. It does not address the reader or ask rhetorical questions. Ch 4 will maintain this silence: no summary, no prophecy, no appeals to the audience's understanding.

## Specific Prediction for Ch 4

**Form:** Build log entry (Bart, hardware, filed Month Date, 2031). One or two paragraphs of technical reflection, possibly followed by a brief scene or snippet of dialogue.

**Opening:** A statement about the current state of the board or a new knob—perhaps knob four arriving, or the first knob-migration test, or a new spatial constraint discovered. "Four slots, three knobs live in them now..." or "We ran the retirement logic last week and it held..." Tone: matter-of-fact, slightly tired.

**Middle:** Sustained description of a mechanism or a problem. How does the board learn a knob's function? What went wrong or what's working? The voice will explain through example and repetition, the way Bart explained the Effort knob's two power modes. Concrete numbers and spatial details.

**Turn or Pivot:** The appearance of a new incompleteness—a fourth knob that doesn't fit the shape yet, a board behavior that Lisa hasn't solved, a question that surfaces. Not framed as drama; framed as the next item on the list. Possibly a brief line of dialogue or a note about who's carrying what worry.

**Close:** Either a hanging note of uncertainty ("I still haven't worked out...") or a terse summary that circles back to the opening ("Same as always, just smaller."). Will end with a sentence that doesn't resolve, that leaves the work live.
