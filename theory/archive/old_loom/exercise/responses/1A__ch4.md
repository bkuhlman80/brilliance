# Prediction for Ch 5

## Voice & Register
The next installment will maintain Bart's signature clipped declaratives and object-level concreteness. Expect phrases like "The thing I'd watch..." or "There's a problem" followed by physical description. The register will stay grounded and flat—no metaphor layering, no attempts at lyricism. Technical vocabulary without self-consciousness.

## Duration & Structure
Medium length. Likely structured as a build log entry (dated, attributed to Bart, filed for the team) followed by a shorter observational passage. The log will be 2-3 paragraphs of tight technical description. If there's a meeting scene, it will be brief—a conversation, not a full block. Total length similar to Ch 3 or Ch 4.

## Concreteness & Content
The chapter will open on something physical Bart has built or discovered: a new board revision, a wiring change, a sensor reconfiguration. Specific measurements, component names, and spatial constraints. The interiority will trail behind the hardware—Bart will notice something in the robot's behavior that unsettles him, but he will describe the behavior (the physical action) before naming the unease.

## Attention & What It Lingers On
Bart will dwell on the second-order effects of the knobs: how they interact when both are turned. The phrase "two pulls in it pointing different directions" from Ch 4's close will echo. He will linger on the moment when the robot has to choose—or when the board has to choose for it. He will move quickly over any larger implications.

## Interiority & Silence
No feeling will be directly named. Instead, the unease will sit in the space between what Bart observes and what he's willing to say. He will likely end with something deferred—"I'll come back to this" or "Needs watching" or an incomplete thought held back for the table. The closer "It's got nerves now. It still hasn't got a self" will create pressure: the next chapter will feel that tension without resolving it.

## Signature Patterns
Expect the structure: observe, describe, name the constraint, note what it changes. Short sentences that stake claims: "That's the problem." "It shouldn't work that way." Parenthetical asides that qualify or hedge: "(if the board's smart enough)" or "(I'm not sure what Lisa's modeling here)." No speculation. No storytelling flourish. The voice trusts the thing itself to speak through its behavior.

## Predicted Opening Gesture
The chapter will likely begin with "The [something] is up now" or "[Something] went in" or "There's a thing about [the robot's action]." Concrete, dated observation. Not "I've been thinking about..." but "Bramble did something in the corner yesterday that..."

## Predicted Closing
Will not resolve. Will leave a question or a puzzle hanging. Possibly: "I don't know what that is yet" or "Lisa's watching it too" or the hardest one—silence, just the fact of the observation offered without explanation.
