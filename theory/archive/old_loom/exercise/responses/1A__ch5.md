## Register
Clipped, direct, the same flat-and-technical register Bart has maintained throughout his build logs. Prose stays in the concrete ("knob," "board," "slot," "wire," "head," "legs") rather than floating into metaphor. Syntax stays tight: short sentences with strong verbs. Object-level throughout. Bart will describe what he built, where it goes, what it does, using direct physical language. The entry will not interpret what the knobs mean; it will say what they *do*. If new knobs are installed this quarter, they'll be named (by Lisa), described mechanically, and dispatched quickly. Speculation will stay grounded in observable behavior.

## Interiority
Inner state will not be explicitly named on the page. Bart does not say "I worry" or "I'm proud" — he shows hesitation through syntax and through what he *doesn't* say. The last section of Ch 5 ("Walking isn't where the thinking goes") is Bart's register for doubt: he states a technical choice (froze the gait) and then offers a veiled reason (it's not where thinking goes). Ch 6 will likely follow this pattern: technical facts narrated, then a short sentence or two that admits discomfort without naming it directly.

## Attention
Lingered-on: the physical placement and function of new components; how the boards connect; what problem was solved or created. Passed over fast: emotional or philosophical significance. Ignored entirely: the broader narrative purpose of the project, the "why" of these machines, the team's hopes for them.

## Signature — Diction and Syntax
- Signature phrase: "[Subject] is [simple descriptor]" ("The knob is the volume of..."; "Walking isn't where the thinking goes")
- Repetition for emphasis: "The knob does the one thing," "All it sets is," "That one didn't come"
- Present-tense action: "The [X] went in," "The legs pick up a limp," "It doesn't get to..." — concrete, unadorned
- Rare, strategic complexity: when Bart turns introspective (last paragraphs of Ch 4 and 5), sentences become more tangled; when technical, they're spare
- Avoidance of judgment words (good, bad, works, fails); instead: "looks like," "seems," "I'm not betting," "mostly comes out as"
