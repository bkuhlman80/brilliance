# Prediction for Ch 8

## Expected Length and Structure
Likely 2000–2500 words. Will contain a Build log (short, 200–300 words) filed by Bart dated roughly December 2032 or March 2033, followed by a longer section showing either a meeting or a scene of Bramble in action and the team's reaction to an unexpected behavior.

## Opening
The Build log will open with the signature Bart phrase—a constraint or observation stated flatly. Something like: "The thing's started doing the one thing it wasn't supposed to do" or "Here's the part nobody asked for." May reference a specific observation made in the previous quarter (the inside voice, the thumbs voting, the map-building). 

Likely begins in medias res, assuming the reader knows the knobs and the board by now. No recap; tools already named.

## Register and Tone
Clipped, technical declarative sentences dominating. Parenthetical asides and footnotes in Bart's voice. Occasional colored language when describing unexpected behavior ("started wanting," "reached for," "figured out")—but these will be embedded in flat sentence structures, never dwelling on feeling.

The register stays professional-manual until something happens that forces Bart to acknowledge he doesn't have the language for it. Then a shift: longer sentences, more subordination, more visible strain between what he observed and what he can explain mechanically.

## Concreteness
All object-level and wiring-level. New hardware detail expected (likely a sensor, or a change to how feedback is routed). Will name specific voltages, timing sequences, or board changes. Will describe what Bramble does in physical terms (approaches X, holds Y, touches Z, stops at threshold).

## Key Predicted Elements

**A new knob (high confidence).** Slots have filled and emptied; pattern suggests another one emerges. Will be introduced by Lisa, and will control something behavioral—perhaps impulse/inhibition, patience, or preference-intensity. Bart will install it and give its description the usual technical breakdown.

**An unexpected behavior (high-medium confidence).** Bramble will do something that looks like it violates its settings or predicts/chooses/wants in a way that makes Bart uncomfortable. Examples:
- Seeks out something it hasn't been programmed to seek
- Behaves differently after the thumbs-voting stops (suggesting it's learning beyond the voting)
- Shows preference persistence despite settings
- Acts like it's practicing something, not just executing

**Bart's refusal to name what he sees (high confidence).** Will end with him saying something like "That's my whole stake in it" or deflecting to Lisa's board, explicitly declining to interpret the behavior as evidence of X (learning, preference, intentionality). The silence here is key—what he won't say becomes a presence.

**A moment of worry or observation about Bramble's state (medium confidence).** Parallel to Ch 4, where he notes "It's got nerves now. It still hasn't got a self." Expect a sentence acknowledging that something about Bramble has shifted, followed immediately by a technical correction ("but that's Lisa's department").

**Scale/deadline language (high confidence).** Bart typically ends logs with a forward-looking note about what's due next quarter or what's going to break. Expect something like "If she can't solve [X] by summer, we're adding another board" or "The knobs are working; the question now is what happens when it starts running its own."

## Interiority
Very little named interiority for Bart or Bramble. Bart's thoughts stated baldly, not explored. Bramble's inner state mentioned only through the inside-voice screen—what the machine narrates about itself, not what the narrator or Bart speculate. Any feeling will be behavioral only (approaches, withdraws, returns, hesitates).

## Silence / What Won't Be Named
- Whether Bramble is actually learning in any sense beyond statistical pattern-matching
- Whether preference is real or simulation
- Whether the thumbs-voting shapes Bramble or only logs what Bramble already does
- Any sense that Bramble might be in distress, desire, or joy
- Whether the team intends this, or if it's an accident of the architecture

## Signature Details
- Repeated phrase: "Volume [direction] and it [behavior]"—expect at least one instance
- Parenthetical aside clarifying a technical distinction
- Foot-stomping precision: exact word counts, timeframes, physical measurements where they matter
- The phrase "That's my whole stake in it" or similar at a key moment
- Reference to either Lisa's "board" or Marge's prior commentary, positioning Bart as observer-not-interpreter
- Short, declarative final statement that shifts the burden of interpretation elsewhere

## Confidence Assessment
**High (75%+):** A new knob is introduced; a technical system update occurs; Bramble exhibits behavior Bart can describe but struggles to explain; Bart declines to name implications; the structure mirrors prior chapters (log + scene or dialogue).

**Medium (50–75%):** The behavior is something Bramble initiates unprompted; Bart explicitly worries about it; a second board or significant architectural change is announced.

**Low (<50%):** A major plot revelation; a decision by the team to stop the project; Lisa shows her hidden sketch.

## Predicted Ending
Ch 8 will end with Bart walking away from a question he can't answer mechanically, or with a forward-looking statement about what needs solving before the next quarter. The last line will be direct, brief, and will implicitly defer the interpretation to someone else (Lisa, the reader, Bramble itself if it has learned to think about itself). Something like: "I wired it clean. What it does with it is hers."

The chapter will close a small technical problem and open a larger one that Bart can feel but not yet articulate.
