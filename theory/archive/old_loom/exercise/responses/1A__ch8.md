# Prediction for Ch 9

## Duration
Medium length, comparable to Ch 6-8 range. Dwells at length on a single technical detail or problem — the "one big thing" pattern that Bart has established across these chapters. Lingers on failure, resistance, or unexpected behavior longer than on success.

## Register
Stays in Bart's characteristic mode: clipped, technical, concrete. Measured expansion when the problem reveals itself. No exclamation or surprise — the voice presents fact on fact, building the weight of something understood piece by piece.

## Concreteness
Highly physical and object-level throughout. Wires, buttons, boards, motion — stays grounded in what can be seen and measured. If a problem emerges, it's located in hardware: something doesn't coordinate, something breaks under load, something interferes with something else. No abstract difficulty.

## Interiority
Minimal. Bart names his own emotional state only when he's naming a shift in what he's worried about (see "bellyaching," "bracing for," "proud of"). The machine's inner state comes through its behavior and the board's output, not through Bart's speculation. When Ch 7 introduces the "inside voice," Bart credits it to Lisa's board and explicitly declines to interpret it ("I couldn't walk you through it if you paid me").

## Attention
Follows the trajectory established in Ch 4-8: introduces one new hardware element or control axis, traces how it interacts with existing systems, then shifts to the constraint or cost it creates. Lingers on the coordination problem. Passes quickly over historical details and installation logistics. Moves away from the knobs themselves — those are "done" — and toward what happens when they all run together.

## Silence
Does not dwell on what the machine is "feeling" or "becoming." Does not ask whether it should be given more autonomy or less. Does not address the broader project context (why this machine, why these knobs, what it's for). Declines to speculate on what comes after the board is full. If something breaks, Bart names the breakage and what he's doing about it; he does not ask why.

## Signature
- Opens with a date and filing note: "Bart, hardware. Filed [date], for the team."
- States problems as arithmetic or topology (space constraints, slot constraints, wiring conflicts).
- Distinguishes between his contribution ("My end," "I wired," "I froze," "I built") and Lisa's ("Lisa's board," "Lisa's knob," "her label").
- Closes by restating the constraint or the shift: "I keep counting," "There's no empty one to land on."
- Uses exact numbers for clarity (four slots, three hundred fifty words, six square centimeters).

## Prediction

**Ch 9 will be a Bart entry, dated approximately one quarter later (March or June 2033).**

It will open with the filing header and likely begin by stating what was added or changed since the last quarter — either a new knob in an existing slot (forcing another withdrawal of an old control) or a new constraint that the full board now reveals (something that couldn't show until all four were running in concert).

The core of the entry will dwell on **interference**: two or more knobs setting the machine at odds with itself, or a hardware limit that surfaces only when all four axes are modulated at once. The problem will be concrete — physical congestion, a wire routing that creates latency, competing signals — not conceptual. Bart will present it as a fact to be worked around, not a flaw to be mourned.

He will likely **diminish his own stake in the problem or solution** ("Not my department," "Lisa's side," "the board handles that"). If he has solved something, he will name it plainly and move on.

The entry will end with a restatement of the new steady state: either "the board is full and won't grow," or a new architecture emerging in Lisa's sketches that Bart is bracing for, or a behavior of the machine in operation that surfaces only when all systems run live.

**Confidence:** High.

The arc of Ch 2–8 traces a steady progression from single knobs to full board, with each chapter introducing one new control and its interference with prior ones.
