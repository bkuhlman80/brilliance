# Ch 10 Prediction

## Duration
Medium length, comparable to Ch 8-9 range. Dwells on technical execution and a specific moment of crossing or installation. The voice sustains focus on a single task or procedure without racing through it.

## Register
Clipped, sparse. The idiom "the thing," repeated objects, short declarative sentences. Technical but not abstract — always grounded in the physical act of making or moving. No flourish. No colored language. The syntax stays close to the mechanism.

## Concreteness
High. Opens with hardware or a physical installation. Objects present: boards, cables, the new unit. Actions concrete — mounting, wiring, running, testing. If names are mentioned (Mira, Lisa), they anchor specific tasks or moments, not philosophizing.

## Interiority
Minimal. The narrator names doubt, worry, and pride only when they attach to work—to the thing not fitting, to what wasn't foreseen. The mind stays external, reporting what was done and what it shows, not what was felt about it. One exception: a moment of recognition or something unsaid between two people, the kind Bart holds in pauses.

## Attention
Fixates on the boundary between the old board and the new, or between the inherited mind and its new body. The narrator lingers on discrepancies—what copied, what didn't, what stays behind. Passes quickly over completed work. Slows at the moment something first runs or first fails to do what was expected.

## Silence
Skips over Lisa's reasoning when it's hers alone to know. Doesn't ask why the new unit was made or what it's for. Doesn't say what the narrator wanted or feared in advance. Stops before saying how the new thing felt to watch. The gap between making and meaning—between Bart's board and what it becomes in the kid's hands—stays unspoken.

## Signature
"The thing," "I don't," "It's not," repeated subjects and tight syntax. Time markers sharp (a weekend, this quarter, by spring). Measurement and space concrete (four slots, the board, the cable, the forehead screen). The phrase "I'll let Lisa tell you" is a marker—a moment where the narrator reaches the edge of what he can know and steps back. The closing holds a fact that splits what was copied from what stayed behind, and the weight lands on the last word.

**Register**: Stays clipped, stays sparse. If there's a feeling, it's named only in the tightest possible form (doubt, satisfaction, a pause). The voice doesn't reflect; it reports and marks the point where reporting stops.
