# Prediction for Ch 2

## Duration
Medium length, roughly comparable to Ch 1 — sustained enough to turn something over multiple times, but not a long dwell. Will move through several distinct objects or moments without lingering beyond what each requires.

## Register
Clipped and practical, with moments of expansive explanation. The baseline is report-like (spare, direct), but it opens up when a mechanism or principle wants describing. No ornament. Sentences tend toward compound structure with "and" — not building drama but tracking parallel things.

## Concreteness
Highly concrete and object-level. This voice moves through physical stuff: parts, materials, motion, time on a surface. When something abstract comes up (physics principle, intention, a character trait), it gets anchored immediately to what it *does* — what it looks like or sounds like or how long it takes. The voice doesn't float.

## Interiority
Inner state is *not* named directly. The narrator tells you what he's proud of, what he thinks about a thing's personality ("busy little thing," "more patience than you'd want in a roommate," "the one only an engineer loves"), but these are side effects of watching the object; they're not felt states. The voice reveals itself through what it notices and what it chooses to explain, not through what it claims to feel.

## Attention
Lingers on mechanism: how the Seebeck effect works, the exact sequence of fill-and-kick, the stagger between kickers. Passes quickly over social/logistical frames ("Lisa's running these over," "if the commission let me ship four") — these are context, not the point. Dwells longest on the personality of each unit, but even then, personality is read off behavior ("lopsided, on purpose") not elaborated.

## Silence
Doesn't name emotion, doubt, or stakes directly — just announces them sideways. Doesn't explain what the units *are for* at the Burns place or why they matter. Doesn't dwell on the fact that one unit is substandard (unit 4). Doesn't describe the actual physical appearance beyond function (no color, no size, no texture beyond "stubby"). Won't explain the political or economic context of a hardware delivery in 2031.

## Signature
- Opening with **need-to-know pragmatism** ("here's everything in the box before anybody makes me explain it twice")
- **Parenthetical asides** that reveal priorities: "(a low capacity supercapacitor)," "just a buzzing phone walking itself across a table"
- **Lists as affection**: numbered units, each with a one-line personality, each explained
- **Physics in plain speech**: technical terms used without apology, but always made legible through analogy or behavior
- **Casual naming**: "the trickle," "the twitch," "the personality" — letting names emerge from use
- **And-logic**: chains of equal weight, not hierarchy ("it hops a hair in one direction, then sits there the better part of a minute filling back up before it can flinch again")
- **Tone of mild judgment without blame**: "the one only an engineer loves" (affectionate flatness); "If the commission let me ship four, this is the cut" (factual, not bitter)
