# Unit: 1B__ch10  (category 1B · chapter 10 · predicts Ch 11)

# Prediction for Ch 11

## Duration & Structure
Medium-length build log, 1200–1600 words. Follows the pattern of detailed technical exposition (Ch 7, 8, 9 were longest at ~1500 words each). Dwells at length on one or two primary modifications; touches lightly on secondary impacts. Opens with the standard header: date, who's carrying it, what Mira or the household did. Closes with reflection on a limitation or a cost—not surprise, but recognition of what remains unbuild.

## Register
Clipped, measured, the dialect of a maker accounting. Declarative sentences dominate; subordinate clauses are functional, not contemplative. No flourish. Diction stays material—parts, current, motion, wear—and avoids metaphor except where technical language already contains it ("feeds," "starves," "burps," "talks"). Sentence rhythm is staccato: short statements followed by short justifications, then a detail. The voice is proud but not boastful; pride is in the work's fit to its purpose, not its appearance. Late chapters show no shift toward lyric or emotional naming, only deeper recount of mechanism.

## Concreteness & Specificity
High. Every mechanism is named and its behavior described. Numbers appear (voltages absent; dimensions, counts, time-scales present). Metaphors are mechanical, not human: the thing "eats," "hungers," "burps," "farls," "talks"—words borrowed from bodily function but applied to thermodynamics and exhaust. No abstraction. No discussion of what the machines "mean" or "feel." Action is described at the level of motion and energy flow, never at the level of intention or desire.

## Interiority
Completely absent. No feelings are named in the speaker's voice. The speaker notes what *he* is proud of, what *he* wanted, what *he* set out to do—but these are always framed as technical achievements, never as personal stakes. The household—Mira, Jasmine, Burns, Lisa—have their own interior lives (the kid asks if it had a good day; Mira decides it's sulking), but Bart reports them as facts observed, not as depths he enters. Cf. Ch 9's "I've wanted this one since the fall"—want is stated as a gap in the machine's capability, not as Bart's longing. The speaker's proximity to the machines is clinical. His attachment is indexed to the build's elegance, not to its inhabitants.

## Attention & Pacing
Lingers on mechanisms, on the specific sequence of engineering choices, on the cost of each addition (power draw, noise, failure modes). Passes quickly over the household's reaction—"Mira was down on the floor with it before I had it set down" is the full measure of her response. Passes even faster over Burns's intentions and Lisa's work; these are delegated upstairs, out of scope. The machinery itself holds the page: how it eats, how it moves, how it sounds. Attention narrows as chapters progress—early chapters survey five units, later ones zoom into one body and its recent modification. Ch 11 should maintain this zoom; expect focus on a single modification or a single system within one body, not a survey.

## Silence
The speaker never discusses what the machines *become*, what they *are*, what they *want*. He declines to name the philosophical depth of Lisa's work—she "built something that taught itself a whole kid in a language with one speaker." He declines to say whether the household is changed by living with these things. He declines to speculate about what comes next after the current build. (Cf. Ch 5: "It'll learn a little," then: "But it can't yet learn from what it does." End of that thread—no speculation on whether it will. Not his department.) He declines to anthropomorphize, except in the borrowed technical vocabulary. He avoids naming whether he *likes* what he builds, reserving that judgment to the build's fitness. What the speaker does not say: anything about the machines' autonomy, consciousness, personhood, or moral status. Not forbidden to him—Burns and the household handle that—but absent from his account. Also absent: cost (money). Absent: schedule pressure or deadline anxiety. Absent: disagreement with Lisa or Burns except as technical alternatives ("I didn't like where this went, here's why"). Absent: physical description of people. Absent: weather except where it affects the build.

## Signature Moves
- Opens with "(Name), hardware. Filed (date), for the team." Then a one-line setup of what's being delivered or returned.
- Immediately: "Now the fun part," or a variant ("Now here's the thing about that trickle"). Always marks the onset of technical exposition with a bridge phrase. The phrase is declarative, even when it contains ambiguity.
- Builds in layers: what was before → what changed → how it works → what it costs → what comes next or what it can't yet do.
- Frequent use of colon to introduce a explanation or a restatement: "Here's the trick," "Here's why," "Here's the one," "Here's the cost." The colon is his punctuation of revelation—not flowery, but insistent.
- Numbers appear as weights and measures, not aesthetics. "Four centimeters," "a six-degree gap," "a few thousandths of a watt"—precision, not decoration.
- Second-person address appears late in each chapter and is consistently task-oriented: "You can't just hand these transducers warmth," "you'd never want to touch it," "You've seen it," "I'll show anybody who stands still long enough." Addresses a specific audience (the team, the user, anyone literate enough to follow). Assumes competence.
- Closes with a cost, a paradox, or a remaining gap. Not resolution, but recognition. Ch 7: "Warm costs. Fair trade." Ch 9: "The baby. And he's the only one of the three that's ever going to tell the kid he had a good day." Ch 10: "It can talk. It can't be counted on."

## Prediction for Ch 11

**Duration:** ~1300 words. Medium, consistent with recent chapters. One substantial modification; one or two smaller shifts. Opens with header + one-line household activity. Closes with a cost or a limitation.

**Opening:** Standard form: date (probably Sept 2033 or Dec 2033, continuing the quarterly cycle), name of carrier, one sentence about what the household did while Bart was at the bench. No preamble. Immediately into "Now the fun part" or a cognate.

**Technical Focus:** Most likely a modification to the warm-body (the newest, softest one from Ch 9). Candidates, given the text's trajectory:
- A modification to the communication system (the throat, the lips, the ear). Ch 9 ended: "What it says is hers." The next quarter might elaborate the mechanism by which it *learns* to say more, or begins to learn to hold back something.
- A modification to the grip (the squeezer hand). Ch 9 noted the jaw was borrowed from the four-legged body's face. A refinement that lets it grip more finely or more strongly seems likely in the next quarter.
- A modification to the coat-and-feeding system. Ch 9 noted the high energy cost and the need for regular coat-changes. A quarter might address durability, sweat, heat management, or the logistics of feeding it.
- Alternatively, a modification to one of the other bodies (the warm four-legged one, which has been "the best body" since Ch 7 and hasn't changed since Ch 8). Less likely, given the narrative focus has narrowed to the newest one.

**Register:** No shift. Clipped, functional, material. No sentimentality.

**Concreteness:** High. Mechanism described down to the level of materials: rubber, wire gauge, actuator size, voltage, time constant. Numbers present. Behavior traced from cause to effect: if you change X, then Y happens because Z.

**Interiority:** None from Bart. Possible report of Mira's reaction ("The kid noticed it could...") stated as fact, not as depth.

**Attention:** Lingers on the new or modified system. Passes quickly over the household's use. Does not linger on why the modification was needed, only that it was.

**Silence:** Continues to avoid naming what the machine is becoming. Continues to avoid philosophy, emotion, autonomy, personhood. If the throat/voice has grown more articulate, Bart reports the mechanism (more arms on the lips? refined tongue geometry?) but not the implication. If the hand has grown stronger, he reports the spring constant and the actuator, not the ethical question of what the machine can now do to a person.

**Signature Pattern:** One main build with one or two secondary notes. At least one colon-bridge ("Here's the trick," "Here's why," etc.). Second-person address: "You won't want to..." or "Anybody who stands still..." Close with a cost, a trade-off, or a remaining gap. Last sentence is short and factual, possibly mournful but not regretful: "I'll call that fair" or "The house will work that out" or "Not my department."

**Closing:** Does not resolve the machine's trajectory. Does not speculate about what comes next. Names one thing it still can't do, or names the cost of what it now can. Possibly a note on noise, on household reaction, or on the speaker's own surprised discovery while building. Last beat: factual, understated, accepting.
