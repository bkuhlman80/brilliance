# Prediction for Ch 3

## Duration
Moderate length, similar to Ch 2 (roughly 400-600 words). Will dwell extensively on technical specifics and the physical mechanisms of a new upgrade or modification. Will move quickly through emotional or relational context.

## Register
Clipped to moderate. Sentences are predominantly declarative and functional, occasionally lengthening when Bart must explain a mechanism or physical principle. The tone remains matter-of-fact, unmarked by sentiment. When building toward a point about what something does, the pace accelerates slightly through accumulated detail.

## Concreteness
Highly concrete. The chapter will be anchored in physical components, measurements, and observable behaviors. Objects dominate: parts, transducers, knobs, materials. Abstract concepts appear only when required to explain mechanism (like the Seebeck effect in Ch 1 or "Aerobic or anaerobic" in Ch 2), and are introduced via physical analogy.

## Interiority
Minimal named inner state. Feelings about the work may be implied through what Bart chooses to describe as "beautiful" or worth explaining, but fear, doubt, frustration, satisfaction will not be stated directly. Personality is revealed through attention and preference (what he takes pride in, what he notices) rather than interior reflection.

## Attention
Sustained focus on a single upgrade or new component; likely a second unit receives a modification similar in scope to how Bramble got the chip and sensors. Passes quickly over: explanations of *why* upgrades were needed, any discussion of the house's preferences or requests, relational friction or resolution. Does linger on how the mechanism works and what behavior it produces.

## Silence
Will not name any emotion explicitly. Will not explain the house's reasoning or needs in detail. Will not describe Bart or Lisa's conversation or negotiation around what to build. Will not reflect on whether the upgrade succeeded or failed. The opening will likely come as a date/timestamp and perhaps a dry summary of what was upgraded, then move directly into technical exposition.

## Signature
Opening follows the established pattern: "**[Name], hardware. Filed [date], for the team.**" The phrase "here's everything" or functionally equivalent framing appears early, setting up the explanation. Baroque noun phrases with nested technical modifiers ("the little fast one," "a dumb little table"). Occasional self-aware asides ("that stagger is the personality"). Strong preference for the specific detail over the category: names the STM32, specifies "relay — coil-and-armature," distinguishes "supercap" from "battery." Short sentences alternate with longer clarifying ones that stack prepositional phrases.
