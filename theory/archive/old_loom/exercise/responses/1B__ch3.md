# Ch 4 Prediction

## Duration & Opening
Expect a chapter roughly 800-900 words, similar to Ch 3's length. The opening will likely continue the same filing structure: "*Bart, hardware. Filed [date], for the team.*" The entry will probably cover work done over a specific period (weeks to a few months), continuing the quarterly or semi-regular update pattern.

## Register
The voice remains clipped and direct, with technical precision blended into conversational rhythm. Expect the same mix: engineering facts stated plainly, then a colloquial observation or moment of personality. No expansion into flowery language. The register stays anchored in the physical and mechanical, with occasional dry humor ("It's worse engineering every way you can measure").

## Concreteness
Very high. Bart describes objects, dimensions, electrical principles, mechanical actions. Ch 4 will likely open with a specific modification or addition to the existing machine — a new sensor, a changed behavior, a different component. The description will move through the system part by part, showing how pieces fit and function. Expect specific measurements (percentages, degrees, voltage ranges) and named components (chips, transducers, ratchets).

## Interiority
Minimal naming of inner states. Bart does not describe feelings; he names his own judgment about the work ("I'm proud of," "I like that"). His interior life shows through *what he built and why* — his design choices reveal what he values. Ch 4 will likely contain one or two brief personal asides ("the kid had names for all five of them"), but the bulk will be technical description. No introspection. No doubt.

## Attention
Bart lingers on mechanism and elegance of solution. He passes over logistics, timelines, and interpersonal dynamics very fast ("Lisa's running it back," "Burns made my weekend hard"). He dwells on the clever parts — how four separate wheels each act as engines, how the vortex tube creates the temperature gap. Ch 4 will likely dwell on a new clever solution or unexpected behavior, moving quickly past context (when it was built, why someone asked for it).

## Silence & Signature Omissions
Bart does not explain why Burns wants the machine to work this way (he accepts "that's exactly the point" without probing). He does not say whether the device *succeeds* at its purpose. He does not describe the kid's reaction to changes. He names what he built; he does not name what it's *for* beyond the immediate function (cleaning, moving, feeding itself). Ch 4 will likely maintain this silence — no philosophical conclusion, no statement of purpose, no moment of standing back and admiring the whole project. The focus will stay on the current increment.

## Diction & Syntax Fingerprints
- Sentence starters favor the subject of the action: "I put," "I didn't buy," "It rolls," "The kid had"
- Explains mechanism through analogy to everyday objects: "like a buzzing phone walking itself," "pass for driving"
- Uses casual qualifiers: "lousy," "sane," "real," "clean-ish," "beats the hopping by a mile"
- Technical terms appear without fanfare or definition: transducers, supercapacitors, Seebeck effect, ratchet, pawls, vortex tube — named as known
- Parenthetical asides for detail: "(coil-and-armature, the kind that clack)"
- Ends chapters with a summary of scope: "five things twitching" became "one thing that drives"
