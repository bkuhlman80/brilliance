# Prediction for Ch 6

## Duration & Scope
Medium length (similar to Ch 4-5 range). Will dwell extensively on a specific technical failure or limitation, following the pattern of naming a problem, explaining its mechanism, and walking through the chosen response. Will likely open with a date-stamped project note to the team.

## Register
Technical and precise, carrying Bart's characteristic bluntness about engineering constraints. Tonal texture: matter-of-fact, with traces of defensiveness when the solution is imperfect or when external constraints (Burns, Mira's needs) force suboptimal choices. Will include at least one moment of wry acknowledgment ("I'll be honest," "it's not pretty," "fine, it's his commission").

## Concreteness
Heavy on object-level detail—specific parts, measurements, observable behavior. Will describe what the machine *does* (its failure mode, its new capability) in concrete terms before or instead of naming the underlying principle. When abstraction appears, it arrives late and carries less weight than the physical description.

## Interiority
Minimal. Bart does not name feelings; he names what he built, what it does, what he had to change. When emotional stakes appear (Mira's disappointment, the vacuum on stairs, the lost relay-clack), they arrive as *externals*—other people's reactions—not as Bart's interior response. His closest approach to interiority is pride or mild regret, always attached to the work itself ("it's not the sound I wanted," "the part I'm proud of").

## Attention
Will linger on: the mechanics of failure; the engineering trade-off (cost, noise, complexity, elegance); what the machine must do differently next time. Will pass quickly over: Burns's reasoning, Lisa's code, Mira's actual needs (these appear as *constraints*, not subjects). Will not explain why the house wants what it wants—only that it does.

## Silence
Will not name success or failure as moral categories. Will not say the design is "better" or "worse"—only that it is or isn't. Will not dwell on whether Mira or Burns will be happy. Will not explain what the machine *is for* beyond its immediate task. Will not justify why Bart chose this approach over another (except in terms of what's mechanical). If a solution is ugly or costly, Bart notes it without defending it.

## Signature Diction & Syntax
- Opens with "Here's [the thing about / why]" to frame the problem
- Uses "the part I'm proud of" as a bridge to highlight what mattered in the build
- Strings together descriptive clauses with em-dashes and semicolons, building a dense picture of motion or mechanism
- Refers to the machine by nickname (Bramble, PacMan) once established, then by pronoun
- Uses second-person address ("you'd" / "your") to invite visualization
- Avoids jargon's shorthand; writes out the *effect* (the transducer reaches into that gap) before naming it
- Final sentences often deflate or close with a practical note ("Burns says that's exactly the point," "I'll call that a good weekend," "the knowing is hers")
