# Prediction for Chapter 8

## Duration
Medium length, comparable to Ch 6 and 7. Will dwell on a technical problem for one quarter (approximately 600-800 words), following the pattern of one focused upgrade per chapter. May linger on either the mechanism of a fix or an unexpected consequence, but not both—Bart tends to resolve one and preview the next.

## Register
Clipped, matter-of-fact. Maintains the dry, spare diction that has been consistent across all seven chapters. Will include brief asides in parentheses or dashes. Likely contains one sentence of restrained pride ("The part I'm proud of" or "Here's the one I like") as this phrase appears in every chapter. No elaboration on why anything matters to anyone else.

## Concreteness
Stays grounded in physical detail and mechanism—specific components, what they do, how they're wired. Will name parts, describe flows of energy or signal, explain a sequence of cause and effect in material terms. Abstraction appears only as names for states or modes (RUN, TUMBLE, STOP; Fast/Slow spend; Aerobic/Anaerobic), not as speculation.

## Interiority
None expected. Bart does not name feelings, not his own or anyone else's. Inner states of the machine are described as behaviors or states (it "hunts," it "sulks," it "points," it "knows"), but only as observable outputs. When Mira or Jasmine or Burns or Lisa have needs or intentions, Bart reports what they did or what he infers they wanted—never what they felt. This chapter will maintain that silence.

## Attention
Will spend time on:
- A new mechanical problem that emerged (likely from the furnace build's side effects: power drain, noise, heat management, or water)
- The specific fix Bart chose and why
- Unexpected consequences of that fix, or deferred complications ("This next thing...")

Will pass over quickly:
- Any emotional reaction from the household
- Why the problem matters beyond its technical fact
- Judgment on whether the trade-off was worth it (though Bart will state the cost plainly)

## Silence
Will not explain:
- What the machine is ultimately *for* or what Burns wants it to become
- How the household is using it day-to-day
- Whether Mira prefers the new version
- Any larger purpose or vision

Will likely decline to answer (or answer sparingly) the question that has been building since Ch 5: whether this machine will ever have a self, or intention, or capacity to learn from its own actions and change its own future behavior. Bart has said "it's got legs now. it still hasn't got a self." That philosophical line will probably recede; the next chapter will be about what broke and what he fixed, not what it means.

## Signature
- Opens with "Carried it back / Lisa's running it back" (the familiar handoff ritual)
- Contains one hardware failure or unexpected behavior uncovered in use
- Includes at least one technical detail that reveals a constraint: a battery cold, a motor that eats current, a signal that takes time to travel, a material that won't hold, a design that creates an unintended side effect
- Names the problem plainly, explains the fix in sequence
- Ends by noting either the cost of the fix, a noise that returned, a new need the machine now has, or a problem deferred to next quarter
- Likely closes with a short statement of fact (not hope): "Fair trade," or "It runs this way now," or "That's the build."

## Confidence and Basis
**Med-High** (70%).

The pattern across all seven chapters is remarkably consistent: one upgrade per quarter, one technical focus, one mechanism at the center, one cost named, one small reversal or irony noted (the relays Bart loved became too quiet; the furnace eats constant current; the body learned to name the thing it sees but can't change what it does based on what it learned). The chapter typically builds from a problem identified in the previous quarter's epilogue (e.g., "that's the next thing, Lisa says") into a solution this quarter. The voice has not wavered: direct, technical, withholding judgment, attentive to physical fact and unintended consequences, resistant to narrative closure.

The one uncertainty is *which* problem gets solved this quarter. Candidates:
- The furnace eats current constantly (Ch 7 ending: "Warm costs")—likely resolution or management strategy
- The water burp happens 2-3 times daily (Ch 7)—might require a secondary fix or redesign
- The machine still cannot learn from its own actions (Ch 5 epilogue deferred this to "next thing")—Bart will either solve it or decline it

Most likely this chapter addresses the power drain or water problem (hardware, visible, Bart's domain), rather than the learning problem (Lisa's domain, philosophical, less certain). The machine will walk back through the door. One thing will be different. One cost will be paid for it.
