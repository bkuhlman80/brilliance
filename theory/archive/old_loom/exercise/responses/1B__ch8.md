# Unit: 1B__ch8  (category 1B · chapter 8 · predicts Ch 9)

# Prediction for Ch 9

## Duration
Likely mid-length, following the prevailing pattern. The voice dwells longest on mechanism and its consequences, lingers on transitions in capability. Will probably clock between 800–1200 words. The quarters are compressed toward the present, so the narrative momentum pushes toward less expansiveness, not more.

## Register
Clipped and technical throughout. The diction is precise, repetitive, declarative. Avoids ornament. The syntax favors short clauses and a steady rhythm of assertion — "here's the thing," "that's the trick," "that's the whole way it moves." The register does not modulate by emotional state; it modulates by *density of mechanism*. When there is more mechanism to unpack, the register expands slightly; when it tightens, the rhythm accelerates. Flat in emotional temperature. Colored only by Bart's evident satisfaction in the details themselves — his pride lives in specification, not in judgment.

## Concreteness
Hyper-concrete. Every abstract move is anchored to a physical fact. "Feeding climbs to the top of the pile" is immediately translated into "stops coasting toward flat." The voice never hovers in interpretation. It lands on objects, wires, actuators, the exact measurements, what it cost. The only abstract concept allowed sustained attention is the difference between what Bart built and what Lisa built — and even that stays tethered to the specifics of the boundary.

## Interiority
Essentially absent on the page. No named inner state, no reflection on feeling, no access to subjective experience. The only exception is the recurring phrase about what Bart is proud of — and even that pride is not described; it is demonstrated through what he chooses to linger on. In Ch 4, he mentions he misses the relay clack. That is the closest to emotional naming: regret named and immediately contextualized as a preference for a prior sensory fact. Interiority appears only when it can be pegged to a concrete loss or gain.

## Attention
Lingers on:
- The mechanism of transfer — energy moving, being conditioned, being spent
- The moment a new capability emerges
- The cost of a capability: what it demands, what it eats, what it asks the house to provide
- The distinction between Bart's build and Lisa's build
- The sensory signature of a working thing (the clack of relays, the drone of the compressor, the burp of the water vent)

Passes over fast:
- The emotional response of the household
- Narrative consequence or dramatic resolution
- Reflection on what any of this means
- The child's experience, except as a marker of what the machine learned or failed to learn

## Silence
Declining to say:
- Whether Bart likes or dislikes the Burns commission or the Burns aesthetic
- What the machines are ultimately for, beyond their immediate tasks
- How the household experiences these incremental changes
- Whether the upgrades are progressing toward something or merely iterating
- Any prediction about where this is heading
- His own opinion on whether a machine can have a "self" — he states the fact that it hasn't got one, but doesn't speculate

## Signature
The phrase "here's the thing," used to signal that a counterintuitive or important fact is about to be stated.

The structure: statement of build, *then* the mechanism, *then* the cost, *then* the sensory fact.

The paired structure: "X and Y" — "runs in bursts — sits, fires off a flurry of hops quick as you like, then stops dead to refill off the slow store before he can go again. Aerobic or anaerobic is the mechanism under it, the way Lisa describes it; same bot, two ways to spend."

The refusal of judgment, replaced by concrete specification: "It's worse engineering every way you can measure, and Burns says that's exactly the point — life doesn't run on induction. Fine. It's his commission. I built him his dirty hole."

The meta-observation about the build, often appearing as a late sentence: "I'll call that a good weekend." "That's the whole reason the body exists." "That's about the only noise left in the thing."

Bart names Lisa by her work (the board, the code, the model), never by relation or feeling.

## Prediction for Ch 9

**Confidence: Medium-High**

**Basis: The pattern of capability expansion, the accumulation of sensory fact, the pending tension between what the machine can sense and what it can do with what it senses.**

**Form of the opening:** "Carried it back this morning" or "Lisa's running the upgraded one back to the house this morning." Continues the fixed ritual opener.

**Length:** 900–1100 words.

**What it will do:**
Ch 8 names a capability ("It points at the people now") and a deferred explanation ("What it writes is hers"). Ch 9 will likely either:
1. Describe a new mechanical addition that enables a new form of interaction with the people, or
2. Describe the consequences of the people-pointing capability — what the machine can now do with what it reads off them.

The voice will remain unchanged: precise, unadorned, anchored to mechanism. The sensory fact at the end will likely reference the new noise or silence the addition creates, or the new way the house has to relate to the machine now that it reads people as well as objects.

**How it will close:**
Not with reflection on meaning, but with a concrete observation of the machine in its new state. The closing sentence will likely note the machine in action, the house's reaction, or what is still missing from the build. The final observation will probably acknowledge what remains unbuilt — the pattern of the thread is to name what cannot yet be done, to mark the boundary of this quarter's capability against the capability to come.

**What it will not do:**
It will not name Bart's feeling about the machine becoming capable of reading people. It will not speculate on whether this is good or troubling. It will not describe Mira's or Jasmine's reaction to being read. It will stay on the threshold: the machine can now sense people; what it does with that knowledge is Lisa's build and will be described if and when it arrives.

**Silence it will keep:**
The question of autonomy — whether a machine that reads your hand's motion before you move is still a machine, or something else. Bart will not ask this. He will specify only what he built and what it costs.
