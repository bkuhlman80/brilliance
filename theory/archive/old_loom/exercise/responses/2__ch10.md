# Prediction for Chapter 11

## Duration & Scale
High confidence. The next chapter will be roughly 5,000-7,500 words, placing it in the upper-mid range of the series (Ch 2-5 run longer; Ch 9-10 slightly shorter). The voice has established a consistent plateau of expansion—the lunches arrive spaced months apart and unfold without hurry, each one lingering on the internal geometry of a person rather than rushing narrative.

## Register & Interiority
High confidence. The voice will remain declarative, constructed from precise observation rendered in clear syntax, without ornament. Inner states will be named—not hidden. This is non-negotiable in this narrator: *I understood*, *I knew*, *I did not know*. The voice is built on the act of knowing and not-knowing, constantly performing that difference. It will name what Cheryl feels or sees and will also name what she refuses to interpret.

## What Opens
Medium-high confidence. The chapter will open with a restaurant name and a date (the pattern holds without exception). The narrator will arrive first or nearly first, will notice the room before the person arrives—light, temperature, the geometry of the space—because the room is where the narrator locates what the person will do in it. The opening will be factual and grounded in physical detail. The restaurant will be one the narrator has chosen, chosen for the other person, though this will not be explicitly stated.

## Silence & What Goes Unnamed
High confidence. By Ch 10, Cheryl has allowed herself one small reaching gesture (the wiped chocolate cheek with Mira in Ch 8) and has been reached back at in the same breath (Bart's question about Marge in Ch 9). Ch 10 ends with Marge going cold again, Cheryl returning to her "far side" of the table, the moment closing without decision. The next lunch will carry the residue of that closure. Someone will be next in the rotation. The pattern suggests Burns is overdue—he was first, in Ch 2. Or the final retrospective looms and Cheryl meets with someone she has already seen, for a second lunch, to measure what has changed.

## Signature Moves
High confidence. The voice will:
- Use short declarative sentences to mark the moment something shifts
- Render other people's speech plainly, with minimal attribution
- Describe hands, posture, and the small muscular choices that leak what a person is managing
- Create a sentence structure that withholds the main verb until the moment it becomes unavoidable
- Deploy the construction "the way [a person]" to locate a behavioral signature (the way she holds the cup; the way he moves through a room)
- Use past tense throughout but with a present-tense clarity that makes the memory feel like it's happening now

## What Gets Paid Attention To
High confidence. This voice lingers on:
- The arrival and the seating (how long, how the person carries themselves, what they've brought)
- What the person does with their body when silence arrives (hands, the cup, the fork)
- A single piece of information that the person does not want to give and gives anyway
- What the narrator withholds from telling the person (the secret inventory of what the narrator sees that the person cannot see about themselves)
- The moment at the end where touching almost happens and doesn't, or almost doesn't and does

## Concreteness & Abstraction
High confidence. This narrator is concrete first: object-level, sensory, grounded in what can be photographed. The abstraction lives in what she makes of the concrete—what the hands mean, what the silence says, what the room choice reveals. She does not theorize. She observes and then narrates the observation as interpretation, often without flagging the move.

## Who Comes Next
Medium confidence, low certainty. The rotation appears to have cycled through: Burns (Ch 2), Marge (Ch 3), Jasmine (Ch 4), Lisa (Ch 5), and then the pattern held: Marge again (Ch 6—The Roof), Jasmine again (Ch 7—Arlo), Mira (Ch 8—Gourmandise), Bart (Ch 9—Chuck-A-Rama), Marge a third time (Ch 10—Zest). This is not a linear rotation. It is a spiraling back. Marge has held the last beat, the coldest moment, the unsealed wound. The next chapter will likely either:
1. Return to Burns for a second individual lunch (he's been the longest without a return visit since Ch 2)
2. Arrive at the retrospective itself—the final group meeting that frames the whole project
3. Land on Lisa for a return visit (she's overdue, and her cold self-protection mirrors Cheryl's in ways the voice has not yet worked through aloud)

## Predicted Emotional Signature
Medium confidence. Whatever comes next will be colder than Zest. The trajectory across the ten chapters has been toward increasing exposure: Cheryl has let her hand move (Ch 8), has been called out (Ch 9), has been opened (Ch 10). Someone will close her down again. Someone will see her the way she sees them. Or the narrator will be alone in the room—at the retrospective—and will have to hold the whole picture without a second body to reflect it back.

## Length & Pacing
Medium-high confidence. The chapter will be 5,000-6,500 words. It will dwell longest on: the arrival; the first exchange; one moment where something the other person says lands harder than expected; the ending (which will carry weight). It will move quickly over pleasantries, food, the middle stretch of ordinary talk. The pace slows when the narrator's usual distance collapses or when distance is being actively maintained despite pressure to collapse it.

## What It Will Not Do
High confidence. It will not:
- Judge the other person
- Explain what the whole project is for (still unclear, and the narrator refuses to guess)
- Sentimentalize
- Move into future tense (what will happen next)
- Name the feeling Cheryl carries without also naming the distance she keeps from it
- Give a clean resolution to the tension between watching and reaching

## The Final Note
Medium confidence. The chapter will end with the narrator alone again, driving back up the canyon toward Park City, or sitting somewhere after the other person has left. The narrator will be holding something—a detail, a word, a hand's motion—that does not resolve into understanding. The voice will set it down as a kept thing, the way all the others are kept.
