# Prediction for Ch 7

## Duration and Pacing
Medium-to-long, dwells slowly. The narrator has moved increasingly inward through the arc—from watching a team, to conducting instrumental lunches, to attending to what the watching costs her. Ch 7 will hold time the way recent chapters do: a full scene, unhurried, the room itself given air. Expect the length of Ch 5 or Ch 6, not the compress of Ch 2.

## Register
Notably spare and direct where interior feeling surfaces; elsewhere composed, almost austere. The voice stays clipped in action and observation, colored only when attention lands on something structural—a distance, a want, a thing not said. This will continue. When the narrator describes Mira (or whoever she's with), the register stays flat until it doesn't—until the flatness itself becomes the measure of what matters. Diction will be exact, syntax clean even when it's tracking something tangled.

## Concreteness
Physical details are precise and chosen: hands on tables, light through glass, the quality of a cardigan, a name written on a form but not sent. The narrator renders the body first and lets feeling follow from what the body does. Ch 7 will track this habit—what someone is wearing, what their hands are doing, what the room temperature promises. These are not ornament. They are the whole language.

## Interiority
The narrator names her own interiority directly now, has since Ch 6—"I had come to reach into someone"—but does so sparsely and always grounded in action, never floating. The people she meets mostly remain opaque to themselves, their insides inferred from what they will not say. This asymmetry is the shape of the thread. Whoever Ch 7 is with, we will know the narrator's feeling about the meeting before we know the meeting's subject. The other person will not leak their inside; the narrator will hold what they don't say.

## Attention
The narrator has become increasingly attentive to what people refuse, withhold, keep inside bags, decide not to ask. She lingers on the small moments of someone choosing not to say something. Ch 2: Burns going quiet when the kid loved him. Ch 6: the form Marge carries but does not send. Ch 7 will attend to this language of refusal—what someone leaves out of a story, what question they decline to answer, what is held close. The attention will be tender but not soft, clinical but not cold.

## Silence
The narrator has been learning, over six chapters, to stop pulling on silences. She lets them stand, lets people withhold, lets rooms go quiet without filling them. Ch 7 will have stretches where nothing is said because the right thing is unsayable or the wrong thing is too easy. There will be a quiet moment—possibly near the end—where the narrator and her lunch companion hold something without speaking. The narrator will not break it.

## Signature Patterns
The opening move establishes the room first—light, season, what the walls are made of—before introducing the person. The middle section tracks a person through their own work or thinking, following them into detail where they become precise and specific. A question gets asked that lands differently than expected. There is a reveal—often small, often about a name or a thing carried—that shifts what has come before. The ending moves away from the table toward transit: driving back up the canyon, noting the light, carrying something inward.

The voice is distinguished by: constructed knowledge (what can be inferred and proven from evidence), refusal of judgment language, a way of describing people through their own categories rather than the narrator's, small moments where the narrator's own distance gets looked at and held. The diction is often formal but never stiff—Burns, the warehouse, the tapes, the commission. Simple words for large things. No self-consciousness.
