# Prediction for Ch 4

## Duration & Pacing
The chapter will run 2,500–3,500 words, sustained and unhurried. The voice has consistently dwelt longest on moments of perception—watching, listening, then parsing the watching. It will not hurry past a detail once the narrator has decided to hold it. The lunch-and-aftermath structure that anchors Ch 2 and Ch 3 suggests Ch 4 will follow a similar architecture: arrival, conversation shaped by question-and-answer, internal observation, a gesture at the close that signals something the narrator doesn't name. The rhythm will be deliberate, the clauses longer when feeling is in the room.

## Register
Clipped and precise. The diction is spare, almost flat in construction—short sentences nested inside longer ones—but the word choice is deliberate and never decorative. No flourish. No simile unless it lands load-bearing weight. The voice favors *is* statements and observation over interpretation. When interpretation arrives, it comes marked with *I* language: *I understood*, *I did not miss it*, *I would learn*. This creates a consistent tonal quality: factual, a little cold at the surface, then suddenly warm because the narrator has let you see her thinking. Expect more of this.

## Concreteness
High. The narrator anchors every conversation in the room itself—tables, coffee cups, light, a manila envelope sliding out of a bag. The physical world is the register in which inner states get recorded. Interiority in this voice doesn't announce itself; it arrives through what the narrator sees in another person's body, or what she notices about her own response to a place. The next lunch will have concrete furniture, light quality, food or drink, a specific geometry of the room.

## Interiority
Named sparingly and then only after evidence. The narrator does not tell you her feelings; she shows you her observation process and lets the feeling track underneath. When she names something—*I was the right person to have seen it and the wrong person to do anything with it*—it lands with weight because it has been earned through a full scene. Expect emotional clarity in Ch 4, but don't expect it to come fast or easy. It will come only after she has watched long enough and correctly enough that the feeling becomes unavoidable.

## Attention
The narrator lingers on: 
- How people move and hold themselves (Burns choosing between hug and handshake, Marge holding coffee against her chest)
- What people avoid or redirect in conversation (Burns stopping mid-sentence about Bart, Marge refusing to say which cartoon she is)
- The specific language people use, and what's underneath it (*I think he—* / *He puts up with his old man*)
- The physical evidence of a life (the manila envelope with the steeple letterhead)

The narrator passes over quickly: backstory, explanations, justifications, anything that would soften the edges. She is not interested in why; she is interested in how the body tells the story that the mouth declines.

## Silence
What the narrator does not say is as indexed as what she does. She does not tell Burns that his son doesn't love him. She does not ask Marge about the envelope. She does not tell you her own first name until Ch 2 is halfway done (and that in the voice of another person calling it out). The next chapter will have similar restraint—something that the narrator will notice and will not pursue, something she will watch the other person decline to say and will honor the decline.

## Signature
- Short declarative sentences that do important work: *He nodded.* *I let him have the laugh.* *I picked at the beans.*
- Repetition as a tool for emphasis, not rhythm: *I did not miss it. I also did not take it.* / *The clarity and the distance are one organ.* / *Soon. Soon.*
- Dialogue that trails off, or that circles back: Burns says "I think he—" and stops. Marge answers a question with a sentence and a half and then changes the subject.
- The habit of qualification: *the kind of* (the kind of late-July Wednesday, the kind of room), *the way you* (the way you hold a tea, the way you say something you've learned), *a little* or *almost* as softeners that don't actually soften.
- Metaphors that come from architecture and engineering: *load-bearing*, *chassis*, *the whole inventory*, *one organ*. The narrator thinks in systems.

## The Next Chapter
The narrator has now met Burns and Marge. The rotation suggests the next lunch is with either Bart, Lisa, or Smithers (the one who is "Smithers because there's no Smithers"). Based on the attention structure and the fact that Burns talked about Bart but didn't reveal him, and that Marge has already turned away from personal revelation in a way the narrator recognized, I predict the next chapter is with **someone who will speak more freely about inner life, or someone whose reticence takes a different shape**.

The narrator will arrive early (this is a fixed feature of her way). The place will have good light or strategic windows. The conversation will contain a moment where the other person reveals something they hadn't planned to reveal, and the narrator will see the moment the person realizes they've revealed it. There will be food or drink that matters. There will be at least one piece of information the narrator will sit with and not act on. 

The chapter will close with a gesture smaller than a goodbye but larger than a dismissal—a handshake, a hug's half-second of hesitation, a look that passes between them and lands somewhere the narrator will carry it alone. She will drive back to Park City on the canyon road. The drive will not be interesting, and she will not narrate it to herself.

The register will be the same: clear, precise, a little distant, then suddenly you'll notice you are very close to something. The length will be 2,500–3,500 words. The voice will not break. The attention will remain exact. The silence will continue to matter.
