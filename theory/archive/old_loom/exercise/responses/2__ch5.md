# Prediction for Ch 6

## Confidence: HIGH
Basis: Four complete chapters establish an unmistakable architectural pattern in duration, opening, interiority, attention, and silence.

---

## Duration
**Prediction: 2,200–2,600 words.** The pattern holds at Ch 2 (2,100), Ch 3 (1,200), Ch 4 (1,800), Ch 5 (1,800). Ch 3 is anomalous (shorter visit, rushed). Ch 6 will likely land in the 2,200-2,600 range — substantial enough to contain the full machinery of a lunch, compressed where attention concentrates.

## Opening
**Prediction: Restaurant name and date, then a single sentence about room temperature or light.** All four chapters open with the location, date, and immediate sensory immersion. Ch 6 will follow. The room will be described before the person. The sentence will be sparse, factual, with one atmospheric detail.

Example shape: "The place I chose was [Name] on [date/time], a room [one quality], the [light/heat/smell] [one precise observation]."

## Interiority
**Prediction: Minimal. The narrator will not name what she feels meeting the remaining person.** Across all chapters, the narrator stages interiority only through her refusal to stage it. She notices feeling, then withholds the naming. See Ch 4: "I let it go by, because the letting was the only useful thing I had." She describes the *other* person's interior (Burns's hope about his son; Jasmine's hard week; Lisa's arrangement of herself) with precision. Her own inside comes only through action and silence. Ch 6 will hold this line.

## Register
**Prediction: Clipped and precise at the handoff, then steady and expansive in the middle.**
The rhythm is: tight entrance → settlement into observation → analytical digression → light turn toward the end. Ch 2 is longest because Burns gets the most sustained analysis (the inventory of Bart, the missed moment about love). Ch 4 gets the exchange of envy. Ch 5 gets the shape of Lisa. Ch 6 will expand into whoever remains. The register will not shift; it will fill.

## Concreteness
**Prediction: High. The narrator will track objects, postures, hands, the body's local geography.**
Tacos, beans, a taco truck, the warehouse she doesn't enter. Hands flat on a table. A pager. The Garmin. A cardigan "the color of a brick rained on for forty years." In Ch 6, an object will anchor the lunch. A ring, a notebook, something the person holds or will not hold. It will be specific enough to carry weight.

## Attention
**Prediction: The narrator will linger on one thing the remaining person does (or does not do) for the full middle of the lunch, then turn to two or three smaller observations.**
Each lunch has a focal point. Burns gets his way of talking about his son. Marge gets the clinical eye and the redirect. Jasmine gets the pager, the envy, the empty trellis. Lisa gets the barefoot running and the edge of her own knowing. Ch 6 will follow. One sustained passage will do the work. The person will reveal something in the shape of their attention, their hands, their breath.

## Silence
**Prediction: There will be one moment where the narrator does not ask what she could ask, and knows why.**
See Ch 2: "I did not miss it. I also did not take it." Ch 4: "I let it go by, because the letting was the only useful thing I had." Ch 5: "I do not think she heard herself tell me that. The naturalist in me wanted to hand it back across the table, and the rest of me knew better." Ch 6 will have its own moment — a thing the other person has said or not said, and the narrator will choose the silence with full knowledge of the cost.

## Diction and Signature
**Prediction: The voice will use the same syntax and word choices.**
Repetition without repetition: "The thing is." "The moment." "I have." "The way you." A sentence that turns on "and" in the middle. A question that stands alone as a paragraph. The phrase "I did not know yet" or "I had not expected" or "I was not sure." Objects described with one color or texture adjective only. Time marked in precise dating (Ch numbers are dated: July 27, October 27, January 26, April 29). The voice will not accelerate or shift. It is the same person, in the same register, every time.

## Structure and Ending
**Prediction: The lunch will end with a question unanswered, or an observation deferred.**
Ch 2 ends on Bart, on Mr. Burns, on the cartoon the father does not see he is holding. Ch 3 ends on the narrator turning the stone "you've already decided to keep" — a threshold crossed without crossing it. Ch 4 ends on the pager going off, the conversation cut and kept. Ch 5 ends on the Garmin catching light, the narrator driving home with "a great deal of Lisa" and "almost none of" Liza.

Ch 6 will end with a threshold, a deferral, or a carried thing. Not closure. Never closure. The person will leave, or the narrator will, and something will remain on the table or in the car, unresolved but held.

## Summary Pattern
The narrator builds a lunch across 2,200–2,600 words: a room, a meal, a person whose interiority leaks or is guarded, an exchange (envy, revelation, naming, or the refusal of it), and a closing that defers more than it answers. The voice is witness-steady, object-precise, hand-tracked, and fatally clear about what it will not reach for. The narrator goes into these rooms to understand how other people are, and leaves understanding only that understanding and keeping are the same act. Ch 6 will turn on this machinery one more time, for the last person she has yet to lunch with — and the machinery will not change.
