# Prediction for Ch 7

## Duration & Opening
The next installment will be substantial — 3,500–4,500 words, matching the span of recent chapters. It will open with a specific location name and a date in the third quarter of 2032 (probably September or October), continuing the seasonal progression (July to October across the thread). The opening will establish the place first, then the narrator arriving, establishing pattern. No direct narration of interior anxiety or purpose.

## Register
The register will remain clipped and precise, with long sentences that hold multiple clauses without visible strain. Moments of naming or directness ("I did not miss it. I also did not take it.") will be interspersed with flat observations (about light, cyclists, the shape of a room). No ornament. No editorializing. The voice remains that of a careful observer using the present tense for past events, which creates a sense of the narrator still living inside these lunches.

## Concreteness
The chapter will be densely physical — furniture, hand positions, the exact temperature of coffee, what the other person is wearing, how light falls. The narrator notices bodies: how they sit, what they hold, the moment their face changes. These are not metaphorical observations but literal ones, recorded like field notes. This chapter will focus on someone new from the team (Bart, the most frequently referenced but least directly encountered).

## Interiority
The narrator will acknowledge internal states only through behavior and only late in the chapter, if at all. What people feel will be inferred from how they hold a glass, whether they hesitate before speaking, how their hands move. The narrator's own interior — the wanting, the reaching, the long history of not-reaching — will be present as shadow, not directly named.

## Attention & Silence
The narrator will dwell on one moment, one exchange, one gesture for the better part of a page — the way she dwells on how Lisa's hands move, or the moment Marge looks away from the temple. Time will stretch around this moment and then release it. The narrator will pass quickly over the meal itself and any conventional small talk.

What will go unasked: the narrator has asked Burns about his work, Marge about her family, Lisa about her running, Jasmine about the hardship. With Bart, the questions will be different. Given that Bart is the one who does the naming, who sees people as cartoons and lands them, and who is constantly being described through his hands and his building and what he finishes — the silence will likely be around what Bart himself *sees* or *thinks*. The narrator will not ask him to interpret anyone.

## The Pattern Predicts
Bart is the missing person in the thread — the one most frequently mentioned but never directly encountered. He is described as having the best hands, finishing what others start, not being known for what he *is* but for what he *does*. He gave the others their cartoon names.

The narrator will meet him at a lunch and will come asking — as the pattern demands — one thing she thinks she knows, and will discover she does not know it. Because Bart sees people as cartoons (accurate, but cartoon-shaped), she may expect him to be the most legible. Instead, he may be the one who sees clearly but says least about what he sees.

The temperature of the chapter will be cooler than the Marge chapter, more functional than the Lisa chapter. Not detached — the narrator never detaches — but less reaching. More observing. Bart builds things that survive contact. He will, in this chapter, be harder to move.

The thread has moved steadily toward greater intimacy and greater risk with each chapter, but Bart — the maker, the one others move around — may reverse that.
