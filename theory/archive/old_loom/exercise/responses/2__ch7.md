# Prediction for Ch 8

## Duration
The chapter will likely run 300–450 lines. The pattern shows chapters ranging from ~200 (Marge) to ~350 (Jasmine, Lisa), with an average around 320. The narrator has found a stable rhythm over six lunches: they establish the place, the person arrives, a substantive exchange happens at mid-meal, and the chapter closes with reflection on the car ride home or at the threshold. This pace is unlikely to accelerate or compress. The narrator is neither rushed nor expansive; the restraint is structural.

## Register
Clipped, flat, uncolored by sentiment. The speaker uses plain nomenclature: a trellis is not "graceful" or "skeletal," it is a trellis; the light bears down, it does not caress. When the register does warm—"I found that I liked it" about Burns, "a hearth" about Marge—the warmth is named as the narrator's own tendency and immediately questioned. A cold eye would log otherwise. The voice admits to its own bias and then continues with it. Register will hold: factual, with the warmth underneath and the narrator watching themselves warm.

## Concreteness
Concrete to the point of specificity. Every object has a detail: coffee held with both hands against the chest; feet on bare dirt versus gravel; the flat light of late July that "does not so much fall as bear down." Hands are everywhere—as evidence, as gesture, as character. The narrator renders time through weather and light, season through what grows or burns. There is almost no abstraction that is not immediately buttressed with a physical detail or an example. This will continue: if the next chapter names an emotion or an idea, it will arrive with its object attached.

## Interiority
The narrator's inner state is named constantly but the team members' is not. When Burns talks about his son, we get his hands and his words but never his feeling—the narrator watches the feeling arrive and leave his face. Jasmine "leaked through her hands"; Marge through "the things she would not let herself say"; Lisa "did not leak." The narrator is the only one whose weather we get directly: "I do not know anymore which of those two reasons I am running on"; "the reaching, this time, was not the naturalist's errand I tell myself it is." For the next chapter, expect the same: the person will be rendered through gesture and speech, the narrator's interior state will be available, the distance between them will be the true subject.

## Attention
The narrator dwells on:
- The body as evidence (hands, posture, the eyes when they go soft, the forefinger catching light)
- What people do not say, and why
- The gaps between what someone claims about themselves and what their behavior suggests
- Rooms, light, and season as mirrors of the emotional circumstance
- One or two repeated details that circle back ("the form in her bag"; "the warehouse without looking at it"; the empty trellis)

The narrator passes over fast:
- Chronology (years collapse; "the drive was, again, not interesting")
- Plot (the lab work is never explained; Bramble is a presence, not a mechanism)
- Sentiment stated in greeting-card language (almost never done)
- Small talk without weight

## Silence
The narrator goes quiet on:
- Their own past, except as it mirrors something in the present (2002 in Sausalito, mentioned twice; thirty years of isolation)
- The plot of the book being written, and its purpose
- Burns's relationship with his son's wife, though the absence hums
- What the robot is for, or how it works
- Whether any of this is going to work

The pattern predicts silence on: what happens to the form in Marge's bag, whether the narrator will act on what they feel, the stakes of the book itself, the future of the lab. The narrator asks about the work, the family, the present moment; does not ask about tomorrow.

## Signature
Short sentences and long ones, neither dominant. The long ones are compound and move forward with "and": "I watched them roll past for ten minutes. The Wasatch foothills stood in the flat overhead light of late July, the grass on them long burned to gold — a high hard light that would not slant and would not soften, the kind of light that did not so much fall as bear down." Breath-length. The short ones cut: "Burns."  "Okay." "I did not miss it. I also did not take it." Dialogue is sparse and often single words. The narrator quotes only to let someone hang themselves on their own truth.

Question at the end, or a small observation. The chapter often closes on something the narrator did not point out or did not do: "I kept it, the way I keep things." "The name with the z I had almost none of." "I understood that I would no more hurry it than I would hurry a tide." The closing is neither resolved nor unresolved; it is held.

## Prediction for Ch 8

**Confidence: High**
**Basis: The pattern of who remains to be lunched with, the narrator's own silences, and the escalation of interiority across the six chapters already written.**

### Who
By elimination, the next person should be Mira. The five team members plus Bart's daughter account for six lunches; the narrator has completed five. Mira is four going on five (now five or nearly five, given the timeline from July 2031 to September 2032). It is the only lunch that could reasonably happen, and the narrator has held Mira in suspension across all six chapters—kept her in the corner of the frame, mentioned her as a figure who names things, reported what Burns and Jasmine said of her without presenting her directly. The pattern is building toward the one person the narrator has yet to sit across from without the lab or family between them.

### Length
280–320 lines. Mira will not hold a long meal. The lunch will be shorter than the others, the conversation different in kind. The chapter will likely close not in the car but in the moment—a turn, a held look, something the narrator does not yet know how to end.

### Setting
Likely a place connected to Mira, or to childhood, or to the ground. The narrator chooses rooms for the other person. For a four-year-old (or five-year-old), the choice would not be a restaurant. Perhaps the trellis itself—the back patio at Smithers. Perhaps a playground. Perhaps the basement where Mira plays. The narrator would choose something warm, bright, and still, the way they chose the roof for Marge. The light will matter. The room will matter.

### Register
It will hold: flat, with the warmth underneath. But the narrator will be newly uncertain. They have not done this before. The clipped sentences might clip more sharply, or the long ones might fray. The uncertainty will be in the voice itself.

### Concreteness
High. The narrator will notice what Mira does with her hands, with her attention. Will watch her name the bots, or play, or show them something. The only abstract moment in the whole thread happens around Mira—"Mira is, in a way I did not anticipate, the thing this project is about"—and it is already concrete, bent toward a relationship, a presence. Expect the chapter to be mostly what Mira does and says, and the narrator's failure to render it into clarity.

### Interiority
The narrator's will be highest here. They will name something they have been circling: why they wanted this lunch, what they are looking for in a child they do not know, what it means to watch someone who has not yet closed, the way all the others have. The team members' interiority stays hidden; Mira's may be the first moment the narrator actually asks a question about someone else's inside and does not turn away from the answer.

### Attention
The narrator will linger on Mira's naming—the fact that the bots have names and the narrator will not be given them. Will watch what Mira chooses to show and what she keeps. Will notice the way a child's attention moves. Will be caught off-guard by directness. Will likely not be able to turn it back toward themselves the way they have with every other person.

### Silence
The narrator will go silent on what they wanted from this lunch. On whether they got it. On whether reaching into a child is the same reaching they've been doing all along. On whether Mira is a specimen too. The form in Marge's bag will not appear. Burns's worry will not appear. The book will not appear. The chapter will leave the question open: what does the narrator do with a person who does not yet know how to keep themselves closed?

### Opening
Something direct and light. "Mira was waiting" or "She opened the door before I could knock" or "The basement was warm and the television was on." Not: "I arrived." The chapter will start with her, not with the narrator's arrival. This is the break in pattern.

### Closing
Not the car drive home. The closing will be in the moment or just barely after—the narrator leaving the lunch, or leaving the house, or leaving Mira playing with the bots and not turning around. Something will be held unsaid between them. The narrator will not have reached the seam, as they thought they would. They will have found something else, and they will not know its name yet.

A small repeated thing: the trellis, or the names, or the bots, or the light. One sentence that sits: "I understood that I would [not do a thing I had thought I would do]." The chapter will be shorter, warmer, more broken than the others, and the narrator will know it, and will say so.
