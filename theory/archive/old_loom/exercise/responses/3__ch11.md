## Signature Patterns to Expect

**Sentence structure**: Short declaration followed by compound expansion. E.g., "She got it at once, the way she got things, whole and certain and wrong." The initial clause is flat; the expansion adds precision through "the way." Rare complex subordination. Parallel structure for mimicry: "He'd come off his rounds and cross the floor, and come to her in the corner, and settle himself against her, and the relief of it was so large she forgot she'd arranged the whole thing herself."

**Rhythm**: Prose moves in bursts. A scene builds (she goes down into the corner, pulls the curtain, waits). Then a full stop. Then the reveal of what she misread. Then a return: "So she did the only thing left / And then because / She had given him / There it still is / And that should have been the end of it. But." The voice is additive, building small declarative moments into a chain.

**Vocabulary fingerprints**:
- "The way" / "the way she would" (establishing patterns and habits)
- "A thing" (constant, abstract the specific into the general)
- "All at once and out of nowhere" (sudden insight without warning)
- Repetition of singular key word: "He came. / She came. / It came. / Nothing came."
- Present tense for performed actions; past for states ("she had kept" vs. "she kept")
- "There is / There was" (establishing fact flatly)
- "The one thing" (isolating a detail as crucial)
- "Whole" (complete, undivided, certain)
