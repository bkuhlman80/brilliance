# Prediction for Ch 11

## Duration
Consistent with the pattern: approximately 1200-1400 words. The narration will linger on the setup and the reach but move more quickly through the aftermath, as the evening's exhaustion has deepened. Less time on the room's response; the weight forward, not back.

## Register
Clipped and tensioned. By Ch 10, a restraint enters Burns's speech—the "restlessness" the narrator clocks. Ch 11 will show that restraint tightening further. Fewer hands-lifting run-ups. More halting searches for the verb. The expansiveness is narrowing, becoming almost grim in its precision. The language will remain colored but the colors will be colder—grays, darks, the palette of something waning.

## Concreteness
The animal will return. Burns always works from the body—cell, rock, jellyfish, larva. But the animal in Ch 11 will be one that does *not* persist, does not adapt, does not venture or quicken or seize. It will be something that runs its course and stops. The speech will move from that creature to the room, as always, but the moral will be inverted: not what to become, but what you cannot prevent ceasing. The concrete will anchor the audience in loss.

## Interiority
Named. More named than before. The narrator will observe Burns's internal state directly—not just what he reaches for, but what he's avoiding reaching for. The sentence about the son (whom we've seen unmoved by all eight prior toasts) will surface here, not as subtext but as explicit noting: Burns is aware of his reach and its failure. He will not speak it, but the narrator will. The gap between what Burns says and what Burns knows will be the shape of the chapter.

## Attention
Two poles: the animal, held in focus far longer than in previous chapters (he will describe it slowly, obsessively, as a man does when describing a thing he wishes to convince himself exists). And the child—Mira, who has been falling asleep earlier each chapter, who is now nearly gone from the start. Her wakefulness will be the measure of how much authority his word still carries. He will linger on her failure to wake, to keep up, to meet his gaze.

## Silence
What he will not say: that the lab's three years are ending. That he cannot name what happens when the warm place closes. That the thing built to outlast him may not. That love as earned might not hold in the cold. The silence will be louder than the speech—the narrator will hold it, the way she has learned to hold his reaches.

## Signature
The verb-hunt will arrive earlier and stay longer. He will try multiple words before landing on one, and even then with visible doubt. Syntax will fragment under the weight—sentences will break mid-reach, reform, break again. Repetition will tighten: *may we* will become the only shape left to work with. The chest-laugh will be gone. What replaces it is harder to write—the sound of a man who has run out of metaphors and must now speak from underneath the scaffolding.

## The Toast
The word will be something like *endure* or *remain* or *persist* (returned to, but inverted—not the bright persistence of the first chapter, but the grinding, joyless staying-in-place of a thing with no choice). He will search for it in the syntax of holding and letting-go—the same territory as autonomy and affiliation, but without the prize at the end. He will raise the glass and the room will drink, and Mira will not wake to say the word after him.
