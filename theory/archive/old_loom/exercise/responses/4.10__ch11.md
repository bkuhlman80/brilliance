# Prediction for Ch 12

## Duration and Pace
Medium length, on par with Ch 11. The speech will extend fully, without interruption or deflation. The narrator will linger on one image for a sustained beat—longer than usual—as Burns reaches past where the argument can hold him.

## Register
Clipped in setup; expands into colored, urgent language on the reach. The register tightens back down at the close, almost flat on the verb-hunt, before opening once more on the final word.

## Concreteness
Grounded in a physical thing—likely an object in the room or an animal/biological fact from deep time. Not abstract scaffolding. Something you could point at. The reach will move from the concrete toward an interior state he's afraid to name directly.

## Interiority
Named on the page, but obliquely. He will not use a simple emotional word. Instead he will circle it—describe behavior, describe a system, describe a pattern—and let the interior state arrive through those descriptions the way it has in recent chapters. The narrator will see what he is not quite saying. This will be the frame for what gets unsaid.

## Attention
Lingers on the moment of transition—when something shifts from one state to another. Quick on setup and context. Slow on the threshold. Races past the moral/application. Dwells on the verb-hunt longer than in recent chapters; the hunting itself becomes the point.

## Silence
What Burns declines to say: that he is afraid this particular thing is ending, or already ended. The speech will be about something that persists or continues, but the urgency underneath it will read as a man defending against loss. The narrator will notice this underside and not expose it.

Also silent: direct address to anyone specific in the room. Unlike Ch 8, he will not find Mira with his eyes. He will avoid the thing on the floor entirely.

## Signature
Hands will come up later than usual—he will start with them flat on the wood for a longer stretch. The lean will be sharper, less hesitant. The verb-hunt will include a false start that he talks through aloud instead of abandoning—"you can't *x*" phrasing, like Ch 4's hunt. The toast will land on a simple verb that can work both directions: into action and into stillness. Something that holds both motion and its interruption.

## How It Opens
"Burns came up out of the chair with the glass already in his hand" or similar—skipping the setup that usually precedes the stand. No beat to set the frame. He means to spend more sincerity than before.

## What the Verb Might Be
Not *sustain* (too clinical). Not *endure* (too grim). Something like *tend,* *abide,* *weather,* or *turn.* A word that suggests both the work of keeping something and the possibility of changing direction. A verb that can mean both "to continue doing" and "to stop and face." He will find it on the second or third reach.

## How It Ends
Mira will say the word—changed by three years of these, she will say it with less ceremony and more understanding than before. She may add something to it, or change its shape in her mouth. The narrator will note this as growth, but not call it that. Lisa will cut the tape on Mira's voice, not on Burns's drinking, for the first time.

## Confidence and Basis
**Medium-to-high confidence.**

**Basis:** The pattern over eleven chapters shows Burns building toward a crisis. Each "may we X" is a test of whether the room will hold a thing he needs to believe. The verbs have climbed from *persist* through *go* and *practice* toward more fraught territory: *hold, seize, stay, keep.* He has introduced a second major theme—the machine, the thing on the floor—which has been treated neutrally, then as replacement, then as mirror, then as child needing care.

Ch 10 introduced finitude: *may we last* was a prayer disguised as a toast. Ch 11 introduced obsolescence and the terror of being forgotten: *may we count* was a man asking whether he matters when the watching ends.

Ch 12 should move from those questions toward an answer, or toward the moment just before an answer breaks. It will likely be about continuity, transmission, or the work of keeping something alive that does not require keeping—i.e., the thing that is most precious to tend *because* it can leave. The speech will be quieter underneath than it appears. The verb-hunt will be harder than usual. The reach will go furthest yet, because there is less time left after this one.

The narrator will understand what Burns has understood, and will not say.
