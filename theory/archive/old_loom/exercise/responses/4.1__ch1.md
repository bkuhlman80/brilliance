# Prediction for Ch 2

## Voice Analysis from Ch 1

**Duration**: The narrative dwells at length. Long opening sentence carrying multiple simultaneous actions (Burns pacing, Lisa on agenda pass, Bart with protobot, Marge in kitchen). The movement into Mira's arrival stretches across several paragraphs of observation before the Bramble naming happens. The entire chapter loops back to re-examine that naming moment from multiple angles.

**Register**: Precise and colloquial without sentimentality. "Worry stone" arrives casually. "The things she was told were sometimes the things" (tautological, almost flat). Descriptors are clean—"slight," "brief," "purple"—without ornament. The voice allows itself the phrase "something small and good" but quickly moves past the feeling.

**Concreteness**: Extremely object-focused. The narrative fixes on physical details: gravel, foam case, the protobot in Mira's fist, the jelly jar, the sprig on top, the thrumming bot against skin, the battery hatch closing. Even emotional states arrive through gesture and speech (Marge pausing, Burns stopping mid-sentence) rather than declaration.

**Interiority**: Sparse. What Burns feels about his daughter, or Lisa about her drink, or Jasmine about her four years of being told things—these are not named. Mira's preference for the word "Bramble" is shown through repetition and consonant work, not reflection. The closest to interiority: "Lisa had been thinking about it for a year by then" (reported, past, distanced).

**Attention**: Lingers on sequences where multiple people observe the same object (Mira and the jar; Jasmine, Mira, and the bot). Passes quickly over team history, the lab setup, the household dynamics—these exist as context, not focus. Dwells on the moment of naming and then dwells again on the uncertainty of it (no one voted, no one objected; the record is fragmentary).

**Silence**: Does not explain why Mira has been allowed to carry Bramble into the lab. Does not say what the founding retrospective is meant to accomplish. Does not name what Burns, Marge, or Bart think about the sudden christening. Does not describe what's in the foam case or whether Jasmine brought it. Does not say whether the team members are pleased, annoyed, or neutral.

**Signature**: Compound sentences held on "and" (Burns "had been pacing for an hour and Lisa was on her second pass"). Repetition for percussion (three times the team considered what to call the bots; three instances of "the jar" in quick succession; "bramble bramble bramble"). A colloquial syntax that avoids the grand—"The light in the warehouse was the long bright light," not "The light shone." A sentence that loops: "The protobot had been number 3 or the unit or the prototype or...and in less than thirty seconds, it was Bramble." Final paragraphs pull back to note the absence of footage and to relay that the speaker (Cheryl) is reconstructing the scene from accounts given later.

May begin to unfold what the meeting is for, but only through what people do and say in it, not through exposition. May use a simple sentence to arrive at an observation after a longer one. May step back at the end of a section or paragraph to note something about the record, the memory, or the account—the voice seems to know it is constructing this narrative from fragments and accounts.

**Basis**: These predictions rest on the voice's consistent habits: showing, not telling; building in sequence and simultaneous action; dwelling on concrete detail and gesture; looping back to examine a moment rather than moving forward. The voice also seems to know it is at a distance from the event (Cheryl reconstructing; Lisa retelling a year later; accounts differing)—Ch 2 may continue to signal that temporal and epistemological distance.

**Confidence**: Medium. The patterns are clear enough to predict register and attention, but the thread is only one chapter, and the next chapter may pivot to a different scene, a different timespan, or a different narrative level entirely.
