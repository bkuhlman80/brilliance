# Prediction for Chapter 11

## Duration
High-medium length, consistent with recent chapters (Ch 6-10 run 600-800 words). The installment will dwell longest on: (1) the moment of arrival and room assembly, (2) a physical gesture or moment of hesitation from a character, (3) the opening statement of the night's ambition. Will move quickly through the setup and voting phases.

## Register
Measured, slightly formal. The register has been steadying toward a kind of ceremonial flatness—not coldness, but the grammatical tightness of ritual. Expect: matter-of-fact descriptions of arrivals and placements; one short sentence of emotional weight embedded in a longer paragraph; sparse qualifiers. Will avoid adjectives stacked in sequence. The voice reads as someone reporting what is visible without commentary.

## Concreteness
Highly concrete, anchored to objects: the kettle, the beer bottles, the tetrapod, the new machine on the table. The voice does not abstract—it names where a hand lands, what temperature a surface holds, how long a pause lasts. Expect: spatial detail (the radiator, the far end, the stool), tactile sensation (warm from the machine, the grip of hands), specific objects (juice box or juice glass, the cigar box). Will not explain why something matters; will show where it sits.

## Interiority
Sparse and behavioral. Inner states are rarely named directly; they are registered as hesitation, a hand's motion held too long, a eye's direction, a voice's tone. When feeling appears, it arrives as action: the tending that "ran under her like a current," the hands that "went still." Expect: one moment where an inner state is visible only as a body's choice or timing; likely a moment of resistance or waiting; no introspection by any character.

## Attention
The voice lingers on arrivals (who comes in, how they move, what they carry), on the assembly of the room into its configuration (where each person settles), on hands and what they are doing, and on the machine. It passes quickly over talk. It notices gaps in expected ritual (the missing cake banner in Ch 9, the forms mailed in Ch 10) and the small disruptions that mark time. Expect: close attention to who is looking at what and in what direction; quick passage through discussion; return to machines and the room's temperature.

## Silence
The voice does not name what happened in the three weeks or three months between meetings. It does not explain the machines. It does not name the building's future, though the banners have appeared twice now in warnings not spoken aloud. It does not linger on grief, change, or goodbye—it reports the evidence and moves on. Expect: a gap or shift in Marge's physical presence or posture that goes unremarked; likely the voice will pass over something significant in the room without stopping to name it; possibly the voice will notice something the room is not ready to talk about yet.

## Signature
- Short sentences at high moments: "Bart did not look up from the sensor."
- Repeated structures that build recognition: "The [object] came in [preposition] [place]"; "The [person] came in and [physical act]."
- The particular construction: subject + verb + adverbial phrase specifying space or time. ("She came in through the back," "He sat at his end of the table," "The tetrapod took the warm spot.")
- Compound objects: "the cigar box and a sensor," "kettle and a beer," describing what is held or what waits.
- The voice does not use contractions; uses semicolons to join related clauses without subordinating them.
- Preference for nouns over adjectives: names the objects, lets their placement and repetition carry weight.

## Prediction for Ch 11

The chapter will open with an arrival—likely Marge coming early, or Bart arriving before the family. It will establish the date (probably November 2033, following the quarterly pattern: Feb, May, Aug, Nov) and the weather (cold, dark). 

The room will assemble in the expected configuration. The voice will note which machines are present and where each settles. There will be a detail of change—either a new object, a missing object, or a shift in how someone moves or holds themselves. This detail will not be explained.

The chapter will dwell on a moment of hesitation or a hand held still—likely Bart's hands on a sensor or Marge's grip on a cup. Someone will notice something they do not say aloud. The tetrapod will be described with its continued warmth and its place by the radiator. The new humanoid machine will be present and likely doing something it did not do before (speaking further, moving with more precision, standing differently).

Burns will stand and set out the ambition—this will be the longest unbroken passage. His hands will move; the ambition will name a new board line or capacity. The voice will report his words or the shape of his hands, not his tone or intention.

The chapter will end at or just after Lisa brings the light up. There will be no closing reflection. The room will be ready. The tape will be running.

**Confidence: Medium.** The pattern of arrivals, assembly, hesitation, and opening is now recursive enough that the voice uses it as scaffolding. The character arcs and quarterly machine developments are predictable. What cannot be predicted: whether the voice will name or stay silent about the banners, the money, or Marge's internal state; what new capacity Burns will announce; whether Mira will do something unexpected. The shape is certain. The content is not.
