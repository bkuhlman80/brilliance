# Prediction for Ch 4

## Duration
The chapter will run approximately 2,500-3,500 words — middle-length, similar to Ch 3. It will sustain at least one segment for 4-6 minutes of what feels like real time (a block of work or a deliberation), dwelling on process and the room's attention distribution rather than rushing through stages.

## Opening
The chapter opens with scene-setting that names the season/time precisely and describes light or temperature — a signature move, and the opening will follow form: a single concrete detail that holds atmosphere ("The cold was in at the back of the building now" or similar). The team is already present. The family arrives after the ground has been laid. The camera is not yet running.

## Register
Clipped to moderate, with long syntactically simple sentences for the driving action ("She handed him Bramble. He was warm. He'd been in the heater.") and moderate dilation when the voice pauses to take in a face, a room state, or a choice. No ornament. No summary. The prose does not tell; it watches.

## Concreteness
High. The chapter lives in specifics: who's holding what, how long something has been running, which hand rests where, the exact phrase someone used, what the camera is filming. The interior state (if it appears) emerges only through these objects and placements, never named directly.

## Interiority
Minimal. Thoughts and feelings do not appear on the page except as they leak through observation — the way Bart's hands moved, or didn't; the way Burns stood; what Lisa chose to say aloud or keep back. If the chapter names an emotion, it will be brief and functional, tied to a body or an action: "Burns nodded at Bart" (approval); "Bart said nothing. Bart had set both hands on the table" (withdrawal, then recalibration). 

The chapter will not tell us what anyone felt about what happened in the "Thank You" segment or the "Trellis block" redistribution. It will show us the room taking shape around that redistribution, and leave the feeling to us.

## Attention
The voice lingers on:
- How the team physically configures itself (who sits where, why that matters)
- Micro-acts of recognition or acknowledgment (fist-bumps, nods, Lisa's "hello" with the jar)
- Mira's relationship to Bramble and to the adults (her body, her choices, her hand resting on him)
- Transitions between states (the camera off, then on; Burns standing, then sitting; the room before agenda and after)
- The equipment and logistics (kettle, beer, juice box, camcorder, cards) — these objects are not background; they are how the room speaks

The voice passes over fast:
- Explanation of why the team does what it does (we infer the structure from what they do, not from narration)
- Dialogue heavy on abstraction or emotion; dialogue is sparse and functional
- Any moment that invites sentimentality or summary

## Silence
The chapter will not name the thing Burns raised in "Thank You" and how it landed — not directly. The room's reception will show itself through what happens next (how people move, what gets brought up, whether the worry gets worked into the actual work). Silence on whether Bart is relieved, vindicated, exhausted, or some other thing. Silence on whether the team notices any shift in Bart, or if that noticing happens and is simply not narrated.

Silence, likely, on Mira's interior experience of the team, though her body and her choices will continue to speak.

## Signature
- Repetition as structure: the same actions (hello, handing off the bot, a drink lifted by way of greeting, the camera coming on) appear in the same order across chapters, variations on a theme.
- The subordinate clause as the unit of precision: "She had spent four years being told things by Mira and had learned that the things she was told were sometimes the things..." — the thought builds in that shape, qualifying and narrowing.
- Names used functionally, without articles or pronouns; the team members are called by name when the action requires knowing who is doing it.
- Objects and their temperature, weight, and state: "He was warm. He'd been in the heater." The bot is never abstract.
- The conjunction "and" as the heartbeat: cascading observations linked lightly, no hierarchy imposed between them.
