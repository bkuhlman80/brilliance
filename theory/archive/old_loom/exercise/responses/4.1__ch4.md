# Prediction for Ch 5

## Voice Model Summary

The narrator operates from a position of intimate documentary witness—someone close enough to the room to catch unfolding detail but distant enough to remain unspoken. The voice is:

- **Interiority**: Fiercely external. Inner states are rarely named directly; they surface through gesture, stance, hesitation, placement in space. When interior moments appear ("the cold abstract thing he had been turning over"), they emerge via the narrator's reading of physical evidence, not confession. The team's feelings exist in their hands, their breathing, what they do not say.

- **Concreteness**: Anchored to objects and bodies. The voice lingers on the texture of things—the warm dome, the twitching bot, the jelly jar, the folded-back book. Abstractions get their weight through sensory detail: the nervous stone becomes a thing to turn in your hands; a worry becomes something you can carry alone.

- **Duration**: Variable dwelling. Brief exchanges (the hello rituals) sit alongside expansive observations about light, heat, the shape of the room. The narrator can hold a single gesture for paragraphs and move through complex logistics in two sentences.

- **Register**: Flat with undertones of formality. The language does not perform emotion; it records it. Word choice is precise and restrained ("the chest-laugh came up out of him in two stages"). There are no exclamation marks. Feeling is announced only when the narrator can source it to something visible.

- **Attention**: The voice gravitates to threshold moments and small ceremonies—arrivals, the opening of the meeting, what Lisa checks before the red light comes on. It notices what people carry, what they set down, where they position their hands. It passes over the technical discussion almost entirely.

- **Silence**: The narrator declines to narrate team sentiment directly. What the room feels about the twelve-knob apology is left in the exhale: "The room exhaled in a way the room did not narrate." Cheryl's visit is reported as hearsay. The shape under the drop-cloth is withheld from both the team and reader. Large structural explanations are avoided; the Pitch, the Clade, the Trellis—these exist as things the team references, never fully unpacked.

- **Signature**: Short, direct sentences that can hold multiple meanings. Repetition for rhythm and permission: "Bramble is stopped. I see that Bramble is stopped." Sentence fragments that operate like stage directions. Names used frequently, often as the opening of an exchange. Present tense mixed with past, creating an immediacy that feels like remembering while watching.

## Prediction for Ch 5

**Duration**: Medium-length. Longer than Ch 1 but likely shorter than Ch 4. The chapter will dwell on a single moment or sequence—probably the opening of the present (the gift, the nerve) or a decision being discussed—without cutting away frequently.

**Register**: Will remain flat and precise. No shift toward the celebratory even though the child's birthday is the context. The voice will continue to treat the team's work and the domestic moment (the cake, the warmth) with the same observational distance.

**Concreteness**: High. The chapter will be anchored in what people are doing with their hands, bodies, and the machines. Abstract discussion of "waking up the nerve" will be minimal; the focus will be on the team's preparation and Jasmine's position as the sole nonscientist in the room.

**Interiority**: Minimal naming of feelings. If anyone is nervous about the experiment, you will read it in hesitation, in how they hold the dome, in whether Bart stands still or moves. Mira's experience of the day will be present in her actions, not her thoughts.

**Attention**: The narrator will linger on Jasmine's house-knowledge and how it differs from the team's technical knowledge. There may be detail about how the dome is prepared or positioned. Mira's awareness of the gift will be present but not centerstage. The eye will stay on the threshold between the team's agenda and the domestic moment.

**Silence**: The narrator will not explain what "running the first nerve through it" means. The technical details of the experiment will be withheld or only half-narrated. No direct account of what the team expects to happen. Cheryl remains absent. The full shape under the cloth is still not described.

**Signature**: Short exchanges and physical descriptions. A greeting ritual or check-in moment (likely the red light coming on, or a quiet moment before it). Possibly a sentence that holds two readings at once: one for the scientists, one for the mother. Hands in motion. The way light or cold enters the description. A moment where the room is quiet or shifts its configuration.

**How it opens**: The chapter likely opens mid-action—possibly with Burns or Jasmine already in the warehouse, or with Mira's reaction to something she notices. Not a long temporal setup, but a quick entry into the present tense.

**What it does at the end**: The chapter may close with the red light coming on (as the last three have done), or with a moment of suspended waiting—the team poised before the experiment begins, or Mira's hand on something about to happen. It will likely end on a threshold rather than a conclusion.

**Feeling named on the page**: Unlikely. Tension, anticipation, care will be visible in motion and silence, not declared. The phrase "waiting to be asked" at the end of Ch 4 suggests Jasmine's readiness; Ch 5 will probably show what she is asked and what she offers.
