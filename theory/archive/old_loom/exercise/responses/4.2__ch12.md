# Prediction for Chapter 13

## Confidence: Medium (basis: structural tension and narrator voice shift)

## Duration
Likely 250-400 words. The chapter breaks pattern in form, not length. Recent chapters average 350-500 words; this one will be shorter because it does less explaining and more sitting with what's been said.

## Register
The register shifts from **demonstrative to static**. No card held up, no cereal-voice title-read, no stand-and-show. Clipped, even flatter than usual. The expansiveness drains out. What was colorful becomes austere.

## Concreteness
Stays concrete but narrows its focus. The twelve chapters have swept across four billion years and four thousand. Ch 13 stays in one room, one moment, possibly one person's experience of a moment.

## Interiority
This is where the break happens. Interiority gets named. Not theorized. Named. The pattern up to now: name the mechanism, insist there's no one home. The mechanism doing the insisting is Marge's job, and in Ch 9 and Ch 12 her voice fractured. In Ch 13, either she finishes the sentence she stopped on in Ch 9—the one about the loop that isn't—or the room finishes it for her, or the narrator (who spoke once at the end of Ch 12) speaks again and says what Marge cannot.

Likely: someone says, plainly, that the thing they've been calling a mechanism is also a pain. Not as metaphor. As observation.

## Attention
Lingers on: the gap between the framework and whoever is standing in it. What the cards can hold and what they cannot. Possibly Marge's hands. Possibly the tetrapod that appeared in Ch 5, which has been sitting at the edge of the light this whole time.

Passes over fast: the clades themselves. We're done with that sweep.

## Silence
Where it goes quiet: around the moment someone speaks what was supposed to stay mechanism. The room's silence after. What Marge does next. Whether she picks up the beer or sets it down and walks.

What it declines to say: Whether anything actually changes after someone names the thing that was supposed to have no name.

## Signature
The signature splinters. Bart's cereal-voice doesn't appear. His hand doesn't pick up the card. Either Marge speaks (and her speech is different—no careful psychologist's remove, just a person)—or the narrator speaks (and the narrator, who broke in at the end of Ch 12, speaks plainly about what the narrator saw and could not unsee). The sentence structure becomes shorter. The asides stop. Technical language stays but loses the grounding in joke and demonstration. Someone says something true and it sits in the room without explanation.

## The Opening
Likely opens with a clade card being set down **face-down**, not face-up. Or no card at all. The announcement doesn't come. The meeting doesn't begin the way thirteen meetings have begun. There's a beat where it should. Then someone speaks into it.

## The Ending
Doesn't land on a new concept. Lands on a question the room cannot answer with the framework it's been using. Possibly Marge's voice. Possibly a silence. Possibly the moment where Lisa's hands—which have been still since Ch 11, when she realized she was reading her own future off the cards—move again, and what the movement means is: I don't know what comes next either.

The chapter ends without a fourth capacity. Three are named. The fourth stays behind the wall.
