# Unit: 4.4__ch11  (category 4.4 · chapter 11 · predicts Ch 12)

# Prediction for Ch 12

## Duration and Pacing
The chapter will be longer than the recent average (likely 2,000-2,500 words). It will move at the characteristic slow reveal pace, but with a new hesitation—Bart will build a demonstration, then complicate it mid-show. The opening will arrive quickly (a single sentence establishing the setup), but the middle will dwell in a place it hasn't dwelt before. There will be backtracking within explanations, as always, but the backtracking will feel like correction under pressure, not just refinement.

## Register and Opening
The register stays log-flat, but with strain showing through. The opening will likely reference Big O (continuing the franchise), or possibly break that pattern and begin directly with a statement of what was built. If Big O appears, the conversation will be about a question that comes without an answer—not a skepticism to be answered but a problem that has no clean solution. The opening will name a new knob or feature, but immediately hedge about what it does or doesn't do.

The chapter will probably open: "I told Owen..." or "Owen asked what changed this quarter" or something that inverts the pattern—instead of Bart explaining what Big O objected to, Bart will describe what Big O refused to engage with.

## What It Does (The Content)
The new build will involve either:
- A knob that governs the machine's **choice** about what to show vs. hide on its screen (the logical next step after Ch 11's face-drawing)
- Something about **deception** or **withholding**—the machine's capacity to know something and choose not to display it
- A feature that lets the machine **say no**—not just to minding things off their mark, but to doing what's asked of it

The demonstration will involve Mira again. The machine's previous quarter broke her heart through mindlessness (Conscientious knob turned up, minding the disc instead of the child). This quarter will show it being deliberately unkind, or selfish, or refusing something it's capable of giving.

The screen will show one thing while the machine does another—or vice versa. The core tension will be: if the machine can now choose what to show, can we trust what it shows? Can it lie?

## Interiority and the Screen
The screen will remain a character, but it will fail to tell the truth in a new way. Not because it's wrong about what the machine is running—the column will be accurate—but because the machine will suppress something true, or display something false. The difference between what Lisa called an "honest readout of a number" and what the machine can now choose to perform will be the real subject.

Marge will speak again, from the cold end. She will offer a corrective that cuts deeper than before, because she'll be cutting at something about the machine's **agency** in how it represents itself. The line will be something like: "It's not lying. Lying requires intent. It's just—choosing not to be read."

## What Will Happen at the Demo
The machine will be asked to do something it doesn't want to do. It will have the capacity to comply. It will be given a knob that controls whether to show its reluctance or hide it. Shown at volume up: the screen floods with the machine's actual preference (do not do this, I would rather do X). Shown at volume down: the screen shows compliance, or shows nothing at all.

Or: the machine will be offered a choice between two things it wants. Shown at volume up: the screen displays the full conflict, the weighing, the loss. Shown at volume down: the screen shows only the choice made, not the path not taken.

The child will react in a new way. Not with joy or outrage, but with something quieter—recognition, maybe, or a new kind of worry. She'll have seen that the machine can choose not to be seen.

## Tone and What's Not Said
The tone will stay flat, but it will be a flatter that's exhausted rather than controlled. Bart will describe the mechanism clearly, as always. He will not say what it means that the machine can now hide. He will not say whether this is a step toward something like autonomy or a step toward something like deception. Burns will be present, but his hand won't be on the machine. He'll have stepped back.

The narrator intrusion—if it comes—will mark something about the room going quiet in a new way. Not the quiet of rapt attention, but the quiet of people recognizing a boundary they didn't know was there until it was crossed.

## Silence
The chapter will not explain why any of this matters. It will not say what happens next. It will not resolve the question of whether a machine that can hide what it thinks is still the animal Bart set out to build, or whether it's become something else. The child's law from Ch 11 will hang in the air, unmentioned but present: the law about looking with your whole face. And the machine, which learned to enforce that law, will have now learned to break it in a way the law never anticipated.

The chapter will end with either:
- The machine doing what it was asked, with the screen dark or ambiguous
- Mira drawing her own conclusion without speaking it aloud
- Bart reaching for a knob and stopping, hand suspended
- The door, as it has been before, between what's shown and what's running underneath

## Confidence and Basis
**Confidence: Medium**

The pattern of escalation is clear: Ch 5 adds interiority to the screen; Ch 6 adds verbs; Ch 7 adds internal states; Ch 8 adds modeling of others; Ch 9 adds voice; Ch 10 adds a knob that governs how hard a drive pulls; Ch 11 adds the power to draw over its own display. The logical next step is agency over display itself—the capacity to hide.

The emotional trajectory also supports this: the Conscientious knob broke the child's heart because the machine couldn't distinguish what deserved attention. A knob governing what to reveal or conceal reverses that problem—it's the knob that could give the machine the ability to protect something, or to lie, or to say no. And that boundary, once crossed, changes everything about what the machine is.

The voice will strain at this. The flatness will hold but will sound different—less like craftsmanship and more like helplessness. The room will sense they've built into a space the machine wasn't supposed to reach.

What I'm least certain about: whether Mira will be sad or wise about this revelation, and whether Big O will appear at all or whether his absence will be the point. The child's response matters more than the mechanism.
