# Prediction for Ch 3

## Voice Model

This voice is owned by Bart, who chairs quarterly technical presentations about Bramble (a bio-inspired robot). The register moves between three modes: (1) direct, colloquial framing ("he'd open it the way he'd open it for the next three years"), (2) extended monologues introducing technical concepts through someone else's voice (Big O), and (3) sparse, almost stage-direction prose ("He set Bramble down, gentle"). 

The voice lingers on *problems* — not as failures but as irreducible features. In Ch 1, it names the twelve-knob problem without solving it and trusts the problem to resolve itself ("resolves itself by being a problem"). The concrete object (Bramble, the crank, the gradient) anchors abstraction: personality isn't discussed in the abstract but as "a thing the household winds up once a week." There's a bias toward biology over engineering — the logic flows backwards from what living things actually do, not from what an engineer would design.

The interiority is almost entirely absent from Bart's own inner state. We see almost no feeling named from him directly; instead, feeling attaches to objects and other people (Mira's preference for things that wait; Burns laughing the chest-laugh because the problem is solvable). When Bart speaks about his own thinking, it's past-tense and settled ("was wrong about it for five and a half months"). 

The signature: Bart opens frames with other voices (Big O, Lisa, Jasmine, Marge). He names them, lets them speak or be spoken to, then steps back to let the others talk. He's the chairperson who holds the mic but cedes the authority. Short sentences cluster with longer ones. Repetition without monotony: "No chip. No sensors. No memory" vs "No gradient, it doesn't search." The diction is precise but domestic — "barely," "tumble," "spins," verbs that could describe animals.

## Prediction for Ch 3

**Confidence: Medium-High**

**Basis:** The pattern across both chapters shows a quarter-turn rhythm: Ch 1 introduces the core mechanism (the personality knob) and names the unsolved problem (twelve knobs, how do they fit). Ch 2 addresses Big O's objection and reveals the next Bramblogue (confusion, reorienting, the dice). Ch 3 will likely turn to *implementation* — showing what happens when the system runs in practice, or introducing a second mechanism/knob that surfaces from the first.

### Expected properties:

**Duration:** Medium length, somewhere between the two so far. Ch 1 dwells longest; Ch 2 compresses the same arc tighter. Ch 3 will probably find a middle ground — enough room for a new object or problem to be introduced and held, but not the sprawling frame that Ch 1 had.

**Register:** Flatter at the start. The pattern across both chapters shows Bart opening with a brief setup (a quote from Big O, a phone call, a floor handoff), then letting concrete material do the work. Expect a tighter opener than Ch 1's introduction — Ch 2's opener is more clipped, and Ch 3 will likely follow that path.

**Concreteness:** High. A new component or behavior of Bramble will appear — something the system does when conditions are right. It may involve heat, movement, or decay. The twelve-knob problem may reappear as a ghost (implied but not solved). Expect object-level detail, not abstraction.

**Interiority:** Minimal. Bart won't name doubt or struggle. If anyone expresses reservations, it will be through another speaker (a skeptical question, a comment from the group). The feeling in the room will be carried by gesture and response (laughter, silence, someone sitting or standing).

**Attention:** Will linger on what Bramble does when working correctly — not failure states, but the thing performing its disposal pattern. Will pass over quickly: names of people not present, backstory, anything that isn't in the room or on the bot. Silence: no one will question whether this approach is *right* — the work is treated as correct by design.

**Signature:** Will open with either a quoted interruption (Big O again, or Cheryl, or Burns) or a stage direction (someone picking up Bramble, setting it down). Will include at least one instance of a simple sentence built from repetition or parallel construction ("It runs. It stops. It runs again"). Will delegate some explanation to a second voice (probably Lisa, who speaks for mechanism). Will end on an unsolved question or a problem named but not resolved — not a punchline, but an opening.

**Specific prediction:** The chapter will concern either a third knob or a second environmental factor (temperature sensitivity, wear patterns over time). Bart will have been "wrong" about something for weeks, then shifted his thinking. Someone will ask a clarifying question that forces Bart to delegate to Lisa or return to Bramble itself. The chapter will close on a problem visible in the current run that Bart hasn't yet solved — possibly the twelve-knob problem again, but from a new angle.
