# Prediction for Chapter 7

## Duration & Dwelling
Medium-length chapter, likely 800–1200 words. Will linger on a single technical moment or failure, with pauses for consequence to settle. The pattern shows Bart holding moments open: "let it sit," silence before the point lands. Ch 6 ends with Marge moving her kettle — an act of care registered without statement. Ch 7 will dwell on something that breaks this gentleness, or reveals it was incomplete.

## Register
Clipped, moving fast through explanation, then expansive in the moment of failure or realization. Not florid; the voice is concrete and report-like. Will name things by what they do, not by feeling. "Head first," "won't fix" — these are titles that *show* the voice arriving at a shape for the problem, not imposing one.

## Concreteness
Entirely object-level. Will show something Bramble does or fails to do — a sensor malfunction, a reflex that generalizes wrong, a moment where the puppet training doesn't translate to autonomy. No introspection from Bart; will report what he saw and what Big O said about it. The frame will be the physical behavior.

## Interiority
None named. Bart does not report his own thought or feeling. Big O's skepticism is his voice; Bart reports it as utterance, not interpretation. When inner state matters, it shows as action: Marge moving the kettle, Burns thumbing the head down, Jasmine noticing that Mira likes things that wait. Ch 7 will follow this pattern — something external, read by others' reactions.

## Attention
Will narrow sharply to one failure or one surprising success. The pattern is: introduce a feature (the crank, the tumble, the puppet rig, the narrating screen), show it working, let Big O challenge it, Bart defends it not by fixing but by restating its necessity. Ch 7 will likely break this: either Bart and Big O will find agreement on something that *needs* fixing, or a third person (Lisa, Marge, Cheryl, Burns) will introduce a constraint Bart hasn't faced.

## Silence
Will not say: what Bramble is for, what the household wants it to do in the world, whether this is working. Will not settle any larger question. Will open a new problem and leave it. The pattern: each chapter adds complexity (personality knob → sun-seeking → latency → force calibration → cephalic priority → volition and rest), and the problem at the end of each chapter *becomes* the crux of the next. Ch 7 will inherit the end of Ch 6: Bramble learning to ignore the camera, to seek warmth, to narrate inaction. That capability invites a new failure or a new question from outside.

## Signature — Diction & Syntax
- Compresses the technical into the colloquial: "personality knob," "head first," "won't fix." Names concepts by their shape or symptom, not their formal property.
- Uses quoted speech to carry the real objection: "Big O says / says quoting:" — his skepticism is the motor.
- Builds in silences: "He let it sit," "Bart let that land." Paces the reveal by withholding the response.
- Moves from setup (the feature, the test, Big O's challenge) to Bart's defense (which is never defensive, always a restatement of why the "flaw" is the point) to a closing image (Bramble doing the thing, or failing to, or surprising everyone).
- Sentences are declarative and short when the point is cold fact, long and nested when the point is a whole system or a piece of reasoning. "A thing that could only learn off a reward landing dead on the move could only live somewhere rewards land dead on the move" — packed, one breath, one logic.

## Prediction for Ch 7

**Title:** Something short, technical, learned by the chapter's action. Likely frame: a constraint, a limit, or an unexpected competence. "The Gap," "Split Focus," "Divided Attention," "Trade," "The Cost." (Pattern: titles are nouns or verbs that summarize the problem that chapter solves / fails to solve.)

**Opening:** Will open with Big O (or Lisa, or a new voice) challenging Bramble on something that's worked up to now. Likely the challenge: now that it narrates, now that it can want and ignore and seek, what happens when two desires conflict? The screen says CHARGE UNIT, but the camera glows. Can it hold two states? The opening will be the challenge, not Bart's response.

**Middle:** Will show Bramble in a test loop where the two things (warmth and light, or rest and chase, or two smells, or Mira calling and the cat moving) pull it in opposite directions. Will show what it chooses, or what it does when it can't choose. Will show the physical result — does it freeze, does it choose wrong, does it show strain in the choosing. Bart will run it twice, three times. The voice will be report-only.

**Closing silence:** Will not explain what the conflict *means* — whether this is a flaw or a feature, whether the animal's real or the decision was. Will either:
- End on Bramble choosing (CHARGE UNIT wins; the camera fades) and someone noticing. Marge or Burns or Jasmine will make a small gesture. The frame will close on their reading of it, not Bart's claim about it.
- End on Bramble stuck between them, or oscillating, or screen flickering between two states. Let the image sit.
- End on Big O's challenge unanswered, and Bart saying "Not solving it tonight" — another problem for the queue, like the twelve-knob problem.

**Length:** ~900 words. Will move fast through setup and test, linger on the moment of conflict, close brief.

**Confidence:** Medium-High. The pattern is stable through six chapters. The voice is consistent in structure (feature → Big O challenge → Bart's restatement → closing image with others' reaction). The escalation is clear: each feature adds a degree of freedom, and each new degree creates a new conflict. A chapter 7 that introduces conflict between drives (rather than adding a new drive) is the natural next step. The confidence drops if the book has other voices taking turns, or if a new character enters with a different concern.

**Basis:** 
- Structure: every chapter opens by introducing or extending a feature, closes by showing it in action and others' reaction.
- Escalation: crank → sun-seeking → silent body → calibrated flinch → headed body → screen narration → now, internal conflict between two narrated wants.
- Voice: Bart's tone never shifts; Big O's challenge never stops; no character judges another; everyone reports behavior and lets it mean itself.
- Silence: the "why" is never answered. Why build this at all, what is it *for*, what does it want — never said. The book may never say.
