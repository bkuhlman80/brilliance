# Prediction for Chapter 10

## Duration
Medium to long. The pattern across all nine chapters holds a steady rhythm of 1200-2200 words. This one will likely stay in that band, organized around a single dense demo or moment rather than a tour through multiple mechanics.

## Register
Clipped to expansive in ratio. The opening will be terse — Bart's short declarative style — but the body of the chapter will dwell. The speaking voice stays present throughout; the narration of what happens will carry more interior detail than the last few chapters, which have moved toward cleaner surface description.

## Concreteness
High. Every chapter lives at the object level — hands on the machine, screens showing words, bodies in a room. This one will too. The prediction is for a moment where the physicality of the interaction is the entire point.

## Interiority
Named, but deferred. The new machine from Ch 9 speaks now. Interiority will come through its own utterances and the screen's honest read, not through narrative summary of feeling. What it thinks will be visible on glass; what the humans think will be held back except where their silence or motion tells it.

## Attention
Lingers on the thing's capacity to misread or learn wrong. Every quarter since Ch 2 has included a moment where Bart names something the machine will *not* do or *will* get wrong. This quarter will linger on a failure or edge case — the Neurotic knob has introduced scaling, and scaling means edges.

## Silence
Will avoid explaining why the thing that learned to ask "Tell me first" would ever make an error of politeness or misjudgment. The gap between the screen's honesty and the mouth's speech is where silence lives. The chapter will show that gap without naming it as a problem.

## Signature
Diction: *short sentences in Bart's voice, then the screen's vocabulary of brackets and columns, then the thing's own halting speech, then reaction from the room.* Syntax: declarative opening, a spec or a problem stated by Owen, then a long walk through what was built and why, then a moment where the build does something nobody planned, then the room's response (usually Marge, usually bracketed with the word "only").

## Opening
Will open with Owen's question or critique — this is the fourth consecutive chapter to start with something Owen said or demanded. The critique will be about the thing's new capacity to speak: either a question about what happens when it lies, or what happens when it wants something it can't say.

## What it builds toward
A moment where the humanoid either says something it shouldn't have known to say, or fails to say something the room expected. The screen will show one thing; the mouth will show another, or will show the same thing but the room will read it differently now that the thing has a voice.

## Ending
Will not resolve the tension between the honest screen and the speaking mouth. Will hand it to the room to sit with. Likely ends with Mira or Jasmine noting what the new thing can do, or Marge holding the line on what it actually is doing. The last sentence will be short and will not tell you whether what just happened was good.

The introduction of speech in Ch 9 and the request for consent ("Tell me first") creates an obvious next step: a moment where speech breaks the perfect honesty of the screen, either by mishap or by the thing's own learning. The chapter will not judge whether this is progress.
