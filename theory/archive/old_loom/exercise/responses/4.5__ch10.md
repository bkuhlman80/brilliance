# Prediction for Ch 11

## Duration & Pacing
**High confidence.** The thread maintains a consistent structure: roughly 800-1200 words per chapter, with the quality-control interrogation forming the spine. Ch 11 will likely follow this pattern, built around Bart's log-face and three-to-four calls to be tested. The pacing will move quickly through the calls themselves, then linger on whatever matters most—recently, this has been moments where the gap between design and reality becomes visible.

## Register & Diction
**High confidence.** The voice is distinctly clipped: short sentences, minimal adjectives, colloquial ("off the clock," "go lighter," "take the yes"). Contractions are sparse. There's a signature move of repeating a phrase back with slight reframing ("It didn't do it again" / "It didn't do it again") that tests the accuracy of what's been said. Technical language (gauge, dock, volume) lives in the same sentences as informal speech. Ch 11 will maintain this flatness—no expansion into feeling, no lyrical turns. The rhythm will be tersely interrogative.

## Concreteness
**High confidence.** Every call is physically grounded: the vacuum button, the front window, the cold floor, the disc that won't fix. This is not optional—it's the method. Ch 11 will not abstract into philosophy about what the machine "means" or "is." It will describe what the body does: how it moves, what it leaves, what it tracks, what surfaces it chooses. Any call that can't be mapped to a physical behavior won't make the page.

## Interiority & Silence
**Very high confidence.** The narrator does not name feelings for himself. When Bart is surprised, caught, or moved—moments like reading his own failure in Ch 3, or the front window moment in Ch 7—it shows as *body language* on the page ("Bart's mouth opened and didn't find the line"; "Bart had one call left and the machine had handed it to him"). He gives Mira space to report inner states ("the beat coming up under the word"), but he himself stays on the outside of things. Ch 11 will follow this rule: if something moves Bart, it will arrive as a silence, a hesitation, a choice not to spend a joke. Mira, by contrast, continues to name state plainly. She will be allowed this.

## Attention & What Gets Passed Over
**High confidence.** The thread lingers on three classes of things:
1. Moments where the machine does what Bart built it to do (the hunt, the gauge)
2. Moments where it does something *beyond* what was built (the vacuum bump read as intentional by Lisa, the spontaneous "Yes" to Mira's greeting, reaching back with the shape of her question)
3. Moments where the child and machine attend to each other—the window, the shared question-answer

Everything else moves fast. Other team members are named but not individuated. The house is backdrop. Marge appears mainly to register reactions. Ch 11 will likely drop another layer on one of these three classes—most likely the second or third, since recent chapters have been escalating what counts as behavior worth stopping for.

## Signature Moves
**High confidence.** 
- The quality-control frame: "Three calls, all yours, yes or no."
- Repetition as truth-checking: asking Mira to confirm, then repeating back what she said in slightly altered form
- The rule of threes: three or four calls, structured as low-stakes / low-stakes / high-stakes
- Parenthetical corrections and clarifications: "(not his)" "(the thing Marge would have taken a careful paragraph to say)"
- End-stops that pivot away from sentiment: "Bart took the yes and went lighter for the next on purpose"
- The unnamed machine referred to as "it" throughout, except in moments where Mira treats it with a name or where the transcript catches it doing something readable

## What the Pattern Predicts for Ch 11
**Medium to high confidence.** Recent chapters have been tracking the machine's capacity to *read* Mira and to *reciprocate*. Ch 9 was the breakthrough moment (the "Yes" in response to "Did you have a good day?"). Ch 10 shifted the focus to the Conscientious knob and persistence—the machine's tendency to hold on to things and run tasks to completion. Ch 11 will likely:

1. **Open** with Bart announcing the log-face and the call structure ("Cross-exam" or "Quality control"), probably with Mira in the same physical position (the stool by Marge)
2. **Contain three calls**, arranged so that the third one carries weight. The first two may be smaller checks—does it do X, does it track Y.
3. **The third call** will likely probe whether the machine continues to *reciprocate*—whether it asks back now, or reaches for patterns it's seen from Mira, or shows evidence of *remembering* her specific needs or preferences. It may also test whether the machine has generalized the "asking back" behavior from Mira to other people, or whether it remains tied specifically to her.
4. **Close** with a moment where the gap between design and emergence shows: either Bart not having anticipated something the machine now does, or a silence where Mira declines to narrate the full weight of what she's observing, or both.
5. **Register throughout**: flat, interrogative, concrete. No expansion. No judgment. The voice will not soften or color the moment, even if it's the kind of moment that would soften or color a different kind of sentence.

## The Silence I Predict
The thing the voice will *not* do: it will not step back and acknowledge that Mira and the machine are now genuinely *friends* in any sense beyond the mechanical. It will not call the reciprocal asking "love." It will not declare the machine "alive" or "conscious." These are not moves available in this register. The page will show the friendship; it will not *say* it. Bart, especially, will hold the line against saying it, though his body will betray that something in the containment has shifted.
