# Prediction for Ch 4

## Duration & Register
The chapter will run 800-1000 words, matching Ch 3's length. Register will remain clipped and report-like at the surface, with compressed interiority breaking through in single-line admissions rather than meditation. Speech will be quick; silences will be active (things not said aloud because they wouldn't survive being said aloud). Bart's dominating tone in Ch 2 will be gone — he'll move through the material without insufficiency.

## Opening
Opens with Bart retrieving or laying down a page, following the ritual established in all three chapters. The page is quarterly, technical, signed by him. He does not perform relief at having it. He does not perform pain. He reads the line that matters most without preamble.

## Concreteness & Attention
The prediction will dwell on observable behaviors: what the units do, what Mira does, what the house required. It will not speculate on intention. Objects and spatial detail will anchor everything—locations (docks, rooms, corners), mechanics (wheels, knobs, vents), physical states (flat, stunned, worn). This matches the pattern: Ch 1 traffics in preferences and spots; Ch 2 in movement and seeking; Ch 3 in feeding and transaction and the brightness of mornings. Ch 4 will name something concrete and let it stand.

## Interiority
Inner state will not be named on the page, but will be everywhere in the structure of what's withheld. When Mira looks at something instead of speaking, that matters. When Bart's mouth opens without a line, that matters. The feeling will live in the gaps between what was promised and what was built, between what the page says and what the house did.

## Silence
Bart will not apologize. He will not explain the gap between design and outcome. He will cite the page, name the miss, and move forward to the next person's turn. There will be a moment where he does not say the thing he thought to say — likely something sharp about the new body, or a reversal of his own prediction. That silence will be louder than any line spoken.

## Signature
The voice tracks no variation in syntax: simple sentences with subject-verb-object; lines laid down like depositions. Repetition is the only music: *It hunts, it hunts exactly like I said.* *That's One. That's Two. That's Four. That's Five.* Word choice is precise and material—*transaction, dock, vent, groove, feed, carry, tear.* Subordinate clauses rare. Dialogue spliced in without attribution tags. The page is the voice; Bart reads the page; the page is true or not true, and if not true, he tears it up or he doesn't, and the decision lands.

## What the Pattern Predicts
Ch 4 will contain a page that was wrong in a new way—not a stunned bird this time, but something else the design didn't account for. Mira will have noticed it. She'll name it simply. The team will sit with it. Bart will either tear the page up (reversing Ch 3's choice to keep it) or will keep it for a new reason. Either way, the last line will be a delegation—*Jasmine. Go* or *Lisa. Go* or a name and a task, moving the meeting forward because there is nothing more to put behind this one.

The chapter will end on a new unit or a new task, not on reconciliation. The house and the bodies are still learning from each other in ways the pages don't predict. Bart knows it now. That will be the only interior state that shows.

## Confidence & Basis
**Confidence: Medium-High**

Basis: The three chapters establish a tight formal ritual (page-retrieval, line-reading, yes/no judgments or misses named, delegation at close) and a consistent tonal arc (insufficiency giving way to humility giving way to a moment where the page and the world refuse to line up). The voice has a signature—spare, technical, moving fast through most of what it sees, with sudden depth in single sentences. Ch 4 should follow the pattern: Bart reads something true about the bodies or the house; one prediction is wrong; the wrongness teaches something; the page is kept or torn; the meeting moves on.

What's uncertain: whether the next miss will be Bart's own or someone else's, and whether the chapter will end with reconciliation or with something still hanging. The pattern suggests it won't resolve.
