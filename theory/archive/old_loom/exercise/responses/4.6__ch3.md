# Prediction for Ch 4

## Voice Model

**Signature**: Jasmine's voice dwells in the space between observation and feeling. Her syntax builds through accumulation—catalogs of fact, held moment by moment, then a turn toward what the facts mean *to her*. She builds patience into her sentences. She knows things by her body first (hand on slate, feeling the relays in her back), then brings that knowing into language. Her diction is precise and domestic: not "noticed" but "turned over"; not "seemed" but "looks like from where I'm standing." 

**Duration & Attention**: She dwells longest on the present behavior of the bots—what they do now, what has shifted in how they move through the house. She passes fast over timeline, over explanations she's already given. She knows her audience and doesn't repeat herself. When she does repeat, it's not for clarity but for precision—another pass at what she's trying to say.

**Register**: Flattened, but colored underneath. She delivers fact without performance. Yet there's a quiet insistence in her flatness—she's right about things, and she speaks as someone who's earned the authority to speak plain. Not clipped (her sentences often run long through listing), but restrained in emotion-language. She will not tell you how she feels. She will tell you what she did and what she saw, and if you're listening, you'll know.

**Interiority**: Inner state stays mostly behind behavior. "I have had ten weeks to find the words for it" is the closest she comes to naming her own process. She models something for the reader: how understanding arrives not through insight but through attention. Her most vulnerable moment (holding on, looking like Bramble is holding on for her) gets immediately reframed by Marge—and Jasmine doesn't object, because she already knew the truth underneath the metaphor.

**Concreteness**: She lives at the object level. Kitchen. Slate. Sunbeam. The actual coordinates of the bots. She doesn't generalize until forced to. When she does abstract ("The lab is trying to keep the others as they were"), she brackets it with fact.

**Silence**: She doesn't narrate uncertainty as uncertainty—she presents what she knows, and leaves what she doesn't know off the page entirely. She doesn't say "I think" or "it seemed"—she says "I didn't know what to make of" or "I don't have it." She's quiet about her own work (the noticing, the waiting, the two a.m. labor she mentions once in passing). She won't tell you if she's worried about the bots or what happens when the experiment ends.

## Prediction for Ch 4

**Confidence**: HIGH
**Basis**: The pattern established across three chapters is structural, not accidental. Jasmine speaks once per chapter meeting. Her speeches follow a template: plain thing first (fact-base), then a turn toward what the pattern means, culminating in a moment where seeing and feeling touch. Ch 1 ends with heat. Ch 2 ends with Mira's hand as parking lot. Ch 3 ends with the bot stopping in front of her, and Marge naming what Jasmine was bringing to it. The next chapter will likely open the question that's been held: what does Jasmine do with the knowledge now? And how does she live with what the bots have revealed?

**Opening**: Jasmine will have the floor again (the pattern of one-per-chapter is intact). She will open with a plain thing—a new behavior, or a shift in behavior already observed. Not a revelation, but something she's been turning over. Possibly: a change in Bramble's patterns over time (faster depletion, new routes), or a change in one of the four protobots (they've been still; now something shifts). Or: what happened in a specific moment she's been holding (Mira doing something new with Bramble, or Jasmine and Burns in the house alone).

**Duration**: Medium-length, comparable to Ch 3. Long enough to catalog, then turn. Not the shortest.

**Register**: Flattened throughout. No judgment-words. She will describe before she interprets. If she names a feeling, it will come late and will be named by someone else first—Jasmine will confirm it or reframe it.

**Movement through the speech**: Fact → attention → the shape of what she's noticed → what it means for how she understands the bot → what it means for how she understands herself (implied, not stated, left behind behavior). Ends either with a question left hanging, or with Jasmine having stated something so plainly that it requires silence in the room.

**What gets named**: Some shift in the bots' autonomy, agency, or relationship to the household. Bramble has been behaving as if it *wants* something (the sunbeams, the cleanliness, the groove or the restlessness). One of the four may have done something unexpected. Or Mira may have understood something Jasmine is still working toward understanding. 

**Signature moment**: Jasmine will do something with her hands while speaking (like standing the juice box up, or touching the table). She will ground abstraction in a physical gesture. The metaphor will be exact and domestic.

**What it doesn't say**: Whether she's afraid of what Bramble is becoming. Whether she thinks the experiment should continue. Whether she and Burns have argued about the heat again. These stay behind the page.

**How it ends**: Does not conclude. Passes the floor to the room—Lisa or Burns or Marge will respond, and their response will open the next question. Or Jasmine ends mid-thought, a statement so precise it doesn't require continuation.
