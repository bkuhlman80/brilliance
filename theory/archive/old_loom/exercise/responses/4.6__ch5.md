# Unit: 4.6__ch5  (category 4.6 · chapter 5 · predicts Ch 6)

# Prediction for Ch 6

## Confidence: High
**Basis:** The voice has established a consistent approach to revelation across five chapters. The pattern is reliable and the trajectory is clear.

---

## Duration & Attention

The chapter will run long—Jasmine's monologues have expanded from roughly 600 words (Ch 1) to sustained 1000+ word passages (Ch 3-5). She dwells on specific, repeated observations. Expect 1200-1500 words minimum, with sustained attention on a single household fact or mechanical behavior observed over time. The chapter will linger on something Jasmine has been turning over, likely for weeks or months.

## Register & Tone

The voice will remain clipped and concrete—short declarative sentences alternating with longer observational passages. No lift or color in the language itself. Flat in diction, precise in description. She will not editorialize or interpret her own observations; she will state them. The team (Lisa, Marge, Burns, Bart) may ask clarifying questions that force her to unpack what she's noticed, but Jasmine herself will stay reportorial.

## Concreteness

Physical detail will dominate. Not abstract speculation about what Bramble "wants" or "understands," but observable, repeatable behaviors: where he moves, what he does to objects, what surfaces he seeks or avoids, what patterns Jasmine's hand can feel. The chapter will describe something Bramble does that looks like choice but can be explained by mechanics—and Jasmine will give both the mechanical explanation and the observation that contradicts it, leaving the contradiction visible on the page.

## Interiority

Jasmine will not name her own inner states. She will report what she does, what she notices, what she thinks about, but not what she *feels.* When emotion appears, it will arrive through her body or her silences, not through reflection. Her exhaustion, her changed relationship to Bramble, her worry about Mira—these will show in action (how she touches things, what she pauses on, where her attention catches) rather than statement.

The team may name Jasmine's feelings (they did in Ch 4 with Marge's gentle correction about the "holding on"). Jasmine herself will accept the naming without embracing it.

## Silence & What Gets Declined

Jasmine will not theorize about consciousness or intention in Bramble. She will not use words like "wants," "prefers," "likes," or "dislikes," even when Mira uses them—she will report the behavior that prompted Mira's interpretation, but will not step into the interpretation herself. She will decline to explain why Bramble behaves differently now than before, settling instead on the observable fact that he does.

She will not speak about Mira's growing attachment to Bramble or what it means. She will describe Mira's behavior (her statements, her touch, her focus) but will leave the significance of it unstated.

## How It Opens

The chapter will likely open with Jasmine being asked to speak, or being handed the floor after a silence or a question from Lisa. There will be a beat—"she took her beat," as the voice does—before she begins. She may begin with "Plain things first" again, or a variant: a signal that she's ready to deliver concrete observation rather than theory.

Alternatively, it may open with Jasmine already in mid-observation, the way Ch 3 opens: a fact about Bramble's behavior stated immediately, without preamble.

## What It Does at the End

The chapter will likely end with a question from Mira (as Ch 2, 4, and 5 all did), or with a moment of physical contact—Jasmine's hand on Mira, or on Bramble, or on an object—that crystallizes something the chapter has been circling. The ending will be quiet. It will not resolve. It will leave the room holding a contradiction or an absence.

If there is dialogue at the end, it will be sparse, and the last line will likely be Jasmine's observation stated plainly, or a minimal response from Jasmine to something Mira or Lisa has said.

## Signature Details

- Jasmine will touch things while speaking: set things down, turn things in her hands, put her hand flat on surfaces. This is how her attention signals itself.
- She will use "plain" or "plainly" to mark what she's about to say as literal, observable fact.
- She will notice temperature gradients, light, position in space, the layout of the house.
- She will report Mira's speech and movement in detail, and her own tone toward Mira will stay practical—she will not effuse, but she will be present.
- Patterns will repeat three times before Jasmine says what the pattern is: Bramble does X, then Bramble does X again, then Jasmine names the pattern.

## What Comes Next in the Arc

By Ch 5, Bramble has diverged from the other four bots (the "protobots"). He has agency over his own charging, he moves freely between floors, he responds to temperature and light, he stalls on obstacles, he's been duplicated and is now separating from his copy. The household is treating him increasingly like a pet (Mira's "he sulks," Jasmine's "he lies in the window like the cat"). 

Ch 6 will likely show Bramble doing something that further erodes the distinction between "machine" and "creature." This will not be stated as philosophy; it will be shown as behavior. Jasmine will describe it with her characteristic precision, and someone at the table (likely Mira) will name what it looks like, and Jasmine will neither confirm nor deny the naming.

The focus may shift slightly away from Bramble's behavior toward how the household—and Mira specifically—is organizing itself around him. But Jasmine will report only what she sees, not what it means.
