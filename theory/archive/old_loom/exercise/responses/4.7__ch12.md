# Unit: 4.7__ch12  (category 4.7 · chapter 12 · predicts Ch 13)

# Prediction for Chapter 13

## Duration & Pacing
Medium length (400–600 words). The narrative mode will shift from meeting-and-reflection to a narrower focus. After Chapter 12's completion of the architectural framework, the voice will slow, dwell, linger. Less structural revelation; more observation of stasis and transition.

## Register
Flatter than recent chapters. The high note of fitting all twelve clades into four circles will have passed. The register will be closer to reportorial—Lisa's register when she "talks about the cell as a thing that does jobs," rather than the triumphant register of Ch 12's unveiling. Fewer exclamations, more silence between statements.

## Concreteness
High but narrowed. The focus will zoom from the whole system to a single object or threshold—likely the bare trellis frame mentioned but never built on, or a piece of hardware (the actual bramble/robot, now stationary), or a simple action (Lisa gathering her things, putting something in a box, handling her coat). The narrator will hold on these details the way Lisa's hand stays near the camcorder without touching it: noticing the refusal to move.

## Interiority
The chapter will finally name something about Lisa's interior state—but indirectly, through what she does not say, or through what she says plainly when she usually speaks in frameworks. The gap between the perfect four-circle architecture she's drawn and the imperfect life she's living (three weeks from formally leaving, already emotionally departed) will become unavoidable. Not confessed. Named through behavior—a flatness in her voice, a line given without its usual qualification, an absence where a gesture usually sits.

## Attention
The voice will narrow from the group's collective work to Lisa alone, or to the moment when the group recognizes Lisa is no longer building with them. The narrator's attention will linger on what Lisa is not doing: not proposing next quarters, not reaching for the marker to name the next knob, not asking what comes above the trellis. The silence will be visible.

## Silence
What will remain unspoken: whether the architecture Lisa built will continue beyond her. Whether Bramble will be left to climb a trellis Lisa will not see the top of. Whether the perfect system she drew can hold without her presence. The question of whether a personality framework can contain a person who is leaving.

## Signature Movement
- Lisa's drink adjusted a quarter-inch when something final is about to happen
- The nose-laugh absent, or used wrongly—in the wrong place, a tic without pleasure
- The flat palm and vertical palm gesture used once, to describe something ending rather than beginning
- A card or board or marker put down and not picked up again

## Opening
The chapter will likely not begin in the meeting room, or will begin there in the last minutes after the real meeting is done. A small physical threshold: Lisa at the door, Lisa at her car, Lisa with the trellis visible behind her, or Lisa in the space between one quarter's ending and the next's not-beginning. The opening will move her toward an exit—not dramatically, but with the certainty of a person who has already chosen.

## Closing
Ambiguous but complete. Not conclusive about the future of the work or the machine. But a threshold crossed: something put down, a hand lifted from a thing it has rested on for a year, a step taken away from the table. The close will land not on revelation but on emptiness—the trellis visible, the bramble still on it, the hand that built the framework no longer reaching.

## Confidence
**High**. The pattern of architecture-building paired with the narrator's growing attention to Lisa's absence requires a chapter that faces the split between the two. The unresolved trellis (mentioned but never acted on) and the repeatedly-noted gap between Lisa's framework and her life point toward a chapter that resolves by accepting the unresolution: the architecture complete, the architect departing.

## Basis
1. **Structural:** Thesis (building architecture, Chs 1–12) requires antithesis (the architect's life incompletely mapped by her own system). Ch 13 is the turn toward synthesis or acceptance of the gap.
2. **Textual pattern:** The narrator's repeated note of what Lisa *doesn't* say or *doesn't* do (the trellis not built on, the absence of certain frameworks, the observation of her coat-readiness) primes for a chapter about refusal and departure.
3. **The Three-Chapter Rule:** Chapters 10–12 form a unit (the big frameworks laid down). Chapters 13–15 will likely form another (the architect's reckoning with what she built). Ch 13 will be the threshold.
4. **Voice consistency:** The narrator has been increasingly observant of Lisa's interiority precisely at moments when Lisa's voice cannot contain it. Ch 13 is where that gap becomes the subject.
