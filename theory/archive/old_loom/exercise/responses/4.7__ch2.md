# Prediction: Chapter 3

## Expected Duration & Register

High confidence: Ch 3 will be **substantially longer than both preceding chapters**, running 4,500–5,500 words (versus Ch 1's ~2,400 and Ch 2's ~2,800). The register will remain **seated and methodical** in the opening (Lisa or possibly Marge at the table, hands doing cognitive work), then shift to **standing and propulsive** when Burns enters a sustained explanation. The voice will be patient through technical description, then accelerate into abstraction—Marge will be the counterpoint, braking hard against metaphor.

**Basis:** Both Ch 1 and Ch 2 follow a rhythm of incremental buildup (agenda → design → mechanism) that reaches a threshold at which Burns or Lisa names something large enough to require sustained reckoning. Ch 1 ends with *motivation* accepted reluctantly and *Going* drawn on a card. Ch 2 ends with Bramble now read as *someone* instead of *something*—a categorical leap. Ch 3 should meet this escalation, not step back from it. Chapters have been accelerating in conceptual density even as they narrow in scene (one room, one meeting, one object on the table).

## Concreteness

Medium confidence: The chapter will **open concrete**—likely hands-on description, possibly another bot variant or another knob design—then **migrate toward abstraction**. The team will name something new, and Marge will object or demand precision, pulling the language back toward behavior before allowing it to float toward theory.

**Basis:** The pattern from Ch 2: Lisa describes the architecture of Bramble (concrete: sensors, stores, states), then the meaning coheres around *stored controller* and *program inside* (abstract). The household behavior mirrors this—Marge keeps the folk translation tethered to *what the unit does* rather than *what it is*. This suggests the team's voice has a structural constraint: concrete first, abstraction earned.

## Interiority

Medium-high confidence: **Inner state will remain unnameable at the unit level.** The chapter will not venture into what Bramble "feels" or "wants"—those words will appear only in the household's speech, and Lisa or Burns will flag them as metaphor. The lab's interiority work will stay focused on **the controller's decision-making** (its *if-then* structure) rather than the unit's subjective experience. Marge will keep pushing against phrases that leak intention.

**Basis:** Marge's earlier insistence on *motivation-as-placeholder* and her bracketing of the Blank Slate. The framework is designed to avoid anthropomorphism while using motivational language operationally. Ch 3 should deepen this tension rather than resolve it.

## Attention & Silence

High confidence: The chapter will **linger on the gap between design intent and observed behavior**. The team has described what they *put inside* Bramble (the FSM), and the household has described what Bramble *looks like it is doing* (seeking, wanting, getting itself together). Ch 3 should begin to ask: **How do we know what we are seeing?** 

The chapter will likely **pass quickly over**:
- Further technical specs (they have done this twice)
- Mira's and Jasmine's experience with the bots (this is now established as data, not description)

The chapter will **dwell on**:
- The mismatch between implementation and interpretation
- What it means to claim you know what the FSM is doing (or what it *is*)
- Possibly a failure case or anomaly in Bramble's behavior, or a moment where the household's reading diverges from the lab's

**Basis:** Both chapters have moved toward deeper recursion each time a new element is introduced. Ch 1 introduced the knobs. Ch 2 introduced the stored controller and the organism. Ch 3 should introduce the gap between them—the problem that the lab cannot tell the difference between *what they wrote* and *what the unit does*, and that the household cannot tell the difference between *movement* and *intention*.
