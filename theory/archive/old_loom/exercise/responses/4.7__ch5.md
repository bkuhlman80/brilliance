# Prediction for Ch 6

## Duration
Expect a chapter of comparable length to Ch 5 (roughly 2000-2500 words). The pattern shows variable length but consistent scale — the meeting transcript form allows expansion through dialogue without thinning the voice. This chapter will likely include a full meeting cycle: opening, technical blocks, framework block, possibly a denouement.

## Register
Consistently table-level and floor-level. The narrator maintains a flat, observational register — no lament, no trumpet. Speech is clipped and precise; physical description is concrete. When feeling appears it appears as body language (hand positions, gaze, silence). No emotional coloring will be added by the narrator. The voice permits tension to live in what's unsaid and in the spacing of breaths.

## Concreteness
The thread favors object-level description. A knob is a knob; a board is a board; a circuit has clicks. Abstractions arrive only when grounded in physical fact — "motivation" only after the bot has been held in a palm. Expect Ch 6 to maintain this discipline. When new concepts arrive they will come tethered to something the room can touch or hear or watch.

## Interiority
The narrator names inner states sparingly and only when they move through the body into action. Lisa's pleasure at Marge's word lands as "the pleasure of being asked to take off a coat she hadn't known she was wearing." Burns's excitement arrives as "too far, the way he did." The thread does not tell you what anyone feels; it shows you what they do when they feel it. Ch 6 will continue this restraint — emotion will leak only through hand-motion, the length of a pause, the eyes turning away.

## Attention
The thread lingers on: the physics of the bots, the vocabulary negotiations, the moments when old frameworks collide with new data (Mira's signature, the worm experiments). It passes quickly over: logistics, personal history (Lisa's runs are mentioned once and filed), social pleasantry. The room's attention is hierarchical — big ideas and small physics matter; weather does not. Expect Ch 6 to maintain this hierarchy.

## Silence
The thread is loud about technical detail and nearly silent about: motivation for the whole project (why does Cheryl want this), the nature of the household (Jasmine, Mira, Bramble are present but their inner lives are strictly off-limits), doubt (none of the four core team members questions the framework itself, only specific word choices or wire paths). Ch 6 will likely extend at least one of these silences — possibly the doubt. A crack in the consensus would register as a change in how the room speaks to each other, not in anyone saying "I'm worried."

## Signature: Diction
- Preferred words: quarter, clade, board, goo, card, knob, dom, wire, drift, layer, regulator, motivation, signature, organism
- Forbidden words: good, bad, better, worse, learns, feels, evolves
- Characteristic phrases: "the thing that," "say more," "on the tape," "in the house," "the only way," "what's left," contrasts built on "but" ("It's slow but," "It wasn't learning but," "Not more of the old goo but")

## Signature: Syntax
Short declarative sentences when stakes are high. Longer, nested structures for explanation. Dialogue establishes authority through compression — the most certain people in the room use the fewest words. The narrator uses sentence fragments to mark observation: "The camera had been logged in." "Nobody said anything for a second." These mark moments where the room has shifted.

## Signature: The Meeting Form Itself
Each chapter follows: opening address → technical blocks (one per person or pairing) → trellis/framework block → usually a denouement moment. The denouement is where the frame cracks slightly — Mira naming herself, the worm experiment landing differently, the affective goo landing as something new. Ch 6's denouement may be the moment when the framework's implications catch up to the room, or when what's been implicit all night gets named.
