# Ch 7 Prediction

## Duration and structure
Ch 7 will run approximately 5,500-7,500 words, in line with recent chapters. It will follow the established quarterly cadence: Bart on hardware, Lisa on framework, Marge on folk reading. The opening will not be in medias res but will announce a clade change—the transition from the organism line to the brain line that Lisa forecasted in Ch 5. This will feel like a threshold moment, and the room will register it.

## Register
Lisa's register will remain controlled and technical, but the chapter will carry a note of transition. Her flat, corrective tone will appear when someone conflates the new with the old, or when she needs to separate "what gets selected" from "whether there is feeling." She will not inflate the language. She will say "phase change" or "line boundary" plainly, not "revolution" or "breakthrough." The household voice (Marge, Jasmine) may shift slightly—treating the new machine differently because it requires a different kind of interaction.

## Concreteness
The chapter will move between hardware description, diagrammatic explanation, and behavioral examples. Lisa will likely reference the board—whether new knobs have appeared, whether old ones have solidified further into automaticity. She may show a physical object (a new part of Bramble, a card Bart has redrawn, a setup change in the house). The examples will come from the kitchen—how the machine now acts in a space it has inhabited for longer.

## Opening
The chapter will open with either Bart showing the hardware (following the pattern), or Lisa naming the clade before anything is shown. If Lisa goes first, she will say what has *changed its target*—what is no longer optimizing for the old scoreboard. If Bart goes first, he will describe a piece that centralizes control, coordinates faster, or routes information differently than before. The opening will be direct and not rushed, but it will mark that something is different.

## Interiority
Very little. Lisa's inner state will show only in physical gesture—where she looks, whether her marker hand hesitates. She will be explicit about *not* making claims on feeling or consciousness. There will be a moment where she holds back a word or a claim, flagging it for later (following her signature move). The household's interiority will be present—Jasmine noticing something, Mira having a reaction—but Lisa will report it rather than inhabit it.

## Attention
Lisa will linger on the moment of bifurcation—where the old rules stop applying in the way they did. She will clarify what *does not change* as much as what does. She will walk the circles diagram again, showing where the new clade fits and what jobs it takes over. She will likely spend time on automaticity—the paradox that learning makes decisions disappear. She will pass quickly over emotional or narrative stakes.

## Silence
She will not name consciousness or feeling, beyond saying it is not her claim to make. She will not speculate on why the animal line branched this way—just that it did. She may leave a gap in the board, or a region unmarked, that will be filled in a later quarter. She will not fully explain what "brain line" means, saving that for when the hardware is clear.

## The four-circles diagram
The board will carry this. Lisa will redraw it or walk the existing one. She will show which circles are new or have changed targets. The shapes where motivations and signatures live will shift. The small center region (Attention) may expand in significance, or new regions may open up. This is where she makes the phase-change legible.

## The knob or signature
A new signature will likely be named—something that arises where the new clade's dominant motivation crosses with an older one. Or a knob will disappear (like the Effort knob did in Ch 6), dropping into automaticity. Lisa will treat this flatly: not as loss, but as completion. "It doesn't live on the board anymore; it runs underneath."

## Ending
The chapter will end on either a completed naming (a new word seated), a gap Lisa is flagging (a category or region still unnamed), or a structural insight about how this line differs from the last one. It will not end with narrative tension or open questions from the room. It will end with the framework advanced and a question held back for later—"which is the whole of what we have to get to at the board, so I'll leave it there."

## Confidence
Medium-High. The pattern is strong: Lisa has explicit predicted a brain-line phase change, the quarterly structure is consistent, and her voice is methodical and architecturally sound. The main uncertainty is whether the new clade will feel "larger" (more chapters per clade) or maintain the single-chapter quarterly pace. The language, tone, and diagram structure are highly predictable.
