# Prediction for Ch 3

## Duration & Structure
The chapter will approximate the length of Ch 2 (roughly 2500-3500 words). It will maintain the established rhythm: opening frame, theoretical announcement, extended case study or conceptual example, structural observation, closure with physical gesture. Marge will dwell longest on the example itself—moving through a scenario or person's behavior pattern methodically—rather than the naming of the fallacy. The opening will be brief; the ending will be under 200 words.

## Register
Clipped and steady, with moments of mild impatience (like the shrug in Ch 1). The syntax will remain simple: short declaratives followed by pauses. No ornament. When Marge handles abstract concepts, she will anchor them immediately to concrete behavior—a person doing something, a choice point, a room. The tone will be slightly professorial but not distant; she is thinking aloud with people she trusts.

## What Will Open It
The chapter will begin with a physical scene—the kettle, the camera light, someone settling. Then a name. Then a pause. Then: "Fallacy three" or a direct statement of the third fallacy's name. The pattern suggests she will *name it first, explain it second*, the reverse of how Ch 2 began (where she named it, then took time to set it up).

## The Fallacy Itself
The fallacy will be a claim about personality, self, or change—the fourth axis of self-understanding. Ch 1 addressed who changes (Ghost: the willing self outside the system). Ch 2 addressed what is there to observe (Blank Slate: no stable inside, just context). Ch 3 will likely address one of: *what can be known about a person* / *what parts of personality are real vs constructed* / *whether change happens to the person or in the person* / *the role of narrative or retrospective sense-making in identity*. The fallacy will be named with a single phrase or short concept, as Ch 1 and Ch 2 were.

## Concreteness
Very high. The example will involve a specific person (likely a client or a person Marge observed), named scenario, or behavior sequence. The person will do something—speak, hesitate, choose, respond to pressure. Marge will not discuss the fallacy in the abstract for long; she will use the example to demonstrate where the fallacy shows up in how people think about themselves or what therapists believe about change.

## Interiority
Moderate. Marge will name some inner states (fear, desire, confusion) but will focus more on *what a person does with* an inner state than on the state itself. She may reference a client's reported experience (what they said they felt) but will redirect to the behavioral or functional level: "The client said they felt trapped, but what was happening was..." The pattern is to use reported interiority as evidence of the deeper program, not to dwell in it.

## Attention & Silence
Marge will linger on the moment where someone *gets stuck* between two ideas about themselves, or where a common belief about personality produces a blind spot. She will move quickly through jargon, definitions, and academic citations (as she did with Mischel in Ch 2). She will not explain how to *fix* the fallacy in this chapter; that is future work. She may reference Cheryl (the audience watching via camera) or acknowledge that the connection between all four fallacies is still being worked out.

## Silence
The chapter will not name what the fourth fallacy is, or how all four connect. It will not offer guidance on therapeutic intervention. It will not step into first-person reflection on Marge's own experience of personality change (though such reflection may come later in the arc). It will not criticize Mischel or other figures harshly; critique will be precise and proportional.

## Signature Moves
- **Opening ritual**: physical scene, camera light, name of fallacy  
- **Pauses**: frequent, roughly every 150-300 words, often after a key claim  
- **The shrug or concession**: a moment where Marge grants an annoying point and moves past it  
- **Embedded dialogue or scenario**: a case study or observed interaction, deployed to show the fallacy at work  
- **Structural summary at the end**: "X fallacy photoshops out Y and pastes in Z"—a metalevel observation that sets up the next fallacy  
- **Closing**: a physical gesture (tea cup, sitting back, hand on table) and handing control to Lisa or naming who the speaker is  
- **Second-person address to camera or Cheryl**: moments where Marge orients toward the audience directly

## Confidence & Basis
**Medium confidence** (60-70%). The pattern is clear across two chapters, but only two data points. The prediction is specific enough to be wrong on details (length, the particular example, how the fallacy is named) but rests on robust repetition of: opening frame → naming → explanation via example → structural observation → physical closure. The register, syntax, and rhythm are highly consistent. The move of photoshopping out X and pasting in Y is a strong signal that the four fallacies will follow a parallel structure, making Ch 3 likely to mirror Ch 2's form rather than diverge.

The main uncertainty is whether Marge will introduce a new *kind* of fallacy (stepping away from personality traits) or stay within the domain of how people misunderstand personality, trait, and change.
