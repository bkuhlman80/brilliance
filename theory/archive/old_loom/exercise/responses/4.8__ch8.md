# Prediction for Ch 9

## Duration & Structure
The next chapter will run **medium-long**, roughly 800-1200 words. Given the pattern: Ch 5-6 are shorter interventions from the broader team (Marge brief, others interjecting); Ch 7 is Marge solo and expansive; Ch 8 returns to group setting with Marge dominating. Ch 9 will likely return to group format with Marge as primary speaker, seated, drinking beer in the two-handed grip. Expect 3-5 named speakers or silence. The architecture will be **scene-first** (what happens in the room), then **idea-second** (what Marge names).

## Register & Voice
Marge's voice will remain **clipped and declarative at the argument's spine, expansive in exemplification**. She speaks with near-clinical precision when naming the thing, then unfolds into longer syntax when showing what the thing *means in a body*—compare "There is no such self" (short, hard) against the extended meditation on the girl and her trust curve (long, anatomical). Ch 9 will hew this pattern: brief naming, then dwelling in consequence. The voice will be **unadorned but not flat**—factual without coldness, because the facts themselves (about minds, attachment, change) carry weight she doesn't decorate.

## Interiority & Silence
Marge names inner states directly but sparingly: feeling vs. being, the wanting of the noble savage, the "worried mouth." She does not narrate her own interior at length. What goes unnamed: **her own stakes in these arguments**. The narrator has hinted repeatedly—the fence she built first to keep herself on the right side of, the one who lights up about held places while leaving hers, the thing at that lunch—but Marge herself does not say it aloud in these installments. Ch 9 will continue this: **the personal will touch the argument without breaking its surface**. A gesture, a silence where she might have spoken, a tightness in the syntax that reveals without confessing.

## Concreteness
The thread moves constantly between **schema and sensation**—from "there is no homunculus" (abstract) to "her hand opened into what everyone but Lisa thought was a monster claw" (concrete), to "the kettle whistling" or "the beer in the two-handed grip" (object-level). These objects anchor the ideas: the kettle marks time, the beer marks pause, the hands mark what she's holding back. Ch 9 will hold this **two-register movement**. Expect a new object or gesture to enter the room—something Marge does with her hands, something that breaks or settles, a change in who's drinking what.

## Attention & What It Passes Over
Marge dwells on: **faces of the people she's treated, the details of animals in tanks, the moment a person guards an unused corner of themselves, what it costs to name a thing**. She passes quickly over: pure abstraction without grounding, her own biography, objections she's already answered, the social pleasantries of the room. Ch 9 will likely dwell on **a case or example—probably the closest yet to autobiography without being autobiography**. The question hinted at in Ch 8 ("why four, not three or forty") suggests the next chapter will either pursue that structural question or, more likely, **refuse it**—will turn toward something the structure can't hold.

Marge will sit. She will have the beer or tea in both hands. She will open **not with a new fallacy** but with the question from Ch 8 unanswered—or with someone asking her to answer it. The chapter will dwell on **why four functions, not five; why the four divide the way they divide**.
