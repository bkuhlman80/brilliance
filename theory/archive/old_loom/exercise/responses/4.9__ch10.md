# Prediction for Ch 11

## Confidence: High
**Basis**: Structural pattern across Ch 3–10 (table ritual + escalation) plus the decisive turn in Ch 10 where the household's answer breaks open the laboratory's answer.

---

## Duration
Moderate length, neither brief nor expansive. The chapter will likely stay at the table or close to it — the ritual space has momentum and the aftermath of Ch 10's collision demands presence. Expect dwelling on aftermath rather than rushing away from it.

## Register
Shift downward from the formal parliamentary tone. The vote has been called and resolved; what remains is personal, quieter, less about the count itself. Flatter in surface but heavier underneath — the work of sitting with what just happened, not performing the logic of it.

## Concreteness
High. Physical detail will anchor the moment: hands, bodies at rest or in motion, objects on the table, the machines themselves as presences rather than abstractions. The child's flat statement in Ch 10 ("it was there") promises that concreteness will drive the next turn.

## Interiority
Named but sparse. The narrator has been observing Marge's internal state off-tape (the church, the three weeks); Ch 11 will likely show more of what the table participants feel *after* the vote, but without overexplaining it. Feelings will appear in body and silence more than in reflection.

## Attention
Will linger on Marge — she called the vote, opened the household into the count, and now must sit in the aftermath of her own gesture. Also on the child, who spoke the truth that split the two counts. The machines themselves may recede slightly (though one may be present and observed). The narrator's own relationship to what was just said will come into focus.

## Signature
The voice will stay in its established mode: observation that holds competing truths without choosing between them, syntax that builds scaffolding for difficult ideas, diction that avoids sentiment while acknowledging weight. Will likely include a line or moment where the narrator steps outside the room's awareness — marks something no one else caught, as she has done throughout.

## Silence
The chapter will not resolve the split between the lab count and the household count. It will not make them compatible. It will probably not explain what anyone does *next*. Marge picked her beer back up at the end of Ch 10; Ch 11 may open there, in the moment after the full utterance, where silence is still available and no one has yet moved to break it or cement it.

## Likely opening
Physical, present-tense. Probably a body detail: someone setting something down, someone still, someone breathing. The ritual is over; what comes after the ritual is the chapter's work.

## Likely shape
Downward arc — not toward resolution but toward a kind of settling. The four-count is unanimous but hollow (because it rested on four different floors). The household refuses the lab's ladder. The narrator has now had to carry all of it. The chapter may end with dispersal — people moving away from the table — or with a small domestic return, something that shifts the focus from the counting machinery to the actual life beneath and beside it.

## Likely ending
Not a conclusive statement. More likely: return to the machines as actual objects in the room, not as the occasion for philosophy. Or return to Mira, the child, as the one who will live inside this disagreement without being bound by the count. Or the narrator, alone, remarking on what stayed unspoken. The chapter will probably not say "and then they moved on" — instead, it will show the grain of what happens when two true answers sit at the same table and don't erase each other.
