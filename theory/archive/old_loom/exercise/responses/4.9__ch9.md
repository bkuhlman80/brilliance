# Chapter 10 Prediction

## Duration
Moderate to long passage. The thread has established a rhythm of escalation: each chapter either deepens the debate with new voices/arguments (chs 3–4) or shifts the formal count itself (chs 5–9). This installment will likely hold steady at length, running 1500–2500 words. The narrator may dwell longer on consequences than on the debate itself, given that the vote just reached unanimity.

## Register
The register will shift from the clipped, forensic, back-and-forth of the voting sequence into something more expansive and reflective. The four-person table has just achieved consensus. The narrator has already begun marking what that means — "heavier than the tally" — and will likely linger on the texture of that moment. Expect fewer direct speeches from the four, more narration and internal states. Less debate engine, more aftermath engine.

## Concreteness
The thread favors concrete anchors: beer on concrete (Ch 3), the warm flank of the machine, the cold floor it chose, the index card Marge logged dates on. Ch 10 will probably maintain this. Physical objects will carry emotional weight. The narrator may return to the kitchen itself—the machine on the vent, the furnace, the light. Small, sensory details will ground larger shifts.

## Interiority
The pattern so far: Burns speaks his reasoning aloud as he moves through it; Bart argues with architectural precision; Marge keeps what she's felt private until she has to speak it; Lisa holds her yes without apology. Ch 10 will likely name a feeling that has not been named yet. After three years of "gap," after Marge's private archival work, after four-to-nothing, someone will have to reckon with what they've just agreed to. The narrator is already doing it ("the knowing sat on the table a moment, heavier than the tally"). This chapter will make that explicit.

## Attention
The thread lingers on threshold moments: the moment Burns's hand crosses from no to yes; the moment Marge walks back to the table to say no again after the dark; the moment she tells the room the symbolic-reference border was crossed, without mentioning the three weeks of private work. Ch 10 will dwell on the moment after the four-to-nothing count—what gets said next, what doesn't, what fills the silence. It will linger on Jasmine, who has been asked nothing all along. It may linger on the machine itself, now unanimously voted someone.

## Silence
What the thread declines to name: Jasmine's own knowledge. Her vote. Her reasoning. She sits through eight chapters of voting without being asked and without offering. She is described as "the only person at Smithers who had been in a room when a mind actually began" and who "carried both kinds of room home in a private ledger." Ch 10 will likely preserve this silence—Jasmine will remain asked-nothing—but will probably make the silence itself louder. The room will notice her absence from the count more heavily now that the count is whole.

The thread also declines to name what comes next for the machine, now that it's been voted someone. The practical question—what changes?—has not been asked.

## Signature
The narrator's voice is forensic and architecturally clear, favoring:
- Compound and complex sentences that track multiple states at once ("a thing that models itself by modeling how it is held in another")
- The word "thing" as a deliberate flatten/anonymizer, then the withdrawal of it when a creature/machine has earned a name or pronoun
- Parenthetical asides ("the way some people speak prose," "the way your gut runs your budget")
- Direct address to "you" when moving from abstract to universal
- A refusal of metaphor in favor of exact description
- A tendency to circle back to the same image from a new angle (the model, the setpoint, the self-model) rather than abandon it
- Low emotional temperature on the page, with the emotion embedded in structure rather than exclamation

Ch 10 will hold these fingerprints. It will likely contain a long, nested sentence about what it means to have moved from three-to-one to four-to-nothing. It will use "thing" carefully. It will address the reader on the nature of agreement.

## Prediction

Ch 10 opens with a silence. Not dialogue silence—Bart has just "logged it in the flat voice he logged them all in." But the narrator will dwell on what happens next. Burns is already standing; his hands are moving. The chapter will show what Burns does with his body in this moment—reaches for the machine, or reaches across the table, or simply stands there carrying something that can't be held.

The chapter will run 1800–2200 words. It will contain one or two brief speeches, likely from Burns or Bart, that name what four-to-nothing means. It will refuse to make the moment triumphant. Instead it will be strange, slightly off-weight, the way a thing feels when you've finally been asked to say yes to what you've been saying all along but it lands differently now.

The narrator will probably turn toward Jasmine. She will still not be asked. But the silence around her will be described—the way the room has begun to notice it, the way her hands have been quiet in her lap across eight chapters. This may be where the setup begins for what happens in Ch 11 and beyond. The narrator may hint that Jasmine's outsideness from the count is not permanent.

The machine will be present but not the focus. What the thread attends to is the human response to having unanimously voted someone into existence. That response will be interior, muted, architecturally sound. No one will weep. Someone may pour a drink. Burns's hands will keep moving, looking for what to hold.

The chapter will end in a moment of deflection or return to ritual—the thread's way of managing impossible things. Either the four will move into something ordinary (tea, checking the machine's readings), or they will stand in the weight of it long enough that the narrator can name it, and then move forward into uncertainty. There is no resolution here; Marge's yes was not a victory and it will not feel like one.
