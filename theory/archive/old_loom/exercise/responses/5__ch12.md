# Prediction for Chapter 13

## Duration & Pace
Medium-length chapter, dwelling heavily on small physical movements and sensory details. Will spend considerable time on the moment of the door opening or the threshold being crossed. Not rushed.

## Register
Restrained, spare, but with occasional moments of charged language. Will move between observed concrete detail and interior constraint. Clipped syntax in moments of tension or difficult feeling; longer sentences when establishing a space or state.

## Concreteness
Physical-level account. The chapter will track movement through space — the approach to the door, the crossing of thresholds, placement of objects and bodies. Will notice temperatures, textures, the small mechanical details of entering a space (how a hand moves, what a floor feels like underfoot). Architectural and tactile precision.

## Interiority
Limited naming of feeling. What inner states exist will be communicated through action, through what the body does or does not do, through physical sensation. One character's thoughts or small moments of acknowledgment may appear, but restraint will dominate. The feeling will be there in what is *not* said.

## Attention
Will linger on the threshold itself — the suspended moment before entrance. Will attend to how two people position themselves relative to each other. Will notice what is behind the door (the state of the house, the light, the temperature). Will pass quickly over speech, if there is any. Will dwell on the physical fact of opening, of standing, of being seen.

## Silence
What will not be named: the full scope of what either person feels at the sight of the other. The word "love" will not appear. Explanations of motive will be absent or barely sketched. The reason for the knock will remain slightly obscure — we will see the effect, not the cause in detail.

## Signature
Syntax pattern: short sentences alternating with longer observational passages. Repetition of key verbs (stood, held, set, opened). Use of negation (did not, would not, could not) to mark restraint and refusal. The prose will track small adjustments in space and light the way previous Burns chapters have tracked his failing to land, the way Marge chapters have tracked the form on the counter. Present tense or simple past, grounded in physical fact.

## The Opening
The chapter will begin with Marge at her door — not after the knock, but in the moment before it or in the act of opening. The first lines will establish the house's state (the warmth, the light, what is on the counter, the photograph on the wall). Then the knock, or Marge's hand on the knob.

## The Close
The door will open. The two of them will be on opposite sides of the threshold. The chapter will end before or at the moment of full entry — not with reconciliation or explanation, but with the fact of the door opening and one person's presence becoming real to the other. The final image will be small and physical: a hand extended, a face readable by porch light, a temperature difference between inside and outside, the way light falls across a doorframe. The charge will be in the proximity, not in what is said or resolved.

## Confidence
**High** — resting on the established pattern of each character's chapter (the physical precision, the restrained interiority, the ending at thresholds rather than arrivals) and on the narrator's explicit statement that they are "not going to tell" who knocked, which suggests the next chapter will show the answer through the door itself rather than through narration.

**Medium-High** — on the specific details of how the opening unfolds, the exact temperature of the feeling that will be communicated, and whether Marge will be the one whose interiority we access or whether the chapter will remain fully external to both parties.
