# Ch 4 Prediction

## Duration
Moderate length, roughly equal to Ch 2 or Ch 3. The pattern establishes chapters at 500-700 words. Will dwell extensively on a single contained moment or space—a room, a drive, a domestic action—without rushing through.

## Register
Sustained quiet throughout. No dialogue. Flat declarative sentences with minimal adjective coloring except when light, temperature, or texture arrives—those precise physical details interrupt the flatness like stones in a stream. The voice stays austere and observational, never explanatory.

## Concreteness
Intensely physical. Will track objects, their positions, how hands move across them. Specific materials: slate, canvas, glass, whatever the chosen space holds. No abstraction unless it arrives through a concrete anchor (the way grief in Ch 1 arrives through a doorway).

## Interiority
Sparse naming of inner states. If anything is felt, it shows first in gesture—a hand held to a chest, a standing pose—then perhaps acknowledged sideways ("the way a person stacks a future"). Will not explain emotion. May show it receding, the way Ch 2 shows Marge's hands holding her up, or dwell on a moment where inner state is *not* allowed to surface.

## Attention
Lingers on: repetitive small actions (the kettle, the bots being set down and moved, objects wound and still). Passes quickly over: dialogue, decisions, why anyone wants what they want. The chapter will loop back to the same physical space or object, returning to it the way Burns returns to Mira's doorway "most nights."

## Silence
Will not say directly whether the character is content, suffering, resolved, or trapped. Will not resolve the tension between duty and desire that sits under every chapter. Will decline to explain the blank space where larger feeling would go—just leave the hand there, the envelope there, the object on its base.

## Signature
Short declarative clauses chained without much subordination. Sentences that move forward by addition, not explanation: "He set it down. He did it most nights. He did not let himself know." Repetition of a word or gesture across a paragraph creates rhythm. The voice performs a kind of emotional evacuation through syntax—leaving room where the feeling was.

## Predicted Opening
Will begin immediately after the previous chapter ends or at a threshold (a character arriving, leaving, or standing in a space they've just entered). No preamble. Likely "She" or "He" + action in past tense.

## What It Will Do
Follow a single character through a closed loop: arrival in a space, a series of small gestures or arrangements with objects, a moment where something almost breaks through (a named feeling, an almost-decision), then a return to the outward stillness. The chapter will probably end with an object held, a space entered, or a silence chosen. Will not resolve anything. Will deepen the pattern of characters who perform their roles well while something else remains unmailed, unpaid, unclaimed.

## Confidence
**Medium-High.** The structure repeats too regularly to be accidental: Ch 1 ends with light going down; Ch 2 ends with a form unmailed; Ch 3 ends with a hand on an object and the light turned off. Ch 4 will almost certainly follow this shape—a contained domestic moment, a small refusal or deferral at its core, an object or gesture that holds still at the end. The voice fingerprint (short clauses, material precision, refusal to name) is too consistent to shift. What I cannot predict: which character, which object, what small displacement or stillness will anchor it. The opening image could be anything—a doorway, a counter, a car seat, a base. But the architecture will feel like this.
