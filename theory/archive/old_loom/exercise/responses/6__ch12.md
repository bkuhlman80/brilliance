# Prediction: Chapter 13

## Expected Direction

Given the arc from Ch 1-12, the next installment will move from confession to presence. The speaker has committed to going to the address. Ch 13 will likely show the arrival and threshold moment—not the metaphor of the wall but the literal standing at a door.

## Predicted Attributes

**Duration**: Short to medium. The momentum is toward closure; ornament falls away. Expect a tighter, more compressed reflection than the sprawling earlier chapters—perhaps 1500-2500 words.

**Register**: Stripped down. The technical vocabulary and biological scaffolding that structured the earlier voice will recede. Shorter, declarative sentences. Less parenthetical qualification. The register will flatten toward something closer to plain speech—not plain, but stripped of the analytic apparatus. What remains is the body and the moment.

**Concreteness**: Return to immediate sensory detail. The address. Cold, likely—mountain, canyon, winter (the timeline implies this is written in cold weather). A doorstep. Hands. Breath. The language will be rooted in what can be seen or felt, not what can be inferred from a specimen.

**Interiority**: The speaker will name what they feel at the threshold—fear, hope, shame, doubt, want. No longer hidden behind biology. The inner state will be direct and, probably, contradictory: desire and dread held in the same moment. This is the one chapter where the speaker permits themselves to be seen as afraid.

**Attention**: Lingers on the moment of arrival and indecision. What happens at the step—the hand, the knock, the waiting. Will dwell on what is not said as much as what is. Attention to the face of the person who opens (or may open). May pass quickly over explanation or backstory; the interior geography of this threshold is the whole content.

**Silence**: Something crucial left unsaid. Either the conversation does not happen (the step is not taken, the door stays closed, the speaker turns away), or it begins and stops at the same place Ch 12 stopped—the unsayable verb. The silence will be the shape of what the whole account has been building toward: the distance that cannot be crossed, or the decision not to cross it, or the crossing of it and what lies on the other side (unknowable from inside the earlier chapters).

**Signature**: The characteristic compound structures with "and" will persist, but tighter. Fewer parenthetical asides. Repetition of a single word or phrase across the passage—likely a word the speaker tries and abandons, tries again. The forensic tone will remain but applied to immediate sensation rather than past analysis. *I did this. I did not do this. I stood there. I could not move.* The rhythm will be more staccato.

## What Will Happen

The chapter will likely open with location and body state upon arrival. Cold, probably; the speaker's hands shaking was mentioned. The address. A house, likely—the paper had an address, "in the small upright hand." The chapter will move toward the door, the moment before the knock (or the moment of standing without knocking). 

The speaker will probably encounter the other person—the woman who "risked the distance" by leaving the paper. Or will not encounter her; the door will stay closed. Or will open on someone else, something unexpected.

What will not happen: the speaker will not resolve the inner conflict or explain it away. The sentence that would close the arc—*I did this because I needed to* or *I turned back because I could not*—will not land. The chapter will either end at the threshold or step past it into radical uncertainty.

## Confidence: Medium

**Basis**: The pattern of increasing vulnerability and the structure of Ch 12, which announces intention but cannot complete it. The voice has moved consistently from observation toward confession toward action. The presence of the ellipsis—the sentence cut short—suggests the next chapter will either finally complete the thought or abandon it entirely. Either outcome fits the established pattern.

**Sources of Uncertainty**: The exact form of the threshold—whether the door opens, whether the conversation happens, whether it resolves or fails—cannot be predicted from the earlier chapters. The speaker might also surprise with a turn away, a decision not to go (the very last thing predicted by the momentum). The specific language the speaker will use at an actual threshold is also unpredictable; the metaphor apparatus has been the voice's native language.

**What Could Be Wrong**: The chapter might not be arrival at all but continuation of the reflection on why the speaker is afraid, which would extend the threshold indefinitely. Or it might jump forward to aftermath, skipping the moment itself. Or the tone might shift entirely—irony, detachment, humor—if the speaker uses those as a defense at the actual precipice.
