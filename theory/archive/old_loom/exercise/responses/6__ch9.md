# Prediction: Ch 10

## Duration and Pacing
The next chapter will be substantial — approximately the length of Ch 9 (1,300-1,500 words). It will dwell at length on one central figure or concept, using the accretive method of Ch 1 and Ch 3 (building from instance to instance, each one deepening the same principle). The pacing will be deliberate, with long sentences that unfold their logic rather than snap it closed.

## Register
The voice will remain expansive and colored, with the particular syntax that marks this thread: nested clauses that hold multiple ideas in suspension, semicolons as breath-points, the occasional fragment standing alone. The emotional register will be cooler than Ch 7 but warmer than Ch 1 — present without being effusive. The diction will be precise and technical (words like *flora*, *fauna*, *parasite* appearing in their strict biological sense) mixed with deliberate plainness (*it happens*, *nobody chose*, *a thing that could die*).

## Concreteness
The chapter will work at the level of physical instance and biological mechanism. It will likely begin with or contain a specific, observable phenomenon — a behavior, a body part, an evolutionary fact — before moving to what that phenomenon carries in meaning. No abstractions will float ungrounded. The concrete will move slowly into the conceptual, but the concrete will stay in the room the whole time.

## Interiority
Inner states will NOT be named. There will be no "I felt" or "I realized." Instead, the mechanism will be shown on the outside, and the reader will be left to infer what it costs or means internally. This is consistent with Ch 5 (the scallop, the old engine surfacing), Ch 6 (the cave fish taking apart its own eye), and the earlier reflections. The "I" of the narrator may appear, but only as a witness, an observer noting what the body is doing, not analyzing what it means to it.

## Attention
The chapter will linger on a threshold or a refusal — something the thread keeps circling (the unreachable inside, what waits on the other side of a crossing, what gets kept back). It will likely attend to a moment of holding-still, or of not-doing, rather than action. The pattern suggests Ch 10 will show something that persists in its position without moving toward or away. It will pass quickly over anything that happens in haste.

## Silence
The chapter will decline to explain why. It will show a body or a system doing a thing that costs or risks something, and it will not offer the reason the thing is done anyway. The reader is left in the silence where the reason would be — the place where the narrator has consistently refused to fill in the answer. Given Ch 9's ending (cut off mid-sentence, mid-thought, at the moment of saying what the ground "goes unsaid"), Ch 10 will likely continue that refusal, or it will come back to it after a turn.

## Signature
- Sentences will often carry multiple layers held in parallel syntax: "The X does Y, and the Z does W, and both of them are..." 
- Frequent use of doubled or tripled phrases for emphasis and rhythm: "slow and rooted," "fast and hungry," "the quick bright one"
- A tendency to name what a thing is NOT before saying what it IS: "not a wall that happened to be there," "not hidden, not shy"
- The voice will use a tool to hold multiple contradictions in view at once — often a metaphor or a biological analogy that makes both poles visible
- Periods that land with finality, even when the idea underneath is uncertain

## Predicted Opening
The chapter will likely begin with an observable act or state — probably something small, something repeated, something that seems to have no purpose until you look at it long enough. It may begin with "A..." or with an imperative: "Watch...", "Look..." It will not begin with abstraction.

## Predicted Content Direction
Given the arc of the thread and the cut-off ending of Ch 9, Ch 10 will probably return to the question of what gets kept out of the account. It may revisit the figure of the "somebody" (mentioned in Ch 9 as what a character is called after three years of argument). It may show a body or a system that is rooted, slow, the ground that others move across, and it will show this without judgment or complaint. It will likely demonstrate this through a biological or behavioral fact — not through the narrator's feeling about it.

## Predicted Ending
The chapter will close on a held position, a refusal, or a threshold not crossed. It will not resolve. It may end mid-gesture, the way Ch 9 did, or it may end on a sentence that lands but leaves something unfinished in the reader's mind. The final image or statement will return to the body, the ground, the thing that does not move while other things move across it.
