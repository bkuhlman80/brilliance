### The register problem and its fix

The chapter as drafted leans too hard on settled answers. Specifically: Lisa's Trellis-block architectural segment lands a fully-formed layered-architecture commitment, an FSM-as-stored-controller declaration, Ashby's requisite-variety theorem, the alley failure case, and the memory board as a four-slot whiteboard sketch — all delivered in eighteen minutes by one labmate to a listening team. That's too much closed-loop for one meeting. It makes the chapter a discovery chapter rather than a *quarter of work* chapter.

The fix is structural and important.

**Operating principle: the farm and the bramble.** Each retrospective has a job. Present the new Bramble. Walk through the cell. Install the new knob. Leave Brandy a record of work done. That is the farm. The team does it competently within their nicknames-shaped roles. Most of every meeting is the farm. *Some* of every meeting is the bramble of inquiry — moments where the lab is figuring out what is going to take three years to figure out. The reader feels the chapter has weight from the farm work rendered with care; the chapter has stakes from the bramble poking through where it pokes through.

The discipline is to keep them distinct. Don't render farm work as bramble (makes the team look chaotic and the chapter feel like it accomplished nothing). Don't render bramble as farm work (collapses the figuring-it-out into delivery and makes the book about epiphanies it doesn't have).

In Ch 2 specifically, the four capacities, the four tendencies, the four motivations — these are mostly farm work. Render competently. Don't dramatize. The category-noun-for-couplings, the FSM-knowing question, the twelve-knob worry, the architectural-shape-of-coordination question — these are bramble. Slow down here. Let things stay open.

**Operating principle: non-linear correction is reserved.** The team — including Lisa — can publicly land something in one quarter and walk it back in a later quarter. The criterion is whether the *wrong-then-right* arc is more interesting than the *right-the-first-time* arc would have been. Default to getting it right when getting it right is the truer thing. Reserve the non-linear arc for the moves where the wrong-walked-back has structural meaning.

For Ch 2: Lisa proposing the layered-architecture commitment as a *partial framing she is working on*, with epistemic-register flags throughout, is the right shape. We don't need her to walk it back later if she's careful about how she lands it now. But if a specific load-bearing piece *does* get said wrong here in service of being walked back at Ch 3 or Ch 5, that move is available — it just needs to earn its place.

**The lunch's actual substance — what would Steinbeck do.**

Two specific people sharing food in September, the patio at Fisher's, the trucks doing their thing, the cyclists coming off the protected lane on 9th South, the Wasatch foothills holding the September light from the brewery side, the pre-Olympics construction starting to reshape downtown, Westminster's enrollment trouble eating Ben's department. Neither of them has it figured out. The reader watches them figure-toward-it for two hours and leaves the lunch with both of them more legible than they were going in, without anything having been *concluded*.

### Poetic

**Title concept:** something Yong-flavored. *Virtuosos of Biochemistry,* or *Lords of Decay,* or just an opening line that lands the register. Future-Claude picks. (The current "Personality as Biographical Signature" title is wrong — too essayistic, signals the wrong register.)

**Candidate organisms (future-Claude picks four or five):**

**Discipline: do NOT close on Bramble.** The poetic's job is to widen the gaze. Bramble does not appear by name. The reader connects the poetic to Bramble's situation or doesn't. The closing should be one of the organisms, or a Brandy-flavored observation about what bacterial personality looks like when one stops trying to categorize it, or a small moment of bemused awe. It should not be a "and so this is what we are building" beat. Do not return the poetic to the framework. The framework is what the lab is doing; the poetic is what Brandy is thinking. **Discipline: do NOT catalog.** Each portrait is a small scene with the organism in the room, not a bullet-pointed entry on a list. Brandy is *with* the organism, observing it, sometimes addressing it the way a naturalist addresses a specimen they have been looking at for a long time. Show, then loft, then drop — Steinbeck's render-loft-drop applies to the poetics too. Render the organism concretely. Lift briefly into observation. Drop back into the next organism. The lifting is unannounced. The reader picks it up or doesn't.

**The bacteria-vs-thermostat question:** can sit in the opening 100 words as the question that organizes the tour. Do not answer it abstractly. Let the portraits be the answer. The thermostat falls away by the second example; the reader feels the difference without needing the category-theory cash-out.

### Vignette

**Anchor fix from the current draft.** The current draft has a problem: *"The four protocells sat on the heated floor in the same places they had been since April."* This is wrong — the household has moved them, played with them, picked them up. The lived account is not static. The fix: *To Lisa it seemed they were where they had been since April.* From Lisa's lab-van morning vantage in the kitchen doorway, the floor looks static. From the household's lived experience, things have moved more than Lisa registers. This is a small Lisa-character beat (Lisa sees the system, not the four-year-old's daily relationship to its components) and a small Marge-character beat too (Marge, composing the vignette from Jasmine's account and the video, knows better and lets Lisa's misreading sit as the vignette's opening register-tell).

**Physics fix from the current draft.** The protocells COOL the floor under themselves. They have heat-transducers that pull heat from the floor and disperse it into the air. They do not sit on warm spots; they *make their spots cool*. This breaks the current draft's bumper-car premise entirely. Bramble cannot bump protocells out of "warm spots" because the protocells' own spots are not warm.

**Corrected bumper-car geometry.**

- *Heat mode.* The contested resource is the floor that has *not* been cooled. Bramble is on heat. The protocells, sitting on their own cooled patches, mark the spots they have been in. Bramble navigates toward warm floor and *avoids* the cool patches its siblings have produced. The protocells, sitting on cooled patches, are unmoved by Bramble's avoidance — they don't care. The comedy is the asymmetry: one unit aggressively pursuing what the others have unintentionally made scarce, the others completely unaware they have made anything scarce. Quiet comedy. Underplayed. Brief.
- *Light mode.* The contested resource is the sunbeam. Bramble pursues. The protocells — sometimes in the path of the sunbeam because the household has carried or set them there over weeks — are sometimes in Bramble's way. When a protocell is in Bramble's path, Bramble runs into it. The chassis does not stop. Bramble pushes through. Not gentle. Not aggressive. *Indifferent.* The protocell slides, ends up out of the beam, sits there twitching, and is now a small mechanical thing with a microscopically-different floor temperature than it had thirty seconds ago. Bramble parks in the sun.

**Steinbeck spirit.** The light-mode contest is the comic engine. The frog-hunt-equivalent here: five small things doing their three rules on a kitchen floor, occasionally colliding, the household watching, the household interpreting, the comedy in the gap between what is mechanically happening and what the household is narrating to itself. Render it with bemused observational warmth. Bramble pursuing sunbeams with single-minded mechanical determination. Protocells skittering away from a unit that does not know they are there. Mira refereeing. Jasmine narrating. Ben at dinner explaining patiently. Render the funny stuff; do not punchline.

All three reads are valid. The system doesn't care. The comedy is in the gap between the readings. The narrator holds all three without correcting any of them.

**The "Are they sad" exchange.** Keep it. Survives intact. But place it earlier, not as the vignette's close. Currently the dinner-table exchange ends the vignette — the sadness is the closing note. Better: the vignette closes on a small image *after* the question, that does not comment. Options: Mira at bedtime asking Jasmine something else entirely about Bramble; a wide shot of the kitchen at night with the protocells humming in their corners and Bramble in the last sunbeam's place clicking; Jasmine writing the question down for Marge's record. The discipline is to let the comedy land first, then the question, then a small unrelated final image. The vignette ends without the household having figured out whether Bramble is a bully or oblivious. The figuring-out is what the lab is for.

**The clicking.** Carry forward as the household's way of knowing what Bramble is doing across rooms. Jasmine recognizes the cadence. Mira talks to Bramble through it. Establish this in Q2; it compounds across the genetic line.

**Length target:** ~1,000–1,200 words. Comic register; observational warmth; multiple voices in the household reading the same scene differently; closes on a small image not a punchline and not a thesis.

### Final note for future-Claude

The interlude is the FIRST in a long series of interludes. The lunch is the first in Brandy's eleven-lunch series. The poetic is the second in Brandy's twelve-poetic series. The vignette is the second of Marge's twelve household records. The shapes you land here become the templates the rest of the book inherits. Take care with the registers; they will set up everything that follows.

A reader meeting Brandy at this lunch is meeting her in scene for the first time since the Pitch. They are forming a picture of who she is at lunches. The picture they form here is the picture they will carry into ten more lunches. Build it carefully.
