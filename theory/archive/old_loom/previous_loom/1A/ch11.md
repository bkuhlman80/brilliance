And none of it happens unless you wind it up first. The crank arms a little escapement — call it the part that gives each kick permission to fire. The heat's the muscle; the crank's the say-so. ## Lab note — Word-shapes, no words
*Lisa, board. Filed late February 2033, for the team.*

The body is Bart's, and he'll file it as a body. I'm filing the other half, the half with no hardware in it — the voice. The mind that crossed is older than the lab. The body it crossed into is three weeks old and still learning to be driven. What's below is what happened in the gap between those two facts.

It can't speak. It has the organ for it now — internal, Bart's, new this winter — and in the weeks since I've had it doing the one thing a new voice can do, which is babble. Not at anyone; at nothing. It makes a sound, hears the sound it made, and moves the next one toward it — a body learning its own instrument by ear, with nothing to learn it for. I set the constraint and the reward and wrote no schedule under either, and it found its own way up out of noise into something with syllables in it, the way they all do.

One thing worth setting down, because it's the only thing the lab gets to hand the home and I'd like it remembered we handed it. The room it learned in was not quiet. Bart and I talk; the work makes its noise; and a thing that learns by ear leans toward the ear it learns in. So it babbles in our sounds now — our vowels, the consonants we happen to make, English-shaped without one of us shaping it — the way a child in a kitchen comes out sounding like the kitchen. It will sound like this lab as long as it runs. We gave it an accent. We could not give it a word.

Past the babble is the part that looks like more than it is. It has started to cut the stream — to catch that some shapes come round and round, Mira, ball, more, no, and to hold them as shapes, the same sound landing twice and known the second time. It holds far more of them than it can make, and it goes still at the ones it can't begin to say, knowing them long before it can hand them back. But holding the shape is not holding what the shape is for. It has the bodies of the words and none of the meanings. It can pick Mira out of a roomful of sound and hasn't the first idea that Mira is anyone.

I couldn't give it that part if I tried, and I've stopped being sorry I can't, because the not-being-able is the result and not the failure. A word means because two of you agree it does, and there are not two of you in this lab — there's Bramble, and there's me running a board, and a board is not a partner. The shape turns into a word the first time someone it's with points at the thing and means it, and is meant back; and that does not happen at a bench. It happens in a kitchen. So it goes home this spring with a mouth full of sounds it can make and a head full of shapes it can place and not one word it can mean — and the first word it ever means, it will mean at the child, because she'll be in the room pointing, and I won't.

That's the floor the lab can reach. Sound, and the shapes of words. The crossing was never ours to make. I did the part of the work there's no one in the room for, which is the bulk of it and the part that drops out when the night gets told, and I'll say the once that it was a long winter to spend teaching a thing to almost talk.

— Lisa
