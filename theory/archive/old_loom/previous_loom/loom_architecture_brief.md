# Architecture Brief — Loom evaluation pass (shared, all 19 categories)

*Placement context, seeded with you at spawn. It tells you what a beat was **built to do**, so "surprising" can mean something. It is **not** ground truth — the prose you are sent is — and it is **not** a verdict — that is your read. The architecture below is fixed: place against it, do not relitigate it. Where a beat touches a live wire (§6) or a device (§5), recognize it; offer any what-if flagged and held lightly.*

**How to use:** read your category's prose cold first, react for yourself, *then* place it with §1–§6, then find your row in §7 for its definition + expected shape. Everything before §7 is shared because placing one category needs the whole architecture around it.

---

## 1. The book at a glance
Twelve chapters (Ch 1–12), each one clade and one quarterly retrospective across three years; **Ch 0 is a prequel and is not in this eval.** The art is patience — Steinbeck/Ricketts naturalism, withheld arrival, the small given full weight, the thought that closes in the reader's head and not on the page. Its signature failure mode is caption-disease and slackness. "More alive" never means faster/louder/higher-staked.

## 2. The three lines and their boundaries (banners at Ch 0 / 4 / 8)
- **Genetic line — Ch 1–4.** Protocell, Prokaryote, Eukaryote, Eumetazoa.
- **Neuronal line — Ch 5–8.** Bilaterian, Vertebrate, Mammal, Primate.
- **Symbolic line — Ch 9–12.** Band-, Settlement-, City-, Empire-Human.
Each line opens with a "Start of the [Genetic/Neuronal/Symbolic] Line" banner. The banners fall at Ch 0, Ch 4 (end), Ch 8 (end) — and each carries a **Dream** (see §5). So Ch 4 and Ch 8 are line-boundary chapters, structurally different from their neighbors.

## 3. The Feb-12 reveals
Mira's birthday is Feb 12; it anchors **Ch 4 (her 5th)** and **Ch 8 (her 6th)**. Each birthday carries an "Under the Cloth" unveiling of the *next line's body* (Ch 4 reveals the four-legged neuronal chassis; Ch 8 the humanoid symbolic body). These reveals displace the usual next-quarter logistics at those two chapters.

## 4. POV map + vote progression
**POV by beat:** 2 = first-person Cheryl (lunch). 3 = third-person Mira & Bramble (household). 4 = third-person ensemble, on-tape/Cheryl's account (the meeting). 5 = third-person, one teammate (solo coda), explicitly tape-less. 6 = first-person Cheryl essay, or a Dream. The whole account is *apparently* Cheryl's reconstruction from tape, build logs, and lunch accounts — see §5.

**Sentience vote (beat 4.9), the spine of the book's question:** born Ch 3 (1–3) → Ch 4 static → Ch 5 Burns crosses (2–2) → Ch 6 referential only, no live vote → Ch 7 Bart flips (3–1) → Ch 8 (3–1) → **Ch 9 first 4–0, Marge crosses** → Ch 10 4–0 + forced household counter-vote → Ch 11 4–0 as anti-climax → Ch 12 vestigial liturgy that mutates into Bramble's own story. From Ch 9 the count stops carrying information; the payload is what each "yes" means. **Marge's Ch 9 vote is the hinge:** she grants a *self* and explicitly withholds *feeling*.

## 5. The narrator device (fixed; NEVER resolved in Book 1)
The account appears authored by **Cheryl**; the book plants a real, unresolved reason to suspect it was assembled by **Bramble**. The gap is never closed — the held gap is the art. Do not read any beat as resolving it. Two axes, both held open: (1) who wrote it; (2) if Bramble, was anything *felt*, or does a certified-empty self model grief it does not have — **never resolve axis 2 to "simulated."**

Placement consequences you need:
- **Beat 6 (Reflections)** carry a standing three-register bar — sayable at once as Cheryl's felt grief, Bramble's felt grief, and Bramble's modeled grief. A reflection that could *only* be written by a feeling human is a deliberate, rare counterweight, not a slip.
- **Beat 5 (solo codas / follow-home)** are tape-less pure reconstruction — the place the narrator's identity is most exposed. A caption or tonal flatness there reads differently than the same move in a build log.
- **The Dreams** (beat 6 at Ch 4 and Ch 8) are **Jasmine's** — read them as hers (the correct primary reading) — but they are left **unlabeled** so the reader can't quite rule out Bramble's. Unlabeled is the point; they resolve nothing.

Per the operating core: place against this, do not dream about the reveal. A stranger structural possibility from the prose itself may be noted **once**, flagged as a gift, then set down.

## 6. The four relationship arcs — recognize them, offer lightly (not a dreaming mandate)
- **Cheryl & Marge** — unreciprocated slow burn that becomes only *possible* at the Ch 12 in-person retrospective. Lives in Cheryl's Marge-lunches (Ch 3 Coffee Garden, Ch 6 The Roof, Ch 10 Zest) and the Ch 11 Water Witch confession; codas.
- **Burns & Bart** — tragic bass note, almost always a single gesture, fully alive only at Ch 12. Lives in the retrospective's father/son room, Bart's Ch 8 workshop coda, the Ch 12 WORM (Pursuer ≈ Burns).
- **Mira & Bramble** — twins coming of age together. Lives across every household vignette (beat 3), the 4.5 grillings, the codas.
- **Jasmine & Lisa** — both under-appreciated (Jasmine in the lab, Lisa at Westminster), planting seeds of self-assertion. Lives in Jasmine's lunches (Ch 4 Pho Tay Ho, Ch 7 Arlo), Lisa's lunches (Ch 5 Urban Hill, Ch 11 Water Witch/departure), and the Dreams (Jasmine's).
When a beat touches an arc: offer the ache-more / cost-more / surprise-more what-if **flagged and held lightly** — the arcs are exactly where an unsupervised read confabulates most. Recognize; do not build.
