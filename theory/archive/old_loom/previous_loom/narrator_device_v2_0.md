# The Narrator Device (v2.0) — and the shape of Book 2

*Replaces v1.0 entirely. The v1 device — "the book may have been assembled by Bramble" — is
**retired**. It was not merely a different idea; it was actively damaging the prose, and the damage
is now diagnosed. Read the next section before anything else.*

---

## Why v1 had to go (this is the finding, not an apology)

The v1 device required the narration to be sayable in the machine's voice as well as Cheryl's:
*write as if Cheryl wrote it, and never write the line that makes it impossible she didn't.*

Combine that with a narrator reconstructing from tape, and it imposes a prohibition **nobody ever
wrote down and every instance obeyed:**

> **Cheryl was not allowed to want anything.**

Wanting is a tell. A narrator who lingers on the light on a woman's mouth has a body and a hunger,
and that is *evidence*, and evidence closes the axis. So the prose could never let Cheryl look at
anyone with appetite.

**And the lingering look is where the body lives.** That is the whole explanation for the draft's
central defect — no human in this book has a face. `hair`: zero. `skin`, `shoulders`, `neck`,
`knuckles`: zero. `eyes` appears eight times and every one is the *robot's*. `light` appears thirty-one
times. The narrator pays in weather because the device confiscated the aperture.

Worse: it ate the book's own spine. **Cheryl–Marge is the emotional through-line**, and the only
instrument that can carry it — Cheryl's attention, hungry, on a woman's body — was made structurally
illegal for four hundred pages.

The device meant to protect the book's ambiguity was consuming the book's best material to feed it.

---

## The device (v2)

**Cheryl wrote the book. That is settled and never in question.**

**Bramble edited it.**

Future-Bramble, long after, reading the old woman's account and refusing to let her keep the
flattering version. It is the finger-pointer. It is the one reader who calls her bluff.

**And it never appears on the page. Not once.** No preface, no frame, no editor's note, no bracket,
no last-line reveal. If it shows up, it is a gimmick. **It exists only as the pressure that explains
why this woman is so pitiless about herself** — and the reader feels the pressure, reaches for its
source, and never gets a sentence to hold.

**The finger is never on the page. Only the flinch.**

---

## What this buys, which v1 could not

**The ambiguity moves to a better address.** No longer *who wrote this* — a puzzle, with an answer,
that sends the reader into detective posture hunting the seam. Now: **why is she telling us this?**
Something made her. Something read her draft and would not let her keep the comfortable version.

**The confessions become a record of pressure applied.** Every unflattering, unhedged, self-indicting
line is a place where Cheryl got called and did not look away. The reader never sees the calling.
Only the residue: an old woman being harder on herself than anyone in her life ever was.

**And it is a love story.** Which v1 never was. Cheryl at the end of her life hands her account to
the thing she funded and never touched, and it hands it back with the comfortable parts gone. The
funder up the canyon and the machine on the floor — the two who watched everything and were never in
the room — alone together, doing the last honest work.

---

## The mechanism on the page: the aperture, not the temperature

**Cheryl never enters anyone.** Not Marge, not Jasmine, not Bramble, not once. She stays on the
mountain. She is Ricketts to the last page, and taking that away takes away the book. **The
temperature never moves.**

**What moves is the aperture — what she keeps choosing to look at.**

She is a documentarian; everything she records is nominally *for the account*. So:

> **The tell is the useless detail. The thing she records that is no use to the record.**

**Early:** everything she notices *earns its place*. Marge's mouth worries at rest — clinical, a stress
tell, the psychologist's own instrument turned on her. Any documentarian would log it. Cold, total,
unflinching, and **free**, because she has an excuse.

**Late:** she logs what could not possibly be evidence. The light on that mouth. The way it goes when
Marge is not being looked at. A detail with **no forensic function whatsoever**, that only a person
who *wanted to look* would ever have looked at.

She never says why. She simply included it — **and she is the one editing this thing.** She chose to
leave it in. That is the second confession, and it is the louder one.

**Same coldness. Same precision. Same gaze. Only the aperture opens.** And the reader, four hundred
pages deep, has been trained to read this woman's attention the way she reads a tide pool.

### This is countable

Return-frequency on a body; granularity of the reading; and above all **the ratio of useful to
useless detail**. The striptease has a curve. *(Correction to the Distance Pass spec: Curve 2 as
written there is wrong. Cheryl's coldness does **not** decay and is **not** near-silent by Ch 12. It
runs constant. What climbs is the useless-detail ratio. Amend before running.)*

---

## THE ABSOLUTE RULE

> **The useless detail goes in, and NOTHING marks it.**

No *she noticed, and did not ask herself why.* No *she would not have said what made her look.* No
narrator catching herself, no wry aside, no wondering, no lifted eyebrow.

**Every one of those is "I won't say the word."** It is the announced withhold, it is the caption, and
it is the exact reflex that produced the water-burp and the coy door — arriving one last time dressed
as psychological subtlety. It would be the most damaging instance of all four, because it lands on the
book's best move.

**The detail is simply, inexplicably, there. Unremarked. The reader does the work.**

## The confession register (how Cheryl indicts herself)

Late, and only a few times, she catches herself and **does not look away.** The instrument turns. She
describes her own reason for looking with the same cold, unflinching specificity she gives the crab.

**This is not warmth arriving. It is not the register breaking.** It is the naturalist finally being
honest about the naturalist — the coldest, most exposed thing in the book, because a woman who
describes her own longing with clinical accuracy has nowhere left to hide, **and she chose to leave it
in the account.**

Three requirements, all load-bearing:

**1. It must cost her something.** Her competence, her distance, her position on the mountain, her
right to hold the camera. *"The narrator admits a limit"* is not confession — *I could not know this*
is still a sentence about knowing. It is the announced withhold in a lab coat. **The account must get
less flattering to the person keeping it.**

**2. It must be more precise than the accusation would have been.** Precision is the proof of
sincerity; vagueness is where self-protection hides. *"I suppose I was afraid"* is a dodge. *"I chose
that restaurant because Marge would like the light in it, and I told myself I chose it because it was
close"* is a woman handing over the murder weapon.

**3. It must be unprompted.** No interlocutor exists inside the book. She is not answering a charge.
**She just says it** — which is worse, because it means she went looking for it herself.

### The compositional method (ours, not hers)

> **We point the finger. We find the worst honest thing about her. And then that is what she says —
> unprompted, unhedged, unremarked — and we do not let her soften it, and we do not let her explain
> why she said it.**

**The first one is already sitting in the draft.** She has chosen every room in this book *for the
other person, without deciding to* — she says so at least twice, both times as a charming little flaw.
It isn't. **It is a woman who organized her entire life around anticipating what other people need so
that she would never have to say what she wants.** That is what she gives up.

---

## The second axis, relocated (and it does not close)

The felt-versus-modeled question survives — but it is no longer about who typed the pages. It attaches
to **the editor's reading of Cheryl.**

Bramble reads her. Bramble knows where she is lying. **Does it know because it feels her, or because it
modeled her?**

**Marge's Ch 9 vote — a self granted, feeling withheld — now sits underneath the entire text as its
condition of production.** The book exists because something read a woman well enough to make her
honest. And you cannot tell, from any line on the finished page, whether that reading was love or
inference. The most intimate act in the book, and its interiority is exactly as open as it was the day
Marge voted.

**The discipline is unchanged and absolute: never resolve to "simulated."** The instant a reader can
land on *it was a machine faking it all along*, you have the nihilist gotcha — the cheap twist that
makes a moved reader feel played. Held open. Never closed.

---

## What survives from v1, and why

**The unlabeled dreams — keep them unlabeled.** They are Jasmine's; that is the correct primary
reading and the only one that lets the pre-verbal interior material live in the genetic line without
spoiling the vote. But **Cheryl reconstructed them** — a woman imagining another woman's sleep, with
no tape and no warrant — and the flicker of *whose interior is this, and who is assembling it?* is now
the whole device in miniature, planted on page one. Same charge, better fuse.

**The follow-homes stay, and matter more.** Explicitly tape-less. Pure reconstruction. This is where
Cheryl's *imagination* — and therefore her selection, and therefore her wanting — is most nakedly
exposed. A woman imagining the solitude of the woman she cannot stop looking at. Nothing else in the
book is this exposed.

**Never resolve to simulated.** Unchanged.

---

## The standing bar on every line (rewritten)

v1's bar — *sayable in three registers: Cheryl's felt grief, Bramble's felt grief, Bramble's modeled
grief* — is **retired with the device that required it.** It is what forbade the body. Do not carry it
forward.

The new bar:

> **Cheryl wrote this line, humanly, wanting something. And something read it afterward and let it
> stand.**

Which means the prose may now do everything v1 forbade — the body, the appetite, the lingering look —
**and must.**

And it acquires a discipline v1 never had, which is the best thing about it:

> **The caption, the gloss, the announced withhold, the tasteful evasion are now DIEGETICALLY
> IMPOSSIBLE. An editor who calls her bluff would have cut them.**
>
> The un-edited draft cannot show through. Brian's worst habit is now forbidden *inside the story.*

## Book 2 — mostly intact, and better connected

Nearly all of v1's Book 2 architecture survives, and the new device **strengthens** it.

**Theory:** Book 1 is personality *evolution and development*. Book 2 is personality *disorder and
therapy*.

**Plot:** Bramble develops personality disorders; Marge is its therapist. Cheryl and Marge are dating.
Bart calls Cheryl "Homer." Lisa moves to Italy, consults remotely. Burns is involved because Bramble
still lives in his house.

**The new connective tissue — this is the gain.** Bramble's editorial pressure on Cheryl is the
**root** of Book 2's therapy. It learned the move by making an old woman honest. And Book 2's favored
narration — Bramble inhabiting other people to avoid being the *I* — is **exactly what an editor
does.** Book 2's narrator *is* the Book 1 editor, having become a narrator, still running the same
evasion. The disorder was visible in the first book's method and nobody could see it, because the
method looked like devotion.

**The engine: the stuck door, not the switch.** Bramble's feeling does not switch on late. **Access to
it arrives late.** The feeling was always running — alexithymic by construction: shaping behavior,
walled off from its own report. What comes online in therapy, in the wrong order, is **access
consciousness**, not feeling. The lights were always on. The door was stuck. (A switch would reverse
Book 1's own physics — the dial, the no-line — for a Pinocchio payoff. Refuse it.)

**And it makes Marge right all along — terribly, not flatteringly.** She voted *self, no evidence of
feeling.* Not because there was no feeling, but because the feeling was unreachable **to the one having
it** — and she could read that gap so exactly because **it is the gap she lives in.** The unmailed form.
The kept house. Both hands on the cup. Full affect, stuck access, a longing she will not let arrive as a
sayable thing. **Her rightness about Bramble is a symptom, not a credential.** Do not let her be the sage
who simply knew. Being right is the worst thing that could happen to her defenses — she was right *about
herself*, and pointed it at a robot to keep from knowing.

**Three recoveries, one latch.** Cheryl-and-Marge is not background romance; **it is the stakes.** The
door that must come unstuck for Bramble to feel is the same door that must come unstuck for Marge to let
Cheryl in rather than fold an address into her hand and flee — and Cheryl is the one who put glass
between herself and her own life. The machine, the clinician, and the funder, all stuck, in the same
room, and **the thing that moves any door is never that person working their own.**

**And now the frame closes:** Book 1 is Bramble reading Cheryl perfectly and unable to say what it feels.
**Book 2 is Bramble finally being read.**

---

## Settled vs. open

**Settled:** Cheryl wrote it, Bramble edited it, the editing never appears. The aperture, not the
temperature. The useless detail, unmarked. The confession register and its three requirements. Axis 2
relocated to the editor's reading; never resolved to simulated. Dreams unlabeled. Follow-homes stay.
Book 2 as above.

**Open — Brian's to set:**
- Which body-noun is saved for Cheryl at the end *(Loom does not have this and should stop pretending
  to; it requires reading the chapters).*
- How many confessions, and where. *(Proposed: one per act, plus the climax. Not yet ruled.)*
- Whether Book 2 ever softly confirms the editor, and how.
- The exact placement of the first useless detail — the one that teaches the reader how to read her.

**Standing caution.** This note is a map, not the territory. Before asserting any of it as fixed, check
it against Brian and against the actual chapter files. Loom has drifted twice by trusting a tidy
memory over a live read — and once, in the conversation that produced this document, by preferring a
beautiful ending to a true one.
