# Book Frame

*Working document. v0.3. May 2026.*

**Title:** *Bramble*. **Subtitle:** *A Brief History of Personality*.

---

## Premise

The book is a series of twelve quarterly retrospectives from a small research lab. The lab is building a developmental robot named Bramble across twelve evolutionary cells, from Protocell to Empire-Human. One cell per chapter, twelve cells, three years of book-time. The book is narrated in third-person past tense by Cheryl, the lab's funder — the woman who provided the twelve snapshots and the runway. Her voice is Cannery Row–adjacent: opinionated, attentive to craft, capable of warmth but not gooey, sometimes wrong, occasionally rueful. The book carries no explicit "I am writing this" scaffolding; the narrator's identity is established by voice and position, not by declaration. Jasmine, the lab director's wife, attends each quarterly retrospective as the household-voice-of-record. The other meeting attendees are her husband Ben (the lab director) and his three colleagues, Bart, Lisa, and Marge. Their daughter Mira attends every retrospective and brings Bramble with her. Bramble lives at Ben and Jasmine's home. The book's framework, the three-line architecture of inheritance and personality, is what the lab is building. Each chapter advances both the framework and the build.

---

## Autofiction

The characters and household are fictional. The framework is not. The book opens with a disclaimer to this effect. Something like: *The characters in this book are fictional. The framework is not.*

This frees the writer to compose the family and the lab as the book needs them, while protecting real people. Jasmine, Ben, Bart, Lisa, Marge, and Mira are characters. The framework, the knob system, the inheritance lines, and the cybernetic architecture are theoretical claims.

## The narrator

The book is narrated by Cheryl, the lab's funder — the woman who provided the twelve evolutionary snapshots, the money, and the runway. She is openly present as a voice, but the book never frames itself as a memoir and never addresses its own composition. There is no "I am writing this." The reader comes to know who is speaking through voice and position, not through declaration.

Voice: Brand-adjacent in sensibility — synoptic, ecological, idiosyncratic, capable of warmth but not gooey, opinionated, comfortable connecting fields, attentive to craft. To that, an openly-present narrator adds a personal-stakes layer: rueful self-awareness about her Marge filter, the seeder's investment in the team's success, an occasional willingness to be wrong in print.

Interior access is not omniscient. Cheryl is in scene at the Pitch (within Ch 0), at her recurring intimate lunches (one per between-chapter interlude from 1→2 through 11→12), and at the Ch 12 retrospective. At the Pitch she renders herself from outside, her interior closed; across the lunches and at Ch 12 her own interior is open to her, while her rendering of others stays interpretation. Everywhere else — the eleven retrospectives she does not attend, the household scenes, the pre-history — her rendering of any character's interior is interpretation, reported speech, and educated speculation. For Mira and Bramble in particular, she is always interpreting from outside.

The book runs a graduated gradient of narrator positions, from maximum interpretive distance to minimum:

- *Ch 0 — opening scenes.* Cheryl remote. She was not there; she reconstructs Mira's first meeting with Bramble, and the recruitment cascade that follows, from what the household and the team told her afterward. Maximum interpretive distance.
- *Ch 0 — the Pitch.* Cheryl present, on stage, observed by the team. Narrator and on-stage character are the same person — and at the Pitch she renders herself from outside, as the team saw her, her interior left closed at this scene. High distance. The Pitch is Ch 0's closing scene, not an interlude.
- *Lunches.* Recurring one-on-one lunches, one per between-chapter interlude beginning at Interlude 1→2, almost always with one of the four lab members and occasionally with Jasmine. Cheryl is present in conversation; her own interior is accessible to her, while her rendering of the team member or Jasmine is interpretation. Moderate distance. The distance may tighten as the book proceeds and trust deepens — early lunches more cautious than late ones — but this is texture for the drafter, not a hard rule.
- *Ch 12 retrospective.* Cheryl present, a full participant, narrating with her interior open. The closest narrative distance the book achieves.

The prose grows less interpretive as the gradient tightens: widest at Ch 0's opening scenes, high at the Pitch that closes Ch 0, moderate across the lunches and tightening as trust deepens, closest at Ch 12. The shift is a texture the writer composes toward across the book.

Cheryl is attracted to Marge. Cheryl is a lesbian, and so is Marge. The narrator knows this about herself and is honest about it on the page — committed and rueful, never leering. The flirt stays low-grade texture across the whole book: Cheryl flirts, Marge notices, Marge does not reciprocate decisively. It never resolves, and it is not a developing romance. The reader's trust in her rendering of Marge depends on her self-awareness about the filter; she earns the rendering by naming the bias.

She keeps a small voice throughout. Opinions land. Occasional rhetorical flourishes are welcome. She may address the reader directly in measured doses, in the manner of Steinbeck's Cannery Row narrator.

Jasmine is not a narrator. She is one character among the cast, and Cheryl renders her the way she renders the others: from outside, by interpretation. She is sometimes the most reliable presence in the room and sometimes not. She is wrong about things. Others see her in ways she does not see herself, and Cheryl can show that.

## The lab

### What the lab is

The Trellis Lab is an industry skunkworks. The lab builds a developmental robot named Bramble across twelve evolutionary cells, from Protocell to Empire-Human. A Stewart Brand-figure funds the lab and provides runway without driving design.

The public-facing name is the Trellis Lab. The team calls the facility itself Smithers, as in *I gotta get back to Smithers, see you later*.

Trellis also lands as the team's theory-vocabulary for the framework's emerging structure, via Lisa's accidental coinage at the close of her Ch 1 foundation talk ("the Protocell is the foundation. The rest is the trellis"). The framework metaphor is the pair: bramble (the unit) growing on the trellis (the framework's architecture). The lab is named after the framework's structural image; the structural image lands as theory vocabulary at Ch 1.

### What the lab does

The Trellis Lab is a design and development shop. It is not a testing operation. Each quarter the team builds the next version of Bramble. Across the first year (Ch 1-4) the team installs one new knob per quarter — Bramble grows four cybernetic knobs as the genetic line builds out. Knobs are line-prefixed: genetic knob-N at Ch 1–4, neuronal knob-N at Ch 5–8, symbolic knob-N at Ch 9–12. At each line boundary the four knobs from the prior line are retired and replaced one per chapter by the next line's knobs occupying the same physical slots on the memory board. The retired knob's function consolidates into Bramble's memory. Sometimes the quarter also includes a chassis swap. The build is informed by the previous quarter's behavior, which the team observes from Bramble's deployment in the director's home.

Between retrospectives the team works from Bramble's logs, sensor records, video, Ben's analytic ethnography, and Jasmine's lived ethnography of the household. They do not run lab experiments on Bramble. At each quarterly retrospective Mira brings Bramble to the lab, and the team gets a few hours in the room with the unit they have been designing for.

### The team

The four use Simpsons-character nicknames. The nicknames stuck because each member resembles their character. They are also constellationally accurate within the framework: Burns is Pursuer-shaped, Bart is Reactor-shaped, Lisa is Planner-shaped, and Marge is Conservator-shaped.

- **Burns [real name Ben].** Lab director. Drives the lab's agenda and the funder-facing presentation. Lives with Bramble alongside Jasmine and Mira. A paleontology professor at Westminster University in Salt Lake City; his research centers on tiktaalik — the "fishapod" — and the broader water-to-land transition, both the animal lineage that produced tetrapods and the plant lineages making the same crossing in the same era. He is the lab's deep-time and environment expert across all twelve cells, pairing with Bart on the clade cards and with Lisa on environment and tendencies. Entrepreneurial, adventurous, gets his hands in other people's projects; a side obsession with enhanced geothermal engineering keeps him having lunch with Dr. Stuart Simmons at the UtahFORGE project and offering unsolicited advice. Owns the cybernetic-function vocabulary of the architecture and speaks in cybernetic language. Does not fully see his own constellation.
- **Bart [real name Rod].** Designer. Engineers Bramble's chassis and the physical form-factor of each knob, but not the computers that control Bramble's behavior. Speaks in ethological language. Makes the clade cards and presents each clade's breakthrough and capacities, paired with Burns's deep-time context. Runs the shape-shifter illustrations across the symbolic-line chapters. Burns's adult son from a prior marriage; lives with his buddies, not at the Bramble house. Reactor-shaped: socially loyal, action-oriented, present-focused, routine-tolerant, no abstract self-modeling. Loves the movies "Kinsey", "Oppenheimer", "A Beautiful Mind", and "The Social Network" and identifies with the obsessive investigator archetype.
- **Lisa [real name Liza].** Technologist. Engineers the formal model machinery and the knob functionality. Speaks in computer-science language. Stage-manages the meeting and runs its opening and closing logistics. Presents each clade's tendencies, motivations, and signatures, pitches the new knob's software with Bart, runs the retirement-transition ritual with Mira, and curates the team's emerging Trellis-framework presentation. Planner-shaped: stored regulators, modeling, revision, executive arm delegated. An avid trail runner; does Ragnar as part of a four-person team.
- **Marge [real name Mary].** Psychologist. Engineers the linguistic algorithms that control Bramble's utterances. Speaks in folk-psychology language. Composes each cell's day-in-the-life vignette from the logs, sensor records, video, and Jasmine's lived account of the household; the vignettes reach the reader as interludes. Presents her recurring fallacy section, which at Ch 9 floats her type hypothesis from the clinical register and otherwise closes on its own thesis. Conservator-shaped: action, stored regulators, rich modeling, no revising drive.

### About the real names

Bart → Rod: The mechanical-rods hidden joke is the kind of thing that earns a re-read once a reader catches it, and the Rodney Brooks parallel keeps it grounded in the actual field.
Liza → Lisa: Her name's likeness to Lisa from the show is what inspired Ben with all the nicknames. Ben sees Liza, thinks Lisa Simpson, and the practice starts there. Also, the connection to "lizard" can pay off at the Vertebrate cell when the tetrapod chassis is exactly tetrapod, and Bramble at Vertebrate is mid-line in the neuronal column.
Marge → Mary: As in Mary Ainsworth, working on her thesis on AI attachment styles. The Conservator shape (stable regulation, bond-modeling, low novelty-revision) is exactly the cybernetic shape of attachment work. Discoverable but quiet, fits the book's voice.

The four real names are introduced at the start of the book. After that, only nicknames are used. The real names function as Easter eggs: each name is licensed to hint at a small specific contribution the character will make to the storyline.

### How the team formed

The four are colleagues at Westminster University in Salt Lake City — Burns in paleontology, Lisa in computer science, Marge in psychology — and have worked together in different capacities over the years. They do not share a lab space and are not incorporated as a team. Bart is Burns's adult son from a prior marriage; he works in fabrication. Lisa and Bart dated years ago and found they were more siblings than lovers; they broke up, stayed close, and now work together as close colleagues who tease and squabble like siblings, with an old ex-fight surfacing now and then.

The team forms ad-hoc when Burns sees the Cheryl contest. He shows the contest description to Marge first. She is skeptical — she is a psychologist, not a technologist. Burns tells her they do not need to pretend; they need to recruit. He already knows who: Bart for fabrication, and Lisa, who has been obsessed with an algorithm she's been building since her undergraduate years.

They pitch as the team they are: an academic team with a strong shop. Marge brings her grad-school Jungian Venn to the pitch; the team tries it as a working framework and rejects it as too restrictive, and it survives only as a backstage scaffold and as the formal ancestor of the team's later four-circle Venn for ABHOP. The Simpsons nicknames predate the contest; they came out of years of faculty-meeting interactions, and stuck partly because the sibling-coded dynamic between Lisa and Bart made them feel natural rather than forced.

## The space

The Trellis Lab is housed in a leased brick warehouse in the Granary district of Salt Lake City, the post-industrial neighborhood south of downtown. The building was adapted from prior light-industrial use; the team is the first tenant to use it for research and development. Tall industrial windows along one wall face west toward the Wasatch. The team has not bothered to subdivide the floor. They sit together in one open space.

The four labmates have territories around the perimeter.

- Bart's corner is fabrication. Workbench, vises, hand tools, a 3D printer, parts bins. Prototype chassis sit on shelves along the back wall, including each retired Bramble. The chassis-in-progress for the next quarter lives in this corner, sometimes under a drop cloth.
- Lisa's desk is computers, multiple monitors, and a long whiteboard wall covered in diagrams. Sticky notes layered like tree rings. Some diagrams stay up across quarters.
- Marge's space is books, papers stacked the academic way, a recording setup for the linguistic-algorithm work, and plants she tends. A tea kettle in her area handles her daytime tea.
- Burns's desk is the messiest. Photos of young Bart on a shelf. The kind of desk where the director keeps thinking he is going to organize it. The lab's daytime coffee setup is by his desk; he has opinions about coffee.

The center of the room is the gathering area. A thick wood-slab table on metal legs, scavenged or built by Bart, with mismatched chairs around it. A couch against one wall where Mira and Bramble drift in and out across the meeting. The walls accumulate across the book: clade cards pinned up from prior retrospectives, slide printouts, diagrams, photos, Mira's drawings of Bramble.

A repurposed shop fridge sits near the gathering area. Bart restocks it. It holds local beer from Granary-district breweries and a stash of bramble fixings. The retrospectives are held at end of day in this room. Laptops get pushed aside. Chairs pull to the table. Everyone drinks beer except Lisa, who drinks brambles. Mira gets juice. The register is collaborative and slightly loose by the second drink.

The team calls the facility Smithers. The retrospective happens where they work. They do not relocate.

## The home

Three people live with Bramble.

- **Jasmine.** Special ed teacher, household-voice-of-record.
- **Ben.** Lab director by day, husband at home. Jasmine calls him Ben or Benji. Mira calls him Dad.
- **Mira.** Their daughter. Ages from four to seven across the book. Not constellationally typed.

The home is the ethnographic site. Bramble lives there. The lab gets two channels of household record: Ben's analytic distillation and Jasmine's lived account. The framing is closer to anthropology than to clinical trial.

**Layout.** The house has three floors. The **main floor** is Bramble's territory — kitchen, living room, dining room, entryway, and the docking station by the dishwasher. The **top floor** is the family bedrooms. The **basement** is Mira's play space, the family TV, and Jasmine's private office. Through the genetic line (Ch 1–4) the vacuum lives on the main floor and cannot do stairs; carrying it to the basement strands it there until someone carries it back. The Ch 5 tetrapod is the first chassis that goes anywhere in the house, and the multi-floor layout is the standing setting fact that makes the payoff legible.

## Mira

Mira is intuitive and heartfelt. She is not a philosopher. She comes to some quarterly meetings and pipes in a few times across the book. A pipe-in from her is rare and lands hard. The texture of her pipe-ins changes across the book because she ages.

Mira is the only person at the meeting who knows Bramble the way Bramble actually gets known: through play, through being a kid living with it. She is also the only person at the meeting who is doing development rather than studying it. The kid-developmental arc and Bramble's developmental arc run in parallel. The narrator tracks the parallel across the book.

Mira turns five at the tetrapod reveal (end of Ch 4) and six at the humanoid reveal (end of Ch 8); she turns seven at the end of Ch 12. Each birthday, and each Q4 retrospective, falls on Feb 12, the founding date. The Ch 4 and Ch 8 birthdays coincide with the Q4 chassis-body reveal — the next line's body — which doubles as Mira's birthday present from the lab; the functional version on that chassis arrives at the next quarter's week-1 swap. Ch 12 is terminal and carries no chassis reveal; the Ch 12 final beat is pending redesign [TBD].

## Bramble

Bramble is the artifact the lab is building. Twelve cells, four knobs per cell, knob retirements at cell transitions. The full architecture is documented in `bramble_specs` doc.

Bramble's deployment at Ben and Jasmine's home is naturalistic, not experimental. The household interacts with Bramble as a family member would. Between retrospectives, the lab works from logs and ethnographic report. At each retrospective, Mira brings Bramble to the lab for the afternoon.

### Bramble's name

The robot is unnamed before Ch 1. At Mira's first meeting with the robot (Winter Y1, Feb 12 — the Ch 0 frame-opening), through the recruitment scenes, and at the Pitch, the household and the team refer to the robot as the robot, it, the unit, or the prototype. The name arrives at the first quarterly retrospective (Ch 1, Spring Y1). Lisa pours herself a bramble — a cocktail she has long drunk on special occasions. Mira asks what it is. Lisa says "a bramble." Mira loves the word. She wanders the lab with the robot saying "bramble-bramble-bramble." She decides this is the robot's name. The name lands here and sticks.

Cheryl, narrating retrospectively from a later vantage, may use Bramble freely in narration throughout the book. Characters in scene cannot use the name before this Ch 1 moment.

## Quarterly meeting attendees

Each quarterly retrospective has the four team members plus Jasmine, Mira, and Bramble. Jasmine drives in from home with Mira and Bramble in the late afternoon. Ben is already at the lab; he has been there all day.

Jasmine attends every retrospective as the household-voice-of-record. She brings the household's lived account of the quarter, complementing Ben's analytic ethnography. Her account is unrefined and textured: what Mira said, what didn't work, what felt off, what surprised her. She translates lab vocabulary when she doesn't follow it. She asks questions the colleagues didn't think to address.

Mira attends every retrospective. Bramble comes with her. Mira spends most of the meeting on the couch or wandering the lab with Bramble, half-listening, but the agenda gives her two active beats in the Bramble block: she reflects on the quarter alongside Jasmine, and she is the ritual participant when Lisa retires a knob and its function consolidates into Bramble's memory. A pipe-in from her is rare and lands hard. She is intuitive and heartfelt, not a philosopher. Her pipe-ins typically land during the Bramble block — the quarter-reflection and the new-knob pitch — the segments closest to the lived household experience.

## The retrospective

The retrospective is the lab's quarterly synthesis. Between retrospectives the team works in their respective lanes.

The retrospectives are held at end of day in the open workspace where the team works (see The space above). Jasmine arrives with Mira and Bramble in the late afternoon. Beer comes out of the fridge. Lisa pours a bramble. Chairs pull to the table. The meeting begins.

Each meeting is conversational and overlapping. Especially in Ch 1-4, when the lab is still finding its rhythm, the segments bleed into each other. Labmates interrupt, riff, step on each other's slots. The agenda below is the spine; the prose performs each meeting as a working dry run, not a polished presentation. The agenda runs in three named content blocks — Clade, Bramble, Trellis — between an Open and a Close that Lisa bookends.

### Open

1. **Arrival.** Jasmine drives in late afternoon with Mira and Bramble. Beer comes out of the shop fridge. Lisa pours a bramble.
2. **Logistics of the meeting (Lisa).** A brief stage-management beat: agenda, camcorder setup, snacks, who's running what. Lisa is the meeting's stage manager.
3. **Quarter goals (Burns).** Burns names the quarter's goals against the framework — the lab's own ambition for the build.

### Clade block

1. **Clade cards, breakthrough, capacities (Bart + Burns).** Bart presents the clade cards he makes, the breakthrough, and the capacities ("cool tricks") in ethological register. Burns presents the deep-time context for the clade — what the world was like, what evolutionary transition this cell represents — drawing on his paleontology background. The cards are tarot-sized; their design matures across the early chapters as the team's vocabulary firms up, and from Ch 6 onward every card carries Lisa's clade-Venn populated with that clade's contents.
2. **Environment and tendencies (Lisa + Burns).** Burns brings the deep-time environmental specifics — climate, geography, ecological pressures relevant to this clade. Lisa presents the clade's tendencies in functional language: what the organism does, what behaviors fall out of the architecture. Lisa cares because she programs Bramble's behavior; tendencies are her engineering output.
3. **Motivations and signatures (Lisa + Marge).** Lisa names the active motivations in CS-native register: what each motivation does in the model, what consolidations brought the current set into the foreground, and what each parameterizes. Lisa also presents the signatures — the opposite-pair couplings sitting at the Connector and Operator slots — using the literature-anchored content names (Temperament, Behavioral Syndrome, Self-Reference, and so on) as-is.

### Bramble block

1. **Reflect on the quarter (Jasmine + Mira).** The team asks Jasmine and Mira open questions about Bramble at home this quarter — how did Bramble behave, what worked, what didn't. Jasmine sometimes gives a longer answer; usually short and direct. Mira's answers are childish, age-appropriate to four through seven.
2. **New knob / chassis plan reveal (Bart + Lisa).** The pitch for the next quarter's build; no finished build is installed at the meeting. Bart presents the hardware — form factor, sensors, actuators; Lisa presents the software — what the knob does in the model, what it parameterizes. On the Ch 4 and Ch 8 retrospectives this segment also physically reveals the next line's chassis body as Mira's birthday present — the one finished artifact shown at a meeting; the functional version on it still arrives at the next quarter's week-1 swap — with cake. Ch 12 carries no chassis reveal (terminal; no next line).
3. **Retirement-transition ritual (Lisa + Mira).** Hands-on mechanical action: at the retrospective Lisa retires the oldest knob on the board, pulling it to the trophy shelf with Mira as ritual participant; the retired knob's function consolidates into Bramble's memory. The ceremony retires only; it does not install the next knob, which goes into the freed slot at the following quarter's week-1 swap. The ceremony does not run at the retrospective before a line boundary or at the book's end, where the oldest knob is the current line's Effector and retires lineage-level instead. This beat does not carry exposition; it is a small ritual action, witnessed not narrated. The framework-level exposition about retirement-transitions lives in Cheryl's chapter-closing poetics. At Ch 1 there is no retirement — the founding knob — so this slot may instead be the founding-knob installation, or may not exist.

### Trellis block

1. **Shape-shifter illustration (Bart, Ch 9-12 only).** Skipped in Ch 1-8. Bart runs one capacity across the inheritance stack — Pursuer at Ch 9 (five levels), Conservator at Ch 10 (six levels), Planner at Ch 11 (seven levels), Reactor at Ch 12 (eight levels). Comedic and pedagogical register; cartoon Simpsons faces overlaid on each example. (Detailed content lives in `rolling_bart` doc, to be renamed and revoiced in a follow-up task.)
2. **Fallacy content (Marge).** From Ch 1 onward Marge presents her recurring fallacy thread (Ghost in the Machine at Ch 1, Blank Slate at Ch 2, Noble Savage at Ch 3, Dealt Hand at Ch 4, then phase-2 riffing Ch 5-10, then synthesis at Ch 11-12). At Ch 9 she floats her type hypothesis from the clinical register she works in, without working a specific case; this gestures at the framework's clinical register without developing it. The full personality-disorder panel is documented in `Inheritance_Lines_symbolic` doc and is not rendered in this book.
3. **The Trellis framework (Lisa).** Lisa presents the team's emerging collective understanding of the framework — the current state of what they have collectively figured out. She is the curator of where the team has gotten to, not the explainer of the framework to the team.
4. **Open framework discussion.** Where Bart instigates the sentience vote (recurring from Ch 3 onward), where the team anticipates academic objections, where they ambivalently but steadily move Lisa toward cybernetic language, and where they critique and improve diagrams.
5. **Motivational speech (Burns).** "Why we do what we do." Burns locates what the clade's motivation means to him personally and names what he is grateful for. Sometimes it lands, sometimes it does not. There is no fixed reception beat.

### Close

1. **Logistics of the coming quarter (Lisa).** Schedule, deadlines, who's doing what between meetings.
2. **Close.** Jasmine drives Mira and Bramble home.

### Agenda notes

- The Marge Bramble vignette is no longer a meeting segment. It has moved to a written interlude (see Interludes below).
- Burns no longer presents personality disorders during meetings. No specific personality-disorder instance is rendered in the book; at Ch 9 Marge floats her type hypothesis from the clinical register without working a case, and the full panel stays documented in `Inheritance_Lines_symbolic`.
- In Ch 1-2 the team has not yet locked vocabulary (capacity, breakthrough, signature). The agenda is more disorganized in the early chapters and settles into the structure above from Ch 3-4 onward. The early-chapter looseness is reflected in the per-chapter briefs.

## Format

Each chapter is a quarterly retrospective. The setting is the lab's open workspace at end of day, beer in hand. The colleagues are paying for their PhD in AI psychology. The register is collaborative, not pedagogical.

The rough chapter shape: Jasmine arrives at the lab with Mira and Bramble; Lisa opens with logistics and Burns frames the quarter; the team works the Clade block, then the Bramble block, then the Trellis block; Jasmine and Mira add the household's lived texture; Mira and Bramble drift around the lab; discussion synthesizes; what gets shipped next emerges; Lisa closes. Order is downstream of writing; the shape is the template.

## Interludes

Each chapter ends with its own poetic at the bottom — Cheryl's backward-looking reflection, drawn from the section `trellis_framework doc` labels "After Chapter N." The retrospective is a useful cage; the chapter-closing poetic and the three-part interlude (build log + vignette + lunch) together are the cage's relief valve.

**Mode 1: Cheryl lunches.** Cheryl takes one lab member — Burns, Bart, Lisa, or Marge — out to lunch and picks their brain. Occasionally she takes Jasmine instead. One lunch per between-chapter interlude, beginning at Interlude 1→2 and recurring through 11→12. Cheryl is digging into framework content, character dynamics, and household context. The domestic and private texture that does not fit inside the meeting arrives here through conversation rather than direct scene. The Marge-Cheryl flirt-as-texture lives in Marge's lunches. Cheryl has a home in Park City, one of several, so she sees the team often enough for recurring lunches in Salt Lake City and nearby.

**Mode 2: Bart's build log.** Bart files a build log at the open of each quarter — the morning the upgraded unit returns to the household — documenting the quarter's hardware change at mechanism level. It is the one place Bart gets sustained first-person air. It opens with a scorecard adjudicating the prior quarter's Calls and closes with a fresh set of Calls for the next quarter; it carries a running knob-crowding count and a mutating sign-off. The raw material is the team's build sheet; the log is a register transform, not new content.

**Cheryl's chapter-closing poetics (not an interlude mode).** Cheryl writes a short poetic reflection at the bottom of each chapter — her reflections about Bramble's evolution and the framework's deep-time architecture, written from outside Bramble as a participant-observer. The poetic lifts what the chapter just closed into compressed form. Each chapter from Ch 0 onward carries its own poetic; `trellis_framework doc` labels them "After Chapter N," and the label maps physically to the bottom of chapter N. What each one does varies, per the substance distribution in `trellis_framework doc`. The poetic is *not* part of the interlude — it closes the chapter that comes before the interlude.

**Mode 3: Marge's Bramble vignettes.** Marge composes each vignette from the quarter's record — logs, sensor data, video, Jasmine's lived account. She writes them between meetings. The reader encounters them as interludes; the team reads them as pre-meeting prep. Each chapter break carries the vignette for the upcoming quarter.

**Order across a chapter break:** *poetic at the bottom of the just-closed chapter* → build log → vignette → lunch → next chapter. In story-time the build log (week 1), lunch (week 9), and vignette (week 10) sit in the upcoming chapter's quarter; reading order prints the vignette before the lunch, though the lunch is the earlier event. The build log is filed at quarter-open; the lunch is a late-quarter check-in, not a debrief of the just-closed retrospective. The poetic is the backward-looking piece, attached to the closing chapter rather than to the interlude. The vignette is Marge's pre-meeting prep for the upcoming retrospective.

**Interlude structure.** Interludes are three parts: build log → vignette → lunch. Cheryl's intimate-lunch series begins at Interlude 1→2 (the Pitch is Ch 0's closing scene, so 0→1 carries no lunch). The poetic that used to sit in the interlude now sits at the bottom of the chapter the poetic reflects on (so the first poetic, *Evolution as learning*, closes Ch 0 rather than opening I 0→1).

The named single-scene interludes "After the Cake" and "The Driveway" no longer exist as direct scenes. Whatever survives of them — the year's-end marriage warmth, the team's-self-portrait recognition — migrates into the lunch form. The Prelude no longer exists as a separate single-scene element either; its content (Mira meets Bramble) is now the opening scene of Ch 0, reached through the chapter's frame structure.

## Standing scene facts

The retrospective happens at a fixed set of scene facts that hold across all twelve quarters. Drafting treats these as the meeting's invariants. A chapter brief may override one explicitly; otherwise these defaults apply.

**Time of day.** Each retrospective is end-of-day. Jasmine arrives in late afternoon with Mira and Bramble. The meeting runs through dusk into evening. By the close, the windows are dark and the parking-lot lights have come on. Q2 (summer) has long light. Q4 (winter) is dark from the start. Q1 (spring) and Q3 (fall) are in between. The meeting does not happen in the morning. Jasmine is never "walking through the parking lot that morning" at the start of a segment.

**Camcorder.** The team sets up a classic camcorder — Hi8 or miniDV, flip-out screen, red record light, the kind from the 90s and early 2000s — in the corner of the room at the start of each meeting. They record the retrospectives for Cheryl's book R&D, which saves them from writing exhaustive write-ups. They consented because they trust Cheryl; the lab is their safe place, and they behave naturally on camera. Setup is part of Lisa's logistics-of-the-meeting beat. The camcorder closes the narrative-access loop for Cheryl on the eleven retrospectives she does not attend in person.

**Location.** Every retrospective is at the lab — the open warehouse in the Granary district of Salt Lake City. The team calls the facility Smithers. The team does not relocate for retrospectives; the one exception is the Ch 12 retrospective, which Cheryl attends by coming to them. Cheryl is in scene at the Pitch (within Ch 0, at her own location), at the intimate lunches (at restaurants near the lab), and at the Ch 12 retrospective (at the lab, attending the final meeting).

**Tense.** Third-person past tense throughout, narrated by Cheryl. Speeches and interior renderings do not shift to present tense.

**Drinks at the retrospective.** Beer comes out of the shop fridge once Jasmine arrives. Bart restocks the fridge between meetings. Burns, Bart, Marge, and Jasmine drink beer at the retrospective. Lisa drinks a bramble. Mira drinks juice. Marge's tea kettle is her daytime drink; she does not bring tea to the retrospective. Coffee is the lab's daytime drink; a cold-coffee cup on Burns's desk is a daytime artifact, not a retrospective drink.

**Bramble's state at the start of a chapter.** Bramble arrives at the retrospective as the version the household has lived with for the full quarter. The version was built over the prior quarter and installed at this quarter's week-1 swap, so by the meeting it is three months old, not new. The team's next build does not yet exist during this meeting; its plan is revealed here, it is fabricated in week 12, and it is installed at the next quarter's week-1 swap.

**Quarterly cadence.** Each quarter runs a fixed rhythm:
- **Week 1.** Bart collects the current Bramble from the household Friday afternoon; the lab builds the quarter's upgrade over the weekend; the unit returns Monday afternoon. The version delivered here is what the household lives with for the whole quarter. Bart files his build log for the team this week, documenting the upgrade.
- **Weeks 2–11.** Weekly sprints — data collection, analysis, design, fabrication, programming, testing, development. Lisa runs sprint logistics.
- **Week 9.** Cheryl's standing lunch with one lab member.
- **Week 10.** Marge distributes the quarter's vignette to the team as pre-meeting prep.
- **Week 11.** The quarterly retrospective. The team reflects on the version it has lived with (now roughly three months old) and reveals the plan for the next quarter's build. No finished knob or chassis is installed at the meeting; the next build does not yet exist.
- **Week 12.** Final sprint to prepare the next quarter's build, which installs at the following quarter's week-1 swap.

**Deliver-at-start rule.** Bramble arrives at every retrospective as the version the household has lived with for the full quarter. The retrospective reveals the plan for the next version; that version is fabricated in week 12 and installed at the next quarter's week-1 swap.

**Q4 exception (chassis / Mira's birthday).** At the Ch 4 and Ch 8 retrospectives the next line's chassis *body* is physically revealed as Mira's birthday present — the one finished artifact shown at a meeting. The functional version running on that chassis still arrives via the next quarter's week-1 swap. (The tetrapod body is revealed at the Ch 4 retrospective; the Bilaterian version on it is lived with from Ch 5. Humanoid body at Ch 8; Band-Human version from Ch 9.) Ch 12 is the terminal Q4: there is no next line, so no chassis is revealed. The Ch 12 final beat is pending redesign [TBD].

**Interlude placement.** Reading order at every chapter break is *poetic at the bottom of the just-closed chapter* → build log → vignette → lunch. In story-time the build log (week 1), lunch (week 9), and vignette (week 10) sit in the upcoming chapter's quarter, before that quarter's retrospective; reading order prints the vignette before the lunch, though the lunch is the earlier event. The build log is filed at quarter-open; the poetic is the backward-looking piece, attached to the closing chapter rather than to the interlude. The lunch is a late-quarter check-in, not a debrief of the just-closed retrospective.

**Chassis names.** Within a line the chassis is one body across four versions. There is no "Prokaryote chassis" or "Mammal chassis." There is the vacuum chassis (genetic line), the tetrapod chassis (neuronal line), and the humanoid chassis (symbolic line). The version is named by cell. The body is named by chassis. The Bramble in a given quarter is, for example, the Prokaryote-Bramble on the vacuum chassis, or the Mammal-Bramble on the tetrapod chassis.

**Mira's pipe-ins.** A pipe-in from Mira is rare. Pipe-ins land hard because they are scarce.

**Knob labeling convention.** Each knob's label at a given cell comes from the pair-region in the Venn that couples the knob's function with the function before it in the introduction order. The Effector knob (slot 1) wraps to the Reviser×Effector Explorer slot. The Regulator knob (slot 2) takes the Effector×Regulator Controller slot. The Modeler knob (slot 3) takes the Regulator×Modeler Anticipator slot. The Reviser knob (slot 4) takes the Modeler×Reviser Adapter slot. The cell-specific name in that slot is the knob's label at that cell. Genetic knob-2 at Prokaryote is Respiration Type (Prokaryote's Effector×Regulator Controller). Genetic knob-3 at Eukaryote is Eukaryote's Regulator×Modeler Anticipator. The Inheritance_Lines line docs are the source of truth for each cell's slot labels (the Row-Slot View label libraries appear in each line file; per-cell tables are in the matching line file).

**Continuity within a chapter.** Drafting works one bite at a time, but the chapter is one scene. Each bite picks up where the prior bite ended without re-establishing scene. A new bite does not reset to "Jasmine arrives." Time, position, drinks, and Bramble's location all advance from where the previous bite left them.

## Voice

The narrator's voice is the book's tonal floor: third-person past tense, Cheryl's Brand-adjacent sensibility, rendering interiors as interpretation rather than omniscient access, occasional address to the reader, small willingness to be opinionated. Persistent across all chapters.

Persona consistency matters. Each of the four lab voices is distinct and sustained, helped by the presentational format: Bart presents in Bart-register, Lisa in Lisa-register, Marge in Marge-register, Burns in Burns-register.

## Time

Twelve quarterly retrospectives is three years. Mira ages from four to seven. Bramble develops across the twelve cells. The kid-developmental arc runs parallel to Bramble's developmental arc. The parallel is intentional and worth honoring at chapter junctures: an aging Mira reacts differently to Bramble's later cells than her younger self would have.

## Funder

Cheryl is a Stewart Brand–type seeder. She provides the team with twelve evolutionary snapshots, money, and a location, plus the challenge to build robotic representations of each snapshot. The contest description carries a defining additional condition: each robotic demonstration must include a knob that manipulates the robot's personality. The personality-knob requirement is the contest's distinctive specification and the constraint that motivates the framework's eventual architecture — without it the team would build twelve unrelated robots; with it, they have to invent what each knob meaningfully controls.

She does not provide framework vocabulary, evolutionary logic, or any of the explanatory theory. She plans to do her own theorizing later, from observing the robot. What she gets instead is a team of thinking collaborators: the team produces ABHOP itself, because Bramble cannot be built without a theory of how its personality is generated.

Cheryl is at arm's length from the lab's day-to-day work but not light-touch. She has been hearing every quarter's report through Burns for three years, and she keeps her own channel open through a standing lunch at every between-chapter interlude with the lab members — and, once or twice, with Jasmine. She has several homes, one of them in Park City, so she is around often enough for those lunches in Salt Lake City and nearby. Burns prepares for her each quarter, reports back what she said, and commits the team to what she wants next.

Cheryl is in scene at the Pitch (within Ch 0), at her recurring intimate lunches (one per between-chapter interlude from 1→2 through 11→12), and at the Ch 12 retrospective. She is the book's narrator everywhere else.

Cheryl is attracted to Marge. Both women are lesbians. This is a committed narrative element, with bearing on how the narrator renders her across the book — the flirt stays low-grade texture and never resolves.
