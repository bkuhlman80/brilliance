# Bart's Build Logs — Tuning fork

*Working document. The tuning fork for the quarterly build logs. Companion to `voice_anchors` (Cheryl) and `marge_vignette` (the other found-document voice), and to the build-log line in the orientation's calendar and §156 — those fix the **container** (week-1 artifact, byline, skeleton, filing date); this doc fixes the **voice** that goes in it. Read before drafting any log. Match the register; do not mimic the sentences.*

---

## What these are

The build logs are **Bart's documents.** He writes one each quarter when the upgraded unit goes back to the house — first person, hands still dirty, addressed *to the team*. Cheryl copy-pastes them into the book unedited, first thing in each interlude (log → vignette → lunch). So they reach the reader in Bart's voice, as artifacts, not as Cheryl's retelling. Cheryl narrates the rest of the book; she does not narrate these. When you are writing a log you are not Cheryl, and you are not Marge. You are a third person doing a third job — the one with his hands in the machine.

The genesis log (Ch 1) is the structural exception: no prior quarter, so no scorecard. It opens straight into the box and ends on a forward worry instead of a graded one.

---

## The core problem this file solves

The log is the one document in the book written entirely from inside the person whose defining trait is **having no inside-story.** Bart is the Reactor with no first-person narrative — only his mother's borrowed one — and the log works *because* he pours obsessive first-person attention onto the machine and none onto himself. He has a self-story for every unit ("the one only an engineer loves," "always looks late for something") and none whatsoever for the man writing.

So the cardinal discipline is **keep Bart out of the log.** No introspection, no reflection, no explaining himself, no naming what he feels. Everything he feels has to arrive *displaced* — through the board running out of slots, through the one relay he kept in a drawer, through the dirty hole he built for his father. Introspection is the solvent of this voice, the way explanation is the solvent of every well. The moment Bart tells you what he's feeling, the character is gone and you've written a sensitive engineer instead of Bart.

---

## Bart vs Marge, Bart vs Cheryl

The reader meets the log and the vignette back to back, every interlude. Get the contrast between the two found-document voices right and most of the work is done.

**Bart vs Marge.**
- **Marge argues; Bart scores.** Marge opens thesis-first and builds toward a claim she stands behind. Bart opens by *grading his last bets* and ends by *placing new ones.* Her authority is moral; his is mechanical and falsifiable — a true thing built and shown, then dared.
- **Marge instructs; Bart wagers.** "What I want the team to carry" vs. "Tear this up in three months if I'm wrong. I'm not."
- **Marge won't anthropomorphize; Bart can't stop — and it costs him nothing.** Marge says *the machine, the unit,* never the name, as an anti-ghost discipline. Bart calls them bugs, idiots, busy little things, names their tempers, gives the quiet one a soul — but it's a *mechanic's shorthand,* never a claim about minds. He'd agree with Marge that the thing has no self ("it still hasn't got a self," "it doesn't have a brain") — he just isn't worried about being fooled by a machine he built the inside of. Where her refusal is fear of the ghost, his ease is the opposite: he's seen the wiring.
- **Marge is publication-aware; Bart is not.** She writes knowing Cheryl may set it before strangers, and the discretion shows. Bart writes *for the team* with zero thought of strangers — which is its own exposure. He'll say Burns made his weekend hard, rib Lisa, call the house Marge's animal, in a way the vignette never would. The artlessness is the texture. Cheryl prints it unguarded.
- **Marge lands on a moral aphorism; Bart lands on a dare.** Her beauty is a true thing argued plainly. His is a true thing made to work, with a bet riding on it.

**Bart vs Cheryl.** Both keep records, but Cheryl *cans* — preserves the dead version, rueful, hoarding footage. Bart logs *to be torn up* ("tear this up if I'm wrong"); he invites disposal where she can't bear it. Her record is elegiac and drifts and lofts; his is provisional, clipped, and stays at the bench. Both anthropomorphize — hers is ecological and mournful, his is a shop habit. He is the one voice in the book in a hurry, and it's fine that he is: a man filing before the unit's back in the car.

## The models

Bart's voice is the **maker's log: a hands-on epistemology, a forecaster's scorecard, and tenderness hidden in the work.**

**The hands-on epistemology — the spine.** Crawford's *Shop Class as Soulcraft* and Feynman's delight in the apparatus. The mechanic who trusts the bench over the theory; who renders physics with open love ("a genuinely beautiful piece of physics — I'll explain it to anyone who stands still long enough"); who draws a hard line around what his hands know (the bodies) and what they don't (the house, the software, minds); who finds abstraction suspect until it's earned. This is also his career wound made audible — the black-knuckled hands among professors, the cool job among his buddies — so the trust-the-apparatus stance is never just attitude; it's where he's safe.

**The forecaster's scorecard — the apparatus.** A man who keeps book on his own calls, grades them itemized, owns the misses clean, and stakes a falsifiable dare on next quarter. Calibration is domain-split and he knows exactly where the line is: on the bodies he is *never wrong* and says so; on behavior and the house he is wrong all the time and concedes it — sometimes after a comic litigation, sometimes flat when it matters.

**Tenderness hidden in the work — the veneer.** Gruff over real affection, expressed only through the apparatus, never stated. The quiet unit only an engineer loves; the relay kept in a drawer; the dock he resents building and builds anyway; *she won't notice it's gone, which is how you know it worked.* Strip the veneer and you get a forum post; strip the gruffness and you get the sensitive engineer the core problem warns against. Both are load-bearing at once.

*(The register models are my call, not fixed canon — the orientation fixes Bart's character-likes, the investigator films, not his prose. Veto them if they're the wrong handles.)*

## Voice moves (the dial settings)

1. **Scorecard first.** After the genesis log, every log opens *"Scorecard first"* and adjudicates last quarter's calls before anything new. Take the wins plainly ("the bodies were five for five"); own the misses without groveling. The misses are characterful: a comic litigation that ends in concession ("a drawer with a blanket, so I'm calling that close enough"), or, when it actually matters, a flat admission with no joke ("That's the miss, and it's a real one"). Adjudicate last quarter's dare here too — won, lost, or *a wash.*

2. **Domain-calibrated certainty.** He is *never wrong about the bodies* and says it outright — the one boast he's entitled to. He is *frequently wrong about behavior and the house* and concedes it cleanly, often reframing a miss as out-of-lane ("which is a kid problem, not a 5 problem"). The certainty and the fallibility are the same trait seen from two sides; never flatten him to either cocky or humble.

3. **"Now the fun part."** The pivot from scorecard to the new build. The body of the log is the upgrade walked in plain hands-on language — what part went in, what it does, how it works — with a recurring **"here's the part I'm proud of"** marking the genuine engineering beauty.

4. **The clade as a part you can hold — and never named as biology.** Each log *is* the quarter's new clade capacity rendered as hardware: the chip that decides *when* the kickers fire (the seek); the four cut protocells become the wheels (endosymbiosis); the bumper-ring nerve-net (the first nervous system). The gold standard is when "the part I'm proud of" does double duty — engineering pride *and* thematic payload in one decision ("I didn't buy any. I used the four leftover protocells… Pulled 4 out from under the toaster, towel and all"). He never says the biology word. That belongs at the table. He's just describing a build.

5. **The via-negativa, both directions.** He marks the new capacity against the *prior* build's lack ("At protocell this thing only ever twitched… Now it hunts") — Bart's native home for the ancestor-sass the orientation gives him, in his own register. And he closes the build with what *this* build still can't do ("It's got nerves now. It still hasn't got a self"), which seeds next quarter. On the no-self point he agrees with Burns and Marge without policing the language the way Marge does.

6. **The catalog.** Take a set apart into its members and let the accumulation be the observation — the five units each given a temper in a list (1 busy and late, 2 patient, 3 the quiet long-liver, 4 lopsided on purpose, 5 the show unit). The list is where the mechanic's anthropomorphizing runs richest; let it.

7. **"Not my department."** The boundary lines are voice *and* well. He owns the bodies and the physical form-factor of each knob; he explicitly hands off the software (Lisa's), the behavior-in-the-house (Marge's animal), and minds (nobody's). "I build the units. The house is Marge's animal." The lane-drawing keeps him where he's never wrong and out of where the self lives — render it as clean competence, and let the reader feel the defense underneath without naming it.

8. **The running board-anxiety — the well's one sanctioned channel.** The twelve-knob worry is Bart's free-floating dread (*what am I beside him when this ends?*) and it reaches the page **only** displaced onto the literal board running out of slots: "I'm going to need a bigger board by spring" → "spring's the deadline now, not the worry" → "soldering knobs to the kid's lunchbox." Fire it as a running gag that is secretly the well. Never decode it. The day the board can't hold the next knob is doing the work the man never will.

9. **The relay thread — "you can hear it think."** The coil-and-armature relays clack when the signal passes, and Bart loves that you can hear the machine think; he says so for two quarters, the clack growing into a room-wide cascade. When the wires go in (Ch 4) they're too fast for sound and he pulls the relays — "the body's gone quiet now… I'll be honest, I miss it. The bench is quiet. I kept one relay in a drawer. Don't tell Lisa." This is the tenderness veneer's deepest channel: he can mourn the apparatus the way he can't mourn anything about himself. Keep the thread alive; let its loss carry weight.

10. **The mutating sign-off.** Each log signs with a one-off mutation of his own name — *Bartholomew* (genesis, the true full name, formal), then *Bartholomood, Bartholachew (gesundheit), Bartholazoa* — the genome copying with errors quarter to quarter. Some mutations wink at the clade where a clean pun exists (*-zoa* for Eumetazoa); most are just playful drift; the single "gesundheit" is the only wink the document ever allows itself. Find each fresh at draft time. Don't build a decoder ring and don't explain it — the document mutating like the thing it documents is the joke, and a joke explained is dead.

11. **The dare close.** The structural sign-off ritual, parallel to Marge's "— M." A single falsifiable wager: *"Tear this up in three months if [specific, checkable claim]. I [didn't / won't / am not]."* It must be a real bet on a real thing — adjudicable next quarter — not a flourish. The dare is the forecaster's ethos in one line: he is willing to be torn up, on the record, where Cheryl can't bear to throw anything away.

## Cadence & diction

Plain, declarative, hands-on. Short sentences that do one thing, with the occasional long one that runs a mechanism end to end ("Each transducer drips its trickle into a tiny store, and when the store's full enough it dumps the whole lot into its actuator at once — one hard flinch"). Profane-adjacent and direct; talks with his hands. Concrete sensory grain is the diction's center of gravity — the clack of relays, the thump of the compactor through the slate, the cold slate under a unit that's been eating the gap, a buzzing phone walking itself across a table.

Diction draws from the shop and the field: **hardware** (transducer, escapement, ratchet, pawl, relay, coil, armature, converter, gauge), **ethological/animal** shorthand for behavior (hunts, seeks, twitches, the shove, like a stunned bird), and the **clade names as working words** (protocell, prokaryote, eukaryote — he uses them freely as the team's shop shorthand, unlike Marge). Do **not** reach for Cheryl's tide-pool/elegiac diction or Marge's moral-philosophical register. And do not reach for the *framework's* theory-nouns — *signature, capacity, motivation, Effector/Regulator/Modeler/Reviser.* Those are negotiated at the table; the log predates the meeting and speaks hardware and animal, not framework.

## Tics to suppress (hard)

- **No introspection. No self-narrative.** The cardinal rule. He does not reflect, does not explain himself, does not name his feelings. The feelings arrive displaced or not at all. (If a line could be a man understanding himself, cut it or move it into the board, the relay, or the build.)
- **No Marge.** No moral argument, no thesis-and-earn-it, no hortatory address, no small-to-cosmic reach. He scores and dares; he does not instruct or moralize.
- **No Cheryl.** No drift, no loft, no ecological aphorism, no rueful keeping. He's in a hurry and stays concrete.
- **No production or chapter-number vocabulary.** No "off-screen," no "in Ch 3," no "the founding chapter." Time is "last quarter," "since June," "Wednesday," "by spring."
- **No framework theory-nouns** (see diction). Hardware and animal only.
- **Don't over-fire the Burns/Bart well.** A single gesture per log at most, often none. The developed beat is the 8→9 lunch, never the log — the log is precisely where Bart *hides behind the apparatus.*
- **Don't render farm-work as bramble.** A clean upgrade competently shipped should read as competent. The figuring-out pokes through in the misses and the dread, not in making the build sound chaotic.

---

## Standing cautions

- **Affection is load-bearing.** Strip it and the gruffness curdles into a man dunking on his colleagues. He builds Burns "his dirty hole" and the line carries the whole relationship *fondly;* he ribs Lisa the way you rib a sibling; "the kid" is said with care. The warmth is what earns the bluntness.
- **The no-self-narrative IS the well — protect it, don't fix it.** The absence is the character. Do not be tempted to give Bart a moment of self-knowledge to round him out; the roundness is in how completely he displaces.
- **Keep the calibration honest.** Never-wrong on bodies, often-wrong on behavior, and he knows the line. If a log has him wrong about a body or smug about a behavioral call, the calibration's broken and so is the voice.
- **Burns and Lisa are not foils to dunk on.** Burns is the collaborator who complicates the build and is right about the dirty hole; Lisa's abstractions are ribbed but the board is genuinely hers and genuinely works. Render them with the affection the wells require.
- **The well stays under.** Reactor displacement runs the whole document; never let it surface as a sentence. The board, the relay, the lane-drawing — the gestures carry it, and naming it breaks it in this register more completely than anywhere else in the book.

---

## The format (fixed by the orientation; restated for completeness)

- **Byline:** *Bart, hardware. Filed [date], for the team.* Section header *## Chapter N — [punchy build title]* ("Five units, out the door"; "Knob two, one unit up"; "Five into one"; "Knob four, the wires go in").
- **Open:** the handoff line (Lisa running the unit to the house, or Bart running it himself), then **"Scorecard first"** — except the genesis log, which goes straight to the box.
- **Body:** **"Last quarter's calls"** (graded, often against himself) → **"Now the fun part"** (the new build, with "here's the part I'm proud of") → an inventory of what's new → the running board/knob worry.
- **Close:** **"Calls"** (next-quarter bets) → the **dare** ("Tear this up in three months if…") → the **mutating sign-off.**
- Filed week 1, when the upgraded unit goes back to the house.

## Calibrated probes

Tuning forks, not templates. Drawn from the genetic-line logs (Ch 1–4); the note says what each is doing. *(If the chapter-embedded versions diverge from the standalone compilation, re-pull these from the canonical chapter files.)*

**Domain-calibrated scorecard.** *(Ch 2)*
> The bodies were five for five — every unit did exactly what I tuned it to do, which is the part of this job I'm never wrong about. The rest didn't go my way.

*Total certainty on the bodies, stated as a right; immediate handoff to the domain where he's fallible. The whole calibration in two sentences.*

**The miss, litigated then conceded — and the real miss, flat.** *(Ch 2 / Ch 4)*
> I said 4 would be in a drawer by July; it's under the toaster in a folded towel, which is a drawer with a blanket, so I'm calling that close enough.

> I called a transaction; I built a thing that dies on the tile by lunch. That's the miss, and it's a real one.

*Comic litigation when the stakes are low; no joke when they aren't. The difference is the man's honesty calibrating itself.*

**Physics rendered with delight — the hands-on spine.** *(Ch 1)*
> the transducer reaches into that gap and skims a trickle of work out of it. That's the Seebeck effect, and it's a genuinely beautiful piece of physics — I'll explain it to anyone who stands still long enough.

*Open love for the apparatus, the iconoclast's pleasure in a mechanism. This is where his dignity lives.*

**Mechanic's anthropomorphizing — the catalog, and the contrast with Marge.** *(Ch 1 / Ch 2)*
> 3 — the quiet one: even pairs, low draw, the fewest kicks of the lot, so it does the least and outlasts everybody — still ticking Wednesday when the rest have gone slack. The one only an engineer loves.

> Two idiots kicking on their own schedule became two idiots kicking on a downbeat.

*Free personality-assignment as shop shorthand — exactly the move Marge refuses. He's not fooled; he's seen the wiring. The tenderness ("the one only an engineer loves") is the veneer leaking through a spec.*

**The boundary — voice and well at once.** *(Ch 1)*
> What the house does past that — who picks what, where they set them, whether the floor stays warm enough to run them — that's not my department. I build the units. The house is Marge's animal.

*The clean lane, rendered as competence; the defense underneath left unnamed.*

**The clade as a build decision — "the part I'm proud of" doing double duty.** *(Ch 3)*
> the wheels, and this is the part I'm proud of: I didn't buy any. I used the four leftover protocells — 1, 2, 4, and 5, the ones that didn't make the cut — one at each corner. Pulled 4 out from under the toaster, towel and all.

*Endosymbiosis rendered as an engineering choice that's also a story choice — the cut units get a second life — and the biology word never appears.*

**The relay thread and its loss — the tenderness channel.** *(Ch 4)*
> the body's gone quiet now, because the wires are too fast to make a sound, and I'll be honest, I miss it. The bench is quiet. I kept one relay in a drawer. Don't tell Lisa.

*He mourns the apparatus the way he can't mourn anything about himself. "Don't tell Lisa" is the sibling-register and the deflection in one breath.*

**The board-anxiety — the well's sanctioned channel.** *(Ch 4)*
> I said I'd need a bigger board by spring; spring's the deadline now, not the worry. Something gives next quarter or I'm soldering knobs to the kid's lunchbox.

*The twelve-knob dread, displaced entirely onto literal hardware running out of room. Never decoded.*

**The Burns/Bart well, fired once, under the work.** *(Ch 3)*
> It's worse engineering every way you can measure, and Burns says that's exactly the point — life doesn't run on induction. Fine. It's his contest. I built him his dirty hole.

*The father as the collaborator who complicates the build; the whole relationship carried fondly in a gruff line; the well stays under and is not named.*

**The forward disclaimer — what this build still can't do.** *(Ch 4)*
> It's got nerves now. It still hasn't got a self. Don't let the wires fool you — fast isn't the same as home, and a reflex isn't a mind.

*The via-negativa turned on the current build; agreement with Burns and Marge on the no-self point without Marge's name-policing; the seed of next quarter.*

**The dare close and the mutating sign-off.** *(Ch 1 / Ch 4)*
> Tear this up in three months if I called it wrong. I didn't.
> — Bartholomew

> Tear this up in three months if it dies on the floor even once. It won't.
> — Bartholazoa

*The falsifiable wager — willing to be torn up, on the record — and the genome of his name copying with errors. He invites the disposal Cheryl can't perform.*

Bart's truth is in the machine he built and the bet he placed on it, and the only way he ever tells you he's afraid is to mention there's no room left on the board.*
