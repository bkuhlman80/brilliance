# Bramble block

The Bramble block is the part of the retrospective that is about the machine. Everything about the bot belongs here and nothing else does: what Bramble did this quarter, what it is now, what it is like to live with, and — at the tail — where it is headed next. In the de-braided retrospective three blocks never toggle into one another. The clade block holds the dead animal and the abstract framework. The Trellis block holds the motivations, the signatures, the tendencies, the cycle and the board, the fallacy and the vote. The Bramble block holds Bramble. Its standing name is the Bramblogues — a bad pun Bart coins and is delighted by, and goes on using past the point anyone else can stand it.

The grouping logic is the machine, not the quarter. That is why the forward-looking pitch lands here even though it points ahead: it is about the bot's next knob and next body, and the bot is this block's whole subject.

## What it's for

The lab is not building a good robot. It is building a faithful one. The job every quarter is to illustrate the clade — to make the machine do the dumb, costly, barely-working thing the real organism did — and a robot built to illustrate biology is, by any sane engineering standard, built wrong on purpose. Nature is the absurd engineer here: it makes bags of barely that run on gaps they didn't dig, things that get lost the instant they're confused, nerve-nets laid down slower than the wire they replace. The lab copies the absurdity faithfully, and the Bramble block is where Bart brags about it.

That is the engine of the whole block: the humble-brag. The humble is the engineering critique, and it is correct — the thing really is badly built. The brag is that the badness is the design, deliberate and faithful, and Bart owns every bit of it with relish. He is not confessing a shortcut he would undo with more budget. He is showing off the inefficiency as the achievement. Get this and the block writes itself; miss it and Big O reads as apology and the grilling reads as a status report.

## The calendar

A chapter is a quarter, and a chapter is self-contained for calls. At the top, Bart files his build log and makes his calls — forward bets about what this quarter's build will do. At the bottom, at the retrospective, the grilling settles those same calls. The bet is placed and called inside the one chapter.

The grilling settles the calls Bart made at the top of this same chapter, and never a prior chapter's. A block that grades last quarter's bets is mispointed — point it at this chapter's own. The calls themselves live in the build log; see bart_build_log_tuning_fork.

The division of labor is strict: the log makes calls and only makes them; the grilling, at the table, is where they are answered. A log that grades — a scorecard, a "good quarter on the calls," any line marking a prior bet kept or missed — has pulled the grilling's work into the log and pointed it a quarter back. Move it: the grading belongs at the table of the chapter whose calls it settles, run as that chapter's grilling with the household judging. Made in writing, answered out loud.

## Big O opens it

Bart opens by handing the floor to Big O — Owen, the absent performance-engineer friend, conjured into the room to put the hard objection on the table. Big O is never present; Bart ventriloquizes him. He is a staple, not a flourish: he comes every quarter, and the absence of an easy target is no excuse to skip him. What he objects to changes — the relay clatter while the body still clicked, a legged chassis bolted under a vacuum, a nerve-net built to run slower than a wire — and when the obvious complaint is gone he finds the next one: the silence, the cost, the thing that works but should never have been built that way.

The objection is correct, and Bart grants it — that is the move. He does not refute Big O and he does not promise to do better with more time. He agrees, and then says the badness was the point: nature built it this way, so we built it this way. Big O is Bart making himself the butt of his own joke, the straight man whose right answer is the wrong answer for the job. He owns the critique gladly. Write him as setup, not as a crisis of engineering conscience.

## What the block carries

Two things are fixtures — every quarter, and a block missing either is not conforming.

The grilling. Bart grills Mira about what happened at home, and her answers settle his calls. Mira is the home-witness: the calls are decided on what she saw the machine do in the house, not on a demonstration at the table. He puts a bet to her as a question about home — did it do the thing he said — and her plain yes or no settles it. The flavor of the beat inverts with the quarter: insufferable when the bets landed, eating it when they did not, the same humble-brag engine running forward or in reverse. Keep Mira age-true, a child's plain testimony about a machine she lives with.

One thing is the customary close — expected, but not mandatory.

The household report. Jasmine's lived account of the machine at home: the warm register, the house and not the lab, what it is actually like to share rooms with the thing. It tends to land late and is handed to her. Where the grilling settles the bets, the household report gives the machine its life. Most quarters want it; a quarter can earn its absence.

The rest are optional. None is required. But each is about the machine, so where one appears it belongs in this block and nowhere else — an optional found in another block is the thing to move back. And anything that looks to next quarter goes at the very end of the block, after everything that accounts for this one.

The live demo. Sometimes Bart shows the build off live, the body doing its new thing for the camera. It is separate from the grilling, which runs on Mira's testimony; the demo, when it comes, is extra, and it never stands in for the grilling.

The next-quarter pitch. The coming quarter's new knob — and, at the line boundaries, the next chassis, dramatized as the reveal. It points forward, so it sits at the end.

The knob-retirement ritual. From the neuronal line on, a full board means each new knob costs an old one, and the retirement is played as ritual. It too looks ahead, and sits at the end with the pitch.

The usual shape is Big O opening, the grilling and the household report carrying the body of it, the live demo dropped in when the build earns it, and the forward-looking pitch and retirement closing the block. The order inside is the chapter's to set — except that next-quarter material lands last.

## What belongs here, and what doesn't

The Bramble block holds Bramble's live behavior and physical particulars, and only those. The block does instantiate the clade's capacity — it shows how this quarter's Bramble embodies the trait the dead animal introduced — and that is exactly its work. What is wrong is toggling back to the dead animal inside the block, or doing the framework's abstract naming here. Show the machine; let the clade block hold the animal and the Trellis block hold the theory.

## Register

Bart owns this block and is steadiest in it — the machine is the one subject he never loses his feet on. The technical precision he uses elsewhere to deflect is, here, simply at home. His private asides and his sign-offs stay in the log, not at the table; see bart_build_log_tuning_fork. Big O is Bart's instrument, not a person in the room. Mira is the home-witness and the yes-or-no judge, five years old and plain. Jasmine carries the warmth and the weight, and the block tends to come to rest on her.

## Cheryl around it

Narrate the block as the rest is narrated. No continuity-bookkeeping — never timestamp a recurring move as habitual ("the way he opened it every quarter"). When a fixture comes round again, give it a fresh characterizing image or leave it bare; the recurrence is not the point, the machine is. Hunt echoes: no beat, image, simile, or construction repeats across chapters. See voice_anchors.

## Headers

A bot-block header is spent once and never reused, and no derivative of a spent label revives it — once a title is used, it and its variants are retired for the rest of the book. This binds the block's own name hardest. Bart coins the Bramblogues in the first quarter; that quarter is the only one that may carry it as a header. Every quarter after titles its bot block for what that quarter's machine actually did — the build, the miss, the live thing on the table — not for the block's standing name. The fixture recurs; the title over it does not.

## Using this fork

This is the block's anatomy and its engine, not a mold to press each quarter into. The fixtures recur; the prose must not. A block conforms when the engine is present and correctly placed — Big O owning the critique, the grilling pointed at this chapter's own calls, the optionals home in this block, the next-quarter material last — and it conforms best when it looks nothing like the quarter before it. The trap of a checking pass is that it tempts you to flatten blocks toward a template, and that flattening is itself the failure. Check that the parts are present and rightly placed. Never check that the prose matches a prior quarter's shape; when it does, that sameness is the defect to fix.
