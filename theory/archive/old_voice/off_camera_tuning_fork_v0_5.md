# Off Camera — Tuning Fork

Calibration for the two bookends that bracket every retrospective: the open (Before the Camera Roll), which follows the interlude's person *in*, and the close (After the Camera Roll), which follows the next interlude's person *home*. The braid in the orientation sets who anchors which slot; this fork says how the slot wants to be written.

It is the structural sibling of the Burns motivationals fork. That one governs the last sincere thing said on camera; this one governs what happens on either side of the camera. The seam between them is the red light — the close begins where the speech ends, when the light goes out. Match the register; don't mimic the sentences. Examples are drawn from the Ch 1–3 Burns and Marge bookends, with the household bookends as the standing reference.

---

## Best practices

**Anchor on the interlude's person; let the household arrive inside the frame.** Each bookend follows one focal person — the one whose lunch brackets this chapter. At the open they are usually already in the room, waiting or setting up, so following them in means making their attention the frame while the household still crosses the threshold, because that is how Bramble physically enters. The recurring arrival beats — the lab hello, the four-knuckle fist-bump, the bramble-drink exchange, Mira taking her seat — pass as things the focal person registers, not as the scene's spine. *The household is the vehicle for the bot and the warmth; it is not the subject of these scenes, and letting it retake the frame is the failure mode the braid exists to fix.*

**Carry the braid's two pointers through the person, never the narrator.** The close points forward to the coming lunch; the open points back to the lunch just read. Render the pointer through the character or an object, in third person — the day's tape riding off to a woman Burns can't yet picture; the hearth Marge rebuilds at home and again in the cold warehouse; the warmth of the rendering itself, on the far side of the lunch — not through Cheryl naming her own appointments. *When a clean pointer would force a first-person self-reference, drop it. Reading order does the work, since the lunch sits one section away on either side.*

**Ghost in the room — third person, the bias in the seeing.** Cheryl is the seeing intelligence, felt in the depth and selection of her attention, never named, never talking to herself. The warmth she runs toward Marge shows in what the narration finds first, lingers on, and renders a size too large — not in a confession that it is doing so. First person belongs to the places Cheryl is present in her body — the Pitch, the lunches, and Ch 12; the bookends are neither, so spend none of it here. *Naming the bias kills it; enacting it through disproportionate attention is the bias working.*

**Standing truths in present, the scene in past — never eulogize the living.** A person's enduring traits belong in the present tense — *she cannot sit in a cold room without warming a corner; there is an hour after a chase when the man reaches for someone else's problem* — while the evening's actions stay past. The trap is the "she was a woman who —" copula, which sets a living trait in the past and makes the person sound dead. *The reconstructed-scene past is for what happened that night; it is not for who someone is.*

**Same building, opposite register — the same-house discipline.** Burns and Jasmine come home to one kitchen, so their codas must read as opposites. Jasmine's house closes around her and holds; Burns cannot find the place in it where a man is meant to stop. Render the difference through how each moves the identical furniture — Jasmine tends the bots; Burns picks one up and turns it over like a fossil and sets it down not quite where it was. *The contrast is the whole reason both codas can exist; collapse them and the second is redundant.*

**The well is the autopilot, fired once and unnamed.** Each home-follow is the person's ordinary coming-home ritual, and the ritual is the well: Burns turning a domestic object over like a specimen, standing a beat too long in the doorway, reaching past his own paper for the granite under Milford; Marge putting the kettle on in an empty house, both hands full so no one sees they are the only thing holding her up, the finished form she will not mail. Fire it in gesture, once, and do not label it — no *Pursuer*, no *exile* — in this register. Bilateral wells (Burns/Bart) fire once here, as gesture only. *The gesture carries the well harder than the name, and naming it in the tender register is the one move that breaks it.*

**One gesture, read three ways.** The same physical act tells the people apart: Burns turns a thing over to *narrate* it, Marge holds a thing to *comfort* it, Bart — when we reach him — turns a thing over to *fix* it. *Use the shared verb to separate them, not to blur them. The difference in why is the character.*

**Let the habitat answer the book's question.** Following each person home is how the book reaches their dwelling, and each dwelling answers whether a place can hold a person: Jasmine's holds; Burns is held by his against his nature; Marge built one she cannot fill; Lisa arranged one around an absence; Bart's real home is a workbench. The recurring habitat motifs — the bots in their spots on the slate, Marge's portable hearth, the radiant heat — carry the deep-time and inheritance spine without arguing it. *This is where the household-as-habitat lens stops being only the Bramble house and becomes everyone's.*

**End on the small true thing; withhold the verdict.** The Burns close lands on the tape gone off to someone he can't see and the eleven still to come; the Marge close lands on the tableau — kettle cooling, form on the counter, family on the wall, the apparatus built around the absence. *A tableau or a small true gesture closes the chapter. A summary judgment closes nothing.*

## Examples

**Anchor on the person; the household arrives inside the frame.** The arrival beats happen at the edge of his attention; he is the frame.

> Burns took all of this in from the bench, the warmth of the held bot still on his hands a second after Jasmine had taken her seat, and it was a small real thing against the abstract thing he had been turning over for six weeks. Lisa crossed to the camcorder and checked the battery and the light. He had perhaps a minute.

**The forward pointer, carried by an object.** The close points to the coming lunch through the tape, not through Cheryl.

> The tape of the day was in Lisa's car by now, bound for a woman he had shaken hands with exactly once, who had handed him twelve words and a warehouse and gone back up the canyon and whom he could not, sitting there, picture. He did not yet know she watched everything twice.

**The backward pointer, carried by the motif.** Post-lunch, no first-person; the hearth she built at home reappears in the warehouse.

> she cannot be in a cold room without making one warm corner of it — her own kitchen, this warehouse going black at the windows, the same hearth wherever the cold finds her.

**The bias in the seeing.** The warmth is in what the narration finds first and how large it draws her.

> The worried mouth was at rest and worried anyway. The cardigan was the brick-red of forty years of rain.

**Standing truth in present; the scene in past.**

> It is the first thing she does in any room she means to stay in — there was no cold to blame it on in August, only the habit of one warm corner — and she made one now in a kitchen with no one else in it.

> There is an hour after a chase when the man is alone with the table and reaches, even then, for a problem that belongs to somebody else.

**The same-house contrast, in one move.**

> Jasmine came home and the house closed around her and held. Burns came home and could not find the place in it where a man was meant to stop.

**The well as autopilot gesture, unnamed.** The specimen hand on a domestic object; the loneliness stated flat, not confessed.

> He turned it over in his hands the way he turned everything over — a knob, a vertebra, his son's drawing — looking on the underside for the part that made it go, and set it down not quite where it had been.

> both hands full so that no one would see they were the only thing holding her up. There was no one in the kitchen to see it.

**The Burns/Bart well, fired once in the doorway.** A single gesture, the deep read supplied and not improved on.

> the tenderness he spent there was owed somewhere else a long time ago and never paid — that he was being, in the dark, to a sleeping four-year-old, a father he had been across town to a five-year-old on occasion and by appointment. The boy was twenty-five now and in a duplex in Millcreek with a refrigerator that held only beer, and Burns was here, in this doorway, good at it.

**End on the tableau.**

> The kettle cooled on its ring. The form lay on the counter. The family stood in front of their temple on the wall. The whole apparatus of a home, built to a kind of perfection around the one absence it was raised to fill.

## Plans

The braid (see the orientation) sets who anchors each slot. This section says what each slot and each person's habitat wants. Each bookend brackets the week-11 retrospective, mid–final-month of its quarter, so the season fixes the light Cheryl reads: spring (Q1) and fall (Q3) sit in between, summer (Q2) holds long, winter (Q4) is dark from the start. The dates below are the retrospective's; the full calendar is in the orientation. Drafted entries are tuned to prose; forward entries are built from the wells and get tuned when their chapter is drafted. Add entries as the calls get made.

**The two slots.**

*The open — following in.* The gathering that ends when the red light comes on. Anchor on the focal person, usually already in the room; the household crosses the threshold inside the frame; close on the focal person about to have to do the thing the meeting asks of them. Carries the backward pointer to the lunch just read.

*The close — following home.* Begins when the red light goes out — contiguous with Burns's closing speech, which the speeches fork governs; the camera is the handoff. The team disperses; we follow the focal person out and into their habitat; carry the forward pointer to the coming lunch; end on the tableau or the small true thing.

**Burns — home (Ch 1 close, mid-May spring; recurs Ch 11 close, mid-November fall).** The Pursuer at rest in the household kitchen; the same-house discipline; the specimen-turn, the doorway, the trough that is reaching for someone else's problem. The Ch 11 close is Confluence-adjacent — coordinate with the speeches fork's canning-as-symptom and Confluence notes.

**Burns — in (Ch 2 open, mid-August summer).** Already in the room, carrying whatever the lunch put in him; the household arrives within his frame; the scene closes on him having to go do it. 

**Marge — home (Ch 2 close, mid-August summer; recurs Ch 5 close, mid-May spring, and Ch 9 close, mid-May spring).** The tended empty house, the portable hearth made permanent, the unmailed form, the photo wall she will not take down, the both-hands grip with no one to read it, the quiet disciplines. The Ch 5 close points forward to the church-exit lunch at The Roof, the records-removal form heavier in the bag; the Ch 9 close sits against the cleanse-lunch and the embodied-contradiction thread.

**Marge — in (Ch 3 open, mid-November fall; recurs Ch 6 open, mid-August summer, and Ch 10 open, mid-August summer).** Anchor on Marge waiting, the hearth made in the cold warehouse; the filter runs warm on the far side of the lunch; the fallacy she is about to guard sits ambient (Noble Savage at Ch 3). The Ch 6 open follows the church-exit lunch, the filter at its warmest.

**Household — Jasmine and Mira (Ch 1 open, mid-May spring; Ch 3 close, mid-November fall; Ch 4 open, February 12 winter; plus Jasmine's home-follows at Ch 3 close, mid-November fall, and Ch 6 close, mid-August summer).** The house that holds: stewardship of the bots, the flat hand on the dome, the radiant heat October through June, the deep-time kitchen. Our most-established register. The Ch 1 open is the genesis exception — anchored by the Bramble naming, which needs the household and Lisa's drink and Mira in the room, not by a prior lunch.

**Lisa — home (forward, Ch 4 close, February 12 winter; recurs Ch 10 close, mid-August summer).** Order built against the bohemian childhood, arranged around an absence; the run logged the moment she is through the door, the bramble at her own table, the kept cross. Likely runs a thin-narration discipline — a home you can describe to the inch and still not get inside. Tune against prose when drafted.

**Lisa — in (forward, Ch 5 open, mid-May spring; Ch 11 open, mid-November fall).** She runs Open and Close logistics, so her follow-in can be the director's-hand register — the one keeping the unpermissioned off the tape.

**Bart — home (forward, Ch 8 close, February 12 winter).** The committed Millcreek duplex: the workshop bay, the customer rebuild in the back, the beer-only refrigerator, the two engineer roommates. The hands need something the moment he is home; opacity-by-busyness, the Reactor who won't stop moving. Chassis-reveal-adjacent (Ch 8 humanoid) — coordinate with the speeches fork.

**Bart — in (forward, Ch 9 open, mid-May spring).** Surface-only, the Reactor who can't be drawn out — opacity from the opposite cause to the thin Lisa.

**Edge cases.**

*Genesis (Ch 1 open, mid-May spring).* Household, the naming, no prior lunch to point back to; anchored intrinsically.

*The Pitch (Ch 0, January, the warehouse).* The opening instance of the in-and-out device and the first home of the first person. Cheryl is bodily in the room — the only group scene besides Ch 12 — so the reconstructed third of the recruitment run clicks to first person at the threshold, runs first through her exit, and follows her up the canyon into the watching-life. Unlike the lunches, the interior stays shut: this is reticent first person, the bias shown through disproportionate attention and never named — the held look at Marge a beat past the end of the meeting, felt by Lisa, given nothing else to see. It opens the frame Terminal closes.

*Terminal (Ch 12, February 12 winter, the Olympics in the city).* The device turns on the narrator. Ch 11 still closes on Burns home, pointing forward to the Confluence lunch, but Ch 12 follows Cheryl in and out — the one retrospective whose bookends are hers, because she is finally in the room in person. Burns's home-and-lunch from the 11→12 interlude stand; his return is subsumed into Cheryl's arrival, the way the whole device resolves into her at the end. This is the terminal inversion: the seeing intelligence becomes a body crossing the threshold she stayed off for three years — the body last in the room at the Pitch — and following her out is the book's last movement, not a quarterly home-coda. It is one of the homes of the first person, with the lunches and the Pitch, and the close of the frame the Pitch opens. The Pitch performs the opening instance of the in-and-out — reconstructed third clicking to first person at the warehouse threshold, first through her exit, up the canyon — so Ch 12 either mirrors that entry or inverts it; the register call settles when the chapter is drafted.

*The seam with the speeches fork.* The close begins where the on-camera speech ends; the red light going out is the handoff. The two forks describe contiguous moments and should not contradict — most carefully at Ch 11, where the Burns-home close and the Confluence material meet.
