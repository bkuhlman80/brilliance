# Striking the Quarter-Notes — tuning fork
*Demolishing Marge's "Notes on the [N] Quarter" across Ch 1–12, and the narrow clause for what survives.*

**The job**

Marge's "Notes on the [N] Quarter" — the household-observation sections in her analytic voice, set after the lunch, identically headed and signed *—M.* — are struck book-wide. The floor episodes (see calvin_hobbes) inherit the slot. This doc governs the strike: how to demolish a quarter-note, and what, rarely, to carry out of it before it goes. Marge keeps her retrospective work and her follow-home; only the quarter-note is struck, and with it, in-world, the writing of quarter-notes at all. Marge composes no such sections.

**The default is demolition**

Toss the whole vignette. The burden of proof sits on keeping, never on cutting — the reverse of the usual instinct, which is to treat what is on the page as canon to be defended. It is not vandalism; it is correction. The quarter-notes were commentary, not action: almost no plot, no dialogue, a household watched from behind a clinician's glass and narrated at arm's length. Everything they carried now lives in better form on either side of them — the household's *experience* shown on the floor, the household's *theory* spoken at the table. The vignette is a third copy of things that already exist twice, in registers that beat it. So it goes, and it goes whole, and the reader loses nothing, because the reader already has it.

**Two reasons that are not reasons to keep**

A fragment does not earn out by being good. Good prose that is redundant is still redundant; cut it and do not mourn it. The quarter-notes had fine sentences in them, and the fine sentences are not the point — keeping a line because it is well made is the first failure mode, and it keeps the whole corpse warm.

A fragment does not earn out by being the theory-core. Theory lives at the table now, drawn out between people. The vignette's argued version is the third copy — the one struck *because* it is argued rather than shown or spoken. "This is the philosophical heart" is a reason to make sure the heart beats at the table; it is not a reason to keep the pen that wrote it down.

**The salvage test**

Carry a fragment out of the demolition only if both are true: it is rendered nowhere else — not shown on the floor, not spoken at the table, not carried by a bookend — *and* its loss leaves something already kept unmotivated or unintelligible. Both, not either. The bar is not "valuable." The bar is "load-bearing for something that survives." Almost nothing clears it.

**The four fates**

Every section of a struck vignette routes to one of four, and three of them are cut.

*Already at the table.* The fact or the theory is already spoken in the retrospective; the vignette was the redundant copy. Cut clean — nothing moves.

*Now on the floor.* The lived beat is rendered, or will be, as a Calvin-and-Hobbes episode. Cut — it lives elsewhere, better.

*Un-homed casualty.* Good material with no new home and no structural claim on the book. Cut, and let it go on purpose, named as gone rather than swallowed. It may resurface later on the floor if an episode genuinely reaches for it; it is never forced there to rescue it.

*Structural salvage.* It passes the test. Carry it to the table, compressed hard, in the right mouth — never as a new pen.

**How the survivor relocates**

What passes moves to the table, not to a fresh piece of writing. The lived fact enters through Jasmine's mouth: concrete, body-first, reported and not theorized. Whatever sliver of theory survives is grazed in conversation between people — Marge and Jasmine, even Marge and Mira — never delivered as a monologue. Cut it to a tenth of its vignette length, then look to cut again; the founding quarter's two whole arguments came out as a handful of spoken lines and a child's shrug.

Prefer the thing shown to the thing explained. When a child's non-answer enacts the point — she finds the question malformed, because there was no reason, which *is* the no-author point — keep the non-answer and cut the adult who would tell the reader what it meant. The reported fact stays; the gloss on the fact goes.

**Disciplines that ride the strike**

Scrub the citations. With the quarter-note retired in-world, every reference to it as a source goes too — a labmate listing "Marge's vignette" among the quarter's materials is now pointing at nothing.

Do not poach the floor. Table-talk reports the outward fact and leaves the interior to the episode that owns it. Jasmine says no one chose and it held; she never reaches for *why*, because the why — the tempo, the recognition — is the floor's, rendered in its own register and not to be spoken twice.

Respect the calendar in relocated dialogue. A beat moved into a specific retrospective inherits that meeting's date and everything keyed to it, the pronoun meter included. At a founding-quarter table the child says the name, not the earned pronoun, because the table sits before the summer that earns it.

**The worked example: Notes on the Founding Quarter**

The Ch 1 vignette ran six labeled sections. The strike sorted all six and carried out a single fragment.

*What we delivered, and what we withheld* — already at the table. The five fixed natures live in the build log; the withholding is the spine of Burns's open. Cut clean.

*The choosing* — the event is shown on the floor: Hello renders the no-author precipitation whole, the child coming down to the one paced like her, no deliberating self behind it. So the staged choosing is cut. What grazes the table is only what the floor cannot speak — the child, asked how she picked, finding the question malformed (*"Bramble,"* by way of an answer), and Jasmine's flat *first hour, never came loose.* The non-answer enacts the no-author point; the report states that it held; Marge's gloss on what it all meant is cut. This is the salvage test and shown-over-explained in one move.

*The house answers* — the holler, Burns wanting to be received rather than efficient: good Burns-and-household material, no structural claim, no new home. Un-homed casualty. Let go, on purpose. It may reach the floor in an episode someday; it is not forced there.

*Jasmine's hand* — the lone structural salvage. The week-two kneeling, the palm on the stone gone cold under the bot, is the *origin* of a decision the retrospective already keeps: Jasmine leaving the radiant heat on all summer. Cut the kneeling and that kept decision floats unmotivated. So it passes both tests — rendered nowhere else, load-bearing for a survivor — and relocates into her own mouth, rooting the radiant-heat reveal: she knew the bots by hand in week two, before April, before words, and that is why she trusted leaving the floor warm.

*The one that never settled* — now on the floor. The fourth-bot is a Calvin-and-Hobbes episode. Cut; it lives there, better.

*The Sunday wind-up* — already at the table. The ritual is absorbed into the knob beat: Mira's weekly crank is the whole personality, the household switching it on. Cut.

Six sections, one fragment carried out — and carried not because it was good, but because the radiant heat needed its reason.
