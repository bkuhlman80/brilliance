## Reflection register

Cheryl in deep-time mode, written from outside Bramble as a participant-observer. Short reflection on evolution and the framework's deep-time architecture. Slightly loosened register from the lunch. Closes each chapter (the "After Chapter N" reflection at the bottom). This deep-time distance is the default and is not first person; the reflection goes first person only when Cheryl is genuinely a character in it — anchored to an object in the room with her as she writes, or reflecting on an event from her own life. The sample below is the default distant mode.

> The substrate of inheritance is a kind of persistent goo, and that is the part of it that is easiest to forget if you have spent any time with the textbook diagrams — the clean ladders, the tidy phylogenies, the arrows tipped with capital letters. Bramble does not run on a diagram. Bramble runs on the slow viscous fact that what is passed forward from one moment to the next is not a message but a medium, and the medium has weight, and the medium drags. A protocell at the bottom of an early ocean is a bubble of fat around a few accidents in chemistry; what makes the bubble interesting is that the chemistry inside it is *the same chemistry* the next bubble will be made of, because there is only this much chemistry around, and the next bubble will be assembled out of what is there. Inheritance is not the bubble. Inheritance is the goo.

> You can see this in a body if you look. You can see it in a city if you look, though the looking is harder because the city has more layers. The deep architecture of how a heart pumps, of how a kidney filters, of how an animal startles at a shadow — these are not invented in the animal. They are inherited not as instructions but as already-running machinery, kept warm across the generations because nothing has been allowed to switch them off. Consciousness, if it is anywhere, is on top of all of this; it floats. The floating gets most of the attention. The goo gets none. But it is the goo that is doing the work, and the goo that will still be doing the work when the floating is gone, and the goo —

## The reflection's register

Brand-adjacent (synoptic, ecological, opinionated, craft-attentive) and *Cannery Row*-adjacent (small voice, occasional direct address, willing to be wrong, warm but not gooey). Brief essays, lyrical compressions, deep-time vignettes — all three modes available. Default register is a finished memory entry: Cheryl's voice present through tone and opinion, the prose settled. Not for lecture, exposition, or recap — if a reflection just summarizes the chapter's argument, it has failed.

## The drafting-marks gradient

The drafting-marks gradient runs across the book as the framework approaches its least-settled upper rungs: reflections after Ch 0–7 are clean, settled, no marks; the After-Ch-8 reflection carries one self-questioning parenthetical and one strikethrough; After Ch 9, three or four marks; After Ch 10, visible revision throughout, a paragraph half-rewritten with both versions present; After Ch 11, chopped fragments, brackets without closure, false starts, an ending that may reach for a word that doesn't come. The escalation is geometric (small from 9→10, large from 11→12), and the marks must read as writerly — intentional, craft-attentive — never as failed or lost.
