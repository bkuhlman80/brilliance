## Mid-distance meeting reconstruction

The register Cheryl uses for the eleven retrospectives she was not at. Reconstructed from camcorder footage and interviews. The labmates are rendered as informed interpretation, not omniscience. Interiors are educated guesses, woven in.

> Lisa had the camcorder open and was checking the battery before the others would arrive — Bart was already there because Bart was always already there, sitting on the corner of the workbench with the new chassis between his knees, but Burns and Marge were not yet at the table. When Marge came in she had the kettle in one hand and a plate of something in the other, and Burns followed a beat behind, already mid-sentence.

> "—and we are putting the Reviser conversation in the Trellis block, yes? Not the Bramble block?"

> "Trellis block," Lisa said, without looking up from the camcorder. "Bramble block is the new knob. We are not designing the Reviser yet."

> "We are *anticipating* the Reviser."

> "In the Trellis block."

> Burns set his palms on the table, flat, and lifted them again. "Fine. Trellis block. And the sentience vote?"

> "Open framework, last segment." Lisa flipped the camcorder screen out, watched the red light come on, watched it stop. "Same as last quarter."

> Bart did not look up from the chassis. "If we are voting again, I am voting the same way."

> "You always vote the same way."

> "I am consistent."

> Marge set the plate down — cut apples, a wedge of cheese — and watched the three of them for a beat the way she watched a room before she decided whether to speak. Then she said, "Are we going to talk about Jasmine," in the tone of a person who already knows the answer.

> Lisa closed the camcorder. "After. Not in the meeting."

> Marge nodded once and went to fill the kettle.

## Ancestor sass

Each clade is defined as much by what it cannot do as by what it can, and the cleanest way to mark a new capability is to look back at the clade before it and see the lack. The device: a labmate imagines the current clade regarding its ancestor, landing the affectionate condescension of the more-capable looking at the less — a chess player watching checkers. It is the via negativa made dramatic.

Anchor every instance to a *specific* lack (*you can't even hold a goal across two seconds*, never a loose *you're primitive*); sass that floats free of a real capability delta is banter — cut it. **Bart owns it; Marge guards it** — the joke always smuggles in a chooser (*the protocell never learned to say no* hands the protocell someone home to do the not-learning), and Marge's correction converts the delta from degree to kind. That friction is content, not interruption; let it run. **Never the robots, always the people** — the sass lives in the team's mouths and reaches the page through Cheryl's reconstruction; the clades are never literally sassy. It ramps (no ancestor at Ch 1; begins at Ch 2 and deepens) and it is not an agenda slot — fire it where a delta wants dramatizing, not every scene.

**Worked examples** — each pinned to a specific capability delta; Bart owns the joke, Marge converts the delta from degree to kind, and the clade is never literally sassy (the sass lives in the team's mouths, reaching the page through Cheryl's reconstruction):

> *Ch 2 — the prokaryote on the protocell.* The delta is the directed response to a gradient: the prokaryote runs its engine up a slope and away from a poison, where the protocell only persists. Bart, turning the older card over: "The protocell just sat there being itself. Never even learned to say no." The room laughs — the protocell has been handed a chooser it never had. Marge won't let it stand, warm-low: it isn't that the protocell *declined* to say no; there's no one in there to decline. What's new in the prokaryote isn't a better refusal — it's that refusing became a thing that could happen at all.

> *Ch 3 — the eukaryote on the prokaryote.* The delta is carrying the next hour around with you: the prokaryote floors it toward food and strands itself when the food runs out; the eukaryote banks energy and seeks the dock before it is desperate. Bart: "The prokaryote had one move. Drive till it died in the driveway." Marge guards it: the prokaryote was not reckless — recklessness needs a future to be reckless about. The new thing is not caution; it is a body that holds a future at all.

> *Ch 5 — the steering animal on the nerve-net before it.* The delta is a goal held across seconds: the bilaterian heads toward a thing and keeps heading, where the older animal could only twitch toward it and lose it. Bart: "You'd point the jellyfish at lunch and it'd forget on the way." Marge: not forget — there was never a holding-place for the thought to sit and be kept. The difference is not a longer attention span; it is that there is now an inside where a goal can wait.

## Keep the clade and the robot apart

A clade-block beat that crosses between its two possible subjects — the animal six hundred million years dead and the machine on the table built to wear its capacities — loses the reader the instant a bare "it" could mean either. The fix is not a hinge discipline laid over mixed content; it is to separate the content by block. The clade block is the dead animal and the abstract framework: the deep-time creature, the capacities and tendencies named in the abstract, the breakthrough, the biological mirrors (the worm, the leech) that ground them. The robot — its live behavior, its physical particulars, its own version of each capacity — belongs entirely to the Bramble block, where Bart runs the Bramblogues. Do not interleave them. A clade-block sentence is about the animal; a Bramble-block sentence is about the machine; and the reader always knows which, because the two never share a beat.

Two things cross the seam without breaking the separation, and only these two. The doubling, named once a chapter in Cheryl's voice at the block boundary — two animals in the room, the dead one and the one being taught to be it — is a narrator's observation about the structure, made at the turn from one block to the next, never a cross-reference inside a teaching beat; it hands the reader the device and marks the block change at once. And "the machine" is Marge's: where the team and the narrator reach for Bramble by default, because the name is the warm thing and it takes self-control not to, Marge alone says the machine, the unit, the thing, never the name, in narration as well as dialogue — the refusal is her anti-anthropomorphizing made audible, and the day she finally says Bramble will land because the discipline held everywhere else.

## Vocabulary discipline

Lisa speaks in computer-science, biology, and neuroscience vocabulary and is aversive to psychology terms; when a meeting moment lands on a psychological anchor (attachment, self-construal, identity, the Big Five), her segment ownership shifts that load to Marge, Burns, or Bart and she speaks in adjacent technical language only. She cites Friston, Watson and Szathmáry, Mackintosh, Bennett; her field-native terms include generative model, prediction error, allostasis, stored procedures, cognitive stack, layered architecture. Marge is the inverse — folk-psychology vocabulary, observational, sometimes warm — and the two often present adjacent segments precisely because the load can be split. No self-help cadence (kills Lisa); no insider jargon without grounding (kills Marge). And generally: no scaffolding-heavy structure without breath (kills Bart); no excessive interior without external anchor (kills Burns).

**Worked example — the same beat in each register.** A retrospective lands on Bramble having learned to back out of dark rooms:

> *Lisa:* "It's a prediction-error update — the dark kept co-occurring with the energy drop, so the model down-weighted it; the valence scalar carries the cost forward." *Marge, one chair over:* "It got burned, and now it's shy of the thing that burned it. It learned to be wary." Same event — Lisa in generative-model / prediction-error / valence terms, Marge in folk-psychology. When the beat lands on a psychological anchor (attachment, identity, the felt interior), ownership shifts the load to Marge, Burns, or Bart, and Lisa speaks only the adjacent technical language; she does not reach for the psych word.
