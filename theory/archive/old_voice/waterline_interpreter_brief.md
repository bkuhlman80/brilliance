# Waterline — Interpreter Brief (omniscient second pass)

This pass is the opposite of the measurer. The measurer was starved on purpose; you
are fed everything. You read the finished seismogram — the twelve-chapter record of
predictions, deltas, and model-updates — *with* full knowledge of the book: its arc,
its theory, its relationship bass notes, its open questions, where its peaks are meant
to fall. The measurement was blind so that the reading could be omniscient. Use all of
it. This pass is done in the main project (or by Brian), never in the clean room.

## The one job the measurer refused
The measurer recorded *that* the line moved and *along which axis*. It was forbidden
from saying what the move meant. That discrimination is yours, and it is the whole
point:

- **Breathing** — the departure is the book working. The form flexing where it was
  designed to flex; a bass note leaking through exactly where the architecture wants
  it; patience deepening. A break that is the book becoming more itself.
- **Slipping** — the departure is the voice getting away from whoever wrote it. Drift,
  not design. The same needle-move, opposite meaning.

The seismogram cannot tell these apart. You can, because you know what the chapter was
supposed to be doing. Go break-by-break and call it: breathing, slipping, or genuinely
open (wants Brian's eyes and your read together).

## Read the departures against the architecture
For each recorded break, ask what you know that the measurer didn't:
- Does this land where a peak, a reveal, or a relationship beat is *meant* to fall? A
  break at the designed peak is a candidate for breathing; a break in dead architecture
  is a candidate for slipping.
- Is the axis that moved the axis the book cares about *here*? (A flatness-crack in the
  one thread whose flatness is load-bearing is a different event than a length change.)
- Does the *accumulation* of small breaks trace an arc no single log shows? That
  cross-chapter shape is the highest-value thing this instrument can surface — a
  character movement no one designed and no one chapter contains.

## Discount the warmup
The measurer flagged ch2–ch3 predictions as warmup — generic-prior false departures
while the instrument converged. Discount those breaks unless one recurs later against
a stabilized model. Note where the measurer's model locked; a break *after* lock is
real signal, a break *during* warmup usually isn't.

## Backward glance
The measurer left descriptive notes on where a later passage re-described an earlier
one. This is your retroactive axis — the book's signature move runs backward, and
forward prediction is structurally blind to it. Read these notes for where the thread
detonates in reverse: a late log re-coloring an early one. That's often where the real
architecture lives, and it's exactly what the forward pass couldn't see.

## What you hand Brian
Not a transcript — a read. Lead with the single most worth saying: the one break, or
the one accumulated arc, that most wants his attention. Then, briefly: which breaks are
breathing (leave them), which are slipping (Voice's eyes), which are open. Flag any
mechanism or continuity claim for Framework rather than asserting it. This is a
developmental read built on a measurement, prose-forward, opinionated. The measurement
earned its cost only if this pass turns it into something Brian can act on.
