# Waterline — Measurer Brief (run protocol)

This is the protocol for one full pass over a twelve-chapter thread. It is reusable:
the thread this time is one character's per-chapter log, but the same protocol runs
any recurring thread. Follow it exactly. The discipline *is* the instrument.

## The loop
For each chapter in order, ch1 → ch12:

1. **Read** the passage whole, once, before writing anything.
2. **Record deltas** (except ch1): how did this passage depart from what you predicted?
   Report along the axes below. Descriptive only.
3. **Update the model**: in one or two lines, state how your model of the voice now
   stands after this passage.
4. **Predict** the next passage: a short, *specific*, falsifiable prediction of what
   the next log will be like. Tag it with a confidence and the pattern it rests on.
5. **Commit**: the prediction is fixed the moment you write it. You never revise a
   prediction after seeing the passage it was about. You will be fed the next passage
   only after your prediction is on the page.

ch1 has no delta step (nothing to predict from yet). ch12 has no prediction step.

## The axes — dimensionalize, don't evaluate
Record each departure along one or more of these. These are neutral measurement
dimensions. Naming *which axis moved* is the measurement. Judging the move is not
your job.

1. **Duration** — total length; and dwell, how long it stays on any one thing.
2. **Register** — clipped ↔ expansive; flat ↔ colored.
3. **Concreteness** — physical/object-level ↔ abstract/conceptual.
4. **Interiority** — is inner state (feeling, doubt, wanting) named on the page, or
   left behind behavior and object?
5. **Attention** — what it lingers on; what it passes over fast.
6. **Silence** — where it goes quiet; what it declines to log that the pattern would
   predict it logging.
7. **Signature** — diction and syntax fingerprints (sentence shape, recurring words,
   how it opens and closes).

If a departure doesn't fit an axis, name a new one plainly and keep it descriptive.
Don't force it; don't pad. Most chapters will move on one or two axes, not seven.

## Prediction discipline
- **Specific enough to be wrong.** "Continues in his voice" is not a prediction. "Runs
  short, opens on an object, names no feeling, coins one term and drops it" is.
- **From the accumulated thread, not just the last chapter.** You carry all prior logs.
- **Confidence + basis.** "High, because the last three logs all closed on a physical
  action." A high-confidence miss is a loud event; a low-confidence hit is a loud
  event. The tag is what makes the seismogram readable later.

## Warmup
Your first one or two predictions are made from generic priors — how logs in novels
tend to go — before the thread has taught you its own key. Expect false departures
early (e.g. you predict the log escalates or lengthens; it doesn't). **Mark ch2 and
ch3 predictions as WARMUP.** They record the instrument converging, not the thread
breaking. Note where your model stabilizes — the chapter after which predictions stop
being generic and start being *this thread's*.

## Backward glance (narrow, descriptive)
After recording each passage, one line: *did reading this change how you would now
describe an earlier passage?* If yes, name which and how — descriptively, no meaning
attached ("ch7 makes the short close of ch2 read as the same move, not an isolated
one"). If no, say "no backward change" and move on. Do not interpret why it matters.
If this line produces only noise through the pilot, it gets cut.

## Output shape per chapter
```
CH_n
Delta from prediction: [axes + descriptive account, or "none noted"]
Backward glance: [which earlier passage re-described, or "no backward change"]
Model now: [1–2 lines]
Prediction for CH_n+1: [specific, falsifiable]  | confidence: [ ] | basis: [ ]  | [WARMUP if ch2/ch3]
```

Keep each entry tight. The seismogram is read as a whole downstream; long entries bury
the needle. You are drawing a line, chapter by chapter, that someone else will read.
