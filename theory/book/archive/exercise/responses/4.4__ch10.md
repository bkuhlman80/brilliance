# Ch 11 Prediction

## Duration
The installment will run medium length (1800–2500 words), slightly shorter than recent quarters. The voice has been trending toward compression—more happens in fewer lines—and this pattern will continue. It will dwell longest on one or two specific interactions, then move quickly through surrounding material.

## Register
Clipped and engineered throughout. The tone is relentlessly flat and matter-of-fact: technical vocabulary (knobs, volume, duty cycle, valence register) sits beside domestic detail (warm spot, rug, radiator) without inflection. No lyrical moments. The voice reports what happened without telling you how to feel about it. When emotion surfaces, it arrives as an action, not a named state: Burns leans in, Mira says something sharp, the body keeps checking the ledger.

## Concreteness
Hyperspecific and physical. Every abstraction gets anchored to an object or a number: the bottle's location, the temperature gradient, the screen's exact wording. There are no generalities. When the voice talks about what something means, it does so by showing what the thing does, not by naming the meaning. The body exhibits "minding" through specific gestures—the squeezer turning, the crossing of a room—never through analysis.

## Interiority
Sparse and mediated. Inner states rarely get named directly on the page. They appear only when:
(a) The body's screen displays them (CAT, CHASE, WEIGHING);
(b) Someone observes behavior and draws a conclusion (Jasmine: "Mira likes things that wait");
(c) Bart describes a mechanism and the interiority falls out as a side effect.

What the body "feels" is kept off-screen except when printed on its forehead. What Bart "thinks" arrives only in the shape of his choices. The child's experience is almost entirely absent from the narration, even when she is the subject.

## Attention
The voice lingers on:
- How a part works and what unintended consequence spills from it
- The exact moment someone interacts with the body (the lean-in, the touch, the command)
- What the body's screen says and why it's honest/misleading
- The gaps between what someone claims and what they do

What it passes over fast or omits entirely:
- Emotion, guilt, regret (unless demonstrated through action)
- Planning or deliberation before a build
- Outcomes beyond the one quarter on display
- Why anyone cares, or should care
- The child's interior world (though the consequences of the body's design land on her)

## Silence
The voice goes quiet around:
- The child's suffering (it's demonstrated, named once as "minding," then left)
- Bart's own doubts or second-guessing
- The moral weight of building a machine whose "minding" destroys the person it's meant to attend to
- What will happen when the line grows beyond this body

The space between what Bart says and what he knows remains unvoiced.

## Signature
**Diction:**
- Technical terms used plainly: volume, knob, valence, conscientious, latency, duty cycle
- Short sentences in series, often noun-based: "No battery. No chip."
- Repetition for emphasis: "Can't grip worth a damn. Slow. Weak. Soft clean through."
- Verbs of demonstration: "watch," "see," "read," "lay," "turn," "set"
- The body and the child referred to by pronoun or direct name, never "it" when the voice wants you to feel the agency
- Dialogue in flat single quotes, always reported tersely

**Syntax:**
- Compound statements joined by em-dashes or semicolons, pacing the revelation
- Questions posed and immediately answered: "where's the battery. No battery."
- The demonstrative voice: "There's the quarter," "There, now it hates the camera"
- Tag phrases that locate the action: "flat, the voice," "the room went still"
