# Prediction for Ch 2

## Duration & Attention
Expect medium length, roughly comparable to Ch 1. The voice dwells longest on small displacements—objects touched and set down, spaces occupied and left. It moves quickly past exposition and named emotion, slower over physical proximity and states of waiting. The opening likely finds Burns in a room again, either home or work, and something small triggers the kind of stillness we see him seeking.

## Register
Clipped but not sparse; the sentences are built from restraint, not brevity. Expansive in structure—long chains of dependent clauses that circle a thought rather than stating it. Colored with temperature, light, and spatial proportion, but the coloring serves observation, not atmosphere-building. Flat across most action, punctuated by moments of precision in how things are described (the bot's twitch, the light in slabs, Jasmine closing around the house).

## Interiority
Inner state is named sparingly and only through negation or irony: he did not let himself know, what he wanted was not to break the room down. The real work happens at behavior-level—what he touches, how long he stands, where he positions himself. Expect Ch 2 to continue this pattern: feeling will arrive as the residue of an action, not announced. If something approaches emotion, it will be undercut, reframed as displacement or ritual.

## Signature Moves
- Negative construction: "The house did not need breaking down," "He did not crank it," "did not let himself know"
- Sentences that double back: "what he wanted was not X. What he wanted was Y."
- Light and time as texture, not metaphor: "the light came down in slabs," "long and slow to go"
- A body in a room, taking up space, not quite belonging to it
- Distances named precisely: "five-year-old on occasion and by appointment," "twenty-five now," "across town"
- Owed/unpaid emotional debts emerging through displacement

## Concreteness
Highly concrete and object-level. The bots are the most specific thing in the chapter—wound out and still, one in the towel, two by the island. Expect Ch 2 to maintain this specificity. Abstract thought will be avoided; if something is understood, it is shown through what is touched, held, set down, or left untouched.

## Silence
The chapter is silent on: Jasmine's interiority, the boy's life or feelings, what the meeting was about, why this displacement matters to Burns morally. Expect these silences to deepen. If we see the boy or hear Jasmine's perspective, it will be mediated through Burns's distance from it. The lab and its purposes remain unnamed.

## Prediction for Ch 2

Ch 2 will open with Burns in a space—home or lab or the car between them—where he is alone or nearly alone. Something small will be the subject of attention: an object, a light change, a sound, or the arrangement of a room. The prose will be structured in linked observations, moving from the particular to its wider pattern without stating the pattern.

A form of work or ritual will occupy the middle—something he does with his hands, something repetitive or precise. His interiority will surface only as negation or misalignment: something he does not feel, or feels in the wrong direction, or owes to someone else.

The chapter will not end with resolution or clarity. It will end with him in a state of waiting or small displacement—a room entered but not inhabited, an object placed not quite where it was, another evening light going down. If there is a name for what he's doing, it will not be given. If there is a reason, it will remain behind behavior.

Length: 1,200–1,600 words, comparable to Ch 1.

**Confidence: HIGH**
**Basis: Consistent voice architecture across sentences, repeated structural moves (negation, doubling back, object-focus), and his repeated positioning as a man seeking solitude within obligation. These are not plot devices but signature patterns of thinking.**
