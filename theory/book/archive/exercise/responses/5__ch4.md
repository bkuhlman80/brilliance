# Prediction for Chapter 5

## Confidence: Medium-High

## Duration
The chapter will be roughly 1200-1500 words, in the medium range like Ch 2 and Ch 4. It will linger on physical detail and interior observation in roughly equal measure, without the sprawling May-evening meditation of Ch 1 or the compressed focus on held objects of Ch 3.

## Register
The register will be primarily clipped and flat in outer description, with occasional expansions into interiority. The voice tilts toward precision and restrained coloring—"the way [something] is"—rather than emotional elaboration. Sentences will have the characteristic modest length punctuated by a few longer ones that coil and contain multiple truths at once.

## Concreteness
Very high. The new character will be introduced through objects, rooms, and small repeated physical acts—a kettle, a coat removed, a route taken, something picked up or set down. Abstract feeling will not be named directly but will live in the gaps between what is done and what is wanted, the way it lives across all four chapters.

## Interiority
Inner state will be present but mostly withheld from direct naming. Where it does appear, it will come late in the chapter, often in the form of an observation about what the character does rather than how they feel—"she did not let herself know," "he knew," "the tenderness... was owed." The voice prefers behavior and its small displacements to the naming of feeling.

## Attention
The new character will be observed in a room or space they occupy alone or at the edge of the group. The chapter will linger on what they do at the threshold of leaving or staying—the deliberation in small acts, the significance of small refusals or acceptances. It will pass quickly over dialogue or explanation; it will dwell on what the hands do, what is carried, what remains.

## Silence
The chapter will not explain why the character is absent from the lab's inner circle or what their relationship to the others is—that will have to be inferred. It will not name shame or longing directly, though both may be present in what is withheld. It will not describe the character's appearance except insofar as their body is a tool for some small task.

## Signature
- Repeated use of "the way" constructions to establish pattern or habit ("the way a person stacks a future"; "the way he turned over")
- Parallel structures that hold two truths at once: "X was Y but also Z," establishing contradiction without resolving it
- Short, factual sentences interrupted by longer ones that contain multiple clauses joined by "and"—accumulation rather than explanation
- Objects used as mirrors for inner state: a kettle, a form unsigned and unmailed, a bot wound out and still, a cross not looked at
- Verb choice that emphasizes small, repeated, almost involuntary acts: "set down," "did not let herself," "reached over," "turned over"
- The final line or image will be a quiet displacement, a small truth held in stillness or left undone

## Predicted Opening
The chapter will begin with a small, concrete action—likely the new character either leaving the lab building or in their car driving away, or already at home doing something at the boundary between ritual and avoidance. Time will be specific (November? evening? after the others have gone?). The voice will establish quickly that this is a fourth interior space we're entering.

## Predicted Closing
The chapter will not end with resolution or comfort. It will end with the character in a state of held tension—not yet doing what they are building toward, or having just declined to do it, or in the middle of a small repeated act that stands for something larger they cannot do. The last image will be physical and still, the way the footwell held its click, the way the fog wanted in, the way Lisa did not think.

## Predicted Length of Dwelling
The chapter will spend considerable time on one repeated or near-repeated action—perhaps a return to a place, or the carrying of something, or the preparation of something that does not get finished. The time spent on this will be disproportionate to the time it actually takes, the way Burns stood in the kitchen and Marge held the cup and Jasmine's hand rested on the dome.

## Pattern This Rests On
- The recurring structure: Leave the lab → Enter private space → Perform a small repeated ritual or act of displacement → Hold a moment of undone tension at the close
- The voice's preference for showing interior state through withholding and displacement rather than naming
- The four characters' structural parallel: each stands at a threshold between belonging and not belonging, each performs an act that is both necessary and a cover for some other act, each ends in a space that is defined by what it holds in or holds out
- The emerging pattern that the fifth character will complete or complicate this geometry
