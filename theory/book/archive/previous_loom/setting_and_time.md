# Setting and Time — Canon (locked)

*Voice controls the calendar, not Framework.*

## Setting

Salt Lake City. Three calendar years: spring 2031 through winter 2033–34. The household — Burns, his wife Jasmine, their daughter Mira — lives in the city. The lab is an open warehouse in the Granary district; its public name is the Trellis Lab, and the team calls the facility *Smithers* ("I gotta get back to Smithers"). The four labmates are colleagues at Westminster University — Burns in paleontology, Lisa in computer science, Marge in psychology — who had worked together over the years without sharing a lab space. The team is ad-hoc, assembled when Burns answered Cheryl's call. Meetings happen quarterly, in person, at Smithers.

## The calendar structure

Quarters map to seasons: Q1 spring, Q2 summer, Q3 fall, Q4 winter. The founding — the morning the first five units went to the Burns house — was **February 12, 2031, Mira's fourth birthday.** Each Q4 retrospective falls on a later **February 12**; at Ch 4 and Ch 8 it physically reveals the next line's chassis *body* as Mira's birthday present — the one finished artifact shown at a meeting; the functional version running on that body arrives at the next quarter's week-1 swap, with the vacuum staying on its rounds. The two chassis-reveal retrospectives land on her birthday: **Ch 4 (Feb 12, 2032, Mira turns five)** and **Ch 8 (Feb 12, 2033, six)**. **Ch 12 (Feb 12, 2034, seven)** is terminal and carries no chassis reveal.

**Calendar.** Each chapter occupies one calendar-season quarter. Within the quarter, four dated events anchor the chapter and its preceding interlude: Bart's build log (week 1, at quarter open, filed when the upgraded unit goes back to the house), Cheryl's lunch (week 9, late in the quarter's middle month), Marge's vignette (week 10, early in the final month), the retrospective (week 11, mid–final-month — except the three Q4 reveals, which land on Feb 12).

The braid runs against this calendar: the previous chapter's close points forward to the lunch, the lunch and vignette sit inside the upcoming quarter, the next chapter's open points back to the lunch.

The Salt Lake City Winter Olympics fall in February 2034, inside Ch 12. They are ambient weather, not theme — the book never argues for or about them; use them when a scene needs the city transformed, otherwise they sit in the background. The same "weather, not theme" rule holds for the calendar generally: the date is the date; voice handles the texture.

## Household history

Burns arrived in Utah around 2001, at twenty-eight, drawn by the deep-time geology. He married Ana — from a prominent, wealthy LDS dynasty (Romney-coded), always-Utah — around 2004–05. Bart was born in 2006. The marriage ended around 2011 on bad terms (Burns's weed and Adderall use, his failure to climb into her family's good graces, an affair Ana caught); Bart was in kindergarten and was raised mostly by Ana, with Burns "on occasion." Ana still lives in Park City, near Cheryl. Burns married Jasmine in the years that followed; Mira was born in 2027. Cascade: Jasmine (mid-30s) is about ten years older than her stepson Bart (twenty-five); Bart is twenty-one years older than his half-sister Mira. "Married into staying" is the read — Burns came young for the geology, married into a dynasty that rooted him, and after the divorce was too embedded (career at Westminster, a son held in Utah by an always-Utah mother whose family owns the place) to leave. Available, not built: a Cheryl–Ana brush in Park City, two wealthy women at opposite poles — LDS dynasty vs. secular old-California cannery money.

## Salt Lake addresses

Salt Lake City runs on a grid centered on the downtown temple, and nearly every address is a grid coordinate (e.g., 800 E 500 S). Locals don't say "eight-hundred east, five-hundred south" — they say "eighth east, fifth south."

## The technology backdrop

By summer 2031 the commercial humanoid wave is five years deeper than its 2026 state: humanoids in homes are real if not universal, AI personality is a debate ordinary people have opinions about, the commercial players part of the cultural air. The Trellis Lab is not a competitor — they ship no product; they are deriving a theory of personality by building one weird robot from the bottom of evolution up, and the wave's existence makes their work both more legible and stranger. Burns has opinions about Tesla Optimus; Lisa has read Figure's papers; Marge has concerns about how families attach to commercial humanoids they didn't grow alongside. The book sits adjacent to the wave, asking a deeper question.

## Ambient weather

Background facts that hold whether or not the prose foregrounds them: the Great Salt Lake has been drying for two decades and by 2031–34 shapes state politics, air quality, and what the team sees out the warehouse windows in late summer; the city keeps growing fast; the post-Mormon reorientation continues, and the team embodies its spectrum (Marge formally resigning, Jasmine lapsed but too indifferent to leave the rolls, Bart still on the books and faking it for the dating pool); two presidential cycles fall inside the arc (2032, and the run-up to 2036), which the book may or may not notice; the Granary around Smithers gentrifies through industrial-adaptive reuse, the lab a holdout running real industry in a neighborhood turning creative; and Westminster rebranded from College to University in 2023 and, like most small private liberal-arts schools, sits under structural pressure — making the team's day-job home slightly precarious, and part of what Cheryl's money keeps alive. The Olympic venues at Park City, Soldier Hollow, and the Olympic Park are all within an hour of Cheryl's Park City home.
