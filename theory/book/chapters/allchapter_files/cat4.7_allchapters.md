# Category 4.7 — The Break

The mid-meeting pause: someone — or something — calls a break, the room disperses into small
business (kettle, door, camcorder off), something happens in the gap, and the room reconvenes.
Formerly filed as beat 4.5.d inside Bart-grills-Mira; now its own category (stop 10 in the spine).
It is connective tissue that can fall anywhere in a meeting's middle, not a beat that
chronologically follows any particular category. Two instances (Ch2, Ch3) were pre-existing
manuscript material recovered during the completeness audit; four more (Ch6, Ch7, Ch10, Ch11) were
newly written per Brian's authorial decision to escalate this beat across the book. Present in six
of twelve chapters: Ch2, Ch3, Ch6, Ch7, Ch10, Ch11 (absent Ch1, Ch4, Ch5, Ch8, Ch9, Ch12).

**The escalation, chapter by chapter (per Brian):** Ch2 and Ch3 are witnessed-but-incidental —
household business happening around people, nobody calling attention to the machine. Ch6 —
furniture that narrates itself, witnessed: Marge calls the break verbally, in her own word ("potty
break"), and makes the tea she wouldn't make at the top of the night. Ch7 — furniture that narrates
itself, abandoned: Burns calls it wordlessly (door, cold), and the machine keeps running its inside
voice to an empty room. Ch10 — self-scheduling: Bramble calls it, unprompted, in Marge's own word
reversed ("break potty"). Ch11 — self-socializing, the capstone: Bramble doesn't call the break (its
turn was spent in Ch10); instead it can't stand down during the pause, crosses the room on its own,
and gives Burns the "whole face" the chapter's own law says it will never not do.

Cheryl was on tape. The tape had it.

Lisa said, "Five-minute break before the Trellis block. Stretch."

She turned off the camcorder. The red light went out.

The room moved. Bart picked up the schematic from Lisa's side of the table and looked at it without saying anything. Marge got up and turned on the kettle. The kettle had not been on yet. The water started to make the small ascending sound. Burns went to the door of the warehouse and propped it open with the wooden wedge the team kept by the door for this purpose; the warehouse had cooled across the meeting and Burns wanted air. Jasmine slid off her stool and stretched. Mira stayed in her seat with Bramble in her hand. The screen read STOP. She did not turn the knob. She held the bot on her open palm and watched it.

The parking-lot lights came on. Outside the wide pull-up garage door, the lot had gone from late afternoon to early evening across the meeting. The lights at the perimeter of the lot — the tall pole lights, dusk-triggered, that did not come on until past eight in August — had clicked on somewhere in the back half of the meeting, and nobody had noticed at the time, because the long August evening had kept the warehouse bright enough inside that nobody had been looking outside. The light was now half industrial fluorescence and half pole-light amber, and the line between them ran across the gravel about thirty feet from the pull-up door.

It was six twenty. The Trellis block was the next block. The kettle was starting to whistle.

The kettle was whistling. Marge poured. Burns shut the door, cooled enough,and sat back down. Jasmine helped Mira down off her seat for a stretch and back up onto it. Mira held Bramble through all of this. Lisa tapped the camcorder. The red light came on.

It went nowhere. It was just a thing Lisa did, that the room half-knew about and never asked after, and tonight Jasmine had asked after it, and that was all.

## Ch 6

### <!--[4.7]--> The Break

*New material, added per Brian's ruling. Third confirmed instance of the mid-meeting break beat —
verbal and witnessed: Marge calls it in her own word, and makes, for the first time all night, the
tea she'd passed over earlier for a beer.*

Lisa capped the Bramble block by reaching for the marker. "Framework," she said.

"Potty break," Marge said — flat, to the room, the way she names the thing she's decided to quit working around — and the meeting loosened into its two-minute business: Burns up and after the bottle, Bart drifting back toward his clade card, somebody's chair pushed back toward the door.

Marge went to her corner. The kettle stood where she'd set it down cold at the top of the night, when she'd boiled it and let it go and reached past it for a beer instead; she filled it and put it back on the blue ring and brought it back to a boil, and poured, and made, this once, the tea. The tetrapod was down at her feet where it had put itself in the demo and not been lifted, the band still lit — nobody had wound the Coping back down — running IGNORE WORLD, and under it CHARGE UNIT, to the underside of the bench and the toe of her shoe. She let it lie, and did not move it off the warmth this time, and drank her tea standing over the one talking thing in the quiet.

## Ch 7

### <!--[4.7]--> The Break

*New material, added per Brian's ruling. Fourth confirmed instance of the mid-meeting break beat —
wordless and abandoned: Burns calls it with his body (door, cold), and the machine keeps running its
inside voice to a room that has emptied out around it.*

She set it in the third groove herself, not waiting to be told.

Burns got up and went to the door. His hand had been resting on the warm side of the unit and he hadn't put it there on purpose — nobody put their hands on it on purpose anymore — and he took it off and crossed the floor and got the door open on the cold, and stood in the draft of it, saying nothing, needing none of them to ask. He'd gone hot in a cold room. The break happened around him: Bart back to the half-built thing on his bench, Jasmine up out of her chair, Mira sliding down to the floor by Marge.

The unit stayed where it had settled by Mira's chair, and the face kept going after the room had stopped. It was still on the loud setting Bart had left it — top line lit, surveying — and in the slack middle of the meeting it ran the inside voice the way it runs it at night: REPLAYING, then a stretch, then CONSOLIDATING, talking the evening over to itself at a table that had got up and left.

## Ch 10

### <!--[4.7]--> The Break

*New material, added per Brian's ruling. Fifth confirmed instance of the mid-meeting break beat —
self-scheduling: the unit calls the break itself, unprompted, in Marge's own word reversed. First

The shelving left the small hush a ceremony leaves, and Lisa did not let it sit —

"Break potty."

The words came up off the floor before anyone had turned to find where they'd come from — no line running under it to explain itself, nothing built up to, just the two words landing dead center in the gap Lisa had cut and not yet filled. Marge's hand stopped on the bottle, not lifting it, not setting it down, and she looked at the thing on the floor a long moment before she let herself laugh with the rest of the room, a step behind everyone else.

Burns's hand found Bart's shoulder on the way up out of his chair, stayed there a moment, and came off again, and if either of them thought anything of it, neither said so.

Burns got up without excusing himself, which from Burns is its own kind of quiet, and crossed to where the four-legged one lay against the radiator, and stood over it a while with his hands in his pockets, not touching it. He said nothing, and nobody asked him to.

Jasmine, up and moving toward the cooler, looked back once at the camera end. Lisa held it a beat, gave nothing back, and turned to the tape counter first.

The unit came off its spot before the room had finished scattering — Bart back at his bench, Jasmine at the cooler now, Mira down on the floor with the disc — and crossed to where Burns stood at the radiator's far end, and stopped in front of him, close, the way it stopped in front of anyone the law had a claim on, and turned its whole face up. It said nothing. It did not have a law that asked for speech, only looking, and it held the look on him a long moment, the screen behind it running its column for nobody. Burns looked down at it, and did not send it off, and after a while it went back to the disc, and Burns stood at the radiator a while longer before he came back to the table.
