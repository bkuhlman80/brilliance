# **The Knob-Name tuning fork**

*One shape (bar knob-1), one set of names, one way to read a setting. Eleven identical two-pole volume knobs and one wind-up; the proper name carries the page; "volume up / volume down" carries the setting. Only two mouths — Mira's and Bramble's — hold the rebrand words. First devotion is to the reader.*

---

## The twelve knobs

> **This doc is the naming authority.** The **Proper Name** column below is canonical. The **Slot-label (Tendency)** column is a second, legal register, not a competing name. When another doc's name disagrees with this table, this table wins and the other doc is the reference to fix.
>
> A knob's **Proper Name names its volume-up (derived) pole**; the **Slot-label names the clinical construct**, which for Neuroticism names the volume-down pole. This is why knob-9's Proper Name is **Resolve** (its up pole, Clutch) while its Slot-label stays **Neuroticism** (the construct, whose high end is this knob's down pole).

**Knobs 2–12 are the same shape**: a two-pole volume knob. It runs from volume down to volume up, and the two poles are the whole of it. **Volume up is the derived setting** — the quarter's new layer active: the tendency, the capacities, and the personality component Lisa's board serves up, all switched on. **Volume down is the primordial setting** — the new layer dormant, the older architecture showing through, so the unit runs as it did the previous quarter. There is no third stop, no "off," no selector, no engagement-vs-position. Two poles, and what each knob is the volume *of*.

**Volume down is not a low dose of the new tendency.** It is the new tendency dark and the ancestor beneath running in its place. The test is behavioural and admits no judgment: a beat in which the unit reverts to the previous quarter is at volume down. Volume up is the tendency present and driving.

**Knob-1 (Going) is the one exception.** It is a wind-up volume knob: you wind it up (alive) and it drains down across the week (dead), a volume running continuously from full up to all the way dead. It is the only continuous knob and the only one that never crosses to another board.

**Orienting (knob-5) is the credit-and-blame knob** — how far back the unit reaches to decide what to thank or blame for the way a moment turned out. It toggles the two heuristics of its own edge. **Volume up (derived, recency) is the eligibility trace**: short-horizon, it pins credit on whatever was nearest and freshest when the moment turned, fair or not. **Volume down (primordial, novelty) is latent inhibition**: long-horizon, it won't charge a thing without seeing it repeat, and writes the standing, familiar world off as furniture. It has nothing to do with time-of-day or the sleep/wake cycle; that cycle is a separate, warmth-driven trait of the body, not a knob.

## Naming a knob, setting a knob

- A knob is **"the [Proper] knob"** or **"a volume knob on [Proper]."**
- A setting is **"[proper] volume up / down"**, or — once the knob is named in the beat — bare **"volume up / down."** Drop the redundant "knob": write *the Coping volume up*, never *the Coping knob volume up*.
- **Volume down is the primordial setting: the new layer dark, the unit reverting to the previous quarter.** The test is behavioural and admits no judgment: a beat in which the unit reverts to the previous quarter is at volume down, whatever the sentence currently says. (Knob-1 has no up/down; its settings are *wound up* and *drained down*.)
- Verbs are **turn / wind**. Never *flip, switch, toggle.*
- Behavior may be described in plain language, but a behavior phrase is never the knob's or the setting's **name**, and it never **anchors** a clause where the volume should (see Bart, below).

## Who says what

**The narrator and everyone in the lab — Cheryl, Lisa, Marge, Jasmine, Burns, Owen — use the proper name and the volume.** No dials, no nicknames, no mode-talk. This is the reader's constant; it holds in prose and in every callback.

**Bart** runs the same discipline, with three riders:

- He **attributes the proper name to Marge or Lisa exactly once — in his written build-log**, at the knob's first appearance (*"Extraverted, Marge's word"*; *"the Going knob — Lisa's word"*). In his **spoken** lines he just says the proper name; the attribution never echoes out of the build-log.
- He keeps **no bench vocabulary of his own.** *Give-way, the keeping knob, the room knob, working-the-room, the going-at-it, engagement/position knob* — all struck. He adopts the stamped word; he does not coin a rival.
- He may **describe behavior vividly**, but he may **not anchor a description to a behavior verb** where the volume should be the peg. The volume is the anchor; the behavior hangs off it.
  - **No:** *"verb up on the face when it leans in, bare word when it hangs back."*
  - **Yes:** *"verb up when the volume's high, bare word when it's low."* · *"volume high it leans in, volume low it hangs back."*

**Mira and Bramble** are the only mouths the **rebrand words** live in (Live/Dead, Sloth/Sprint, March/Dance, Busy/Warm, Improvise/Think, Little/Beast, Wide/Deep, Respect/Savage, Clutch/Stall, Preppy/Messy, Boss/Vibe, Real/Flex):

- When either child uses a rebrand word, it carries **"-mode"** — *Little-mode, Savage-mode, Boss-mode.*
- If a child **throws out a word the chapter has not yet coined**, the scene stops to let **someone ask what it means**, and the child **maps it onto volume up/down** (*"Boss-mode's volume up"*). A rebrand is never left floating as a bare pole-name; at its coining it is always tied to the volume it stands for.
- A rebrand word **never climbs into the narrator's prose or a callback.** Prose says *Extraverted*; the child says *Boss-mode.* This is why the most-nicknamed chapter stays the most anchored — the anchor lives in a register the children never touch, exactly as *it* survives every *he* Mira spends.
- **Before a knob's rebrand is coined**, Mira's spontaneous in-the-moment reading is allowed only if it is **anchored to the volume** the same way — the pole word rides "volume up/down," it does not replace it (*"Volume up, it's pushy; volume down, it hangs back"*). The coined "-mode" names arrive later, in the naming game and its backward-extension to the older bodies; they are not seeded early.

## Cheryl at the gap

The narrator never defines a framework word. She stops the tape to notice the *person*, not to gloss the *term* — that Bart's contempt for a word is itself a theory he doesn't know he holds; that the word he won't say does the most of the loving. Spent on definitions she becomes a glossary and flattens every voice into her diction. Hold her at the gap. The table does the teaching.

---

## Left alone

Three things keep the word *dial* and are never normalized: the **toy disc** the unit fixates on (its own dial), belief-and-intensity **metaphors** (*"your dial was at eleven," "the dial that never quite hits zero," a dimmer, not a flip-switch*), and **mechanism terms** used as description (aerobic/anaerobic, dopamine) — which may appear, but never as the setting's name.

---

## The law, in one line

Eleven two-pole volume knobs (up = derived, down = primordial, nothing between) and one wind-up; the proper name owns the prose and the callbacks; the setting is "volume up / down," and volume down is the previous quarter showing through; Bart attributes once, in his build-log, and coins nothing of his own; Mira and Bramble alone carry the "-mode" rebrands, tied to a volume at their coining and never let into the narrator's voice. First devotion is to the reader.
