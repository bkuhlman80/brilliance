## Ch 2

### <!--[4.14]--> Marge

Lisa came around to where Burns had been.

"Two minutes of logistics and then we are done."

She picked up her bramble. She drank from it.

"Q3 starts tomorrow. Chassis swap during Q3. The new chassis is small-roomba-sized. It is going to integrate the prokaryote-tier unit — Bramble — with the four protobots into one larger creature. Bart will be doing the chassis fabrication at Smithers through November and December. The memory board installs at that point. Knob three comes online; the next motivation arrives."

She paused.

Mira, who had not been listening to the logistics, said, "The bots."

Jasmine said, "The bots stay here. Bart is going to bring a new body for them."

Mira said, "A new body."

Lisa said, "Yes. A bigger body. All five bots become part of one bigger Bramble."

Mira said, "All of them."

Lisa said, "All of them. Bot four also."

Mira looked at the table. She looked at Bramble in her hand. She looked at the spot in the middle of the table where the protobots would have been if she had brought them. She had not brought them; they were at home.

"Will bot four still be bot four."

Marge looked up. Marge had not been looking up.

Jasmine said, "What do you mean, sweetheart."

Mira said, "When Bart puts them in the bigger body. Will bot four still be bot four."

Lisa said, "Bot four will be part of the bigger body. He will still be the part that was bot four."

Mira said, "Oh."

She paused.

"He needs his towel."

She turned Bramble over in her hand and turned it back. She did not say anything else for a long moment.

Lisa, watching her, said, "Next retrospective is fall. Q3. Smithers, end of day, same agenda. Camera off."

She crossed to the camcorder. She turned it off. The red light went out.

## Ch 3

### <!--[4.14]--> Jasmine

"Two minutes," Lisa said, "logistics, and then we're genuinely done, I can see Mira's about out." She picked up her drink and drank from it.

"Couple weeks left in the quarter. The unit goes back to the lab for a weekend during Q4 — Bart'll come collect it, a Friday, back to you by Monday, same as the swap — and there'll be a build on it while it's with us. Sprints through the middle, the usual. Next retrospective's the winter one, end of day, here, same agenda." She paused, and looked at Mira, who was being folded into her coat by Jasmine, and something in Lisa's logistics went deliberately smooth and incurious. "I'm not going to say tonight what the build is. Some of it's a — " she chose the word over Mira's head, " — it's a birthday thing. We'll keep it a birthday thing."

"What's a birthday thing," Mira said, instantly, from inside the coat.

"Coats," Jasmine said. "Arms in."

Lisa crossed to the camcorder. "Winter, then. Camera off." She turned it off, and the small red light on the front of it went out, and the warehouse was just a cold room in the fog again, with the work lights on and the meeting gone out of it.

The team broke. Marge cleared the pears and put the kettle, back on its cold ring. Bart folded his clade cards into the cigar box, and Cheryl's twelve on top of them, the Eukaryote card going back into the deck with two quarters of vocabulary written small on its face. Burns walked Jasmine and Mira out across the lot.

## Ch 9

### <!--[4.14]--> Marge

The light off, the room was a kitchen again — seven people and four machines, deciding nothing, reaching for coats.

Lisa ran the close the way she ran the open — the swap weekend onto the calendar, the slot Bart had emptied left open for next quarter, the cartoon shapes already a line item where they had been a surprise. She decided what the tape saw and what it never would, and set the evening down. Marge took it in from the cold end the way you take in weather, and gathered her coat and her bag and the beer she had made last two hours and not finished, and gave her goodnights, which were warm and short and gave nothing away, and went out to the lot.

## Ch 11

### <!--[4.14]--> Where a Man Is Meant to Stop

The red light went out, and the meeting came apart the way meetings do — chairs scraped back, the camcorder zipped into its bag, Lisa saying the logistics over the noise. The swap the first week of December, the last knob going in. The next retrospective on the twelfth, Mira's birthday, the way the winter ones always landed. The Games would open two days before it, and the city was already tipping toward February the way a room tips toward a door someone is about to open — the test runs on the mountain, the trucks of scaffolding up the canyons, the countdown that had stopped being measured in months. Lisa logged it all and snapped the case shut, and the tape of the day went out to her car.
