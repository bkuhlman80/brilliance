## Ruling: development is not write-back, unless the edit is computed from what the unit met

For the author to confirm before you freeze; the treatment's write-back test now carries it.

A change in a store that unfolds on a schedule the lineage set, and would unfold the same way whatever the unit met, is development: a reference-write distributed over time, not an edit computed from a run. The developing unit's acts are the occasion of the change and not its source — differentiation, morphogenesis, programmed cell death and the GO developmental terms built from them are the genome being read out, and the test's own words already say that content placed in the store without passing through a run is a reference-write. The discriminator is not whether the unit was active while the change happened; it is whether what the unit met or produced *computed* the edit, so that a unit meeting something else would have ended up different. Activity-dependent refinement, experience-dependent plasticity, immune repertoire shaped by encounter, a plant's stress priming: write-back, during development or after it, because the edit is a function of the encounter. A reaction norm — an environmental cue throwing a lineage-specified switch — is not: the mapping from cue to outcome was fixed before the cue arrived, and the cue selects among outcomes it did not compute.

Brief language, if you want it in v6 rather than in the pre-registration: *A change that unfolds on a schedule the lineage set, and would unfold the same way whatever the unit met, is development and not write-back, even though the unit is active while it happens. Write-back requires that what the unit met or produced computed the edit: a unit meeting something else would hold something else.*

The eight developmental reads at the genetic line are then the brief under-specifying, not a precursor. If after the wording readers still fire write-back on developmental GO terms, that is a finding about the test and we want it.
