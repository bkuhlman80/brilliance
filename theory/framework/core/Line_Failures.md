# Line Failures

Failures of tendencies and capacities sort by the line at which they emerged. Genetic-line failure is **disease**: cells, tissues, and organs that no longer maintain homeostasis. Nemetic-line failure is **disorder**: dispositional constellations and capacities that no longer regulate the organism's life against contextual demands. Symbolic-line failure is **disorganization**: cultural, institutional, and normative structures that no longer regulate the constituent humans' collective life against contextual demands. Durkheim's anomie is the founding sociological description; social disorganization (Shaw and McKay 1942) is the modern technical term, used across criminology, urban sociology, and family sociology. Disease, disorder, disorganization: three lines of failure, three vocabularies, three intervention modalities, one for each line whose failures already carry a name of their own. The taxonomy is stated for those three, and the digital line's failures are not among them.

An empire-human can develop either kind. Cancer is a genetic-line failure in the same person who can also develop a personality disorder, a nemetic-line failure. Both are real failures of the unit; they live at different lines. Civilizational collapse and institutional breakdown are the memetic-line cases of disorganization.

Failures are also modeled at higher lines than the one they live in. Genetic-line failures are modeled in the nemetic line via interoception, the organism's internal sensing of cellular and tissue state, and in the memetic line via medical knowledge, the cultural apparatus by which physicians categorize and intervene on disease. Nemetic-line failures are modeled in the memetic line via psychological and psychiatric frameworks. The line of the modeling is not the line of the failure.

Personality disorders enter the record at Empire-Human because the industrial era is when these nemetic-line failures became serious academic objects: Freud and the early psychoanalytic tradition framed neuroses as pragmatic failures, and that framing shaped how the disorders were measured and described. The record is the industrial-era memetic-line model of what nemetic-line failures look like; the failures themselves live in the brains of the affected individuals, not in the memetic line that describes them.

Why personality disorders resist medication is then explained. Not because the failures are non-biological, but because they are constellational rather than disease-like. The brain is not broken the way a diseased cell is broken; it is configured in a way that misfits contextual demands. Therapy works because it shifts constellation through experience-driven learning, which is the nemetic line's native modification modality. Medication targets cellular and molecular machinery — the machinery of disease rather than the machinery of disorder.

Two failures of upkeep have worked cases at their own lines. At the genetic line, cancer is a lineage of cells defecting from the body's held reference, and much of that defection is the epigenetic homeostat failing — cells losing their maintained state and rolling back up the landscape toward a less-differentiated one. At the memetic line, the characteristic form is the unowned seam: an institution runs a fast layer and a slow one, a quarterly clock and a mission measured in decades, and if no load-bearing seam is placed between them, the fast clock silently re-cuts the slow kernel one defensible decision at a time until the thing the institution was built to do is gone. Nobody chooses this, which is what makes it upkeep failing rather than a crime — the granularity was never owned.

*Epistemic status: the line-indexed taxonomy is framework. Anomie and social disorganization are established sociology; the epigenetic reading of cancer is established biology in its weak form. The therapy-versus-medication rationale is framework inference consistent with clinical practice, not derived from it. The unowned-seam account of institutional drift is a framework commitment.*
