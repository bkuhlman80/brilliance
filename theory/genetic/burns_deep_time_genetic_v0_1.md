Burns presents the deep-time and environmental context for each clade as Lisa presents the clade's tendencies. This doc gives him the substrate to draw from: where the clade most likely emerged, when, under what climate and atmospheric conditions, against what evidentiary record. The doc is neutral reference. Burns transforms it into his own voice at draft time.

The biological clades (Protocell through Primate, Ch 1–8) use one category set. The symbolic-line clades (Band-Human through Empire-Human, Ch 9–12) use a category set adapted to a timescale where plate tectonics is essentially static and where geography, climate stability, and ecology carry more weight than tectonic and atmospheric variables.

Dates carry implicit uncertainty bars; "approximately" and "contested" are used where the science is actively debated.

## Biological clades (Ch 1–4)

Category set:

- **Time period.** When the clade's defining transition occurred.
- **Environment of emergence.** The most likely setting for the clade's origin.
- **Climate.** Surface temperature regime and major climatic variability.
- **Plate tectonics.** Continental configuration and tectonic regime.
- **Geological events.** Discrete events that shape the clade's window.
- **Atmosphere.** Atmospheric composition during the clade window.
- **Ocean and surface chemistry.** Marine and surface chemistry relevant to the clade.
- **Biotic context.** What else was alive, what the clade competed with or depended on.
- **Key fossil and evidence sites.** Where Burns can anchor specifics.
- **Signature evidentiary signal.** The marker that anchors the transition in the rock record.

### Protocell (Ch 1)

**Time period.** Approximately 4.0–3.8 billion years ago (Hadean to early Archean). Earliest unambiguous microbial life follows shortly at approximately 3.5 Ga.

**Environment of emergence.** Strongly contested. Leading candidates are alkaline hydrothermal vents (Lost City–style, white smokers), volcanic surface ponds with wet-dry cycling (Mulkidjanian's hypothesis), and acidic deep-sea black smokers. Lane and Martin favor the alkaline-vent setting on bioenergetic grounds.

**Climate.** Hot surface, faint young Sun compensated by a thick CO2 / methane greenhouse. Liquid water present from at least the early Archean.

**Plate tectonics.** Pre-modern. Likely a stagnant-lid or proto–plate-tectonic regime. Continental crust forming but limited in extent.

**Geological events.** Late Heavy Bombardment (~4.1–3.8 Ga) if it occurred — currently contested. Continued accretionary impacts. Heavy volcanic outgassing.

**Atmosphere.** Reducing or weakly reducing. CO2-dominated, with N2, water vapor, methane, H2S, and ammonia. No free oxygen. Active hydrogen escape to space.

**Ocean and surface chemistry.** Anoxic, mildly acidic, iron-rich (Fe2+ in solution). Hot relative to modern seas. Salinity uncertain.

**Biotic context.** None — this is the threshold of life. Possibly competing or cooperating populations of pre-cellular replicators (RNA world or precursors).

**Key fossil and evidence sites.** Isua supracrustal belt (Greenland, ~3.8 Ga, contested biogenic carbon); Pilbara craton (W. Australia); Strelley Pool stromatolites (~3.43 Ga, earliest unambiguous biology). Modern analogue: Lost City hydrothermal field.

**Signature evidentiary signal.** Carbon-isotope-depleted graphitic inclusions; the oldest stromatolites; geochemical signatures suggesting metabolic activity.

### Prokaryote (Ch 2)

**Time period.** Earliest bacteria and archaea by approximately 3.5 Ga. Major diversification through the Archean and Proterozoic. The Great Oxidation Event is the clade's pivotal window at approximately 2.4–2.0 Ga.

**Environment of emergence.** Shallow seas, hydrothermal systems, microbial mats on sediment surfaces. Anywhere with water and an energy gradient.

**Climate.** Warm to temperate through most of the Archean. The first major glaciation — the Huronian — falls in the GOE window (~2.4 Ga) and may be the first Snowball Earth, triggered by methane drawdown as oxygen rose.

**Plate tectonics.** Modern-style plate tectonics likely beginning in the late Archean (~3.0 Ga, contested timing). Continental cratons assembling.

**Geological events.** **Great Oxidation Event (~2.4–2.0 Ga).** Cyanobacterial photosynthesis builds up atmospheric oxygen. The single most consequential biogeochemical event of the prokaryote window. Banded iron formations record the transition.

**Atmosphere.** Pre-GOE: reducing, methane-rich. Post-GOE: oxygen climbs from trace amounts to roughly 1–10% of modern levels. Methane crashes.

**Ocean and surface chemistry.** Pre-GOE: ferruginous (dissolved iron abundant, oxygen-poor). Post-GOE: surface waters oxygenated, deep waters anoxic and increasingly sulfidic (Canfield ocean).

**Biotic context.** Bacteria and archaea diversifying. Explosion of metabolic types — chemolithotrophs, anoxygenic phototrophs, methanogens, sulfate reducers, and eventually oxygenic photosynthesis. Competition is among metabolic strategies.

**Key fossil and evidence sites.** Apex chert (W. Australia, ~3.5 Ga, contested); Strelley Pool stromatolites; Gunflint chert (Canada, ~1.9 Ga); Hamersley Group banded iron formations.

**Signature evidentiary signal.** Stromatolites, microbial mats, banded iron formations (the GOE marker), molecular fossils such as hopanes and steranes.

### Eukaryote (Ch 3)

**Time period.** First eukaryotes approximately 2.0–1.5 Ga. Bangiomorpha at approximately 1.05 Ga is the oldest unambiguous eukaryote fossil with sexual reproduction. Major diversification approximately 1.0–0.7 Ga.

**Environment of emergence.** Shallow marine, sediment-water interfaces. Increasingly oxygenated near-surface waters.

**Climate.** Warm Proterozoic stability through the "Boring Billion" (~1.8–0.8 Ga). Snowball Earth events — Sturtian (~717 Ma) and Marinoan (~650 Ma) — fall at the late end of the clade window.

**Plate tectonics.** Supercontinents Nuna/Columbia (~1.8–1.3 Ga) and Rodinia (~1.1–0.75 Ga). Rodinia's breakup correlates with the onset of the Cryogenian Snowball Earths.

**Geological events.** Endosymbiotic origin of mitochondria (an alpha-proteobacterium engulfed by an archaeal host) and later of plastids (a cyanobacterium engulfed by an early eukaryote). Snowball Earths at the close of the clade window.

**Atmosphere.** Oxygen at intermediate Proterozoic levels through most of the clade window — well above pre-GOE values, well below modern. Major second rise (Neoproterozoic Oxygenation Event) toward the end.

**Ocean and surface chemistry.** Canfield ocean — surface oxygenated, deep waters sulfidic and anoxic. Trace metal availability (molybdenum, copper, zinc) shapes biological possibility space.

**Biotic context.** Background of prokaryotic life continues. Eukaryotes acquire mitochondria, then plastids; multicellular forms emerge late in the clade window.

**Key fossil and evidence sites.** Gabon biota (~2.1 Ga, contested as macroscopic eukaryotes); Bangiomorpha (Arctic Canada, ~1.05 Ga); Doushantuo Formation (S. China, ~600 Ma, including possible embryos).

**Signature evidentiary signal.** Increasing organic and morphological complexity in acritarchs; steranes as biomarkers for eukaryotes; Bangiomorpha as the type fossil.

### Eumetazoa (Ch 4)

**Time period.** Late Ediacaran (~635–541 Ma) through the Cambrian explosion (~541–520 Ma). Molecular clocks place the cnidarian-bilaterian split closer to 700 Ma; body fossils appear from approximately 580 Ma onward.

**Environment of emergence.** Shallow continental-shelf seas. Soft sediment bottoms, microbial mats.

**Climate.** Recovery from the Cryogenian Snowball Earths; warming through the Ediacaran. The Cambrian itself is warm and ice-free.

**Plate tectonics.** Rodinia broken up by approximately 750 Ma. Pannotia briefly assembled approximately 600 Ma, then fragmented. Continents distributed in low and mid latitudes.

**Geological events.** Sturtian (~717 Ma), Marinoan (~650 Ma), and Gaskiers (~580 Ma) glaciations. The Cambrian explosion (~541–520 Ma) follows.

**Atmosphere.** Oxygen continues to rise through the Neoproterozoic Oxygenation Event, possibly crossing thresholds required for animal metabolism. Still high CO2 by modern standards.

**Ocean and surface chemistry.** Increasingly oxygenated surface waters. Rising phosphate availability supports biomineralization. Deep waters remain patchily anoxic.

**Biotic context.** Ediacaran biota first — Avalon, White Sea, and Nama assemblages, with forms like Dickinsonia and Charnia. Then the Cambrian explosion delivers nearly all modern animal phyla as recognizable body fossils. Cnidarians serve as the canonical Eumetazoa exemplar.

**Key fossil and evidence sites.** Ediacara Hills (S. Australia); Mistaken Point (Newfoundland, the Avalon assemblage); Doushantuo (S. China); Chengjiang (S. China, ~518 Ma); Burgess Shale (Canada, ~508 Ma).

**Signature evidentiary signal.** First soft-bodied animal body fossils; trace fossils (bilaterian burrows); Lagerstätten preserving soft tissue from the Cambrian explosion.
