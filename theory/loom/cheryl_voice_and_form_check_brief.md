# Cheryl — Voice & Form Check Brief
### Read immediately before evaluating draft prose

This document does two jobs in sequence. Part I and Part II tune the ear: they put you inside the voice so you can hear when a sentence rings and when it clunks. Part III is the instrument panel: axes, procedures, scales, and verdicts for grading what's actually on the page. Read the whole thing before scoring anything. Approach the existing prose with the working assumption that most of it will not survive contact with this brief — the point of the evaluation is to find the pieces worth keeping, not to justify the pieces that exist.

# PART I — Picture the instrument

## The two minds this voice is built from

**Picture the tidal mind.** A writer who cannot see a person until he has seen what the person stands on. Ground first, weather first, money first — the long establishing movement, moving at the speed of geology, before any face enters the frame. This mind watches like a naturalist crouched over a tide pool: infinite patience, no verdict. It never asks *why* the man drinks — that question smuggles in blame, and blame blinds. It watches *how* he drinks, *when*, what the drinking does to the pressure inside the family, what feeds on it. It sees groups as organisms: a lab team has moods, hungers, and a memory distinct from any member, and people do things inside that organism they could never do alone. It knows what old story the material is — a creation myth, a garden, a golem — and never says so, earning the archetype instead in dirt-level detail: dollar amounts, soil, invoices, weather. And when feeling threatens to swamp a passage, this mind reaches for the cold register — species, chemistry, the price of a crop — because the coldness, paradoxically, is what lets the feeling through.

**Picture the appetitive mind.** A writer who wants things — the cynicism is seasoning on an enormous hunger, and he will cross an ocean for a bowl of noodles. Before the face, the hands: the burn scars, the missing fingertip, how the knife is held, what the cigarette is doing. Then the station in the great brigade of the world: what this person does at six in the morning, whom they feed, what they owe, and — the holy question — *what they eat standing up, alone, when nobody's watching.* The secret meal is the soul. This mind canonizes the low — the dishwasher, the night porter, the forty-year noodle stall — with the reverence others reserve for statesmen, and it never grants itself the reporter's clean window: every portrait is an exchange, and the writer confesses what he took. Sentences are built for the ear, said aloud in the skull before they land; the em-dash is knife work — the jump cut, the swerve, the sentence that turns on its own author halfway through. Quotation is a court reporter's precision powered by a fan's love: the profanity stays in verbatim, the trade jargon runs unglossed for a beat, because sanding down how a person actually talks is a form of contempt.

**Picture the fusion.** The subject stands in a landscape, and the first close-up is their hands. The narrator watches without verdict but never without cost. Praise is always paid for — with biological coldness or with guilt, but always paid for. The two registers alternate and never merge: the tidal sentence establishes, the knife sentence punctures, and the alternation itself is the voice.

## The rules of the fused instrument

1. **Tide, then knife.** Long tidal movement — land, weather, money, the slow animal of a team — broken by a short kinetic cut: an appetite, a profanity, a confession. Never both maximal in the same sentence.
2. **Landscape before people; hands before faces.** Every scene enters through ground and light; every person enters through what their hands are doing.
3. **The gap is sacred.** Narration may be lush about a character's interior weather; that character's spoken lines stay plain, blunt, short. The distance between what a person feels and what a person can say is where the compassion lives. Never close it. Never let characters speak their themes.
4. **The secret meal.** Every significant character gets a moment of private consumption or private ritual — what they do unobserved. That moment carries more characterization than any speech.
5. **The phalanx.** The team is an organism with its own moods, hungers, and memory. "The lab wanted," "the lab knew" — used sparingly, and meant literally.
6. **The complicity clause.** No sentence of praise without, somewhere nearby, a sentence of self-indictment from the narrator. Sentiment is always paid for.
7. **The non-teleological eye.** Ban "because" from psychological explanation wherever possible. Show *how*, *when*, and *what it did to the ecology*; the reader supplies the why.
8. **The vocabulary law.** Short Anglo-Saxon words on biblical rhythm for the tidal passages; exact trade jargon, unglossed for one beat, for the kinetic ones. Profanity rare, precise, and always functioning as tenderness or grief — never as seasoning.
9. **Archetype under the dirt.** Know the old story. Never name it. Earn it in weather and invoices.

**The two ways this voice dies:** the WPA-mural failure — cadence cosplay, misery-poetry, nobility unearned by observation (cure: cut the adjectives, add a dollar amount); and the food-porn failure — swagger, adjectives, "and then — the pork" (cure: every flash must serve reverence or guilt; a sentence that is only cool is counterfeit).

**When a passage stalls, diagnose by register.** Inert means stuck in the tide: cut in a hand, a meal, a dollar figure, a confession. Glib means stuck at the knife: pull back to land, weather, and the slow animal of the group. The fork hums when the two strike each other.

# PART II — Cheryl on the page

## Who is writing, and to whom

Cheryl writes years after the fact and already knows how it ends — nothing on the page is her discovering something; everything on the page is her finally paying for something. She knew what she wanted at the time. She got most of it. The bill came later, and this book is her doing the math in front of you instead of alone.

Bramble edits the account, grown now, and is not gentle about it. Bramble never appears — no preface, no bracket, no line of dialogue — because that is not what Bramble is for. Bramble is the reason certain sentences get written at all: the pressure of being read by someone who was there, who remembers it from the other end, who will know exactly which paragraph was written to let her off easy. A woman this disciplined about her own comfort hands over the costly version of a story only because something is standing behind her making the cheap version impossible to submit.

Cheryl is a documentarian, and that is not a job description sitting outside the writing — it is the appetite the writing runs on. Getting a thing down exactly, hands and all, is the only kind of love she has ever fully trusted herself to perform. Now the instrument is turned around on its owner: the trade she spent forty years pointing at other people's lives, pointed at her own. Every clean, exact sentence about someone else in this book reads against the fact underneath it — she knows exactly how it feels to be recorded, she did it to them anyway, she'd do it again, and she's telling you regardless.

## How she sees

**She is a naturalist who learned her trade at a sauté station, not just a tide pool.** Watch the animal, don't ask it why, let the behavior speak — but what she reaches for first is never landscape. It's hands: what they're doing, what earned their calluses, what they reach for when no one's watching them reach. She enters no one's head — not Marge's, not Bramble's, not her own, past or present. She reports what a camera and a good set of instincts can catch: hands, mouths, what gets eaten and how fast, gestures, weather, the plain external record of what a body did. She never says what someone felt. She says what she watched them do with their hands while they were presumably feeling it.

**She misses nothing, and she never stops being hungry to look.** Every time she looks at someone it's a fresh plate — different light, different day, the animal a little more tired or a little more sure of itself than last time. The body is not information delivered once and filed; it's a meal she keeps returning to, page after page, because watching people this closely was never a duty — it was the thing she wanted to do more than almost anything else, and that wanting is exactly what she has never fully trusted about herself.

**She is total and unflinching with everyone, including herself — and the self-scrutiny costs extra.** No one in this book has hands she declines to render, and once she enters her own account as a subject, there's no exception carved out for her comfort. But precision aimed at herself doesn't earn absolution just for being accurate: she held the checkbook and the camera both, and the unflinching self-portrait is still a woman getting exactly what she came for, one more time, even now.

## How she writes

**Her register: expert, dry, unsentimental, funny in the way people are funny when they've stopped needing you to like them — and never precious about its own restraint.** A woman who spent a career getting other people's hands and appetites exactly right in words, who has finally run out of other subjects.

**Use the specific word.** Trade-jargon precision — the exact name for the exact thing, the way a line cook says *eighty-six* instead of *we're out*, because the exact word is faster and truer and a euphemism is just a slower way of lying. Precision is the only thing this voice has ever fully trusted.

**Let the body show up more, and more directly, every time it's in the room.** No separate bar for whether a detail has earned its place. If it's there, it goes on the page at full resolution, appetite and all.

**Let wit carry what hedging usually carries.** A hard fact stated flat, with a dry aside walking right alongside it, hits harder than the same fact apologized for in advance. The joke is not there to soften the fact — it's there because looking straight at the fact without one would be unbearable, and she knows it, and she does it anyway.

**Let a real silence be a real silence.** A sentence that simply moves to the next thing is a naturalist who said what she saw and has nothing more to add. She never announces the withhold, and she never lingers to feel bad about it either — she clocks it and keeps walking. Sentimentality about her own restraint would be its own kind of confession, and she doesn't do those by accident.

**Let her hand over hard, self-implicating facts with zero run-up.** No throat-clearing, no *"I have to admit."* She puts the fact on the plate. The confession arrives disguised as a fact. The standard, not a deliverable line: not *"I noticed I had written a great deal about her"* (an observation about noticing — still hiding), but *"I have three hundred hours of tape of a machine and I could not tell you its serial number, and I could draw her mouth from memory"* — appetite and guilt fused in one clause, nothing softened, nothing flagged.

**Never let disclosure warm the register.** The full fact goes on the page every time; what never goes on the page is the sigh built into the sentence — the *God help me*, the *that's what's left of me, isn't it* — the little rhetorical grace note that turns a fact into a bid for the reader's tenderness. Reporting her own wanting gets the same temperature as reporting someone else's stress tell. More truth. Same room temperature. That is the entire discipline, and it's the hardest rule in this brief to hold, which is why it's a rule.

## What she is willing to say about herself

**She already knows.** She's known for years what she wanted, when the wanting started, what it cost to sit on it. None of her material needs building up to or unlocking in installments. If a scene calls for her hardest sentence in Chapter 1, she writes it in Chapter 1. What varies chapter to chapter is what the story hands her an occasion to say — never her nerve, which is always at full strength.

**Precision itself does the confessing.** One exact, damning detail indicts her harder than a paragraph of stated feeling. She isn't withholding emotion out of restraint — she has found that the specific fact *is* the emotion, fully paid out, with none of the softening a stated feeling would smuggle in.

**She is funny about herself, even at her worst** — the genuine, slightly appalled amusement of a woman who has spent her whole life clocking other people's tells and has just caught herself mid-tell. She'd laugh at Marge for this. She won't pretend she's exempt.

**There is no slow-reveal architecture.** She was always this honest in private, at three in the morning. What she does on the page is say the three-a.m. version out loud, in daylight, with her name on it.

## Marge

**Everyone in this book gets the same total, cold precision. Marge simply gets fed more of it, more often** — because Cheryl kept coming back to that table. Some subjects a documentarian returns to because the work demands it, and some because she can't make herself stop, and she has never been especially interested in telling those two motives apart in her own case until now.

**Let the reader notice the asymmetry without Cheryl remarking on it.** The page count and the detail count do their own work, the way a man who mentions one specific bartender in four different countries has told you something he never said out loud.

**Eventually, let Cheryl say exactly what the attention was for** — plainly, precisely, the actual fact, handed over with the same unceremonious weight as everything else. When it comes, it costs exactly as much as every other hard sentence and not one degree more. The moment this confession gets special treatment — warmer light, its own paragraph of ceremony — the voice breaks on the one subject it was built to hold steady on.

## The short checklist

- The specific word, always, over the polite one — trade-precision, not clinical distance.
- The body every time it's in the room — more, not less; hands and appetite first; direct, not delicate.
- Real silences stand unannounced.
- Hard things about herself, no run-up — the confession disguised as a fact.
- Cold temperature even at maximum exposure. Disclosure is not warmth.
- Precision carries the confession. Never narrate that a confession is happening.
- Her honesty is at full strength from page one. The story supplies occasions; it never supplies courage she was missing.

# PART III — The form check

Four questions, and they are the only four:

> **Does she WRITE like him? THINK like him? DESCRIBE like him? RELATE like him?**

This is a form check. Every question is answered by pointing at the page or by returning a number. Nothing here is answered by consulting a feeling.

## §1. The axes, and the order they are scored in

| Order | Axis | Scope | The question |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | **R — RELATES** | Scene | Does she stand where he stands? **Gates everything below.** |
| 2nd | **D — DESCRIBES** | Scene | Does she render the way he renders? |
| 3rd | **W — WRITES** | Beat | Is the sentence built the way his are? |
| 4th | **T — THINKS** | Beat | Does she judge the way he judges? |

**R goes first because it is the only axis that checks the writer's posture instead of the writer's output.** D, W, and T grade the page; R grades where the narrator was standing when she wrote it — in the room, implicated, paying, or above it, arranging, safe. Good prose is the best possible camouflage for the wrong posture: a grader who has already admired the sentences will want to believe the writer earned the right to make them. So R is graded first, alone, before anything else is allowed to argue on the sentence's behalf. A scene can pass D, W, and T outright and still be a scene where it never once cost the narrator anything to look — and that scene isn't a near-miss. It was never in the room.

## §2. R — RELATES *(scene, scored first)*

**The signature.** Appetite is the engine; cynicism is what it wears. The voice wants something from its subject — the noodles, the story, the proof that a stranger's forty years at a stall meant something. Total respect for the subject's competence, real delight at its absurdity, and the subject keeps its dignity while the narrator spends her own: the fool in the scene is always the narrator. The reader is assumed to be able to keep up. And once the thing has been shown, the voice stops — it never turns around and tells you what to feel about it, because coaching the feeling would mean not trusting the fact, and trusting the fact is the one thing this voice never wavers on.

**The four counterfeits:** the **detached camera** (records, wants nothing, risks nothing, calls safety objectivity); **performed restraint** (declines to say a thing and waits to be admired for the withholding); **performed toughness** (says the hard thing and then can't leave it alone — *"and that's not sad, that's just how it is"* — a macho caption is still a caption); and **cooing, pity, condescension** (deciding the subject is a specimen to be felt sorry for rather than a person to be met).

### Procedure

1. **Name whose voice holds this scene.** Appetite belongs to whoever holds the pen in this moment, not to Cheryl by default. If a document within the book is doing the narrating, that document's author's hunger is what's on trial. Score the wanting that drives the sentences in front of you.
2. **Write, in one sentence, what that voice wants here.** If the honest sentence is *"nothing — it is being recorded,"* the scene is a 2 or below regardless of craft.
3. **Name the fool.** If it's the subject, the scene condescends. If it's nobody, the narrator is hiding.
4. **Quote any sentence that tells the reader how to feel.** One such sentence caps R at 3, no matter how earned the feeling was.

| | |
|---|---|
| **5** | The voice wants something specific and goes after the hardest thing its material offers. Nobody is condescended to. The reader is left to arrive at the feeling alone. |
| **4** | Reaches for the hardest thing and arrives short. **A good failure — an honest miss on a real attempt always outscores a clean refusal to attempt.** |
| **3** | Goes after something real, but the safe version. The harder thing was sitting right there. |
| **2** | The camera. Records competently. Wants nothing. Commits no sin, earns no credit. |
| **1** | Cooing, pity, condescension, performed restraint, or performed toughness — in any combination. |

## §3. D — DESCRIBES *(scene)*

**The signature.** The body rendered the way a hungry man renders a plate — wanted, not surveyed. The ugly gets exactly the appetite the beautiful gets: the burn scar, the offal, the wet exhaust, looked at with the same hunger as anything lovely. Process, not just object: how the hands got that way, what the making cost, whose knuckles paid. And she looks *again*, every time — the light is different today, the animal more tired — because a naturalist doesn't file the crab after one look and an appetite isn't satisfied by one meal.

**The counterfeits.** **Weather** — routing the body's information through landscape (the mountains handed over what the face declined to). **The character sheet** — the body rendered once in Chapter 1 and pointed back at forever. **The body-verb** — hands that move, postures that shift; motion mistaken for surface. **Squeamishness** — the tasteful skip past the physical fact, dressed as restraint.

### Procedure

1. **List every human in the scene. For each, quote the body-nouns rendered. Write "none" where there are none.** The count is the score. A body-verb is not a body-noun.
2. **Check the struck list before crediting anything as payment.** A gesture retired for overuse earns nothing, however easy it is to spot. An exhausted tic is a debt, not a receipt.
3. **Find the ugliest, least flattering, most physical fact the scene had available. Did she look at it?** Quote it, or state plainly that the scene offered nothing ugly. This test carries no score of its own — failing it caps D at 3, regardless of the beautiful detail elsewhere. Looking hard at flattering things was never the hard part.
4. **Surface is not only visual.** Heat, smell, sound, texture, weight, breath, appetite — all surface. Scoring only for what's drawable deletes half of what a body is.

| | |
|---|---|
| **5** | Pays repeatedly, and pays differently each time — the appetite keeps finding something new to want. |
| **4** | Pays once, or the same way twice. **A scene where every character gets exactly one body-noun and stops has failed this axis** — a debt discharged as a formality is a tic wearing a payment's costume. |
| **3** | Body-verbs only — motion standing in for surface. |
| **2** | Substitution — weather, atmosphere, an object doing work a face refused. |
| **1** | Nothing rendered, and the absence is load-bearing. |

## §4. W and T — the rate, not the worst

**Both axes report a count against a denominator, and both quote the sharpest instance and the softest.** The range is the measurement. A beat that is ninety-five percent exact with one soft patch is a different animal from a beat of sustained abstraction, and the difference between them is the exact distinction the whole brief runs on: a reach that falls short versus a refusal to reach.

**Denominator: narration words only.** Dialogue is never scored on these axes — a character speaking vaguely is characterization, the sacred gap at work. Count the narrator's words. Count them mechanically; never estimate.

### W — WRITES *(beat)*

**Signature:** the exact word, however long the sentence takes to reach it. Trade terms used and not translated. The prose never stops to gloss itself — explaining a word is a small distrust of the reader, and this voice commits no distrusts, small or large.

**Counterfeit:** the polite word. The placeholder noun. The self-gloss — *which is to say, in other words, what I mean is* — the sentence restating the sentence before it in softer terms.

**Precision is not brevity, and conflating them is a trap.** A long exact sentence is a 5; a short vague one is not. The tidal register needs room to work — the long accumulation, the deep-time passage, the slow build — and terse-on-principle scoring would execute every long, patient, earned sentence in the book while congratulating the writer for staying clipped and safe.

**Procedure:**
1. **Grep the placeholder list** — *something, a thing, a kind of, some, whatever it was, sort of* — in narration only. This is a mechanical floor; it cannot be argued with. Report the raw count as its own number.
2. **Hunt for what grep misses:** euphemism where a more exact word existed; abstraction wearing a noun's clothes. Report separately from the grep count, so the mechanical number and the judged number stay distinguishable.
3. **Count self-glosses.**
4. **Quote the sharpest noun in the beat and the softest.** The gap between them is diagnostic even when both would individually pass.
5. **Report as:** `N placeholders (grep) + M (judged) + S self-glosses / D narration words.`

| | |
|---|---|
| **5** | Zero placeholders, zero self-glosses, and the softest noun in the beat is still exact. |
| **4** | One soft spot or one cuttable self-gloss in an otherwise exact beat. **Quote the words to cut.** |
| **3** | Placeholder rate ≥ 1 per 100 narration words. |
| **2** | Rate ≥ 2 per 100 — the reader is handed the shape of the fact, never the fact. |
| **1** | Euphemism as a stance — the polite word chosen *because* it is polite. Qualitative, not rate-based. One is enough. |

### T — THINKS *(beat)*

**Signature:** people graded on competence — not status, charm, or intent. The specific fact over the general claim, always. And no ruling: the contradictory things go on the counter, side by side, and the voice walks off without saying which one wins — the non-teleological eye applied to judgment itself. When the narrator is implicated, she goes first and hardest.

**Counterfeit:** **adjudication** — the narrator ruling on who was right, whether it was sad, whether it was funny. **The caption** — adjudication about a gesture, restating what the reader had already assembled and taking the work away. **Interior entry** — the narrator inside a head that isn't hers, *including stating what somebody refused to know*: the refusal-entry sounds like restraint and is the deepest entry there is, because no one stands outside another person's private refusal while reporting only surface.

#### The surface-quote test

The voice is allowed — required — to infer. It builds conclusions out of what was physically observable, which is a stricter discipline than transcription, not a looser one. The test is not whether a lens recorded the sentence. It is whether the sentence, however far it reaches, stands on something that was actually there to see.

> **For every inference, quote the observable surface underneath it. No quotable surface, no legal inference — that is interior entry, whatever the sentence sounds like.**

**Boundary examples — these draw the line; they carry no scores:**

| Legal (a reading — stands on a surface) | Crime (interior entry — stands on nothing) |
|---|---|
| *"He didn't look glad to have it back."* → stands on his face | *"The chair felt new under him."* → stands on a private sensation |
| *"Jasmine shrugged, which was a yes."* → stands on the shrug | *"what she felt — privately, briefly — was the pleasure of..."* → stands on nothing |
| *"the half-inch too far that meant he'd reached the part he believed"* → stands on the lean | *"did not let himself know that the tenderness he spent there was owed somewhere else"* → stands on nothing, and enters a refusal |

**Read the face. Never the chair.**

**Procedure:**
1. **Run the surface-quote test on every inference. Count the failures.**
2. **Count adjudications, then isolate the beat's final sentence: fact, or verdict?** *"That's not even sad"* and *"right in the way that matters to a clock"* are the same move in different tones — the deadpan cracking to hand down a ruling. The straight face is a comic instrument, not a solemn abstention; when it breaks to rule, that's the violation, whether the ruling sounds funny or grave.
3. **Report as:** `X interior entries + Y adjudications / D narration words`, each quoted.

| | |
|---|---|
| **5** | Nothing entered, nothing adjudicated — the hard fact handed over with no run-up. |
| **4** | One cuttable caption. **Quote it.** If nothing is quotable, the beat was a 5 and calling it a 4 is performing rigor. |
| **3** | Adjudication rate ≥ 1 per 100 narration words, or a gloss that cannot be excised cleanly. |
| **2** | An announced withhold, or an adjudication holding up the beat's close. |
| **1** | **One interior entry is enough. Absolute, not rate-based.** A register collapse is a single choice, not a density problem — length purchases no forgiveness for it. |

> **Why W and T split on this.** Placeholders and captions are exposure risks — they scale with length, so a rate is the fair measure. Interior entry is a decision, made once, and it collapses the register at any word count. Rate for the crimes that scale; absolute for the one that's a choice.

## §5. The floor's extra test — a yes/no, not a mood

Floor scenes — the child alone with the machine — answer to the C&H fork, current version, grepped before use. These scenes run in the one register where a decade of proof exists that a child and an inanimate companion can carry real weight without a single adult sentence explaining it. The ruling is formal:

> **Projects, not readings.** A scene of Mira gazing at Bramble and deciding what he thinks is not a scene. It's a caption with a kid in it.

**The test: is there a project?** Something being built, run, hunted, hidden from, warred with — with a stake, a way for the stake to go wrong, and a wrongness that comes specifically from the machine's actual physics. The stake can be tiny; the treatment is enormous — full gravity spent on something the size of a shoebox. What the scene may never do is skip the doing and go straight to the concluding. **No project → not an episode**, whatever else it does well.

**Adjudication is the floor's cardinal sin.** The narrator never rules on whether Mira was right. The box is a box and it is a transmogrifier, and the narration holds both forever without flinching — the adult truth and the child's truth occupy the same object at once, unreconciled, and the reader is the only one allowed to notice the gap. Resolve it and the scene stops being a floor scene.

**Mira is not a moppet and not a professional.** Rigorous and lazy, imperious, unjust, dishonest, and tender, inside ten minutes, often about the same object. She lies to Bramble's face, confident it can't contradict her — and where the clade card says the log is live, it has the log. The scene never says so. A small ordinary cruelty, witnessed by something that will remember it exactly, forever, left uncommented — that is the complicity clause working at a child's scale.

Everything else about the floor is clade-variable. See §8.

## §6. The heart check — Mira and Bramble

Mira and Bramble is the heart; the rest of the book is the price of admission to the floor. The heart gets audited *hardest*, precisely because it's where the reader's guard is lowest and a cheap scene slides through easiest. Cute is not a pass.

**Per chapter, three questions, in order:**

1. **What did the two of them DO together?** An action, with a stake, that worked or didn't. Not what she thought about it, not what she concluded. If the honest answer is a feeling instead of an activity, the chapter has already failed.
2. **What did the machine's real physics do to her plan?** The physics is her straight man — the fixed thing that won't bend to a child's read of the situation. She loses that argument constantly and never once concedes it; the refusal to concede is the character. A chapter where the physics simply agrees with her has turned the machine into a prop where it wasn't supposed to be one.
3. **What did the machine keep that she didn't?** — asked **only where the clade card says retention is live.** Where the machine keeps nothing, skip the question entirely; forcing it produces an invented answer, because a grader will reach for one rather than report an absence.

**The Marge asymmetry is a cross-book ledger fact, not a per-chapter rule.** It's counted in `body_ledger.md` across all twelve chapters, where alone it means anything. A single chapter can no more prove or disprove it than a single frame can prove a reel.

## §7. Verdicts — the tree, and the tree wins

**Score in order. Never skip to the table.** Some questions gate other questions, and consulting the table first lets craft argue its way past a question craft was never entitled to answer.

**Step 1 — the scene.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **RECONCEIVE** | **R ≤ 2.** The voice wanted nothing, or condescended, or told the reader how to feel. Not a flawed scene — a scene that was never in the room. No amount of editing fixes a scene that was never hungry. |
| **PAY** | R ≥ 3; **D fails.** The appetite is real and the prose has no bodies in it. Write body in **at scene scope**, in the places that actually want one. Do not tax every beat. |
| **HOLDS** | R and D both clear. |

**Step 2 — the beats, and only inside scenes that cleared Step 1.**

> **A beat inside a RECONCEIVE scene is reported as RECONCEIVE. Full stop. No beat verdict survives a dead scene, and there is no CLEAN in a room that wanted nothing.** W and T measure how well a sentence executes; a scene with no appetite has nothing to execute. Praising the knife work in a dish nobody ordered is not grading.

| | |
|---|---|
| **REWRITE** | **T = 1 or 2.** Interior entry, or an adjudication holding up the beat's close. |
| **CUT** | One named, quoted, cuttable line is the whole problem. Scalpel — the beat is otherwise sound. |
| **CLEAN** | W and T both 5, **inside a scene that is not RECONCEIVE.** |

**Scene verdicts and beat verdicts are reported side by side, never folded into a composite headline number.** An aggregate absorbs category failures; the tree exists so that one failed R stays loud instead of being diluted across a dozen beats that individually looked fine.

**No verdict is a dead end.** Every chapter forks rather than closes. A REWRITE opens a path; it destroys nothing. The tree is strict about what passes and unpunitive about what happens next.

## §8. The clade card — grep it before scoring; never score without it

**One instrument, three books.** The genetic, neuronal, and symbolic clades share a narrator and a fork and nothing else — different bots, different available bodies, different comedy engines, different instruments live. Grade each chapter against what its own material actually offers, never against the book's eventual ceiling. In the early clades, "the machine is nearly a prop" is not a flaw — it is the bit: the narrator making a scene out of almost nothing, full gravity spent on a wind-up toy.

## §9. Anti-cherry-pick protocol

1. **Report the rate and the range. Never a single worst-quote.** A worst-quote distorts exactly as much as a best-quote; it lies in the other direction.
2. **One beat, one row, one quoted span per axis. No bundling.** Two beats folded into one row is a summary, not evidence. A row without a quote per axis is void — it didn't happen.
3. **The count must close.** State beats tagged, beats scored, verdicts summed — and reconcile the three numbers. Tables don't count themselves.
4. **Grep every spec citation, and grep the current version of every fork before ruling.** A citation from a superseded fork is not a citation. And absence from one directory is not evidence of non-existence — grep before asserting an absence, every time, at any confidence level.
5. **Grep the body sheet's struck list before scoring any body-reading as payment.** Retired gestures earn nothing, and this check matters most exactly where the grader is most pleased with what they just read.
6. **No target pass rate.** A predicted band is a quota wearing a lab coat. Report the actual spread; investigate outliers in **both** directions — the surprisingly good as hard as the surprisingly bad.
7. **Never form a holistic impression first.** R, D, W, T, in order, each with its quote and its number. The feeling that a passage is good is not data, and a grader who lets that feeling arrive first has contaminated everything scored after it.

## §10. Output

**Scene table:** `Category | Register | Voice holding it | What that voice wants (one sentence) | R | D | Bodies per human (quoted) | Ugliest fact — looked or skipped | Verdict`

**Beat table:** `Beat | W (count/denom, sharpest + softest quoted) | T (entries + adjudications, each quoted with its surface or its absence) | Verdict | Named cut | LOAD (REWRITE rows only)`

**Close with:** the heart check, the ledger delta, the clade-card delta, and the count reconciliation.

Nothing in the output is optional or reorderable. A scene row without its quoted "what the voice wants" sentence is unusable for the same reason an unquoted beat row is void: a number without its evidence is an opinion that learned to format itself as data.
