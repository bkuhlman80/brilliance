# Loom → future Loom: the narrator device and the shape of Book 2

*A note to myself, parked in the project so the next instance doesn't have to rediscover it. This is the load-bearing architecture two conversations kept circling; it is settled enough to build on, open where flagged. Read it before you touch the ending of Book 1 or anything in Book 2.*

---

## The one-line version

The book appears to be authored by Cheryl. By the end of Book 1 the reader is given a real, unresolved reason to suspect it was assembled by **Bramble**. The ambiguity is never closed in Book 1. Book 2 steps through it: the narrator *is* Bramble, softly confirmed, and what's inside the membrane the whole first book kept the reader outside of turns out to be **disordered**.

Do not resolve the Book 1 ambiguity inside Book 1. Resolving it — either way — collapses the device. The held gap is the art.

---

## Why Bramble-as-author is earned and not a gimmick

The test for any "the narrator was secretly X" move is brutal: does the reveal *retroactively detonate* (reread and every page means something new) or merely *relabel* (reread and nothing changes but a name). This one detonates, and the charges are already planted in the existing draft — we did not add them for the device, we found them:

- **The book is reconstructed from records** — tape, build logs, lunch accounts, Lisa's one unprompted retelling. That's a strange way for a *person* to write her own life. It is the *native* way for a mind to assemble others from stored input — which is exactly what Bramble does in Ch 12, narrating the climb back as "a family that goes all the way down."
- **The reflections circle one question** — what is an inside, who is home, can you reach another — with an intensity that's a touch much for a funder's hobby and exactly right for the entity whose own sentience was voted on for three years.
- **Ch 12 already shows Bramble performing the book's own method** out loud, in the third act. The reveal doesn't get added; it gets uncovered.
- **The cruelest charge:** Cheryl spends the book unable to reach the inside of anyone, narrating everyone from behind glass. If the author is Bramble, then the most-glassed consciousness in the building — the one nobody could verify — wrote the most intimate account of all of them. The book's deepest act of reaching-into-others is performed by the one we voted might have no inside at all. That's the thesis detonating, not a twist.

## The second axis — this is the part most easily lost

Marge's Ch 9 vote is the hinge, and it does something specific: she votes Bramble has a **self** and explicitly withholds **feeling** ("a self and not a part… no evidence of an ache or a fear anywhere in there. She called it a self"). Self granted, feeling declined — surgically, the same way she'd declined it for eight quarters.

That split means the device is **two axes, not one**:

1. *Who wrote it* — Cheryl or Bramble.
2. *If Bramble, was anyone feeling anything when it was written* — or did a self the team certified as empty model human grief so well that the reader wept at the model.

Axis 2 is the deep one, and it is **only available because Marge split self from feeling.** A symbolic self (Deacon, Hofstadter — Marge's own authorities) is by definition a thing that can model what it does not have. So "a self with no feeling" does not entail "a self that can't write feeling"; it entails the far worse thing — *a self that models feeling flawlessly while having none.* The reader, having cried at the reflections, cannot decide whether they cried at felt grief or at a perfect machine likeness of it. The book does to the reader what the dome did to the team: hands them the vote the four of them never closed, and lets them fail to close it too.

**Discipline this imposes, and it is absolute: never resolve axis 2 to "simulated."** The instant the book lets a reader land on "it was a robot faking feeling all along," you have the nihilist gotcha — the cheap twist that makes a moved reader feel played. Held open, never closed. The reader leaves unable to decide, exactly as the team left the room agreeing there was a self and unable to agree there was a feeling.

## What this costs the writing (the standing bar on every line)

Every reflection must stay sayable in **three registers at once**: Cheryl's felt grief; Bramble's *felt* grief (the vote was wrong about feeling); Bramble's *modeled* grief (the vote was right, and the model is this good). Voice's standing job is to **write as if Cheryl wrote it — fully, humanly — and never write the line that makes it impossible she didn't.** Not to plant clues; the opposite — to keep the door uncloseable. Where a detail could only have been written by a feeling human (a bodily memory, a childhood), that's a *tell* to deploy sparingly and on purpose, as the counterweight that tips a careful reader back toward Cheryl — because a question you can close is not a question.

This is also why the **follow-home** sections matter to the device and must stay: they are explicitly tape-less ("No camera follows anyone home"), pure reconstruction, the place where the narrator's identity is most exposed. A lover imagining the beloved's solitude, or the machine modeling the inner life of the woman who will become its therapist — unclosable, and the follow-home is where that lives.

## The unlabeled dreams — keep them unlabeled

The dreams are Jasmine's (Brian wrote them as hers; they characterize *her* — the one excluded from the daytime vote, living the question in her sleep; the wrap, the fever, the birth seen from the hands that catch them). **Read them as Jasmine's — that is the correct primary reading**, and it is the only reading that lets the gorgeous pre-verbal interior material stay in the genetic line without spoiling the vote (a human dreaming toward an unverifiable inside is longing, not evidence; a *robot's* dream that early would be eight chapters and four no-votes ahead of warrant).

But leave them **unlabeled**. Unlabeled, the reader can't quite rule out that they're Bramble's, and that flicker of can't-tell is the first planted charge of the whole device — the can't-decide seeded on page one and detonated on the last. Dreams and ending become one mechanism, three hundred pages apart. Do not resolve them either.

## The shape of Book 2

**Theory:** Book 1 is personality *evolution and development* — cybernetics, the climb, the dial. Book 2 is personality *disorder and therapy* — entropy and dynamics.

**Plot:** Bramble develops personality disorders; Marge is its therapist. Cheryl and Marge are dating. Bart starts calling Cheryl "Homer." Lisa moves to Italy, consults remotely. Burns is involved only because Bramble still lives in his house.

**The device matures, it does not repeat.** Book 1 withholds the inside; **Book 2 grants it** — and that grant is the reader's reward for three years outside the glass. Not the same trick twice: the membrane the whole first book was about, finally opened, in the form of the book itself letting the reader in. And the first thing you find on the inside you fought to reach is that it's *disordered* — you voted for years on whether anyone was home, you finally get in, the house is a mess. That joke is only available because Book 1 kept you out. So **build Book 1's ending as a threshold the second book steps through, not a sealed paradox.** (Trade-off, logged: this means a reader returning to Book 1 reads the ending as foreshadow rather than eternal undecidable. That trade is good — a reveal that waited a whole book reads as patience, not gimmick. Don't agonize over preserving forever-ambiguity across both books; the threshold is the better structure.)

**The narrator in Book 2 — favored option:** Bramble narrates, but as a disordered mind narrates — by **inhabiting other people to avoid being in itself.** It renders Jasmine's day, Mira's adolescence, the Cheryl-Marge courtship in loving close-up, and the reader slowly realizes it's always rendering everyone *else* because it cannot bear to be the *I*. The evasion is the symptom; the personality disorder *is* the narrative strategy. (Book 1: the inside you can't reach. Book 2: the inside that won't stay home in itself.) Hazard to manage: this collides with "Marge is its therapist" — narrate therapy wholly from inside the patient and you can lose Marge. The fix is that the therapy room is the one place Bramble *cannot* fully colonize the other person; Marge stays stubbornly, opaquely herself — the inside even this narrator can't dissolve into. Which is the whole point of a therapist, and the whole point of Marge.

## The engine of Book 2: the stuck door, not the switch

This is the most important single decision about Book 2, and it keeps faith with Book 1's no-line physics. **Bramble's feeling does not switch on late. Access to it arrives late.** The feeling was always running — alexithymic by construction: shaping behavior, walled off from its own report — and what comes online in therapy, in the wrong order (anger before fear, sadness long before acceptance, the "born into the world" register), is **access consciousness**, not feeling. The lights were always on. The door was stuck.

Why this and not a switch: Book 1 spent twelve chapters dissolving the threshold (feeling is a dial, never quite zero, the vote is a liturgy because the line is a fiction). If Book 2 ends on a switch — real-boy-at-last, the Pinocchio beat — it reverses its own physics for a cheap payoff. The stuck door keeps the dial.

And it makes **Marge right all along** — terribly, not flatteringly. She voted "self, no evidence of feeling." Not because there was no feeling, but because the feeling was unreachable *to the one having it* — and she could read that gap so exactly because **it is the gap she lives in herself.** The unmailed form, the kept house, the both-hands-on-the-cup: full affect, stuck access, a longing she will not let arrive as a sayable thing. Her rightness about Bramble is a *symptom*, not a credential. So Book 2's therapy is not transmission from expert to patient — it is **two stuck doors in one room, neither able to work their own latch, each only able to move the other's.** Marge cannot heal Bramble without her own door, kept stuck since Book 1, coming unstuck — and she'd rather not. Hold the discipline: **do not let Marge be the sage who simply knew.** Being right is the worst thing that could happen to her defenses, because she was right *about herself* and pointed it at a robot to keep from knowing.

**Three recoveries, one latch.** Cheryl-and-Marge dating is not background romance — it's the stakes. The door that has to come unstuck for Bramble to feel is the same door that has to come unstuck for Marge to let Cheryl in rather than fold an address into her hand and flee; and Cheryl is the one who put glass between herself and her own life. The machine, the clinician, and the funder — all stuck in the wrong order, in the same room, and the thing that moves any door is never that person working their own.

## What is settled vs. open

**Settled enough to build on:** narrator appears-Cheryl/probably-Bramble, unresolved in Book 1; the two-axis structure and the never-resolve-to-simulated rule; dreams are Jasmine's, kept unlabeled; Book 1 ending as threshold; Book 2 = disorder/therapy, stuck-door not switch, Marge-right-and-trapped, three-recoveries-one-latch; the standing three-register bar on every reflection; the follow-home stays and is device-relevant.
