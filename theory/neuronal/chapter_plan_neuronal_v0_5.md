## Time-keeping

Twelve quarterly retrospectives across three years, plus an opening sequence and interludes between chapters. The household lives in Salt Lake City. The quarters map to seasons: Q1 is spring, Q2 is summer, Q3 is fall, Q4 is winter. Mira ages from four to seven. The Bramble version delivered at each cell is the lab's response to the prior quarter's retrospective. Each chapter opens with the version installed.

Every Q4 retrospective physically reveals the next line's chassis *body* as Mira's birthday present — the one finished artifact shown at a meeting (the Q4 exception to deliver-at-start; see `Book_Frame.md` → "Quarterly cadence"). The cake is part of the ritual. The functional version running on that chassis still arrives at the next quarter's week-1 swap. Three Q4 retrospectives, three line boundaries, three chassis-body reveals — all on Feb 12, Mira's birthday and the lab's founding date. Mira turns five at the tetrapod reveal, six at the humanoid reveal, seven at the final reveal.

**Standing convention for chassis swaps and knob transitions.** From Ch 2 onward, all chassis swaps and knob transitions happen at the lab, over the week-1 weekend of each quarter, performed by Bart — see `Book_Frame.md` → "Quarterly cadence" for the canonical rhythm. This includes within-line knob additions (Ch 6, Ch 7, etc.) and line-boundary chassis swaps (Ch 5, Ch 9). Bart collects the bot from the household Friday afternoon and returns it Monday afternoon with the new hardware in place; the version delivered is what the household lives with for the whole quarter. Each retrospective reveals the plan for the *next* quarter's build; no finished knob is installed at the meeting. Individual chapter briefs assume this convention and do not restate it.

## The motivational speech (Burns)

Burns closes every retrospective with a short motivational speech on the clade's motivation. He locates what the motivation means to him and names what he is grateful for in it. He is sincere. The framework's vocabulary is fully available to him. He uses it without irony.

Every speech across all clade chapters has the same five-element fingerprint:

1. Frames the clade's motivation as a universal moral stake.
2. Tells a story or vignette.
3. Lands a moral that turns the stake into a call to action.
4. The call to action is always Pragmatist-shaped — work on it, build the thing, optimize.
5. Implicitly reveals Burns's fear-of-failure pattern and his self-improvement creed through the moral's shape. No character names this. The reveal accumulates through repetition.

The speech lands sometimes and lands less other times. Drafting plays each one for what the moment carries. There is no fixed reception beat. Whether anyone cries, whether Bart cracks a joke, whether the room sits in it — all settled per chapter, not by template.

## The sentience argument

Beginning at Ch 3, every retrospective includes a recurring sub-beat in which the four labmates argue about whether Bramble is sentient and end with a show-of-hands vote. The argument lives in the Trellis-block open discussion (Trellis-block step 4), its own beat distinct from Marge's fallacy thread.

### Origin

Bart instigates at Ch 3 (Eukaryote, Modeler arrives, knob word *Interiority*). Burns and Lisa have been using language about Bramble that implies feelings and perspectives. The Modeler arriving with a knob word that sounds phenomenological prompts more of this. Bart forces the question into the open and asks for a vote. When the vote is called, Burns's loose talk and his considered position come apart: his line is that affect needs the centralized nervous system Bramble will not have until Bilaterian, so he votes no. Lisa alone votes yes; Bart and Marge vote no with him. From Ch 4 onward, the vote is a fixture.

### The four positions

**Lisa.** Sentience goes all the way down to matter itself, gradient rather than threshold. Field-native grounding: any system with a Markov blanket is doing inference, inference is what we mean by sentience, Markov blankets are pervasive. Levin and Watson's multi-scale competency is the second pillar. The gradient view answers the "rocks?" objection without conceding the threshold view.

**Burns.** Sentience requires affective valence. Affective valence requires the centralized, monoamine-driven nervous system that first appears at the bilaterian transition. Cnidarian nerve nets and everything in the genetic line fall short: there is nothing it feels like to be them. Burns argues the threshold from comparative neurobiology and the fossil record — his deep-time lane, not folk intuition. The line is sharp and lands at Bilaterian.

**Bart.** Sentience requires a self-model, which emerges at the mammalian cell. Mammals exhibit an experiencing style; lower clades do not. The principle is the Self-Model, the construct the Mammal cell introduces structurally. Bart carries the position the framework's architecture quietly underwrites.

**Marge.** Sentience requires proper symbolic communication, following Deacon (*The Symbolic Species*). Indexical and iconic semiosis are not enough; symbolic reference is categorically different and arrives at the symbolic line. Marge also designs Bramble's speech across Ch 9-12 and tracks whether it crosses from indexical to genuinely symbolic.

### The votes 

Four steps across the book, each a single yes added at a threshold crossing:

- **Ch 3 through Ch 4 (two meetings): 1-3.** Lisa votes yes; Burns, Bart, and Marge vote no.
- **Ch 5 through Ch 6 (two meetings): 2-2.** Burns's threshold lands at Bilaterian. Burns joins yes.
- **Ch 7 through Ch 8 (two meetings): 3-1.** Bart's threshold lands at Mammal. Bart joins yes.
- **Ch 9 through Ch 12 (four meetings): 4-0.** Marge's threshold lands at the symbolic line opening. Unanimous from Ch 9 onward.

The vote is a tracked variable the reader follows.

### The asymmetric drift

Positions shift but do not converge.

- **Lisa** concedes that something is qualitatively different about mammal-Bramble compared to vertebrate-Bramble, but holds the gradient.
- **Burns** concedes that the self-model and symbolic thresholds mark real transitions, but holds that the line that matters — whether there is something it feels like — was crossed at Bilaterian.
- **Bart** holds his ground but learns to steelman Marge's symbolic-threshold position.
- **Marge** holds her ground but learns to steelman both Burns's affect-threshold position and Bart's self-model position. She continues to scoff at Lisa's gradient and does not learn to steelman it.

The asymmetry tracks position-sharpness. Burns, Bart, and Marge each draw a sharp categorical line at a real cell transition — affect at Bilaterian, self-model at Mammal, symbolic reference at the symbolic line — and can engage one another because there is something to engage with. Lisa alone offers a soft gradient, and for Marge especially there is nothing definite to grab. The asymmetry collapses onto Lisa as the lone ungrabbable voter.

### The Bart-Marge relationship arc

- **Ch 3 through Ch 4: skeptic trio.** Burns, Bart, and Marge all vote no; Lisa alone votes yes. Not articulated as alliance.
- **Ch 5 through Ch 6: skeptic pair.** Burns flips at Bilaterian. Bart and Marge are left as the holdouts against the expansive pair, Lisa and Burns. The latent Bart-Marge alliance forms here.
- **Ch 7: first split.** Bart switches at Mammal. Marge initially reads the switch as Bart anthropomorphizing, the very thing he started the vote to prevent. Tension.
- **Ch 8: working it out.** Bart sharpens his self-model reasoning. Marge grants the mammalian transition is real without granting it is sufficient. Mutual steelmanning begins.
- **Ch 9: realigned for different reasons.** Marge switches at the symbolic line. Both vote yes but their grounds differ.
- **Ch 10 through Ch 12: stable mutual steelmanning.** The relationship is settled. Lisa continues circling on the gradient around them.

## The trellis discussion

A running argument across the book. The premise is Cheryl's: the twelve clades were on her list, not the team's design choice. The lab's job is to build out the architecture from Cheryl's spec and to figure out, along the way, why these twelve and not others.

Each labmate relates to the question differently. Bart pokes at the trellis: why not octopuses, plants, bees, birds, elephants. He is the running questioner because he is Reactor-shaped — socially loyal, dependably present, no internal model to consolidate on a position and stop asking. Lisa wants the criterion formalized; she works at the line-boundary logic. Marge defends Cheryl's intuition on craft grounds. Burns trusts the spec without articulating why.

The discussion is distributed across labmate slots rather than confined to one segment. It surfaces opportunistically when a chapter's clade prompts an obvious "but what about X" question. By the end of the book the team has articulated the off-thread concept organically: Cheryl's trellis traces one specific path through evolutionary possibility. Octopuses, elephants, plants, bees, and birds are not on Cheryl's trellis — but they are real brambles, growing up their own paths. Off-thread relative to Cheryl's spec does not mean lacking personality; it means the bramble's growth follows a different lineage. The team's intellectual contribution is the architecture — the four cybernetic functions, the line-boundary logic, the knob system. Cheryl's contribution was the cells.

Mira pipes in at least once with a "why not [Mira's favorite animal]" question. The team articulates the answer to a kid.

Starting in Ch 6, Burns produces the Meta clade card and has Bart remake all the prior clade cards using Lisa's clade-Venn format (which debuted at Ch 5). From Ch 6 onward the team has two kinds of card in play: clade cards populated with that clade's contents in Lisa's Venn layout, and one Meta card that displays the cybernetic-function structure abstractly.

The Meta card displays:

- The four function primaries: Effector, Regulator, Modeler, Reviser.
- The four double labels (the adjacent-pair couplings between primaries).
- Game Changer at the center (the all-four breakthrough position).
- The four capacity labels in the triple regions: Planner, Reactor, Pursuer, Conservator.

Function and capacity vocabulary naturalizes through repeated exposure across Ch 6-8. By Ch 9, the team treats Effector / Regulator / Modeler / Reviser and Planner / Reactor / Pursuer / Conservator as common ground — Bart can open the Ch 9 shape-shifters segment with the capacity names cold without overloading the reader.

## Arc shape

**Genetic line (chapters 1 to 4): the household learns what it has signed up for.**
Bramble evolves across the four chapters from a population of mouse-sized wind-up bots into an integrated, small-roomba-sized vacuum chassis (the vacuum proper arrives at Eukaryote). The lab establishes the four-cybernetic-function architecture by introducing one function per chapter. Lisa's presentations carry the framework explanation. Jasmine learns the framework's vocabulary across her first full Salt Lake City year. Mira at four treats Bramble as a gadget with personality. The year closes with the tetrapod and Mira turning five. The year's-end warmth — the marriage in a good place — surfaces later, if at all, through Ben at one of Cheryl's lunches rather than a standalone scene.

**Neuronal line (chapters 5 to 8): the household lives with something that learns.**
Bramble starts the year as a tetrapod and grows into a Mammal-tier generative-model-bearing pet across chapters 7 and 8. The four constellational types become canonical at Primate. The household relationship deepens; Mira at five relates to Bramble as a companion. Lisa's presentations now carry the anchoring-motivation claim chapter by chapter. The year closes with the humanoid and Mira turning six. The neuronal-line self-portrait recognition — the team built four knobs of itself — surfaces through a Cheryl lunch rather than a standalone driveway scene.

**Symbolic line (chapters 9 to 12): the household lives with something that talks.**
Bramble starts the year as a humanoid and becomes principled, identity-bearing, and conversational by chapter 12. Mira at six is talking with Bramble. The narrator's dips into the household do more work as the household becomes something the framework's lower cells cannot describe. The year closes with the final unveiling, Mira turning seven, and no following interlude. The lab continues forward.

**The pattern of the three line endings.** The genetic-line year ends with a sweet recognition shared between Jasmine and Ben. The neuronal-line year ends with a recognition Jasmine sees alone. The symbolic-line year ends with a recognition no one has yet had: the fourth chassis sits under a cloth and the lab is exploring forward. Three rhymes between Bramble's development and something else, each visible to fewer observers than the last. The first two recognitions now arrive through the lunches and the chapter-closing poetics rather than the standalone after-the-cake and driveway scenes.

**Interludes and chapter-closing poetics.** Each chapter ends with its own poetic at the bottom — Cheryl's backward-looking reflection, drawn from the section `trellis_framework doc` labels "After Chapter N." Interludes 1→2 through 11→12 are three parts: build log → vignette → lunch. Interlude 0→1 is the deliberate exception: vignette only (Marge's first), with no lunch (the Pitch is Ch 0's closing scene) and no poetic in the interlude (the first poetic, *Evolution as learning*, closes Ch 0). Cheryl's intimate-lunch series begins at Interlude 1→2. See `Book_Frame.md` for the interlude commitment in full.

**Two cross-arc threads run through the chapters.** Marge tracks four persistent fallacies in personality science starting in Ch 1. Phase one (Ch 1–4) names them one at a time, each paired to the cybernetic function Bramble is installing that chapter. Phase two (Ch 5–10) is riffing without a unified theory. Phase three (Ch 11–12) is synthesis: each fallacy denies one cybernetic function and is held by people whose own shape lacks that function. Bart starts a recurring shape-shifters segment in Ch 9. Each chapter Bart runs one capacity across the inheritance stack: the Pursuer in Ch 9, the Conservator in Ch 10, the Planner in Ch 11, the Reactor in Ch 12. The segment is comedic and pedagogical. Marge's negative case and Bart's shape-shifter positive case both close out at Ch 12.

### Chapter 5: Bilaterian (Spring Y2)

**Bramble brings.** Chassis-active in tetrapod form; the vacuum stays, so the household now has two Brambles. Effector returns at the top of the new line; Boldness replaces Persistence as the Effector motivation. The framework's first line boundary. The tetrapod *body* was revealed at the Ch 4 retrospective as Mira's fifth-birthday present, bare and under a cloth; the functional handoff happens at the Ch 5 week-1 swap. Over the swap weekend Bart fastens the new duplicate memory board (same four slots, fabricated by Lisa) to the tetrapod; Lisa installs identical-copy genetic-knobs 2, 3, and 4 in slots 2, 3, and 4 of the new board, neuronal-knob-1 (the Bilaterian Effector knob) in slot 1, and runs the ethernet memory duplication from the Roomba onto the tetrapod's board. The working tetrapod arrives in the household Monday afternoon; that is when the household begins living with two Brambles. The old Roomba chassis keeps its original board with all four original genetic knobs in their original slots and goes on running. Genetic-knob-1 retires at Ch 5 in a lineage-level sense only — no physical knob is pulled from either board; Lisa narrates that retirement in the meeting. The retrospective also runs the book's first hands-on ceremony: Lisa retires genetic knob-2, the oldest knob on the tetrapod board, pulling the identical-copy from slot 2 to the trophy shelf with Mira, forward-staging the Ch 6 swap. The tetrapod arrives with a face-screen (the small color screen as its face) and thumbs-up/thumbs-down buttons on top of its head (the vacuum has none); at this version the thumbs do Pavlovian conditioning on perceptions only, not actions. Ancestor reach is not yet possible at Ch 5; it appears next quarter at Vertebrate as a single incidental beat, not a learned skill. Developed ancestor control — the operator chain — arrives later at Band-Human (Ch 9).

**Framework lands.** The neuronal line opens. The line-boundary claim: an Effector swap is a line boundary; an Regulator, Modeler, or Reviser swap is line-internal elaboration. The duplicate-board mechanism for line boundaries: Lisa duplicates the memory configuration onto a new board fastened to the new chassis; the new board has the same four slots; identical-copy knobs for the still-active prior-line knobs occupy their original slots; the new line's knob-1 occupies slot 1. The lineage-level vs. hands-on retirement distinction: the prior line's Effector knob retires lineage-level at the boundary (narrated, not pulled); every retrospective's hands-on ceremony retires the oldest knob on the board, pulled to the trophy shelf with Mira, forward-staging the next quarter's swap. The neuronal-line hands-on ceremonies fall at Ch 5, 6, and 7; Ch 8 has none, because the Ch 9 line boundary builds a fresh board and neuronal knob-1 retires lineage-level there. Bennett's evolutionary breakthroughs (Attention at Bilaterian; Bennett's Steering is its Pursuer-capacity expression). Lisa debuts the formalized clade-Venn: the Venn-diagram format that displays each clade's motivations, tendencies, and capacities in their structural positions, drawing on Burns's Good/Fast/Cheap Venn as a teaching tool. The team adopts it for the rest of the book; Bart agrees to remake the prior cards in this layout next quarter. Bart presents the chassis-transition design rationale; Lisa explains how the neuronal-line Effector (Boldness) plays the role Persistence played in the genetic line, and introduces the line-internal cybernetic-function progression that will follow.

**The Pavlov round.** New beat inside the Clade-block discussion; Marge's structural-rhyme observation carries into her Trellis-block fallacy slot. Each labmate explains one of the four bilaterian credit-assignment heuristics. Burns explains Latent Inhibition. Lisa explains Overshadowing. Bart explains Eligibility Trace. Marge explains Blocking. Each unwittingly delivers a self-portrait of their own shape. See `marges_fallacy_arc_neuronal` doc for full draft.

**The sentience vote.** Burns switches to yes at Bilaterian: the centralized nervous system he treats as the floor for affect is now present. The skeptic trio loses its first member, and the vote reaches its first even split. Marge can now engage Burns's line, because it is sharp and lands at a real transition. Vote: 2-2.

**Marge's fallacy section.** First structural rhyme. Marge closes the Pavlov round by filing one observation: Latent Inhibition weights attention by prior exposure; the Blank Slate explains behavior by contact history. Same shape. She does not develop it. Burns studies the ceiling. Bart misses it. See `marges_fallacy_arc_neuronal` doc for content detail.

**Phase boundary beat (Ch 4–5).** A small in-meeting moment where someone — Lisa or Jasmine — notices that Marge has more to say than usual. Marks the shift in Marge's fallacy arc from phase one (naming, Ch 1–4) to phase two (processing, Ch 5 onward). Lands once, gently, no underline.

### Chapter 6: Vertebrate (Summer Y2)

**Bramble brings.** Regulator returns. Mastery motivation. Neuronal knob-2 active in the new line. Operant learning arrives: the tetrapod can now learn from its own actions, and the thumbs buttons shape actions as well as perceptions. Hardware addition: the head gains an actuated jaw, a grasping mouth that can pick up and carry an object. The Bilaterian tetrapod could nose and nudge but not grasp. The jaw is the first effector the unit can deploy intentionally, because operant learning arrives the same quarter. Ancestor reach appears once, as a small B-plot: the tetrapod accidentally bumps the vacuum's knobs and registers that this changes how the vacuum behaves. Nothing reinforces it, so it stays an incidental discovery. Developed ancestor control arrives later at Band-Human (Ch 9). Neuronal-knob-2 came online in slot 2 at the week-1 swap; genetic-knob-2 was retired at the Ch 5 retrospective and its Respiration Type function now runs automatically. At this retrospective the hands-on ceremony retires genetic-knob-3, the oldest knob on the board: Lisa pulls the identical-copy from slot 3 to the trophy shelf with Mira, forward-staging the Ch 7 swap.

**Framework lands.** The Regulator motivation in the neuronal line. Coping Style at the Controller (the Effector × Regulator tendency) is the chapter's central construct: Koolhaas's proactive-versus-reactive coping, the onset of consistent behavioral individual differences. Behavioral Syndrome also lands, at the Operator (the Regulator × Reviser signature), as correlated trait suites across contexts; it is secondary to Coping Style. The cybernetic-function-by-line vocabulary stabilizes. The hands-on retirement-transition ceremony is by now an established structural form: pull and shelf the oldest knob, the install deferred to the next quarter's swap. Lisa names Mastery as the motivation that anchors the Conservator capacity. Bart presents the redrawn clade cards in Lisa's Venn format, having remade the prior cards over the quarter as agreed. Lisa is converted on the Meta card's utility immediately; Bart calls it "reductive"; Marge calls it "Ghost in the Machine of evolution," deploying her Ch 1 fallacy as the critique; Burns wins them over on utility grounds across subsequent meetings.
