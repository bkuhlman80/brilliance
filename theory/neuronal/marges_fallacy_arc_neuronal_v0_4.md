# Marge's Fallacy Arc — Neuronal Line

## The arc

Marge tracks four persistent fallacies in personality science across the full book. Each chapter she presents a short fallacy section in the Trellis block of the retrospective. She does not arrive with a theory. She accumulates one across twelve quarters of working in the same room as Bramble.

The arc has three phases.

**Ch 1–4: naming.** One fallacy per chapter, paired to the cybernetic function Bramble is installing that chapter. Marge does not connect them. She is a psychologist noticing four things in her field that bother her, one at a time, as the lab's work gives her vocabulary to name them.

**Ch 5–10: riffing.** Marge senses a pattern she cannot yet articulate. Each chapter she works one angle. Structural rhymes. What each fallacy gets right. Why they persist. She hunts for a fifth and comes up empty. She starts to suspect each fallacy is held by people who are themselves a certain shape, but she does not yet trust the suspicion.

**Ch 11–12: synthesis.** Marge presents the unified theory. The four fallacies are not random. Each denies one of the four cybernetic functions. Each is held by people whose own shape lacks that function. The book's central anti-optimization claim arrives from her.

Marge is a lesbian.

At Ch 9 Marge floats her type hypothesis from the clinical register she works in: if a clinical pattern attaches to a shape, a fallacy may attach the same way. She does not work a specific case in the book — the hypothesis arrives as a clinician's pattern-recognition, not a documented instance. The fallacy arc closes on its own thesis at Ch 11–12. The clinical register itself is documented in the `Inheritance_Lines_symbolic` doc and is not rendered in this book.

## Per chapter

### Ch 5: First structural rhyme

**Bramble.** Tetrapod chassis. Effector Boldness installed. Neuronal line opens. The Pavlov heuristic round happens this chapter (each labmate explains one).

**Marge's angle.** First structural rhyme.

**Her stance.** Marge is sitting through the Pavlov round. Burns has just explained Latent Inhibition. Latent inhibition weights attention by exposure history — the habitually seen gets filtered, the novel stays live. Marge says, quietly: "That is the same shape as the Blank Slate. What gets attended is a function of what has been habituated." She does not develop it. She files it.

**What she covers.** Just the one observation. Latent Inhibition and the Blank Slate share a structural move: both make attention a function of contact history. She gestures at the rhyme and stops.

**Connection to chapter.** The Pavlov heuristic round is the setup. Marge picks up one structural homology from it. She does not yet realize there are four. The homologies are a rhyme-layer she is filing; the connection to the cybernetic architecture is a separate move that lands at the Ch 11 synthesis.
#### The Pavlov round

**Context.** Ch 5 is structurally busy: line boundary, new chassis, new Effector motivation, Marge's first structural-rhyme observation in her fallacy arc. The Pavlov round is a single beat inside Marge's vignette-and-clade segment. The clade is bilaterians. The clade-defining cybernetic move is credit assignment: when a cue precedes an event, which cue caused the event? Bilaterians solved this with four heuristics inherited by every nervous system that came after. Marge brings these into her clade discussion. The four labmates each explain one.

This beat sets up two later things: Marge's structural-rhyme observation (her first move toward type-provincialism), and the bilaterian level in Bart's later rollings, which reuses these four heuristics as the foundation each shape's rolling rests on.

**Meeting position.** Inside Marge's segment. She introduces the topic, hands it around the table, and closes with her observation. The four labmates each give a short explanation in the same beat.

**Self-portrait read.** Each labmate explains the heuristic that matches their own shape. The reader on first pass sees four people explaining four credit-assignment moves. The reader on reread sees four people each holding up their own mirror. No one in the room sees the pattern. The neuronal-line self-portrait recognition surfaces later through a Cheryl lunch, no longer a standalone driveway scene. The round is a bread crumb on the way.

This four-character / four-heuristic mapping is the content that Marge's grad-school Venn organizes for the writer (see Marge's Venn). The Venn does not appear on the page here. The round dramatizes the mapping through the four labmates rather than by displaying any diagram; Marge works it with her own thinking, not by showing the team a Venn.

The mapping:

- **Burns explains Latent Inhibition.** Privilege novel cues over habituated ones. Extroversion (Reviser) is built on this. Neophilia.
- **Lisa explains Overshadowing.** When multiple cues are present, the strongest wins the credit. Thinking (Modeler) is built on this. Stimulus potency.
- **Bart explains Eligibility Trace.** Credit the cue that just happened. Intuition (Effector) is built on this. Stimulus recency; spontaneity.
- **Marge explains Blocking.** Once an association is established, block other cues from forming the same one. Introversion (Regulator) is built on this. Neophobia.

Order in the meeting: Burns goes first (director, gets first), Lisa second (technologist, formal mode), Bart third (designer, energy), Marge closes with hers plus the structural-rhyme observation.

Marge had her notes spread out. She had pulled an old Bennett quote out of *A Brief History of Intelligence* and read it aloud to set up the round.

"Bilaterians solved credit assignment with four heuristics. Pavlov mapped them originally. Kamin and Lubow tightened them later. They've been running in every nervous system for five hundred and fifty million years. We agreed last week that each of us would take one."

Each of them had an assignment. They were about to perform it like a science fair project. There was something lovable about that.

Marge nodded at Burns.

Burns leaned forward, elbows on the table, no notes.

"Latent inhibition. You see a cue. You see the cue again. You see it a hundred more times. Nothing happens. Brain flags it: background. Now when the cue does appear next to something important, the brain has already filtered it. The cue you didn't notice doesn't get to form new associations. The cue you've never seen before gets full attention. Mouse in a cage. Trader in a market. Animal already knows what to ignore."

Burns sat back. He had taken thirty seconds and he looked pleased. He had earned it.

Lisa had notes. She turned to a clean page.

"Overshadowing. Several cues are present at the same time and one event follows. The strongest cue takes the credit. Quieter cues get overshadowed even when they're also predictive. It's attention concentration. The trade-off is real. You lose information from the weaker signals. You also don't waste capacity tracking every signal at once. There's a paper from Mackintosh in the seventies that formalizes it as competitive associative learning, which I can pull if anyone wants the math."

Bart was not going to want the math.

Bart did not want the math.

"Eligibility trace. The cue right before the event gets the credit. Half a second window, maybe a second. The bell rings, then the food shows up. Bell gets the credit. If the predictor was three seconds before the food, the trace is too cold and the brain can't connect them. Long-horizon stuff is invisible. The trace is only what just happened."

He paused.

"Now is everything."

Bart did not hear himself.

Marge had her own notes. She looked at them and then closed the folder.

"Blocking. You've learned that cue A predicts event X. Now cue B starts showing up at the same time as cue A, also predicting X. You'll never learn the B association. The A predictor blocks B from forming the same connection. It's an efficiency move. You already know what predicts what. Don't waste capacity rewriting it. Kamin worked this out in the sixties. It's been replicated in every animal with a nervous system that anyone's bothered to test."

She paused, looking at her own notes again.

"Latent inhibition," she said. "Weights attention by exposure history. The habitually seen gets filtered. The novel stays."

The room quieted slightly. She wasn't looking at Burns. She was looking at her notes.

"That's the same shape as the Blank Slate. Context explains behavior because behavior is exposure-weighted. What gets noticed is what wasn't habituated. Same move."

She tapped the page.

"Huh."

Burns was studying the ceiling.

Lisa looked up. "Same shape how?"

"Exposure weighting. The heuristic is asking which cue predicted reward, and its answer turns on what was already habituated — on prior contact. The fallacy is asking what shapes this person, and its answer turns on what they were exposed to — also prior contact. Both make attention a function of contact history."

"Huh," Lisa said.

Bart was already moving on. "OK, knob design for next quarter."

Marge let it go. She wrote a single line in the margin of her notebook and closed it.

Marge had just noticed something and put it away. Whatever she had written down, no one in the room knew yet.

#### Voice and beat notes

**Burns.** Direct. Short sentences. Examples that betray restlessness (trader, market, "animal already knows what to ignore"). No softening. The Pursuer's signature in language: no hedge, no qualifier, end on a punchy line.

**Lisa.** Hedges, frames trade-offs, references the literature, offers the math. The Planner's signature: model-rich, attentive to costs and benefits, willing to dig deeper if anyone wants. The aside about Mackintosh shows she's been reading.

**Bart.** Fast. Vivid examples. Doesn't reference the literature. Ends on a thesis statement about himself that he doesn't recognize as a thesis statement about himself. "Now is everything" is what the Reactor believes about time — no self-model to situate the moment in sequence. Bart doesn't see it; the reader does, especially on reread.

**Marge.** Practical. Closes her notes folder before speaking (she knows what she's saying). References Kamin (she's an academic; she knows the source). Her observation is small and deflating. She doesn't sell it. She files it and moves on. The Conservator's signature: she has noticed a structural rhyme, she has saved it, she is not yet ready to develop it.

**Narrator dips.** Several short dips across the round, into different characters. Into Jasmine watching the four of them perform themselves. Into Burns at the ceiling-studying beat. Into Marge as she closes her notebook. Into Bart as he moves on without noticing what Marge just did. The dips are brief and unmarked. The reader does not need to track whose head we are in for each one; the narrator moves freely.

**Burns studying the ceiling.** When Marge maps Latent Inhibition to the Blank Slate fallacy, Burns is the one who just explained Latent Inhibition. The Blank Slate is the fallacy that gets generalized from his shape (the Pursuer). Marge has just put both halves of a connection on the table — Burns's heuristic and Burns's fallacy — without knowing it. He looks up at the ceiling. He does not say anything.

The reader does not yet know this is happening. The reader on reread sees Burns's ceiling-studying as the first moment he could have caught the connection and chose not to.

**Bart moving on.** Bart not noticing what Marge just did is the Reactor's signature. Eligibility trace runs his own attention: what just happened was a heuristic explanation, what's next is knob design, the long-time-horizon thread (Marge's structural rhyme as a step in her fallacy arc) doesn't fit in the trace window. He moves on. Marge lets him.

#### Chapter-level decisions (locked)

**Marge's structural-rhyme observation is explicit to the room.** Marge says it aloud; Lisa asks "same shape how?" and Marge gives a one-paragraph explanation. The reader gets the structural homology on first read; the rhyme does not have to live only in subtext.

**Burns reacts via the ceiling-studying.** Lightest possible reaction. He looks up at the ceiling and does not say anything. Just enough that the reader on reread can locate it as the first moment he could have caught the connection and chose not to.

**The narrator's dips stay at full density.** Several short dips across the meeting moment, into different characters. The four labmates performing themselves is the chapter's emotional center; the density of dips is the point.

**Structure of the four-mapping.** The four pairs follow one rule: each fallacy rhymes with the heuristic that builds the function opposite the one it denies (opposites are the Venn diagonals, Effector–Modeler and Regulator–Reviser). Equivalently, in the Pavlov round each labmate explains a heuristic for a function their own shape has, and that heuristic rhymes with the fallacy denying the function their shape lacks, which is their own fallacy. The four-mapping is a rhyme-layer, not a derivation of the Ch 11 fallacy-denies-function synthesis.

### Ch 6: What each gets right

**Bramble.** Regulator Mastery installed. The tetrapod is getting good at things. Bramble's competence is increasing.

**Marge's angle.** What each fallacy gets right.

**Her stance.** Marge is going to bat for the fallacies. She points out that they are not stupid. Each one is built on a real observation. Each one is held by reasonable people for reasonable reasons. The error is not in the observation. The error is in the inflation.

**What she covers.** Brief pass through all four. The Noble Savage is right that fit matters and environments shape outcomes. The Blank Slate is right that context matters and behavior varies. The Ghost in the Machine is right that change is possible and the reviser is a real function. The Dealt Hand is right that traits are stable and heritability is real.

She emphasizes: she is not saying these views are wrong. She is saying each takes a real observation and makes it the whole story.

**Connection to chapter.** Bramble's growing mastery is stable. The Dealt Hand's "traits are stable" insight is exactly right about this. The mistake is not the observation. The mistake is taking the observation as the architecture.

### Ch 7: Why they persist

**Bramble.** Modeler Autonomy installed. Generative model online. Bramble is now socially modeling. Family-dog tier.

**Marge's angle.** Why these fallacies persist.

**Her stance.** Puzzled. Marge cannot understand why these views are still alive. Pavlov was a hundred and ten years ago. Pinker named three of these myths twenty-five years ago. The fallacies should be dead. They are not.

**What she covers.** She tries different answers and rejects them. Incentive structures in the field. Market demand for self-help. Generational forgetting. Each answer feels incomplete. The fallacies persist for some reason she cannot name. She admits this openly.

The thing she cannot name is the type-provincialism point. Each fallacy is held by people for whom it feels true to their own experience because the function it denies is the function their own shape most lacks. Marge does not have this yet. She will get there in chapter nine.

**Connection to chapter.** Bramble at Mammal tier can model others. Marge wonders aloud whether Bramble, given a personality theory test, would generate any of the four fallacies. She suspects yes. She does not know which one. The question hangs.

### Ch 8: Looking for a fifth

**Bramble.** Reviser Affiliation installed. Mira turns six. The four primate triples become canonical in the framework. Humanoid chassis under the cloth.

**Marge's angle.** Hunting for a fifth fallacy.

**Her stance.** Methodical. She suspects she is missing one. She does not know there cannot be a fifth.

**What she covers.** She workshops candidates. Determinism. Reductionism. Behaviorism. Genetic essentialism. Existentialism. Each one she tries turns out to be a flavor of one of the four she already has. Determinism collapses into Dealt Hand. Behaviorism is closer to Blank Slate. Existentialism is a romantic Ghost in the Machine.

She comes up empty. She tells the room she keeps thinking there should be a fifth, but every candidate ends up being one of the four she has. She does not know why.

The reader, who has the framework, knows. There are four cybernetic functions. There are four ways to deny one of them. There is no fifth shape.

**Connection to chapter.** The four primate triples become canonical in the same chapter Marge looks for a fifth fallacy and cannot find one. The framework's structural claim is sitting in the room. Marge is two moves away from connecting her four fallacies to the four triples. The framework's four triples are now visible in Bart's design work. The connection between the two fours is the thing she will arrive at in chapter eleven.

The neuronal-line self-portrait recognition surfaces after this chapter through a Cheryl lunch, no longer a standalone driveway scene: the labmates-as-types, with Burns unable to see it. Marge is the labmate who has been closest to the type idea all along through her fallacy work. She still does not see it.

## Marge's Venn

Marge developed an idiosyncratic Venn in grad school: a Jungian, MBTI-based mapping she built to organize personality the way she had been trained to see it. She brings it to the pitch. The team tries it briefly as a working framework on the office patio afterward and rejects it as too restrictive — prokaryotes do not have a thinking/feeling preference. The Venn does not return as a structural element in any chapter.

It survives in two other ways.

**As an authorial scaffold.** The Venn organizes the four characters, the four fallacies, and the four Pavlovian heuristics for the writer. It is a backstage instrument — a way to keep the mappings straight while drafting. It does not appear on the page after the pitch interlude.

**As a formal ancestor.** The team's later four-circle Venn for ABHOP keeps the geometric structure of Marge's Venn and replaces its content. Marge's instinct to think in a Venn was structurally right even when her Jungian content was wrong; the shape she reached for in grad school is the shape the framework needs, and only the labels change.

The mapping below is authorial reference, not in-book material. It records how Marge's grad-school Venn lines up with the framework — each cybernetic function taking one MBTI label directly and the four pair-syntheses taking the other four — so the writer can compose the characters, fallacies, and heuristics coherently. The convergence with standard MBTI is asymmetric, but the structural relationship is intact.

### The singles

The four cybernetic functions take MBTI labels, each grounded in one of the Pavlovian credit-assignment heuristics from the Ch 5 round:

- Effector = Intuition, built on eligibility trace (stimulus recency; spontaneity).
- Regulator = Introversion, built on blocking (familiarity; neophobia).
- Modeler = Thinking, built on overshadowing (stimulus potency).
- Reviser = Extroversion, built on latent inhibition (novelty; neophilia).

### The doubles

The four adjacent doubles take the remaining MBTI labels:

- Controller (Effector × Regulator) = Feeling
- Anticipator (Regulator × Modeler) = Judgment
- Adapter (Modeler × Reviser) = Sensation
- Explorer (Reviser × Effector) = Perception

### The dichotomies

The four MBTI dichotomies run between Venn regions:

- Energy-direction (E/I) runs between two singles: Reviser (E) and Regulator (I).
- Information (N/S) runs between a single and a double: Effector (N) and Adapter (S).
- Decisions (T/F) runs between a single and a double: Modeler (T) and Controller (F).
- Lifestyle (J/P) runs between two doubles: Explorer (P) and Anticipator (J).

Two dichotomies cross between regions of the same type; two cross between a single and a double. That is the asymmetry. It is the cost of giving each cybernetic function a direct MBTI label rather than mapping MBTI onto axes-and-pairs symmetrically. The fifteen-region Venn still places a person at a continuous position rather than sorting into one of sixteen types, and the borderline-score sorting is what produces MBTI's weak psychometric reputation.

### The labmates

Each labmate occupies one capacity. Three of their four MBTI letters are forced by the regions inside their triple. The fourth wobbles between two regions inside the triple. The wobble is a context-dependent shift, not a continuum point.

- Marge is Conservator-shaped. Forced: I, N, J. Wobble: T/F. Her Architect side (INTJ) is where she shines. Her Advocate side (INFJ) is more comfortable.
- Lisa is Planner-shaped. Forced: S, T, J. Wobble: E/I. Her Logistician side (ISTJ) is the class nerd. Her Executive side (ESTJ) is more agentic.
- Bart is Reactor-shaped. Forced: N, F, P. Wobble: E/I. His Campaigner side (ENFP) is the class clown. His Mediator side (INFP) is warmer.
- Burns is Pursuer-shaped. Forced: E, T, P. Wobble: S/N. His Entrepreneur side (ESTP) is the one he shows Cheryl. His Debater side (ENTP) shows up around the team.

Bart and Lisa wobble within a single MBTI category — Diplomat and Sentinel. Burns and Marge wobble across categories. The triples that include both Reviser (E) and Regulator (I) as singles produce E/I wobbles within one category. The triples that include only one of those produce wobbles in a different dichotomy and end up crossing categories.
