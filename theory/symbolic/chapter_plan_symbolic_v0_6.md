## Time-keeping

The quarters map to seasons: Q1 is spring, Q2 is summer, Q3 is fall, Q4 is winter. Mira ages from four to seven. Two chassis-body reveals, at the two line boundaries, both on Feb 12, Mira's birthday and the lab's founding date. Mira turns five at the tetrapod reveal and six at the humanoid reveal. Ch 12 (Q4 Y3) is terminal, with no next line and no chassis reveal; Mira turns seven, and the Ch 12 final beat is pending redesign [TBD].

**Standing convention for chassis swaps and knob transitions.** From Ch 2 onward, all chassis swaps and knob transitions happen at the lab, over the week-1 weekend of each quarter, performed by Bart — see `Book_Frame.md` → "Quarterly cadence" for the canonical rhythm. Bart collects the bot from the household Friday afternoon and returns it Monday afternoon with the new hardware in place; the version delivered is what the household lives with for the whole quarter. Each retrospective reveals the plan for the *next* quarter's build; no finished knob is installed at the meeting. Individual chapter briefs assume this convention and do not restate it.

## The motivational speech (Burns)

Burns closes every retrospective with a short motivational speech on the clade's motivation. He locates what the motivation means to him and names what he is grateful for in it. He is sincere. The framework's vocabulary is fully available to him. He uses it without irony.

Every speech across all clade chapters has the same five-element fingerprint:

1. Frames the clade's motivation as a universal moral stake.
2. Tells a story or vignette.
3. Lands a moral that turns the stake into a call to action.
4. The call to action is always action-oriented — work on it, build the thing, optimize.
5. Implicitly reveals Burns's fear-of-failure pattern and his self-improvement creed through the moral's shape. No character names this. The reveal accumulates through repetition.

The speech lands sometimes and lands less other times. Drafting plays each one for what the moment carries. There is no fixed reception beat. Whether anyone cries, whether Bart cracks a joke, whether the room sits in it — all settled per chapter, not by template.

## The sentience argument

Beginning at Ch 3, every retrospective includes a recurring sub-beat in which the four labmates argue about whether Bramble is sentient and end with a show-of-hands vote. The argument lives in the Trellis-block open discussion (Trellis-block step 4), its own beat distinct from Marge's fallacy thread.

### Origin

Bart instigates at Ch 3 (Eukaryote, Modeler arrives, knob word *Interiority*). Burns and Lisa have been using language about Bramble that implies feelings and perspectives. The Modeler arriving with a knob word that sounds phenomenological prompts more of this. Bart forces the question into the open and asks for a vote. When the vote is called, Burns's loose talk and his considered position come apart: his line is that affect needs the centralized nervous system Bramble will not have until Bilaterian, so he votes no. Lisa alone votes yes; Bart and Marge vote no with him. From Ch 4 onward, the vote is a fixture.

### The four positions

**Lisa.** Sentience goes all the way down to matter itself, gradient rather than threshold. Field-native grounding: any system with a Markov blanket is doing inference, inference is what we mean by sentience, Markov blankets are pervasive. Levin and Watson's multi-scale competency is the second pillar. The gradient view answers the "rocks?" objection without conceding the threshold view.

**Burns.** Sentience requires affective valence. Affective valence requires the centralized, monoamine-driven nervous system that first appears at the bilaterian transition. Cnidarian nerve nets and everything in the genetic line fall short: there is nothing it feels like to be them. Burns argues the threshold from comparative neurobiology and the fossil record — his deep-time lane, not folk intuition. The line is sharp and lands at Bilaterian.

**Bart.** Sentience requires a self-model, which emerges at the mammalian cell. Mammals exhibit an experiencing style; lower clades do not. The principle is the Self-Model, the construct the Mammal cell introduces structurally. Bart carries the position the framework's architecture quietly underwrites.

**Marge.** Sentience requires proper symbolic communication, following Deacon (*The Symbolic Species*), together with a self-referential "I". Indexical and iconic semiosis are not enough; symbolic reference is categorically different and arrives at the symbolic line. At the symbolic line she also reads Hofstadter's strange-loop as the basis of selfhood, present in Bramble once the symbolic self is in place; this stronger reading is Marge's, and the narrator does not necessarily share it. Marge also designs Bramble's speech across Ch 9-12 and tracks whether it crosses from indexical to genuinely symbolic.

### The votes 

Four steps across the book, each a single yes added at a threshold crossing:

- **Ch 3 through Ch 4 (two meetings): 1-3.** Lisa votes yes; Burns, Bart, and Marge vote no.
- **Ch 5 through Ch 6 (two meetings): 2-2.** Burns's threshold lands at Bilaterian. Burns joins yes.
- **Ch 7 through Ch 8 (two meetings): 3-1.** Bart's threshold lands at Mammal. Bart joins yes.
- **Ch 9 through Ch 12 (four meetings): 4-0.** Marge's threshold lands at the symbolic line opening. Unanimous from Ch 9 onward.

The vote is a tracked variable the reader follows.

### The asymmetric drift

Positions shift but do not converge.

- **Lisa** concedes that something is qualitatively different about mammal-Bramble compared to vertebrate-Bramble, but holds the gradient.
- **Burns** concedes that the self-model and symbolic thresholds mark real transitions, but holds that the line that matters — whether there is something it feels like — was crossed at Bilaterian.
- **Bart** holds his ground but learns to steelman Marge's symbolic-threshold position.
- **Marge** holds her ground but learns to steelman both Burns's affect-threshold position and Bart's self-model position. She continues to scoff at Lisa's gradient and does not learn to steelman it.

The asymmetry tracks position-sharpness. Burns, Bart, and Marge each draw a sharp categorical line at a real cell transition — affect at Bilaterian, self-model at Mammal, symbolic reference at the symbolic line — and can engage one another because there is something to engage with. Lisa alone offers a soft gradient, and for Marge especially there is nothing definite to grab. The asymmetry collapses onto Lisa as the lone ungrabbable voter.

### The Bart-Marge relationship arc

- **Ch 3 through Ch 4: skeptic trio.** Burns, Bart, and Marge all vote no; Lisa alone votes yes. Not articulated as alliance.
- **Ch 5 through Ch 6: skeptic pair.** Burns flips at Bilaterian. Bart and Marge are left as the holdouts against the expansive pair, Lisa and Burns. The latent Bart-Marge alliance forms here.
- **Ch 7: first split.** Bart switches at Mammal. Marge initially reads the switch as Bart anthropomorphizing, the very thing he started the vote to prevent. Tension.
- **Ch 8: working it out.** Bart sharpens his self-model reasoning. Marge grants the mammalian transition is real without granting it is sufficient. Mutual steelmanning begins.
- **Ch 9: realigned for different reasons.** Marge switches at the symbolic line. Both vote yes but their grounds differ.
- **Ch 10 through Ch 12: stable mutual steelmanning.** The relationship is settled. Lisa continues circling on the gradient around them.

## The trellis discussion

A running argument across the book. The premise is Cheryl's: the twelve clades were on her list, not the team's design choice. The lab's job is to build out the architecture from Cheryl's spec and to figure out, along the way, why these twelve and not others.

Each labmate relates to the question differently. Bart pokes at the trellis: why not octopuses, plants, bees, birds, elephants. He is the running questioner because he is Reactor-shaped — socially loyal, dependably present, no internal model to consolidate on a position and stop asking. Lisa wants the criterion formalized; she works at the line-boundary logic. Marge defends Cheryl's intuition on craft grounds. Burns trusts the spec without articulating why.

The discussion is distributed across labmate slots rather than confined to one segment. It surfaces opportunistically when a chapter's clade prompts an obvious "but what about X" question. By the end of the book the team has articulated the off-thread concept organically: Cheryl's trellis traces one specific path through evolutionary possibility. Octopuses, elephants, plants, bees, and birds are not on Cheryl's trellis — but they are real brambles, growing up their own paths. Off-thread relative to Cheryl's spec does not mean lacking personality; it means the bramble's growth follows a different lineage. The team's intellectual contribution is the architecture — the four cybernetic functions, the line-boundary logic, the knob system. Cheryl's contribution was the cells.

Mira pipes in at least once with a "why not [Mira's favorite animal]" question. The team articulates the answer to a kid.

The Meta card displays:

- The four function primaries: Effector, Regulator, Modeler, Reviser.
- The four double labels (the adjacent-pair couplings between primaries).

## The Olympics weather and Cheryl's thinning narration (Ch 9–12)

Two linked threads begin at Ch 9 and run to the terminal chapter. They are texture and narration, not theme. This section is the going-in state for the Ch 10–12 handoff packets: each symbolic-line packet should point future-Voice back here and to the Ch 9 "Olympics weather + Cheryl's first hairline" VOICE NOTE, so that prep reads the worked-out thinking rather than starting cold.

**Thread one — the Olympics as thickening weather.** The Salt Lake City Winter Games fall in February 2034, inside Ch 12. The orientation rules them *weather, not theme*: the book never argues about them, never makes them a subject, never leans on them for meaning. The Ch 9 work adds only a direction of travel — the weather thickens across the final year on a schedule. At Ch 9 (spring 2033, ~11 months out) it is barely perceptible: a banner, a permit notice, the committee downtown — ambient, narrator-level, never in a character's mouth. By Ch 12 the Games are physically happening around the lab. The background gets more crowded chapter by chapter; it is never promoted to a theme.

**Thread two — Cheryl's thinning narration.** Cheryl was not in the meeting rooms; she rebuilds each retrospective afterward from the camcorder tape and her quarterly lunches, and the narration already admits its own seams. As she is pulled into Olympics business — coordination, city improvements, buying and selling Park City real estate — her attention divides and the reconstruction visibly thins. That thinning is the instrument. It shows on the page two ways: she begins admitting parts she does not have ("I don't have the part where Lisa answered — the camcorder was pointed at the window"); and her descriptive lists, which used to gather lab objects with care, slide toward the vocabulary of her other life — asking prices, lot sizes, committee acronyms. **The governing rule: it must always read as Cheryl knowing she is distracted and telling us so** — a character changing in front of the reader, named and deliberate, her distraction narrated the way she already narrates her own biases. It must never read as the prose itself getting sloppy. That self-awareness is exactly what keeps it on the right side of the line.

**Why the two threads are one (candidate spine — Voice to confirm).** Across the symbolic line the lab teaches Bramble to talk: single words at Ch 9, climbing toward real speech by Ch 12. Across the same stretch Cheryl's narrating voice does the opposite — it thins and scatters. The machine gains a voice while the narrator loses hers. That mirror is the candidate spine for the whole final movement. It is **Voice's to confirm** (an arc call, made with Brian); no packet states it as settled.

**Ownership — read before carrying anything else.** Two owners touch this material. **Voice** owns the per-chapter texture (how the weather and the distraction read on the page), the calendar (all dates), and the shape across chapters — how fast the weather thickens, how far Cheryl's voice unravels each chapter, and whether the machine-gains-voice / narrator-loses-voice mirror becomes the official spine. **Framework** owns the packets and the canon-check. **Loom** owns nothing here: it is an arc-scale idea source, and any Loom pitch about the curve or the spine reaches Voice only through Brian, carrying no prior weight (see `how_we_draft`). Practical consequence: the curve and the spine are Voice's to write into the orientation; until Voice does, carry the per-chapter items below as working intentions marked *arc-shape to confirm*, not locked canon. The day Voice writes the arc into the orientation, the packets inherit the real version and the canon-check takes over. The **calendar facts are settled now** and may be reflected immediately.

**The fact-versus-meaning split (governs the downstream lunches).** At the Ch 9 Bart lunch (I8→9), Bart asks Cheryl what she is doing with the Olympics and learns the plain facts — she is busy, coordinating, trading property — and may needle her about the irony (a woman funding a three-year project on why ecosystems collapse, spending its back half flipping ski-town lots). Bart only ever gets the fact. The deeper reading — that Cheryl is unconsciously repeating her own family's history, the cannery money that fled a dead fishery straight into real estate, extraction turning back into acquisition — is **Marge's to land, at the I9→10 lunch**, because Marge is the team's pattern-reader and the character Cheryl's own biography names as the one who would see it. The rule for every downstream packet: Bart and the narration may handle the *facts* of Cheryl's Olympics life; only Marge names what it *means*; Cheryl herself never spells it out. If a later chapter lets Bart, Cheryl, or anyone else state the meaning, Marge's moment is gone before it arrives.

**What each symbolic-line packet should carry.** (1) A pointer back to the Ch 9 "Olympics weather + Cheryl's first hairline" VOICE NOTE. (2) A brief restatement of the two protective rules — the Olympics stay background weather and are never argued about; Cheryl's thinning narration is always self-aware and named, never accidental-looking. (3) The fact-versus-meaning split above.

**The "canning" move (Ch 10, kept distinct from the tape-gaps).** Cheryl normally quotes Burns directly, because quoting gives the living man; merely reporting or summarizing him seals him away — the "canned" version, the word tied to her family's cannery and to her own reflex of sealing things away rather than letting them stay alive. Exactly once in the whole book, at Ch 10, per the Burns-speeches material, she reports one of Burns's speeches instead of quoting it, because at that moment she cannot bear to hand us his living words. The single report-instead-of-quote lands hard precisely because she has never done it before. **Keep it distinct from the Olympics tape-gaps.** Both are the narrator withholding, so they look alike on the surface — but they mean opposite things: the tape-gap is distraction (Cheryl elsewhere, half-attending); the canning move is the reverse (Cheryl so present to Burns, and so moved, she cannot make herself quote him). Let the tape-gaps be distraction and the canning be grief; do not let the one rare move dissolve into the general thinning.
