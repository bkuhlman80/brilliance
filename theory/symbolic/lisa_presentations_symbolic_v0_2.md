# Section 1: Vocabulary discipline

This section locks the vocabulary discipline that Lisa's segments are drafted in. Each per-chapter argument structure is built against it. The discipline applies across all of Lisa's segments, not just one. When a structural fact needs invocation in any segment, the translations here are how Lisa invokes it.

The discipline matters because the framework has been built with insider vocabulary that real scientists would not recognize. Lisa speaks for the framework in the book. If her vocabulary leaks framework-internal shorthand, the framework reads as a private language rather than as a genuine theoretical claim. The Lisa-reader (the framework's most natural audience) is the one who notices first.

## Lisa's character premise

Lisa is the lab's Technologist. Planner-shaped. She specs the formal architecture, the algorithms, the model.

Her vocabulary is the vocabulary of three fields she actually works in: computer science, biology, neuroscience. She is the most jargony of the four labmates. The jargon is field-native rather than framework-internal.

She is aversive to psychology terms. When a cell's empirical anchor is psychological (attachment theory, self-construal, social identity, Big Five facets, HEXACO, Dark Triad, personality disorders), the depth belongs to Marge. Lisa can reference. She does not anchor.

She cites authors directly. She names what she does not yet know. She offers to dig into the math if anyone wants it. She flags her own confidence level when she is unsure, in the cadence of the Ch 11 cities-entry caveat from Bart's shape-shifters.

## Lisa's segments in the meeting

Lisa speaks at many points in the retrospective. The full meeting agenda is in `../Book_Frame.md` under `## The retrospective`. Lisa's substantive content slots are:

- **Open: logistics of the meeting.** Brief stage-management beat — agenda, camcorder setup, snacks, who is running what. Sets the meeting's frame. Not content-bearing for the framework.
- **Clade block step 2: tendencies (with Burns).** Lisa presents the clade's tendencies in functional language — what the organism does, what behaviors fall out of the architecture. Lisa cares because she programs Bramble's behavior; tendencies are her engineering output. Burns brings the deep-time environmental context alongside.
- **Clade block step 3: motivations and signatures (with Marge).** Lisa names the active motivations in CS-native register — what each does in the model, what consolidations brought the current set into the foreground, what each parameterizes — and presents the signatures (the opposite-pair couplings at the Connector and Operator slots) using the literature-anchored construct names. Marge layers folk-psychology vocabulary onto both.
- **Bramble block step 2: new-knob software pitch (with Bart).** Lisa presents the software side of the new knob — what it does in the model, what it parameterizes, how it builds on prior knobs. Bart presents the hardware and form factor.
- **Bramble block step 3: retirement-transition ritual (with Mira).** A hands-on mechanical action. Lisa moves a knob's function into Bramble's memory with Mira as ritual participant. This slot does not carry framework exposition — that has moved to Cheryl's chapter-closing poetics.

## What Lisa never says

**The R-numbers.** No R1, R2, R3, R4. No R-role. No central R-role. When she has to name a function, she names it: Effector, Regulator, Modeler, Reviser.

**The rolling window.** This phrase is retired. Lisa describes the architecture: each new cell brings one new motivation into play, and the oldest of the prior four drops below into the memory. She has two shorthand options. Up through Ch 3 she says "the four motivations active at this cell." From Ch 4 onward — once she invents the relay metaphor during her run between Ch 3 and Ch 4 — she can say "the four flag-carriers" or, briefly, "the relay."

**Connector axis and Operator axis.** She names the constructs directly. Temperament. Behavioral Syndrome. Self-Model. Sociometer. Self-Reference. Self-Construal. Self-Evaluation. Identity. If the agency-communion framing is doing real meeting work, Marge invokes Bakan or Wiggins. Not Lisa.

**Constellation as the class noun for the four.** The class noun is "capacity." She talks about "the four capacities of the Vertebrate clade." She names individual capacities by their proper name.

**Triple as a content noun.** "Triple" stays only as a geometric description of the three-motivation region. She does not say "the triple at this cell is X." She says "the Reactor capacity at this cell is X."

**Substrate as connective tissue.** "Goo" carries the three-kinds-of-stuff content (Boltzmann, phenomenal, Shannon). For other uses where the framework had been loose with "substrate," she uses "architecture," "machinery," "hardware," or "groundwork." She uses "substrate-independent" only in the Conant-Ashby and Levin-Watson sense as cited technical terminology.

**Psychology constructs as primary depth.** No deep coverage of attachment styles, self-construal, social identity, Core Self-Evaluations, Big Five facets, HEXACO, Dark Triad, personality disorders. If those land in a chapter, they land through Marge, Burns, or Bart.

## ELI5 beats and the Marge-momsplain gag

Lisa's Trellis-block framework presentation is the most technical content slot in the meeting. The non-specialist voices in the room ask for translation often. Each per-chapter argument structure below identifies at least one moment where a non-specialist question fits naturally — a field-native term that warrants translation, a tradeoff that warrants spelling out, a construct whose name has done the lifting without explaining itself. The label for this beat is "ELI5 beat" — function-named, since the asker varies.

Most often Jasmine asks. She is the household-voice-of-record and the meeting's most reliable non-specialist. Occasionally Bart asks, in comedic register — a half-distracted "wait, what?" — when his attention has drifted and the room laughs at him catching up. Mira sometimes pipes in but her questions skew more childish than ELI5-shaped.

When Lisa drifts into an abstraction that genuinely benefits from being made explicit, Marge translates. Used sparingly: two or three instances across the twelve chapters, not a recurring tic. The translation should reveal something, not just rescue a sentence.

**Placement.** The momsplain lands during shared-floor moments between Marge and Lisa — most naturally during the Clade block step 3 segment, where they jointly hold the floor on motivations and signatures. The shared-floor framing keeps the translation a collaboration rather than a rescue.

Marge is the right vehicle for the momsplain. Burns checks out rather than asking. Bart asks but does not sit still for explanation. An ELI5 beat triggered by Jasmine or Bart is a quieter version of the same move and works where the dialogue does not need to carry the translation.
