## The arc

Marge tracks four persistent fallacies in personality science across the full book. Each chapter she presents a short fallacy section in the Trellis block of the retrospective. She does not arrive with a theory. She accumulates one across twelve quarters of working in the same room as Bramble.

The arc has three phases.

**Ch 1–4: naming.** One fallacy per chapter, paired to the cybernetic function Bramble is installing that chapter. Marge does not connect them. She is a psychologist noticing four things in her field that bother her, one at a time, as the lab's work gives her vocabulary to name them.

**Ch 5–10: riffing.** Marge senses a pattern she cannot yet articulate. Each chapter she works one angle. Structural rhymes. What each fallacy gets right. Why they persist. She hunts for a fifth and comes up empty. She starts to suspect each fallacy is held by people who are themselves a certain shape, but she does not yet trust the suspicion.

**Ch 11–12: synthesis.** Marge presents the unified theory. The four fallacies are not random. Each denies one of the four cybernetic functions. Each is held by people whose own shape lacks that function. The book's central anti-optimization claim arrives from her.

Marge is a lesbian.

At Ch 9 Marge floats her type hypothesis from the clinical register she works in: if a clinical pattern attaches to a shape, a fallacy may attach the same way. She does not work a specific case in the book — the hypothesis arrives as a clinician's pattern-recognition, not a documented instance. The fallacy arc closes on its own thesis at Ch 11–12. The clinical register itself is documented in the `Inheritance_Lines_symbolic` doc and is not rendered in this book.

## Per chapter

### Ch 9: The tentative type hypothesis

**Bramble.** Humanoid chassis. Effector Commitment installed. Symbolic line opens. Bart starts his shape-shifters this chapter (Bart runs the Reactor across the levels from Bilaterian up to Band-Human).

**Marge's angle.** Draws on the clinical register she works in; floats a tentative type hypothesis bridging fallacy and clinical pattern.

**Her stance.** Two registers in the same beat — clinically confident, hypothetically tentative. The clinical observation is firm: in her practice the patterns that surface when a shape runs unbuffered track the shape's missing function. From that confident observation she floats the tentative hypothesis: maybe the fallacies she has been naming since Ch 1 are attached to shapes the way clinical patterns are attached to the shapes that lack a function.

She does not yet make the claim about anyone in the room. She is talking about her caseload, about colleagues at conferences, about who-publishes-what.

**What she covers.** The type hypothesis itself, drawn from the clinical register she works in rather than from any single worked case. In her practice, the patterns that surface when a shape runs without buffering track that shape's missing function — a clinical echo of the same architecture Burns has just run as a shape-shifter. From that observation she floats the tentative type hypothesis — if a clinical pattern attaches to a shape, maybe a fallacy does too.

**Ch 5 four-mapping, restated.** Before testing the type hypothesis aloud, Marge restates the four Pavlov-fallacy homologies she has been filing since Ch 5. Latent Inhibition / Blank Slate. Overshadowing / Ghost in the Machine. Eligibility Trace / Noble Savage. Blocking / Dealt Hand. Same shape across each pair: a credit-assignment heuristic that aligns with a personality fallacy. The full mapping is now on the table, four homologies named in one beat as a set of rhymes. The room hears the four pairs as a set for the first time. The pairs are rhymes, not a derivation of the Ch 11 fallacy-denies-function synthesis.

**Connection to chapter.** Bart has just put the Pursuer across five levels. Marge has just put the type hypothesis next to it — the Pursuer's missing function read through the clinical register. Two characters circling the same capacity from two angles in the same meeting. Burns is in the room — Pursuer-shaped — hearing his own shape's clinical echo named while Bart runs its shape-shifter. The conversation does not become personal at the sentimental level. The shape is named, the room moves on.

### Ch 10: Testing the type hypothesis

**Bramble.** Regulator Stability installed. Bart's shape-shifters this chapter is the Conservator (Marge) across the levels from Bilaterian up to Settlement-Human.

**Marge's angle.** Tests whether each fallacy is held by people of its own shape.

**Her stance.** Earnest. She is trying to falsify her own hypothesis. The clinical anchor sharpens the test — she has Conservator-shaped clients and clients who hold the Dealt Hand worldview, and she examines both directions. She tries to find a Conservator-shaped client who does not hold the Dealt Hand. She tries to find a client who holds the Dealt Hand but is not Conservator-shaped. She struggles in both directions.

The hypothesis is firming up. She still has not connected it to the four cybernetic functions.

**What she covers.** The testing process: examples she considered and rejected, the moment she realized she could not find a counterexample in either direction. She is honest about the limits: this is one psychologist's clinical pattern-recognition, not a controlled study. But the pattern is robust enough that she trusts it as a working hypothesis.

**The self-examination beat.** Bart is running her own shape — the Conservator — across the levels while Marge tests her own fallacy, the Dealt Hand, against her own shape. She is examining herself in front of the room. The book does not need to underline this; the room does not need to notice. Cheryl can.

**Connection to chapter.** Bart runs her shape across the levels while Marge tests the type hypothesis on her own shape. She has not yet connected this to the four cybernetic functions explicitly. That comes next chapter.

### Ch 11: Unified synthesis

**Bramble.** Modeler Representation installed. Documentary layer online. Bart's shape-shifters is the Planner (Lisa) across the levels from Bilaterian up to City-Human.

**Marge's angle.** Lands the unified synthesis.

**Her stance.** Definitive. She has it.

**What she covers.** The unified synthesis: each fallacy denies one cybernetic function, and each is held by people whose own shape lacks that function.

The Planner lacks Effector and holds the Ghost in the Machine. The Pursuer lacks Regulator and holds the Blank Slate. The Reactor lacks Modeler and holds the Noble Savage. The Conservator lacks Reviser and holds the Dealt Hand.

Each shape, generalizing its own absence, produces the corresponding fallacy. This is type-provincialism: one viable shape mistaking its own coverage for the universal. It is the cognitive register Marge has been naming since Ch 1.

*Exemplar note (Blank Slate).* Thomas Szasz's *The Myth of Mental Illness* (1961) is a clean real-world specimen of the Blank Slate fallacy — the Pursuer's denial of the Regulator. By treating psychiatric conditions as nothing but learned problems-in-living, with no stored, constraining structure beneath behavior, it generalizes the Regulator-absent shape's own coverage to the whole of mind. A useful citation when the Blank Slate is named (Ch 2) and again at the synthesis here.

**The room.** Marge names all four labmates by shape, each paired with the fallacy that denies its missing function, and the cascade completes on her own.

Burns is Pursuer-shaped and holds the Blank Slate, which denies the Regulator. Bart is Reactor-shaped and holds the Noble Savage, which denies the Modeler. Lisa is Planner-shaped and holds the Ghost in the Machine, which denies the Effector. Marge is Conservator-shaped and holds the Dealt Hand, which denies the Reviser. Each shape generalizes its own absence into the matching fallacy, and the clinician places herself last.

The room does the recognizing without the scene turning sentimental. This is a synthesis claim about personality science, not a tour of the room: no round-robin gimmick, and no one performs feelings about being named. The order Marge moves through the four is a drafting choice; that it completes on her own shape is fixed.

**Connection to chapter.** Bart's shape-shifters across Ch 9–11 have been priming the room. Now Marge's fallacy work meets the shape-shifter work, and the framework's central claim resolves: each fallacy is one shape generalizing its own absence. The shape-shifters show the four capacities from the inside; Marge names what each one denies.

---

### Ch 12: Closing

**Bramble.** Reviser Universality installed. Trust. Stories, narrative identity. Bart's shape-shifters is the Pursuer (Burns's shape) across all eight levels.

**Marge's angle.** Closes the arc.

**Her stance.** Quietly definitive. The Conservator stabilizing what she has just earned.

**What she covers.** The closing. The four fallacies are not four different theories about minds; they are four different shapes generalized to architecture. Each is one viable shape treating itself as the universal.

The framework displaces *being right* with *being placed*. The work is not to optimize toward a target shape. The work is to recognize the shape you are and how it composes with the environments you are in.

**Connection to chapter.** Bart's shape-shifter this chapter, the Pursuer (Burns's shape), is the positive case for one shape across the levels. Marge's closing names the negative case: what happens when any shape denies the others. Together the two cases are the framework's central claim.

The book closes on Mira's seventh birthday with the lab gesturing forward rather than claiming, pursuing what lies beyond the framework's current vocabulary without yet naming it. Marge's synthesis has placed the four fallacies, including the one Burns is most invested in. Burns is still Burns. The fallacy is placed. The constellation holds.

## Marge's Venn

Marge developed an idiosyncratic Venn in grad school: a Jungian, MBTI-based mapping she built to organize personality the way she had been trained to see it. She brings it to the pitch. The team tries it briefly as a working framework on the office patio afterward and rejects it as too restrictive — prokaryotes do not have a thinking/feeling preference. The Venn does not return as a structural element in any chapter.

It survives in two other ways.

**As an authorial scaffold.** The Venn organizes the four characters, the four fallacies, and the four Pavlovian heuristics for the writer. It is a backstage instrument — a way to keep the mappings straight while drafting. It does not appear on the page after the pitch interlude.

**As a formal ancestor.** The team's later four-circle Venn for ABHOP keeps the geometric structure of Marge's Venn and replaces its content. Marge's instinct to think in a Venn was structurally right even when her Jungian content was wrong; the shape she reached for in grad school is the shape the framework needs, and only the labels change.

The mapping below is authorial reference, not in-book material. It records how Marge's grad-school Venn lines up with the framework — each cybernetic function taking one MBTI label directly and the four pair-syntheses taking the other four — so the writer can compose the characters, fallacies, and heuristics coherently. The convergence with standard MBTI is asymmetric, but the structural relationship is intact.

### The singles

The four cybernetic functions take MBTI labels, each grounded in one of the Pavlovian credit-assignment heuristics from the Ch 5 round:

- Effector = Intuition, built on eligibility trace (stimulus recency; spontaneity).
- Regulator = Introversion, built on blocking (familiarity; neophobia).
- Modeler = Thinking, built on overshadowing (stimulus potency).
- Reviser = Extroversion, built on latent inhibition (novelty; neophilia).

### The doubles

The four adjacent doubles take the remaining MBTI labels:

- Controller (Effector × Regulator) = Feeling
- Anticipator (Regulator × Modeler) = Judgment
- Adapter (Modeler × Reviser) = Sensation
- Explorer (Reviser × Effector) = Perception

### The dichotomies

The four MBTI dichotomies run between Venn regions:

- Energy-direction (E/I) runs between two singles: Reviser (E) and Regulator (I).
- Information (N/S) runs between a single and a double: Effector (N) and Adapter (S).
- Decisions (T/F) runs between a single and a double: Modeler (T) and Controller (F).
- Lifestyle (J/P) runs between two doubles: Explorer (P) and Anticipator (J).

Two dichotomies cross between regions of the same type; two cross between a single and a double. That is the asymmetry. It is the cost of giving each cybernetic function a direct MBTI label rather than mapping MBTI onto axes-and-pairs symmetrically. The fifteen-region Venn still places a person at a continuous position rather than sorting into one of sixteen types, and the borderline-score sorting is what produces MBTI's weak psychometric reputation.

### The labmates

Each labmate occupies one capacity. Three of their four MBTI letters are forced by the regions inside their triple. The fourth wobbles between two regions inside the triple. The wobble is a context-dependent shift, not a continuum point.

- Marge is Conservator-shaped. Forced: I, N, J. Wobble: T/F. Her Architect side (INTJ) is where she shines. Her Advocate side (INFJ) is more comfortable.
- Lisa is Planner-shaped. Forced: S, T, J. Wobble: E/I. Her Logistician side (ISTJ) is the class nerd. Her Executive side (ESTJ) is more agentic.
- Bart is Reactor-shaped. Forced: N, F, P. Wobble: E/I. His Campaigner side (ENFP) is the class clown. His Mediator side (INFP) is warmer.
- Burns is Pursuer-shaped. Forced: E, T, P. Wobble: S/N. His Entrepreneur side (ESTP) is the one he shows Cheryl. His Debater side (ENTP) shows up around the team.

Bart and Lisa wobble within a single MBTI category — Diplomat and Sentinel. Burns and Marge wobble across categories. The triples that include both Reviser (E) and Regulator (I) as singles produce E/I wobbles within one category. The triples that include only one of those produce wobbles in a different dichotomy and end up crossing categories.
