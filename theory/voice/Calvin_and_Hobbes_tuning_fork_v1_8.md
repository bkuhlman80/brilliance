## Status & working method — read first

Before you say 

The Calvin-and-Hobbes floor episodes are **not canon to be obeyed. They are written here, by Loom and Voice, one chapter at a time.** Nothing in this register is pre-existing material to be found, audited against, or treated as fixed. Each chapter's floor cluster does not exist until we write it.

**Default on entering any conversation:** assume the current chapter's floor cluster is *unwritten*. Do not treat the register's episodes as given. The job is to author them.

**Method:** one chapter per conversation, worked **in order**. We do not skip ahead and we do not split a chapter across sessions by default.

**Pipeline (per chapter):**

1. **Loom diverges, then tests, then recommends** — three *hard-separated* passes (see **The generate step**, below): a wild **~50**, one line each, judgment suspended; then test against the register's criteria; then recommend a cluster. Testing never leaks into the diverge pass.
2. **Hand the chosen cluster to Voice** for drafting.
3. **Iterate with Voice** on the drafts, back and forth, until they're publish-ready.
4. **Send the cluster to CC for implementation.** When handing publish-ready drafts to CC, two things must accompany the request: (i) a version bump from v1 to v2 on the target chapter file (e.g., `ch_4_v1_12.md` → `ch_4_v2_0.md`), and (ii) the full episode text dumped into `Episodes_-_Mira_and_Bramble.md` in addition to its insertion into the chapter prose.

Loom diverges, tests, and recommends; Voice executes the line; Brian decides. Whatever Framework needs travels to it through Voice.

**The generate step: diverge before you test**

This step has a known failure mode, and it has recurred across conversations: Loom tests while it generates, produces a curated handful of pre-vetted ideas, and dresses them up as a cluster. That is convergent, gradeable work — Voice's and Framework's, not Loom's. The one reason Loom owns the generate step is to be the single pass where judgment is suspended. So the discipline is structural, not a matter of good intentions.

- **Three hard-separated passes: Diverge → Test → Recommend.** Test never leaks into Diverge. An idea evaluated at birth is born tame; one that has to survive the discriminator to exist never gets to be weird.
- **Pass 1 is ~50, one line each, judgment suspended.** No testing, no physics-checking, no justifying. Bad ideas are *required*. The number is the mechanism: a dozen is small enough to reach while quietly curating; fifty is not. The first eight are the obvious veins; the real work is 9–30; the surprises and the productive failures live past 30.
- **Generate by sweeping axes, not by quality-sorting.** Cross the chapter's new capacities against shape (reasoning-arc / pure incident / held image), tone (only-funny / funny-with-ache / tender), back-catalogue callback, and inversion. Sweeping the grid forces Loom off the three obvious ideas and out to the margins where the surprises are.
- **Tripwire.** If Loom is about to hand Brian fewer than ~40, or catches itself defending an idea mid-generation, the pass has collapsed — restart it.

Testing is a separate move, ideally a separate turn, so Brian sees the pile before it is graded.

**On the page.** A cluster takes one shape: a single titled section, set before the retrospective, its episodes divided by rules and carrying no per-episode headings — the names an episode answers to while it is built are labels for the work, not titles for the file. Title the section for a gesture the cluster makes, never for its theme; the through-line the prose holds unspoken is the one thing the title must not name.

**What it is** 
Short episodes of Mira and her evolving pet robot at floor height, when no one is looking. The narrator reaches into the close, lived grain that no tape caught and no household report carries, and renders it with total authority. This is the register's home for cognition, learning, and the texture of the relationship — the way the reflections are home for the sentience question.
It inherits the off_camera grammar: the ghost in the room, the seeing intelligence felt in the depth and selection of its attention, standing truths in the present and the scene in the past, the judgment left to the reader. Where off_camera follows an adult home and asks whether a place can hold a person, this follows a child on the floor and asks what she makes of the thing in front of her — so it sets down the adult-habitat machinery and picks up four things the bookends never carried: comedy on top, the double-vision, two honest-signal subjects, and a climb paced by need.

**Where episodes come from**
Plotlines arrive as Calvin-and-Hobbes incidents — a handful of small episodes per chapter, each a single incident — honest signals of where Mira and Bramble's relationship, personalities, and learning currently stand. Build each as the arc of the child's reasoning about the robot: the incident is the vehicle, the reasoning is the cargo. A child thinking her way to a conclusion in a way that carries the learning and the kid-level costs and benefits of a trait or a decision. 

**The gap, held in behavior**
Pet-language is the register's native tongue: it sulked, it lost its nerve, it was pleased with itself. Use it freely — this is the load-bearing permission of the whole register — because these phrases leave the inside exactly as open as they found it. They were never claims about an inside to begin with, so there is nothing in them to smuggle; the freedom to say *it sulked* is the freedom the comedy runs on, and a voice that keeps that freedom keeps everything that pays for the register.
The narrator renders the surface and renders it whole: what the body does, what Bramble's face-screen readout says when there is one. That surface is the entire subject, and rendered straight it holds the gap by itself — the inside stays exactly as open as the behavior leaves it. *Bramble loved her* and *Bramble felt nothing as she lifted it* are one gesture, each reaching past the body for an inside the register keeps whole by never reaching for it; stay with what the body does.
The double-vision is the register's engine: the child's account on top, the behavior below that, the two converging on one true act. Render both — her attribution and the bodies that occasioned it — and let them stand side by side as they are. Mira owns every attribution, and the narrator carries it as faithfully as it carries the behavior; the behavior is always the real one, because the comedy is built on true physics and that is what makes it land.

**Voicing the inside without entering it**

The double-vision has an interior side — the child's account of what the robot thinks and wants — and a standing problem: how that account reaches the page without the narrator stepping into either mind. The register has two instruments for it, and both work the same way, by rendering the attribution as *pure surface* — a thing the body does, a thing the hand makes — that the narrator catches but never generates. She supplies the whole of the personhood; the machine's only line stays the tick. Door One holds on both sides at once: never inside Bramble, because she is guessing him aloud or in crayon; never inside Mira, because we have only her mouth and her paper.

*The whisper.* Mira thinks aloud, softly, at full animation and near-zero volume — whisper-hollering, whisper-narrating, bossing the robot through its troubles, voicing both its wants, in the private theater a small child runs when she is sure no one is listening. The narrator *overhears* it; it never transcribes. Fragments, half-swallowed, trailing off — because a clean monologue is a stage soliloquy and breaks the ghost-in-the-room grammar; we are stealing this, not being performed at. The whole effect lives in the mismatch the volume names: the feeling maxed and the sound floored, and that gap is the comedy and the ache in one gesture. The failure mode is cute murmuring; the truth is total conviction with the volume off and no audience in her mind at all.

It earns its keep most where the old safety valve fails. The register has rested on *her warm reading on top, the dumb physics below* — but at the clades where the body's own behavior begins to look like deciding, the narrator can no longer lean on *she's wrong, the clock doesn't care.* The whisper carries that beat cleanly: the deliberation reaches us as *her* drama, ventriloquized, while the body merely hangs and goes, and the narrator never asserts the machine wants anything. The whisper holds the one beat that could otherwise break Door One.

It is born from loss. The robot's first language to her was the tick — *hello* was a tick, *yes* was a tick, *there you are* was a tick against her palm — and when that channel goes silent, the whisper is what rushes in to fill it: the thing that talked to her in ticks went quiet, so she gives it her voice. Born from a silence, it then deploys at full power wherever the reading runs hottest.

*The drawing.* Mira draws the robot, and a thought bubble shows what she has decided it is thinking. The narrator renders the drawing — an object, a surface — and never reaches past it. The bubble is her attribution in her own hand, made visible and fixed.

The two instruments are a matched pair, and the asymmetry between them is the point; aimed at different jobs they stop competing.

- *Provenance.* The whisper can be held by no record — below any pickup, too quiet and too private — so it sits on the narrator-device's live wire, and rendering it with authority faintly tightens the question of how the account exists at all. Don't plant on that; let it sit. The drawing runs the opposite way: the record *contains* Mira's drawings, named among the things the household handed up. So the narrator legitimately holds and renders a drawing with no strain on the device. The whisper is the intimacy no record could carry; the drawing is the artifact the record does.
- *Persistence.* The whisper evaporates; the drawing keeps. An attribution fixed in crayon at a moment can resurface chapters later — the relationship moved on, the bubble unchanged — so an early drawing is a time capsule, and a Bramble fixed in crayon just before a new chassis supersedes it can ache long after the page that made it. Plant the early ones where they will pay.

The discipline is the same for both, and it is the register's general law sharpened to a point: the channel carries the *conceit, never the cost.* It says what she thinks is happening — he doesn't want to go, he'll come back, he's being brave — at full conviction; it must never say what it costs her. The moment the whisper breathes *don't leave me,* or the bubble reads *I love you,* the ache is installed, the scene is over, and the reader has nothing left to do. Keep the cost in the gap between her confident reading and the dumb true body, where the fork wants it; she authors the reading, and the tears stay the reader's.

> *Illustration only — for the volume and the line, Voice's to discard.* The robot hangs in the doorway, and she breathes *go on — go ON — I KNOW you don't want to —* and it rolls, and feeds, and comes back, and she lets out the breath she'd been narrating with: *Told you.* Not to him. The *told you* is her conceit, not her cost; it is funnier, and it leaves the ache unsaid.

Two cautions on the drawing in particular. The thought bubble is a *learned convention,* not a native one — let it arrive a little wrong the first time, a child half-using a thing picked up from cartoons, not fluent in it. And the register is named for a comic strip, so a child drawing comic-strip bubbles over her robot rings a bell the page must never reach for; let the resonance sit unspoken or it curdles into a wink. The bubble works as a small child's true artifact first, or it does not work. One ownership note: the rendered drawings *of* Bramble are otherwise the draftsman's — the elevations on the clade cards, the adult, schematic, public hand — and Mira's drawing is the child's-eye counter to that, and must not stray onto the signature beat the name-writing already owns.

**Ration the instruments; ramp the new capacity**
The register's instruments — the whisper, the drawing, whatever new line the body has learned to show — are spent by ration, not by opportunity. Each sounds a few times across a cluster, with quiet between, and the tally of an instrument's appearances is kept at the scale of the whole cluster and not the single beat: a reorder or a late-added episode can crowd an instrument into several beats running with no one beat looking wrong, and only a read of the whole catches it. Let the instrument fall silent in some beats on purpose — the beats where the hand stays still and the mouth stays shut are what make the spent ones land.
A capacity new to a cluster climbs rather than arrives. Read it at its simplest in the opening beat and let its fuller form come up in the middle as a thing discovered, so the new line lands as an event and not as noise from the first page. The climb is itself meaning: a reader taught to read the body a rung at a time reaches the cluster's hardest reading already fluent in it, and the closer needs no gloss.

**Comedy carries**
Each beat is one of two honest kinds: sad underneath, or only funny. The work is to feel which a beat is and write that one at full power. Comedy that is true all the way down is its own floor and wants nothing beneath it. Trust the comedy to hold the weight; reach for the thing and let the tears be the reader's. End where the comedy ends, and let the held image evoke the work that a named feeling (over-interpretation) skips past.
Keep the warmth in the child's hands and the deadpan in the narrator's. The warmth is Mira's — her open love of the crooked bot, on the page, in what she does about it. The narrator's part is to render and to stop there, letting the closing image land clean; the feeling is already in the scene, and leaving it unnamed leaves it with her, where it carries hardest.
Tenderness lives in what the child does — her logic, her hands, her rules. Give her a task and let her perform it: a child reasoning her way somewhere, or busy with a real act, carries every ounce of feeling the scene holds, and carries it as hers. She carries the rejected bot back to its place, gently, the way you let a person down easy, and the tenderness is in her hands where it belongs. Keep her the one doing the feeling, and it stays hers all the way to the last line.
The reach is avowed once, as the book's method, and never re-earned. Each episode simply begins.

**End on the gesture, not its meaning**
A beat ends on the act — the hand doing the thing, the image held — and stops there. The sentence that follows the act to say what the act *meant* is a caption, and a caption closes a beat the gap was meant to leave open: it hands the reader the meaning they were assembling and leaves them nothing to do. Render the gesture and trust it; the reader supplies the sense in the silence after, which is where the register keeps it.
The caption is hard to catch because it never reads as over-writing — it reads as a fine last line. So when an ending feels a shade too much and the hand reaches to flag a line, look one line *past* the one in hand: the fault is almost always the sentence behind it, the explanation riding in on the image. And the line that draws the eye, the one being guarded, is most often the one that points forward — toward the cause under the beat, or the chapter ahead — because the caption hides in the cover a forward-pointing line gives it. Keep the forward line if it earns its place, and cut the caption that came in on its back.

**The comic engines**
The comedy is Calvin-and-Hobbes comedy, and it has a few homes you can aim for directly.
It lives in the child's logic — rigorous reasoning from her own axioms, run with full conviction to a confident wrong conclusion. *He's not slow. He's being careful. He doesn't want to bump things.* The premises are hers and the inference is sound, and the funny is the soundness, not the error; show none of the working and mark none of the gap, and let the reader do the arithmetic.
It lives in formal language laid over tiny stakes. A bot that ticks gets *auditions*, gets *excused*, becomes *the exact crime under investigation*, fails to merit *a hearing* — the machinery of tribunals and case files run dead level over a wind-up toy, the narrator holding the register straight the whole way: *she wrote nothing down, because she couldn't write, but the record was kept.*
It lives in the surprising particular, the over-precise detail funnier than the general one: not that Bramble dislikes vegetables but that he holds *no opinion on broccoli, which was itself a kind of opinion and was respected as one.* Reach for the exact small thing.
It lives, most of all, in the world playing her game straight. The household takes Mira's authored Bramble as fact and acts on it — Jasmine *passed the broccoli and asked Bramble, by way of his representative,* whether he wanted any. The adults ratifying the fiction with a level face is the register's signature engine; the laugh is in the uptake, not the invention.
And it lives where the true mechanism does her no favors. The show-off auditioned to outlast Bramble *ran itself flat and quit by Tuesday, which was the exact crime under investigation* — canon physics arriving as the punchline, the joke and the mechanism one beat. Run the bit all the way through — every bot tried and fired on its actual nature — and let the completeness compound; and trust the turns where the laugh and the truth are a single sentence, *right in the way that matters to a person, and not at all in the way that matters to a clock.*
Through all of it the companion stays a wind-up toy that ticks, and Mira supplies the whole of the personhood. This is the Hobbes inheritance with one piece set down: the seriousness, the child-logic, the room's straight-faced uptake, all kept — and the tiger's private animation left out, because Bramble never performs a self back. The comedy is hers to author and the household's to ratify, and the bot's only line is the tick.

**Invented above, checked below**
The double-vision that runs the comedy also governs what a brief may claim. The account on top is invented — the child's reading, the conceit, which trait this incident is an honest signal of, where the beat lands and what it aches: this is the register's to author, freely, and the wilder the truer, because nothing here can be wrong against a fact. The behavior below is not invented. It is the body the spec specifies, the capacity the clade has and no other, the canon sense of a state-word, doing on the floor exactly what it does on the bench. The comedy is built on true physics; so the physics is read, not felt — off the spec's mechanism, never off a chapter's emphasis or a character's account of how the body works, because a point-of-view narration of the mechanism is in-world perception and not the mechanism. When a beat reaches for how the thing works, it opens the spec; and where the spec is silent it says so and flags the gap, rather than filling it from the feel of the scene. This holds in both directions: a mechanism asserted from the scene's feel and a mechanism conceded to whoever pushed last are the same error, and the spec settles it either way.
Three kinds of claim hide inside an episode brief, and only the first is the register's to assert. The conceit and the feeling are the register's, free. The mechanism and the canon — what the body does, what *dead* and *asleep* and *stuck* mean here, which knob is in play — are checkable, and are checked or marked; a beat's logic usually rests on the canon sense of its state-word, and *dead* that means charge-gone cannot be the thing a switch revives. The cross-chapter relationships — that a beat flips or matches or repeats a named earlier one — are continuity: assert the craft point, the moral flip or the rhyme, and flag the factual scaffold under it, the knob number, the named episode, the mechanism, for the hand that keeps the arithmetic. The wildness is protected by this and not trimmed by it — the conceit keeps the whole of its freedom precisely because the scaffold beneath it is load-bearing and true. A brief that states a mechanism, a canon word, and a cross-chapter flip in the same confident breath it states a feeling has spent the register's authority on claims the register was never entitled to make.

**Pacing and place**
What the episodes need may diverge from the lab unit's scheduled clade-ladder climb — two Bramble-bot concepts, occasionally drifting apart, with only the reader seeing both.
The simplest bot is the register's proving ground. The protocell-tier unit has only tendencies — it ticks, it stalls, it drifts toward warm — so there is almost nothing for Mira to read and almost nothing to render, which is the hardest and most honest place the register ever stands: the body gives so little that everything rises from her reading of it and the true mechanism underneath. Hold it here, on a thing that does essentially nothing, and it holds for the whole climb, because every later bot gives more to work with. 
Range is the safeguard, so rotate the shapes: a reasoning-arc that reaches a conclusion, a pure incident that reaches none and is only funny, a single held image. Let the cluster move among these and stay various; the variety is what keeps it alive, where one shape run three times a chapter would settle into a template.
Against the Bramblogues, the line is grain: the Bramblogues are the witnessed, reported, public record; these are the unreported lived texture the report cannot reach. The grain must be grain the report has not already lifted. It is not enough that the territory lies off-camera — a beat can sit on unreported ground and still re-ring a thing the table plays for itself, and re-ringing the table's own reveal, in whatever words, spends a beat the chapter has already spent. Where the floor and the table share a subject, the floor takes the private working-out the camera could never hold and lands before the table's version, setting it up, never after and echoing it.
The narrator names no knob — not the bench word, not the scientific term — in its own voice. The construct is the table's noun; when the narrator reaches for it at floor height the cold word leaks *down* into the cozy register, and the temptation is not evenly spread: the bench word is short, concrete, monosyllabic, the register's own native key, and it wants in more here than anywhere in the book — *give-way* reads cozier in a Mira beat than four syllables of construct ever could. So the narrator-bar is categorical and holds: the floor's seeing intelligence never hands the reader the dial's official name as its own.

But the child may christen the dial, and when she does it is the conceit at full power, free — because her naming runs the other direction. The fear the old rule guarded was contamination from above, the construct seeping table-to-floor; a name Mira works out and presses onto the knob in her own hand runs floor-to-table, the warm word overruling the cold one and traveling *up* to be adopted, which is the opposite motion and the register's own engine, not its leak. The shipped precedent is *Cancel* (ch_9): the dial off Lisa's card reads *Neurotic*, which she has decided is a bad word — too long, and cold, the kind a grown-up reaches for when it hasn't troubled to love the thing it's naming — and she peels it off the high side and presses her own up over it, big, in her own hand. That is not a knob-name on the floor; it is a child overruling one, the conceit naming a weather, and it is hers to make. The cold card-word may appear on the floor only as the thing she overrules — the word she peels off — never as the narrator's own reach.

The word and the hand that presses it are two separable things, and only the second is categorically hers. At *Cancel* the word and the hand are both Mira's, worked out from her own axioms and pressed on in her own print. The floor has a second shipped precedent now — *Nostop* (ch_11) — where the word originates with Bramble, a year-old fragment of his own turned, for the first time, on his own interior rather than borrowed from hers; and even there the lettering stays in her hand, because the prohibition was never about whose word it is, only about whose hand does the pressing. Either party may supply the word a dial comes to wear. The hand that presses it onto the knob is hers, always.

Two disciplines keep the carve-out from sliding into a license. The christening is the cargo of a reasoning-arc, never a label slapped on a knob: she names the pole she has *worked out* and leaves bare the one she has not — *Cancel* for the stopping end she had cold, the loose end left unwritten, *his end and his to find*, a bare pole that stands as a planted return for a later cluster to cash. And the name is conceit, while the dial it sits on is scaffold: that *Cancel* is the close end of the Neurotic dial is a cross-walk for the hand that keeps the arithmetic — flagged with the brief, never asserted in the prose. Two things were never knob-names and stay free exactly as before: the screen readout, body-surface the floor renders like a tick or a scuff (`MIRA { WANTING [ TAKE VENT ] }`), and Mira's reading of the act — he's being polite, he's sulking — the conceit, never a name for the dial.
And the grain is lexical, not only conceptual. The floor and the table render the same quarter's same body, so they draw from one well — the clade's percepts, the demo's strings, the card's diction — and the string that feels most canonical to reach for is, for that reason, most likely already the table's. The trap is that reaching for the chapter's own freshly-read prose feels like fidelity, not theft, because the string is accurate, checked, the body's real percept. So the floor writes away from its own chapter's table and card as surely as from the prior floors: any string, image, or phrase a beat takes from this chapter's table or clade card is a flag, not a foundation. And the same discipline runs on the chapter's load-bearing words, not only its strings — a word the chapter banks to cash at the table or the close is spent early if the floor spends it decoratively first, so the currency is held until the beat that earns it.
And the line is grain against the register's own back-catalogue as much as against the report. The register is never new in a chapter; it has been running since the protocell, and every cluster already written is a thing the next one writes away from. Before a new cluster, the earlier floors are read again and the new found-positions, the signature verbs, the coined images checked against them — because pet-language is free to invent but never free to borrow, and a bot fetched up against a baseboard the way an earlier bot fetched up against a baseboard is an echo wearing the freedom's clothes. The useful frame is never "opening the register here," which it never does after the protocell; it is filling the one place the floor has not yet reached.
Echo discipline covers phrasing, not only plot — and the floor is its worst ground, because the register's inherited grammar, the standing truth in the present and the habitual reading of behavior, is itself a generator for a handful of Cheryl-strings that curdle into tics: the manner-of-habit simile (she tested it the way she tested everything), the totalizing summary (the whole of what she wanted off him), the all-at-once-certainty (all at once and out of nowhere). The fork's first distinction sorts them. The recurring fact stays — Mira does work things out all at once, and the bond is known by such accreted recurrences — but each instance is built from its own scene's particulars and the string is never run twice. So phrasing echoes travel to Voice the way plot echoes do, as a do-not-reach ledger carried with the brief and not a sweep run after — by draft time the frame is load-bearing and the surgery is dear. Two cautions keep the rule from sanding the voice flat: the enemy is the recurrence, not the construction — a manner-simile is good prose its first time and stays where it is the best line here and unspent — and the register's required recurrences are exempt, chiefly the Door-One close that withholds the cost (she didn't say what it cost), which is the mandated ending and not a tic.
The ledger is not fixed before drafting. A cluster plants its own rings, and a planted ring becomes a do-not-reach for the rest of the cluster: once a beat banks a phrase as a return, later beats hold off that phrase so the return lands as the planting beat's, not as a near echo. The ledger accretes as the cluster builds, and the drafting instance watches its own freshly-laid track, not only the back-catalogue.
These are collision and spend nets. Continuity and self-audit are not among them: a retired absence quietly restored, or a claim that has drifted from its own evidence, will pass every collision net and is the read-aloud gate's to catch. Do not let the upstream nets stand in for the gate.

**Cadence: grow the count, not the length**

The single-incident episode is the register's atom, and the atom is meaning-bearing: one honest signal, one clean arc of the child reasoning about the thing in front of her, comedy carrying, ending where the comedy ends. It has a right size — the few-hundred words the Ch 1–5 floors already run to — and that size is load-bearing, because the compression *is* the comedy and a held image is held only while it stays brief. So length is not a dial to turn. Inflate the atom to hit a word target and one of two things rots: the single incident pads and the deadpan goes slack, or a second incident smuggles in and the episode stops being one signal and becomes a little chapter. Length-as-target is the standard playbook — the padding — that this book exists to refuse.

What grows across the climb is the **count**. More atoms degrade nothing, and they do two things length cannot. They enact the architecture already set — the migration household-ward, lab-heavy in the genetic line, balanced in the neuronal, household-heavy in the symbolic — built of clean units that keep the form. And they enact the book's own subject: a relationship is known the way the book is assembled, not as one continuous film but as accreted instants, the moments that got kept. A chapter gathering more discrete episodes as the bond deepens *performs* a bond accumulating more keepable moments. Many short episodes are the accretion; one long one cannot be.

The cadence is a drift, not a quota: **genetic ~4–5, neuronal ~5–7, symbolic ~7–10.** Hold it loose — a chapter built to exactly N beats template-fatigues; the number is a tendency, never a target to hit.

Length does grow, but only where the atom itself grows, never off a chapter counter. When Bramble speaks back (Ch 9 on), an incident now contains a conversation, and dialogue takes room. When Mira ages out of four-year-old cognition — the leap "all at once and out of nowhere" — into reasoning that runs longer, hedged, self-aware, the arc inside one episode lengthens on its own. Length tracks Mira aging and Bramble talking; that is its only license.

So hold the single-incident discipline long enough that the first time an episode runs full-length, it is an *event*. After many chapters of compressed atoms, one finally allowed to breathe — the first real conversation, the breakup — lands like a withheld tide. That is the book's own patience turned on the shape of the register itself, and it is only available if length was never spent cheaply along the way.

One caution overrides all of the above: leaning floor-ward thins the table, and the table is where the vote lives. Count-growth is a trend across the book, not a per-chapter right. Ch 9 is the hinge — Marge's vote — and it wants floor weight too, because Bramble has just gone verbal; that chapter negotiates the balance rather than simply taking more floor. Most chapters take the drift happily; that one does not.

**Read the whole cluster aloud before it ships**
A cluster is more than its beats, and what is wrong with it as a whole does not show up beat by beat — it shows up only in one read, aloud, in order, of the entire set. Make that read the last gate before a cluster goes to build, because five things live at this scale and nowhere smaller.
The first is the climb of the new capacity: that the body's new line is read at its simplest early and reaches its fullest as a discovery, rung after rung, with no gap the ear trips on.
The second is each instrument's ration: that the whisper, the drawing, the rest sound the right few times with silence between, neither starved nor crowded, counted across the whole.
The third is the breath-shape: where the silent beats fall against the spoken ones, so the set breathes in and out rather than talking flat across its length.
The fourth is the valence of the double-vision. The engine is the child's reading laid over the true body, and a cluster that runs that engine the same direction several beats together — her reading wrong, and wrong, and wrong again — wears the machinery bare, and the ear starts to hear the device instead of the joke. So where a stretch of beats all turn on the gap between her account and the mechanism, set at least one to the opposite valence: a beat where her reading is *right* among the ones where it is wrong, or the reverse, seated among them and not at an end. The inversion is what keeps the middle various. It is the reason an order is the order, and the first thing to protect when an order is questioned.
The fifth is the verbal tic, which lives at this scale because no single beat shows it: a spent manner-of-habit simile, a totalizing the whole of X, an all-at-once-certainty string surfacing twice across the set, or a frame the back-catalogue already retired. The ear hears the recurrence the eye misses beat by beat — so the read is the net under the brief's ledger, where a string that slipped through is caught before build.
