## ch_5_v2_1.md

**L18**
> "The head's a boxy little thing... It's got a screen for a face, and the screen shows the one thing it's looking at right now, in a word — CAT, MIRA, COUCH, the vacuum across the room, the plant. Whatever's got its attention, the face says so."

Introduces the robot's attentional display — a readout of whatever currently captures/holds its attention. Engineered analog of selective attention/orienting to salient stimuli.

**L55**
> "...that the point of the barefoot running was a kind of attention where the one paying it and the thing attended to stopped being two — no runner standing off to the side watching herself run and keeping score, just the running, the foot and the ground and nobody in between."

Lisa's flow-state model of attention: collapse of subject/object distinction during fully absorbed, non-self-monitoring attention.

**L248**
> "Overshadowing. Two cues, both there before the food, both honest — a loud tone and a dim light, both reliable. The animal learns the loud one and ignores the light. Not because the light's the worse predictor; it's just as good. The loud one simply got to the credit first and left little behind. The salient cue overshadows the quiet one."

Explicit discussion of attentional capture/salience in associative learning (the overshadowing effect) — salience determines which stimulus gets the attention/learning credit.

## ch_7_v2_1.md

**L28**
> "The knob is called Attention. Two modes: immersive and surveying. Throw it into immersive and the new top line goes dark and it drops straight back to last quarter — bare band, dark asleep, the doing-word read right off the brain, no guessing. Throw it into surveying and the top line stays lit around the clock, sleep and all."

Introduces "Attention" as an explicit named dial in the book's framework: immersive (absorbed/focused attention) vs. surveying (vigilant, broad-scope monitoring).

**L172**
> "Attention is how far down in the minute it lives — immersed, the cold water on the skin and nothing else in the world, or surveying, stood back with the whole field at once and the day going past like weather. That's the dial."

Elaborates the Attention dial as depth-of-absorption vs. breadth-of-monitoring — the focused-attention/vigilance-scanning distinction, paired with a "Remembering Style" construct (Kahneman experiencing/remembering self).

**L304**
> "Attention sits where two side-by-side jobs cross — which is what keeps it a dial and not a drive, a thing the board holds and a hand can turn. Down-in-it, or stood back from it."

Places Attention architecturally as a modulatory dial (state), not a drive (motivational trait).

## ch_8_v2_4.md

**L83**
> "Then the bot... put a person up on the glass. Not its own line, not Mira's — a third name, lettered across its forehead with a guess strung after it: `SITTER { ATTENDING [ WATCH MIRA ] }`."

Robot models and displays another agent's attentional state — theory-of-mind about someone else's gaze/attention.

**L228–230**
> "Three little ruled columns came up, a name lettered at the head of each and under the name a guess: what that person was running, and what they were running it on. `JASMINE { ATTENDING [ WATCH MIRA ] }`... `BURNS { ATTENDING [ WATCH SCREEN ] }`... 'It's reading the room,' Bart said. 'Not the room — the people in it. It's built a little model of each of you as a thing with your own stuff running, and it's posting what it figures the stuff is.'"

Extended passage on the robot's mechanism for modeling multiple humans' attentional states simultaneously — computational theory-of-mind for attention/gaze, with an explicit caveat that the model reports the machine's inference honestly but isn't necessarily accurate ("Honest about the looking. Not gospel about the looked-at").
