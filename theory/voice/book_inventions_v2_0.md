# Book Inventions

*Working document. Named world-commitments made by the voice project that the framework project should see and respect.*

## Purpose

The framework project produces packets with mandatory content. The voice project drafts prose from those packets and, in doing so, invents named details the packet did not specify — a character's ex-partner, a paper that does not exist, the name of a restaurant. Many such inventions are pure texture and need no tracking. Some name a person, place, work, event, or organization that could plausibly recur, and those become world commitments. This file is the running record of them.

This is a sibling artifact to voice_anchors doc. The voice anchors file is the voice project's tuning fork; the inventions log is the cross-project canon for invented named detail.

---

## The criterion

File it if the invention names a person, place, work, event, organization, phrase, or recurring pattern that could plausibly recur in later content.

Skip it if it is one-off texture — a song playing, a beer brand, a piece of decor, a single anecdote that does not return.

When in doubt, file. A line in this log is cheaper than a contradiction in Ch 8.

---

## Propagation

Each packet draft from the voice project hands back with a short "inventions made in this packet" list appended at the end of the draft. That list folds into this file before the next packet is produced. The framework project reads this file before producing the next packet so it can reference, reuse, or correct any commitment made.

---

## Pre-clearance

A narrow class of inventions are routed back to the framework project *before* being committed, not after: anything that could carry theoretical weight if a reader engaged with it seriously. The test is whether the invention could be mistaken for a framework claim.

Examples: naming a research paper that does not exist, naming a methodology, naming a grant program, naming a piece of scientific apparatus, naming an institution, naming a published book by a character. These are proposed in chat before the prose commits to them.

The bar is whether a reader checking the citation or institution would find it real. If the prose implies it should be real and it is not, that is a framework-adjacent commitment, not a flourish.

**Face-screen — consolidated readout system (revised post-Ch-6).** The forehead word-screen grows one display layer per neuronal cell, Ch 5 through Ch 8, in step with the cognitive architecture each cell adds. The system is involuntary: the unit cannot author or suppress what shows. Decisions the voice project owes consistency to:

- **Growth law (the layers).** Ch 5: `OBJECT` — the bare thing the unit's attention has caught. Ch 6: `VERB + OBJECT` — the model-free action output. Ch 7: `FUNCTION [ VERB + OBJECT ]` — the self-model naming the operation it runs over the action layer. Ch 8: `PERSON { FUNCTION [ VERB + OBJECT ] }` columns — up to three agent blocks, the same structure re-run on a modeled other; the unit need not be among them. Higher layer renders higher. The face-screen caps at the Ch 8 `PERSON` columns and does not grow a symbolic layer; full sentences live on Speaking — a separate, voluntary conversation channel, not a face-screen layer — and the two channels are distinct and can diverge. The involuntary readout caps there; from City-Human (Ch 11) the screen gains a voluntary overlay on top of it — Bramble drawing ASCII art on its own face (not free prose), which takes over the whole screen when it chooses to draw and yields to the honest readout when it does not. The honesty gap applies: the drawn channel, like Speaking, is the dishonest-capable one — art up means Bramble is performing, readout up is the truth. The authored face debuts as play at Ch 11 and escalates to performance at Ch 12 (see the Ch 11–12 face-authoring entry below). Owned in `bramble_specs` Part IV.
- **Higher layers model lower layers; the expressive setting drives the layer always-on.** This is the central mechanism — not gating, but modeling. A new display layer is the online computer's *model* of the layer beneath it. On its knob's *suppressive* setting the layer is dormant and the unit falls through to whatever the cell beneath is doing — including that lower cell's on/off. On its *expressive* setting the layer runs always-on, generating display content out of its own model whether or not the lower layer has anything live for it to read. The expressive setting therefore overrides the lower cell's sleep-dark.
- **Per-cell rules.**
  - **Ch 5 — Effector / Credit Style (short-horizon ↔ long-horizon).** OBJECT shows while awake; dark during sleep. No "dreaming" at this cell. Wake/sleep timing runs on the emergent allostatic process installed at Eumetazoa, not on this knob — Credit Style shapes which percept wins the attention race once the unit is awake, and has no bearing on when that is. *Voice consequence:* the Ch 5 screen carries single nouns and goes dark at night.
  - **Ch 6 — Regulator / Coping Style (proactive ↔ reactive).** *Reactive* (suppressive) → falls through to Ch 5: OBJECT during waking (CAT, MIRA, CAMERA), dark during sleep. *Proactive* (expressive) → VERB + OBJECT always, including during sleep (CHASE CAT, NUDGE MIRA, WATCH CAMERA awake; IGNORE WORLD, CHARGE UNIT asleep). The dial is therefore a legible cross-room tell: verb up means forward, bare noun or dark means held-back.
  - **Ch 7 — Modeler / Experiencing Style (immersive ↔ surveying).** *Immersive* (suppressive) → falls through to Ch 6, with a load-bearing extra fact: immersive reads the Ch 6 brain *directly*, so the displayed VERB + OBJECT is never an estimate. *Surveying* (expressive) → FUNCTION [ VERB + OBJECT ] always, including during sleep — REPLAYING [ CHASE MIRA ], CONSOLIDATING [ CLIMB CURTAINS ], PRACTICING [ IGNORE WORLD ], FINISHING [ CHARGE UNIT ]. Surveying cannot tap the Ch 6 brain; it is modeling it, so the VERB + OBJECT under surveying is *always* the self-model's best estimate, never a readout. "Confabulation" is shorthand for this; it is honest, not dishonest — the layer reports its model.
  - **Ch 8 — Reviser / Agreeableness (self-favoring ↔ agreeable).** *Self-favoring* (suppressive) → falls through to Ch 7. *Agreeable* (expressive) → at least one (up to three) PERSON columns of FUNCTION { [ PERSON1 ( VERB + OBJECT ) ] + [ PERSON2 ( VERB + OBJECT ) ] + … } always, including during sleep — e.g., PRACTICING { [ BRAMBLE ( CHASE MIRA ) ] + [ MIRA ( CLIMB CURTAINS ) ] }. Cheney-Seyfarth triadic tracking is in scope; the unit need not appear in its own modeled triad.
- **Three honesty grades.** The screen never lies about its own layer's activity; what it *refers to* gets more fallible up the stack. (1) OBJECT and VERB + OBJECT, when produced by direct read (Ch 5 awake, Ch 6 proactive, Ch 7 immersive-over-proactive), are honest first-order outputs — though the unit can sincerely orient toward a bowl that isn't where it believes it is. (2) FUNCTION (self, the surveying overlay) is honest about which operation the self-model runs; its VERB + OBJECT is always estimated, never read. (3) FUNCTION [ VERB + OBJECT ] inside a PERSON column (other-block, agreeable) is honest about the unit's model of another agent; projective when the target has no first-order mind — a [ VACUUM (…) ] column is the unit performing the intentional stance on a reflex machine.
- **Surveying estimates always; reactive-surveying is the sharpest case.** In proactive-surveying the underlying behavior is agency-shaped, so the estimated VERB + OBJECT is usually close to true. In reactive-surveying the underlying behavior was capture, not agency, and the estimate dresses a reaction as agency — surface output identical to proactive-surveying, but the self-model has invented the action it claims to be naming. This is the illusion of conscious will, rendered on the face.
- **Conjugation rule.** **FUNCTION words are present-progressive (-ing form). VERB words are bare-stem.** No word appears on both layers; the conjugation is what keeps the layers visually distinct on the face. Examples: VERB = CHASE, NUDGE, WATCH, SMELL, CLIMB, IGNORE, CHARGE, FETCH. FUNCTION = PERCEIVING, INFERRING, RECOGNIZING, RECALLING, PREDICTING, SIMULATING, ATTENDING, CHECKING, WEIGHING, LEARNING, REVISING, CHOOSING, EXPLORING. **Consolidation register** (surveying-during-sleep): REPLAYING, CONSOLIDATING, PRACTICING, FINISHING. (This corrects the previous mis-specification under which VERB words like CHASING / NUDGING / WATCHING were on the Ch 6 line; the Ch 6 prose pre-dates this rule and is being swept separately.)
- **The other blocks at Ch 8.** Up to three agent blocks; Bramble need not be one of them. Agent labels appear at Ch 8, the first cell where self-vs-other ambiguity exists; the Ch 7 self block is unlabeled (default self). **Modelable roster:** Mira, Burns, Jasmine, Vacuum, Stranger. The Humanoid joins once it enters the household at Ch 9; roster grows as new agents enter the scene. Over-attribution to non-minds (a Vacuum block) is intended — Bramble performing the intentional stance, the machine's rhyme with the team's worry about anthropomorphizing.
- **Ownership / drift control.** The system (growth law, modeling-not-gating, immersive-reads-directly, surveying-always-estimates, FUNCTION vocabulary, conjugation rule) lives in `bramble_specs`, Part IV.

**Face-screen — the lexicon's training (Ch 5–8).** The words on the screen are not hand-coded. Each is a label trained onto the unit's own learned representation, whose internal form Lisa cannot know in advance, so the readout is grown one slot at a time across the neuronal line rather than written into a table. At runtime it is involuntary — the unit neither chooses nor understands the words, which is what keeps the screen unsuppressable and honest about its own layer (see the consolidated face-screen entry above). Division of labor: Lisa builds and trains the readout (software); Bart builds the physical apparatus that grounds it (the exposure rigs, the teleoperation rig) plus the screen and its lettering; the one slot Bart cannot rig from outside is FUNCTION, which Lisa supervises from inside the architecture. Voice distributes who-says-what; the split is the framework default.

- **Ch 5 — OBJECT, trained first.** The slot with the most outside evidence: a referent you can point at. Lisa exposes the unit to a referent across senses while supplying the label, and trains the readout to emit the label when the matching internal cluster fires. The paradigm case is Mira — her face on the cameras, the smell of her clothes, her voice off a recording, all paired with the typed word, until the screen reads MIRA whenever she is present. The unit forms the Mira-cluster on its own; only the label is supervised. *Prose home:* Marge's Fifth-Quarter vignette, "The name and the thing" — they wrote the name, not the thing it names; the household's *knows her* named and declined.
- **Ch 6 — VERB, tagged and elicited.** Less outside evidence than OBJECT (an action is observable but not point-at-able from across the room). Primary method: real-time tagging — Lisa labels the unit's spontaneous schemas as it performs them. Secondary method: teleoperation — Bart's puppeteering rig drives the body through a rare action on cue so it can be caught and labeled. *Prose home:* Bart's Sixth-Quarter build log (the rig is his; the catching and the words are Lisa's board).
- **Ch 7 — FUNCTION, supervised from inside, the most fallible slot.** No outside evidence at all: a cognitive operation cannot be pointed at or watched, so there is nothing for Bart to expose the unit to. Lisa instruments the architecture to read which operation is actually running and trains the readout against that truth. But the slot displays the unit's self-model of its operation, and the self-model is the least-anchored thing in the unit — nothing in the world ever corrects "recalling" to "inferring," where OBJECT is corrected by the world and VERB by consequences. So the readout is honest about its own layer and answers to nothing outside; it is the most fallible slot. *Prose home:* Marge's Seventh-Quarter vignette, "The word nothing checks." The dramatized confabulation (the reactive-surveying self-model inventing the action it names) is banked for later and is not spent at Ch 7.
- **Ch 8 — the lexicon turned outward.** No new training method. The same three readouts, built for the unit's own states, run on its models of other agents to produce the per-person columns; nothing is added but the per-person labels. *Prose home:* the Eighth-Quarter Bramblogues narration — "taught no new word to do it," the same set turned on its model of the people.

**Forward (the chassis swap, Ch 9 line).** A slot carries to a new body when its ground truth carries, and re-trains when its ground truth is the body itself. OBJECT and FUNCTION are body-independent (the world's referents, the mind's operations) and come over clean. VERB is body-bound (it reads the unit's own motor schemas) and is re-grounded on the new plant by the same tagging-plus-teleoperation method. **Two interpretive lenses (committed; from §2).**

*The two Docs.* Marge and Jasmine are the figures who actually *know* and *tend* — and the framework holds them least well (Jasmine not typed at all; Marge flattened to "Conservator"). The theory is blindest exactly where life is most present. Because Cheryl is a fallible narrator who keeps her distance, she may not fully see this — which lets the reader see it past the narrator.

*Three exiles, three homes.* The worry that Cheryl, Lisa, and Marge are three versions of one "distance-keeper" is dissolved by reframing distance as **exile**. A wall is static (the rut); an exile is dynamic — return is possible, so each woman has an arc. Cheryl is exiled *from intimacy*; Lisa is trapped *beside* a love that isn't a home, having given up her element; Marge is exiled *from her home* (the church) and builds portable ones. The three arcs interlock: Marge and Cheryl may home each other; Lisa leaves to find hers elsewhere. The triptych is bonded to the book's deep-time / inheritance / household spine — what gets passed forward, who finds a habitat they can live in.
