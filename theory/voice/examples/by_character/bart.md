### Braid

**"A prokaryote is a bacterium or an archaeon. A cell without a nucleus. The DNA floats in the cytoplasm. The genome is small. The cell divides fast. The cell can take up DNA from other cells — horizontal gene transfer — which means inheritance is not strictly vertical, and which means a single bacterium's *lineage* is not the simple tree that humans like to draw. A bacterium can swap with a stranger. A bacterium can swap with a virus that has its DNA in it. The lineage is a — I am going to call it a *braid*. It is not a line."**

**"The cell senses. The cell moves. The cell can pick up food. The cell can be food. The cell can pair up with another cell and exchange material across a small bridge — a pilus — and the receiving cell will be different afterward. The cell can secrete molecules that other cells in the neighborhood sense, which is how bacteria sing to each other. They sing about how many of them there are. When the number is high enough, the song changes the singers. This is called quorum sensing. We have known about it for fifty years and most people who are not in the field do not know about it."**

**"That is what a prokaryote is. That is the kind of thing it is."**

"Same engine, except it doesn't run at the same pitch in every animal," Lisa said. Run it low and you get one that hangs back and waits to be sure. Same wiring, same chemical, two settings of the one dial. There's a name for the axis, and it's on the card now. Coping Style."

### The Bramblogues

The clade block had been about the first one. Lisa capped it and turned the room to the second. "Bramble block," she said, and, because Bart already had the card half-turned in his hand and the look of a boy who'd done the reading: "Bart."

He stood. It was still a part he liked.

**"The Bramblogues,"** he said — it was a franchise now, and he said it like one — and Marge made the sound she made, smaller this quarter, a woman who had decided some words you simply outlast. He picked the tetrapod up off the slate; it stopped the instant its feet left the floor, the gait with nothing under it, and turned its head to the camera so Cheryl would get the look.

[…]

**"Last quarter,"** Bart said, **"this exact thing brained that exact camera. Walked its head into the lens, full speed, and the only move we had to answer it with was to teach it to hate the camera — sour it on the sight. Burns did it, right there on the tape: thumb-down, word goes dark, it turns away. That was the whole of what a thumb could reach. The looking."** He set it down a few feet off, facing half away, and it did what it had always done: found the red glow, swung the whole body around to square its head to it, and committed — CHASE CAMERA, head-first, full speed — and as it committed Bart pressed the thumb-down on its head the instant it went, on the going and not on the camera it was going at, and the thing pulled up short of the lens, three feet out, and stood. The screen flickered off CHASE and came back WATCH CAMERA.

**"There,"** Bart said, the single sharp exhale. **"Watch the face. It still wants the thing — still pointed dead at it, still lit up about it, the good-and-worked-up running same as ever. The wanting didn't move. The doing did. Last quarter the thumb reached the looking; this quarter it reaches the act, and the act got cheaper, so it does less of it. CHASE went to WATCH and nobody touched the wanting. And the screen never shows you that part anyway — can't; there's no word up there for it."** He reached over and turned the coping dial to held-back — it had been set forward all afternoon, Mira's doing — and the verb fell off the word: WATCH CAMERA went to CAMERA, the bare noun, the way it had read in spring before it knew a verb at all. The body didn't move; only the screen did. **"Forward, it tells you what it's doing to the thing — chasing it, watching it. Back"** — he left the dial where it sat a beat — **"it drops the doing and just names the thing it's stuck on. Same machine, same want, same lens; the screen quits narrating and goes back to pointing."** He turned it forward, and CAMERA grew its verb again. **"And that's your cheap tell — nobody has to go read the dial, it's right there on its face. Verb up, it's pushy; bare word, it's hanging back. But the part that holds whichever way you turn it: forward or back, doing or just-looking, there's no wanting on it anywhere. The thumb moves the doing, the dial hides the doing, and neither one ever lays a finger on the want or shows it. The face tells you what it's up to, or what it's fixed on, and not once what it's after. That's the truest thing the screen does."** He crouched, pleased with the machine and with himself. **"That's the whole quarter in one move. The thumb reaches the doing now."**

**"And the ones that stick stop being decisions. Train a thing in deep enough and it quits working it out fresh each time — the paper's second nature now, it doesn't think the paper, it just runs the paper. Frees it up to mind other things. Like —"**

Like the thing it did then, which was break off. Mid-room, with the camera right there to want, it turned off the light and started across the floor, slow, unhurried, toward the cold end of the bench where Marge's kettle sat on its ring throwing the one patch of warmth in the warehouse, and laid itself flat a foot from the heat, belly to the gap, and went still.

**"—that,"** Bart said, pointing. **"Nobody sent it. It read its own empty and broke off a thing it wanted to go feed, off its own clock, before any of us clocked it was low. It's had a sense of its own gauge since the legs; what's new is the gauge gets a vote in what it does now. Reads like a gut telling it to eat. It isn't a gut. It's a number about itself it finally gets to act on."** He nodded at the slow amble it had taken to get there. **"And it didn't bolt for it. Ambled — slow, cheap — because it's low, and slow's cheaper than a burst. It picks slow draw or burst off its own state now. No dial. That used to be a knob; it runs it itself."**

Marge looked at the thing laid out at the warm edge of her kettle the way you'd look at a cat that had chosen your lap, and said nothing, and moved the kettle an inch farther off, and that was all she let the tape have.

Then Bart turned on Mira, which was the other half of the bit.

**"Quality control. I made five calls in the log; you watched it all quarter. Yes or no, no speeches. One: it learns a new chore off the thumbs. The paper, off the step, every morning. Did it or didn't it."**

Mira set her juice down. "It brings the paper." A beat. "Even when it's raining."

**"Taking the yes. Two: it drops a habit. Quits knocking stuff over."**

"It quit the shoes."

**"...I said knocking stuff over."**

"It still knocks stuff over."

The room laughed; Bart kept his books. **"It dropped a habit. Taking the yes, and not proud of how."** He moved on. **"Three: it feeds itself off its own empty, nobody sending it."**

Mira looked at the thing lying by the kettle. "It's doing it right now."

**"It's doing it right now. Three for three. Four —"** and this was the one he wanted clean **"— the two of them share the house. Big one, little one. Nobody messing with anybody."**

Mira gave it the seriousness she gave the questions that earned it. "It pushed the vacuum's button."

Bart stopped. **"It what."**

"It bumped into the vacuum and the button went down and the vacuum changed. Once." She picked her juice back up. "It didn't do it again."

"It didn't do it again," Lisa said, fast, because that was the part that mattered to her — a thing the tetrapod had done to the other unit exactly once, that paid it nothing, that it built no habit out of because nothing in there was keeping score on the vacuum at all. "It's not running the vacuum. It bumped a thing, the thing changed, that was the end of it." But the call was the call. "That's your no, Bart."

**"That's my no,"** he said, and didn't pretend it wasn't. **"One in five."** He looked at Mira for the last one, lighter. **"Five. You can tell across the room which way it's set. Pushy, or hangs-back."**

"Pushy walks into stuff faster," Mira said. "Hangs-back waits to see."

Which was the whole of Coping Style read off a five-year-old in nine words, and Bart took it and sat, four for five and pleased about the four. Lisa let it settle a beat. "Bramble block," she said. "Jasmine."

### Down in It

Lisa took the room forward. "Next quarter. No new body — the legs stay; the line won't turn over for a while yet. Bart."

Bart had nothing to build this time and was plainly glad of it. **"Nothing much from me. Maybe a few more words on the face. It's Lisa's slot — her knob, her board. I'm mostly watching."**

"It's the next knob." Lisa didn't draw it; it was a pitch, not a build. "Slot three. Right now the thing takes the world in one way — flat, even, everything weighted the same. The knob lets that vary. Down in a thing — all of it close and loud, the edges gone, nothing else in the room — or stood back from it, the whole field at once, nothing pulling harder than anything else. Immersed, or surveying. We're calling it Experiencing Style, and it installs the next drive up the board — Autonomy, the modeling layer running more under its own steam. That's all you get tonight; the rest is next quarter's."

### Off the Board

[…]

"You needed to get good enough at one thing to be done with the knob for it."

Burns came in low and pleased, eyes half down, the glass going in its slow circle — riding alongside her for once instead of over the top of her. "Same move the floor pulled," he said: the floor he'd named an hour back, the old regulating folded down under the brain and run blind. "Half a billion years of it. Smaller board's all." It came out a shade thick at the edges, the drink sitting in the words, and he didn't hear it, and nobody marked it but the tape.

Marge was drinking the beer now, two-handed still but drinking it, and when she set it down the careful was gone the way it had been gone all night and what came up under it was harder and a beat slow. "And that's the one to watch. The thing it just took down to automatic is the part with nobody home to run it. Your breath. Your heart. The most finished thing an animal does is the thing it does without anyone inside deciding to." She found the rest through the drink and said it anyway. "So when it folds a job down and quits minding it, don't let mastered slide across into minded. A thing it no longer has to attend to has not started attending to itself. It's one more thing running in a house with the lights off." No one took it up, because drunk or sober it was airtight, and because she'd built it, three deep, out of the listener's own lungs.

**"So I don't get the bigger board,"** Bart said into the quiet, working it out loud the way he worked a part in his hands.

"You needed to be done with a knob." Lisa handed him back his own line off the log, which was as near a victory lap as the two of them ran. She set the marker in the tray. "That's how it climbs without growing. Every drive that comes online, something already learned folds down under it and makes the room. The board stays four. The animal keeps going up." Which was the framework, capped. And down at the warm end of the table, Burns had stopped turning his glass.
