- **Substance:** Ashby's Theorems. The Good Regulator theorem (Conant & Ashby 1970) — every good regulator of a system must be a model of it, so a surviving lineage is a regulator whose inherited architecture is a distributed model of its environment. # Section D — Empirical menu

**Deep-time cluster** *(Burns's domain; source: `burns_deep_time` Vertebrate)*
- Tetrapod water-to-land transition ~375 Ma (Devonian); fin-to-limb recorded in Tiktaalik's wrist, first digits; Ellesmere Island. Burns's research sweet spot.
- Vertebrate origins in Cambrian seas ~520 Ma — Chengjiang's *Haikouichthys*, *Myllokunmingia*. Jaws by ~430 Ma (Silurian gnathostome transition).
- Lineage sequence: jawless fish → jawed fish → lobe-finned fish → tetrapods.
- Late Ordovician glaciation ~445 Ma — the first major mass extinction, bracketing the early vertebrate window.
- Late Devonian extinctions: Kellwasser (~372 Ma) and Hangenberg (~359 Ma), both significant, against the tetrapod move.
- Devonian climate hot and humid; oxygen rising; CO₂ drawn down as land plants and forests spread.
- Environments: Devonian shallow seas, freshwater swamps, tidal-floodplain margins; estuarine and floodplain habitats central to the land transition.
- Biotic context: vascular plants by mid-Silurian; forests by late Devonian (Gilboa fossil forest, NY); arthropods and early insects on land.
- Evidence sites: Chengjiang; Ellesmere Island (Tiktaalik); East Greenland (*Ichthyostega*, *Acanthostega*); Old Red Sandstone; Gilboa.
- Signature signal: vertebral column with cranium; jaws; bony skeletons; the fin-to-limb wrist; first digits.

**Behavior cluster** *(source: `clade_reference` Vertebrate)*
- Proactive vs reactive coping: proactive copers hold territory aggressively, habituate fast, SNS-dominant; reactive copers freeze, stay hypervigilant, HPA-dominant. Replicated across fish, rodents, pigs, primates (Koolhaas et al. 1999, HIGH).
- Dopamine coupling: proactive copers show stronger dopaminergic responses to reward cues — the neurochemical link from Coping Style to the Reinforcing breakthrough (Koolhaas et al. 2007, HIGH).
- Behavioral-syndrome correlations average r ≈ 0.20 after publication-bias correction; mean repeatability R = 0.37 — weak but selectable (Bell et al. 2009; Royauté et al. 2018, HIGH).
- Pace-of-life: predicts bold/exploratory individuals run hotter metabolisms and develop faster, but individual-level behavior–physiology correlations are largely unsupported (Royauté et al. 2018, HIGH). *Lighter-touch flag — contested basis, per Development Style.*
- Trial-and-error skill acquisition and action-outcome learning: see the goldfish maze (Procedural Memory) and the fish lever-press (Assign Temporal Credit) below.

**Breakthrough material cluster** *(source: `clade_reference` + new ‡)*
- Temporal credit assignment, mechanism: a fish presses a lever, food arrives seconds later, and the basal ganglia assigns credit backward in time via dopamine — neurons fire above baseline when reward exceeds expectation, below when it falls short, and the difference reweights the action-outcome link (Schultz et al. 1997, HIGH).
- Conservation: the basal-ganglia architecture runs from lampreys to humans (Stephenson-Jones et al. 2011, HIGH).
- **Actor-critic mapping (NEW ‡):** the basal-ganglia model in which dopamine carries the critic's temporal-difference error and dopamine-dependent striatal plasticity is the actor (Joel, Niv & Ruppin 2002 ‡). This is the substrate anchor under "actor-critic RL."
- **Vertebrate selection device (NEW ‡):** the basal ganglia framed as the vertebrate solution to the action-selection problem — a centralized switch resolving competition for motor and cognitive resources, present across all vertebrates (Redgrave, Prescott & Gurney 1999 ‡).
- *Epistemic note for Voice and for the card:* this is an architecture-level claim. Short-latency associative learning already exists at Bilaterian (Aplysia, Cell 1). What is new at Vertebrate is the basal-ganglia architecture for assigning credit across delay — not the bare ability to learn from outcomes.

**Capacities cluster** *(source: `clade_reference` Vertebrate)*
- **Assign Temporal Credit** (Reactor): the fish-lever/basal-ganglia/dopamine case above (Schultz et al. 1997; Stephenson-Jones et al. 2011; + Joel 2002 ‡, Redgrave 1999 ‡).
- **Gut Sense** (Planner): the enteric nervous system — ~500 million neurons lining the gut, more than the spinal cord, signaling up the vagus to shape mood, energy, and decision-relevant states the cortex doesn't see directly (Furness 2012; Mayer 2011, HIGH).
- **Procedural Memory** (Conservator): a goldfish learns a maze until the turns run automatic; the basal ganglia and cerebellum hold the program, conserved sharks-to-humans (Squire & Knowlton 1995; Stephenson-Jones et al. 2011, HIGH). Ch 6 gloss: "second nature."
- **Approach** (Pursuer, CARRYOVER): the VTA→nucleus accumbens dopamine circuit that turns "I see it" into "I want it"; guppy boldness studies show high- and low-approach individuals in one school (Wise 2004; Salamone & Correa 2012, HIGH). Bilaterian honeybee-scout catecholamine state → Vertebrate dedicated mesolimbic pathway.
