# Bart's Shape-Shifters: Chapters 9–12

*Tuning fork and content reference for Bart's shape-shifter segment, Ch 9–12. It governs how each rung should sound and which exemplar sits on it. The per-level pitches are reference register and specificity targets, not transcription-ready prose.*

---

## Where these sit

Bart runs a new recurring segment in the symbolic-line meetings (Ch 9–12). Each chapter he takes one capacity and runs it across the levels from Bilaterian up through that chapter's level. The shape-shifters live at Trellis-block step 1 of the meeting (see `Book_Frame.md` for the full retrospective structure): the Trellis block opens with Bart's shape-shifter illustration in the symbolic-line chapters; in Ch 1–8 the Trellis block skips this step.

Bart proposed the shape-shifter format — his own thing, made in the spare cycles of Q1 Y3, with the humanoid delivered and nothing new on his bench to build. He likes the Simpsons cartoons; he likes the goofiness of overlaying cartoon faces on real examples; he likes that the joke compounds across eight levels. The format is his bit. The team consented.

The slides are visual aids the reader doesn't see directly, but the book describes them. Each level's slide is a cartoon image of the example (animal, band, settlement, etc.) with the relevant Simpsons cartoon character's face on it. At the primate level the slide is just the cartoon character, no animal. The lab cast's real-person faces never appear on slides. The joke lives in the abstraction.

## Naming convention in the room

Bart uses "the Reactor," "the Conservator," "the Planner," "the Pursuer" most of the time. He uses the cartoon character names when describing the slides. He does not use the labmate names when running their shapes — the room does the math, and Bart never makes it personal. The cartoon is the cover: because he is talking about a cartoon, each labmate can watch their own shape run across the levels and let the recognition in sideways, without having to take it personally. What each one does with the catching is the shape's own answer, and the answers differ — that difference is the point, not a uniform shrug. And the recognition frees no one from the shape: naming it is one more turn of the thing named. The cast could not see their own shapes for eight chapters; the symbolic line is where the cover lets a little of it in.

At Ch 9 Bart opens the run with his own shape (the Reactor). The cartoon Bart Simpson face appears on every slide — that's the slide convention for the Reactor shape, established by which cartoon character occupies which shape on the show, and it happens to share his own name, the same clean match Marge and Lisa get. He doesn't make a meal of running his own shape; he gets it out of the way first and moves on. At Ch 12 he closes the segment on his father's shape (the Pursuer), cartoon Mr. Burns on every slide — the same name-match, one generation up.

## How the rungs should sound

These pitches are reference, not transcript. They set the register and the specificity each rung has to hit; they are not lines to lift. When the chapters draft, the rung does what the pitch does — same body, same discrimination, same dryness — in fresh words.

Bart's register here is performative, fast, examples-first. He throws the animal up, names the move, and is off the rung before it turns into a lecture. He never narrates the framework; the slide carries it and he points.

Five disciplines govern every rung, in every run.

**Every rung is a body.** The gag is recognition — *look, the same creature* — and nobody recognizes a definition. The animal rungs already obey this; the human rungs are where it slips into glossary ("a merchant quarter with no civic identity" is a definition, not a creature). The cure is synecdoche, not literalization: don't shrink an empire to one man, find the one frontier commander good at exactly one thing and let him stand for the imperial move, the way Old Yeller stands for the whole mammalian Conservator. The reader should still be looking at something alive at the top rung, the way they were at the bottom.

**Every rung passes the discrimination test.** Ask of each example: could this be a different shape? "A careful clever bird that reads the situation" could be three shapes; it fails. The Lisa-cartoon rung — argues, persuades, never throws the punch, hands the physical off — could only be the Planner; it passes. Aim every rung at that specificity, and watch the confusable pairs: the Pursuer and the Reactor both act first and both improvise, and the only thing telling them apart is that the Pursuer reads the other mind and the Reactor doesn't — so any rung in that territory has to make the social-modeling knob visible, not just the action. The distinguishing knob lives in the example itself, never only in the frame that follows it.

**Show the hole; don't name it.** Each shape is an absence — one slot empty — and the rungs that land let the reader *feel* the empty slot instead of hearing it labeled. The granary everyone depends on and nobody loves shows the missing Affiliation without anyone saying "Affiliation slot empty." Let the absence ache; cut the label. (Where the pitches below still name a slot, that is the spec talking to you, not the line talking to the room. Strip the naming when you draft.)

**The shape is an adult, not a life stage.** Each capacity is a stable, lifelong adult type — that is the framework's whole claim, and the primate punchline depends on it. Mr. Burns at the end of a long life with no rulebook banked is the joke only if the Pursuer was never a thing you grow out of. So the exemplars are grown animals living the shape for good, not juveniles who'll settle down by spring. Where a rung reaches for a young animal to explain a not-yet-settled regulator, reach instead for an adult in whom the slot is simply, permanently empty.

**Each run shows its edge once.** The four runs are the positive case; Marge's Ch 11–12 fallacy/disorder synthesis is the negative case — each shape, over-committed and denying the others, curdles into a disorder. For that synthesis to land, the bestiary has to have shown each shape's edge at least glancingly, so the disorder version arrives recognized instead of cold. The cost is shown dry, at animal or institutional scale, and never pointed at the labmate in the room: the form-holding village that outlives its world is the Conservator's cost, and Marge is never the example of it.

## Ch 9: The Reactor (Bart)

**Setup.** "First. Get mine out of the way. The Reactor. Action-first, high skill, runs itself, doesn't model what's going on inside other people. Cartoon Bart Simpson on every slide — show's convention for this shape, same name I go by, which is the whole reason I go by it. Five levels, worm to band. Go."

**Per level:**

**Bilaterian (low Affiliation).** *Slide: cartoon flatworm with Bart Simpson cartoon face on it.* "Eligibility trace. Credit goes to whatever cue just fired. The Reactor's whole deal at the smallest scale: action follows the recent, no model kept around. Short window. Now is enough."

**Vertebrate (low Affiliation).** *Slide: cartoon barracuda with Bart Simpson face.* "Pike. Barracuda. Fast-twitch ambush predator. Hunts brilliantly with zero representational depth — doesn't need the model, action's the whole thing. The Reactor at vertebrate scale: the apex hunter that never once wonders what the prey is thinking."

**Mammal (low Affiliation).** *Slide: cartoon snow leopard with Bart Simpson face.* "Snow leopard. Wolverine. Solitary, disciplined hunter, no pack bonds. The mammal-tier model is rich enough to plan with, it's just not modeling anybody else. The Reactor at mammal scale: the apex hunter alone in its territory."

**Primate (low Affiliation).** *Slide: Bart Simpson the cartoon character, full frame.* "Bart. The cartoon. Same name, yeah — that's the point, not the joke. Acts first, pulls the prank, never once runs the model of how the other guy's going to feel about it. Affiliation slot empty — not because the Reactor's mean, just because the model-of-other-people machinery isn't running. Same name as mine. That's the convention."

**Band-Human (low Affiliation).** *Slide: cartoon pirate band with Bart Simpson face on the captain.* "An extractive raiding band. Mercenaries. Pirates with no fraternity. The crew coordinates, trades, innovates — but there's no internal we. The Reactor at band scale: the predatory crew without the bond."

**Closing.** "Reactor across five levels. Worm to band. Same move every time — action first, models other people as functions, bonds don't slow it down. That's the shape. That's me. Next."

Narrator dip: Bart opened the run on his own shape and got off it fast. The Reactor doesn't do sentimental about itself — the shape doesn't model itself from outside — so he ran it the way he runs everyone else's: punchy slides, fast pace, joke about the cartoon, on. Burns is amused. Lisa is taking notes. Marge has her pen down already.

## Ch 10: The Conservator (Marge)

**Setup.** "Second shape. The Conservator. Stable, skilled, reads people deep, doesn't bother revising. Marge said go ahead. Same slide rules."

**Per level:**

**Bilaterian.** *Slide: cartoon flatworm with Marge cartoon face.* "Blocking. Once the worm's learned what predicts what, it won't let new cues overwrite the old association. Efficiency through stability. The Conservator's whole deal at the smallest scale — protect what's already been learned."

**Vertebrate (low Excitability).** *Slide: cartoon old draft horse with Marge face.* "Old draft horse. Steady, skilled, tuned to the handler, unflappable — doesn't startle at anything. Low excitability holds the vertebrate-tier model in place. The Conservator at vertebrate scale: the workhorse that knows the route and runs it without drama."

**Mammal (low Excitability).** *Slide: cartoon faithful family dog with Marge face.* "Old Yeller. Lassie. The faithful family dog. Deep bond, real skills, and novelty doesn't move the dial. Mammal-tier modeling with no revision drive. The Conservator at mammal scale: the bonded, competent companion."

**Primate (low Autonomy).** *Slide: Marge Simpson the cartoon character, full frame.* "Marge. The cartoon. She's a cop for an episode, a real-estate agent for an episode, whatever the week calls for. Then the format absorbs it. The hair stays the same. The voice stays the same. The values stay the same. The Conservator takes a run at revising and it never sticks — one episode and the form snaps back."

**Band-Human (low Autonomy).** *Slide: cartoon hunter-gatherer band with Marge face on a community member.* "A foraging band loaded with cooperation, skill, and bonds, and the practices don't change generation to generation. The Sentinelese in their stable phase, if you want a real one. High Affiliation, high Mastery, high Impetus, low Autonomy. The Conservator at band scale: the deep-craft community that just stays itself."

**Settlement-Human (low Autonomy).** *Slide: cartoon walled hill-town with Marge face on a village elder.* "A village that hands the tools, the calendar, the norms down intact, generation after generation — a marvel of it, right up until the river shifts its course or the trade road moves, and the same holding that kept the place whole for three centuries is the thing it can't put down. Doesn't update. Can't. That's the edge of the shape: the form outlasts the world it fit. The Conservator at settlement scale — the held form, and the price of holding it."

**Closing.** "Conservator across six levels. Worm to settlement. Same move every time — hold what works, don't let new stuff overwrite the old reliable, trust the form. Marge is the form. Marge is fine."

Narrator dip: Marge takes being described the way the Conservator would, and the not-minding is itself the shape. The brush is small and sits in one place — the settlement rung shows the Conservator's cost (the form that outlasts the world it fit), and Marge, who is building her own synthesis around exactly that cost, registers it for a beat: the one who types, typed, the cost landing a little nearer than its village scale. Then the Conservator settles — stable in the position, no need to revise, the "oh, you" warmth closing over it. The room reads comfort; the narrator clocks the brush. Naming the shape doesn't move her off it; the not-revising is the shape demonstrating itself.

## Ch 11: The Planner (Lisa)

**Setup.** "Third shape. The Planner. Has stored rules, rich models, keeps revising, lets someone else do the physical. Lisa asked me to do hers third. She wants the cities entry on the record."

**Per level:**

**Bilaterian.** *Slide: cartoon flatworm with Lisa cartoon face.* "Overshadowing. Strongest cue takes all the credit. The Planner's whole deal at the smallest scale: attention piles onto the dominant signal."

**Vertebrate (low Impetus).** *Slide: cartoon scrub jay with Lisa face.* "Scrub jay. Hides its food, and if another bird watched it hide the stash, it comes back later and moves it — because it ran the model of the thief. Plans against a mind it can't see. Wins the whole contest in its head and never risks a feather. The Planner at vertebrate scale: foresight does the work, the body stays out of it."

**Mammal (low Impetus).** *Slide: cartoon elephant matriarch with Lisa face.* "Old elephant matriarch. Carries the whole herd's map in her head — every water hole, every drought year, every place a lion waits — and the herd goes where she decides. She doesn't charge anything; she routes around it. Mammal-tier social modeling running the show through knowledge, not force. The Planner at mammal scale: the one who knows the way and moves everyone by knowing it."

**Primate (low Impetus).** *Slide: Lisa Simpson the cartoon character, full frame.* "Lisa. The cartoon. Writes a letter to the President. Plays the sax. Argues, persuades, never throws the punch. Direct action gets handed off to somebody else. The Planner runs its power through symbols and reasons — one inheritance line up from where Impetus lives."

**Band-Human (low Commitment).** *Slide: cartoon Pythagorean-style scholars with Lisa face among them.* "A scholars' commune. A Pythagorean band. An intellectual collective held together by the shared work, not by the bond — so deep in the question that the ties are incidental, the kind of band that would scatter the day the question's answered. The Planner at band scale: the inquiry-collective that's all model and barely a band."

**Settlement-Human (low Commitment).** *Slide: cartoon monastery with Lisa face on a monk.* "Monastery. Research station. Academic colony. Retreat center. Settlements built around bonds, stability, and inquiry instead of trade. The Planner at settlement scale: the place that thinks for a living."

**City-Human (low Commitment).** *Slide: cartoon imperial Chinese city with Lisa face on a scholar-official.* "Imperial China at its most inward-facing. Late Ming. A city built around governance, codes, and intellectual autonomy more than commerce. Lisa wanted her caveat read as written, so: confidence here is medium. China's relationship to trade is more complicated than this one entry suggests, and it needs more refining. But the Planner at city scale is what's on the card."

**Closing.** "Planner across seven levels. Worm to imperial city. Same move every time — concentrate on what matters, run it through the model, let the executive arm be someone else's problem. Lisa's the model. Lisa is also fine."

Narrator dip: Lisa meets the model of herself the way the Planner would — by managing it. The cities-caveat-on-the-record is the bristle: she'll let her shape be spoken in the room, but hedged, corrected, on her terms, the frame kept in her hands. Then it lands anyway — the scrub jay that plans against a mind it can't see is *her* — and a tell gives the soften (the palm-rotation that stalls) before she recovers into the analysis and closes over it fast. The catch is the irony: the managing is itself the Planner shape running, so even her control of the model demonstrates the model. Naming it doesn't get her out from under it. Bart doesn't second-guess her on the architecture; she's the Planner — and so is the way she takes being made one.

## Ch 12: The Pursuer (Burns)

**Setup.** "Last one. The big one — full stack, all eight. The Pursuer. Goal-driven, reads social stuff sharp, always revising, doesn't bother with stored rules. This one's Burns's shape. Cartoon Mr. Burns on every slide — show's convention, and the name lines up the same way mine does. Worm to empire. Here we go."

**Per level:**

**Bilaterian (low Mastery).** *Slide: cartoon flatworm with Mr. Burns cartoon face on it.* "Latent inhibition. Worm's brain throws out boring stuff. If you've seen it before, the worm doesn't care. The Pursuer's whole deal — chasing whatever's new, ignoring whatever's known. That's the move in a flatworm. Same move all the way up."

**Vertebrate (low Mastery).** *Slide: cartoon kea with Mr. Burns face.* "Kea. Grown alpine parrot, and it never stops — pulls the wipers off a car, solves the latch, works a crowd of tourists, and banks none of it as a rulebook. Not a teenager who'll calm down; an adult that runs on improvisation for life. The Pursuer at vertebrate scale — the bright social animal that treats every day as new because it keeps nothing."

**Mammal (low Mastery).** *Slide: cartoon raccoon with Mr. Burns face.* "Adult raccoon. Coyote working a suburb. Grown generalists that crack every new latch, read the neighborhood, take what's there and move on — improvising a living instead of running an inherited one. Mammal-tier modeling pointed at whatever's new. The Pursuer at mammal scale — the social opportunist that solves it fresh every time and stores no playbook."

**Primate (low Mastery).** *Slide: Mr. Burns the cartoon character, full frame.* "Burns. The cartoon. Ancient — older than anyone — and still after the next scheme, still reading people sharp, still never once banking what he already knows. A whole long life and no rulebook at the end of it. That's the Pursuer: chase what's new, ignore what's known, for good. Same name as the labmate whose shape this is. That's the convention."

**Band-Human (low Mastery).** *Slide: cartoon breakaway band with Mr. Burns face on its instigator.* "A breakaway band — splits off easy, raids on the fly, builds no craft to hand down. The hippie-commune type. A scene, not a tradition. The Pursuer at band scale: the band that does it for the doing, not for the legacy."

**Settlement-Human (low Stability).** *Slide: cartoon gold-rush camp with Mr. Burns face on a prospector.* "A gold-rush camp. Throws up a main street in a season, works the strike, and the day the gold thins it's already packing for the next creek. Builds nothing it means to keep — no granary, no church that'll outlast the vein. The Pursuer at settlement scale: the camp that's only ever a staging post for the next strike."

**City-Human (low Stability).** *Slide: cartoon boomtown with Mr. Burns face.* "A boom city that tears itself down every decade chasing the next industry — oil, then aerospace, then whatever's next — never lets an institution set, never banks a charter it won't rip up. Always reinventing, never consolidating. The Pursuer at city scale: the city that's all next and no keep."

**Empire-Human (low Stability).** *Slide: cartoon conqueror's empire with Mr. Burns face on the conqueror.* "The conqueror's empire that expands faster than it can hold — takes the next territory before the last one's even administered, dazzling on the advance, and the moment the conquering stops there's nothing underneath to keep it standing. No stable spine was ever laid. It outruns its own reckoning right up until the reckoning arrives. The Pursuer at empire scale: all advance, and it falls the day it stops."

**Closing.**

"Pursuer across eight levels. Worm to empire. Same move every time — goals first, read the room, ignore what's known, bank no rulebook, never stop. Worm, kea, raccoon, the cartoon, the breakaway band, the gold camp, the boom city, the conqueror who couldn't quit. Not a phase. The whole life. That's the Pursuer — and that's all four. Done."

Bart hits the lights back on. The slides go away. The meeting moves to Marge.

Narrator beat: the room while Bart runs his father's shape — and it lands differently as the last slide of the last run, the place the Burns/Bart well runs closest to the surface. Burns is watching his son explain him: eight Mr. Burns cartoons, worm to empire, the conqueror who couldn't quit for the finale. He is amused, not embarrassed — he likes the architecture and is willing to be the example, gladly. What Bart does or doesn't let show running his father's shape at exactly this altitude is the chapter's to find. Lisa is taking notes. Marge has put her pen down. Jasmine is watching too. ## Structural payoff

Across the four shape-shifters, the framework's claim is demonstrated rather than asserted. Bart runs four shapes across the levels. The shapes are the labmates. The labmates are in the room. The room comes to know the shapes by watching, and by Ch 11 Marge's synthesis is ready to land because the shapes are recognized.

Bart's Ch 12 shape-shifter and Marge's Ch 12 closing synthesis are the book's two closing structural moves. Bart gives the positive case (one shape across the levels, four times — opening on his own shape at Ch 9 and closing on his father's, the Pursuer, across the full eight at Ch 12). Marge gives the negative case (what happens when one shape denies the others, the fallacy/disorder synthesis). Both arrive in the same meeting. The framework's central claim is complete.

The shape-shifters were Bart's pitch. He brought the format to a team meeting and the team consented because it worked: pedagogical, comedic, four runs, eight levels at the end. Marge developed her fallacy theory in parallel across the same chapters. By Ch 12 the two halves meet in one room.

## Resolved decisions

**The Lisa cities-entry caveat is kept** as a Planner-honesty teaching moment. Lisa requests the cities entry go on the record despite its uncertainty. The beat shows the Planner being honest about model weak points and gives Bart's shape-shifters room for confidence-level marking.

**Capacity-name vocabulary is established earlier** via the Cybernetic Functions Venn on the back of every clade card from Ch 4 onward (see "Card-back Venn" above and `chapter_plan_genetic` doc). Bart can open the Ch 9 shape-shifter with the four capacity names cold without overloading the reader.

**Ch 12 is the eight-level closing portrait** of the Pursuer — Burns's shape, run by Bart across the full stack, worm to empire. It lands as the segment's closing run, where the Burns/Bart well runs closest to the surface; the weight it carries there is the chapter's to find. Bart's own shape, the Reactor, is the Ch 9 opener — by design the run that doesn't crest, since the Reactor doesn't model itself from outside.

**Each shape-shifter runs the shape that foregrounds its chapter's introduced function.** Band introduces the Effector, settlement the Regulator, city the Modeler, empire the Reviser (see `trellis_framework`); each chapter's run is the capacity that leads with that function. So the Reactor (action-forward) opens at Band/Effector, the Conservator (stability) holds Settlement/Regulator, the Planner (model-rich) takes City/Modeler, and the Pursuer (always revising, the hot-bet shape for the doubled-heat line-completing rung) closes at Empire/Reviser.

**The Planner band rung shows its thinness; it does not name it.** The Planner runs low on Commitment, which is the band level's line-defining motivation, so a Planner-band barely coheres — a scholars' commune so deep in the work that the bonds are incidental, the kind that would scatter the day the question is answered. That strain is felt as an absence, with no caveat language on the rung. The cities caveat stays the run's single named hedge, because it teaches empirical confidence-marking — a different move from the band rung's structural strain, and conflating them dilutes it.
