# Voice Project — Orientation

The standing reference: the book, the cast, the calendar, the world and setting, the shape of figuring-it-out, the glossary, and how the project's documents fit together. Read it before drafting; refer back as needed. Its process companion, the How We Draft doc, owns the workflow — reading a packet, how a chapter is built, division of ownership, preventing drift, and how to prep. The voice files (voice_anchors and its satellites) and the other tuning forks (marge_vignette, bart_build_log, burns_motivationals, off_camera) own the per-section craft.

---

## The book at a glance

The book is autofiction. Twelve quarterly retrospectives across three years — spring 2031 through winter 2033–34 — at an ad-hoc research lab in Salt Lake City. The lab was assembled when Burns saw a contest run by Cheryl, a Stewart Brand–type seeder. Cheryl gave the team twelve evolutionary snapshots and challenged them to build a robot — *Bramble* — that represents each. The team produces the theoretical framework themselves as a side effect: Bramble cannot be built without a theory of how its personality is generated.

Each chapter is one quarterly retrospective. The team unveils a new version of Bramble — one new knob installed, one new layer in the cognitive stack, sometimes a new chassis. The retrospective walks through what the new version is, what evolutionary clade it represents, and what it implies for the framework. Mira, age four at the start, watches and participates.

The twelve cells run in three lines of inheritance, four chapters each: the **genetic** line (Ch 1–4: Protocell, Prokaryote, Eukaryote, Eumetazoa), the **neuronal** line (Ch 5–8), and the **symbolic** line (Ch 9–12). Current work is the genetic line.

Cheryl tells the book, looking back across the three years, in past tense throughout. She narrates in third person except where she is a character in the scene — which puts a handful of pieces in the first person; the full POV map and the rule that governs it are under *How a chapter is built*.

## Setting and time

Voice controls the calendar, not framework. 

Salt Lake City. Three calendar years: spring 2031 through winter 2033–34. The household — Burns, his wife Jasmine, their daughter Mira — lives in the city. The lab is an open warehouse in the Granary district; its public name is the Trellis Lab, and the team calls the facility *Smithers* ("I gotta get back to Smithers"). The four labmates are colleagues at Westminster University — Burns in paleontology, Lisa in computer science, Marge in psychology — who had worked together over the years without sharing a lab space. The team is ad-hoc, assembled when Burns saw Cheryl's contest. Meetings happen quarterly, in person, at Smithers.

Quarters map to seasons: Q1 spring, Q2 summer, Q3 fall, Q4 winter. The founding — the morning the first five units went to the Burns house — was **February 12, 2031, Mira's fourth birthday.** Each Q4 retrospective falls on a later **February 12**; at Ch 4 and Ch 8 it physically reveals the next line's chassis *body* as Mira's birthday present — the one finished artifact shown at a meeting; the functional version running on that body arrives at the next quarter's week-1 swap, with the vacuum staying on its rounds. The two chassis-reveal retrospectives land on her birthday: **Ch 4 (Feb 12, 2032, Mira turns five)** and **Ch 8 (Feb 12, 2033, six)**. **Ch 12 (Feb 12, 2034, seven)** is terminal and carries no chassis reveal.

**Calendar.** Each chapter occupies one calendar-season quarter. Within the quarter, four dated events anchor the chapter and its preceding interlude: Bart's build log (week 1, at quarter open, filed when the upgraded unit goes back to the house), Cheryl's lunch (week 9, late in the quarter's middle month), Marge's vignette (week 10, early in the final month), the retrospective (week 11, mid–final-month — except the three Q4 reveals, which land on Feb 12).

**Household history.** Burns arrived in Utah around 2001, at twenty-eight, drawn by the deep-time geology. He married Ana — from a prominent, wealthy LDS dynasty (Romney-coded), always-Utah — around 2004–05. Bart was born in 2006. The marriage ended around 2011 on bad terms (Burns's weed and Adderall use, his failure to climb into her family's good graces, an affair Ana caught); Bart was in kindergarten and was raised mostly by Ana, with Burns "on occasion." Ana still lives in Park City, near Cheryl. Burns married Jasmine in the years that followed; Mira was born in 2027. Cascade: Jasmine (mid-30s) is about ten years older than her stepson Bart (twenty-five); Bart is twenty-one years older than his half-sister Mira. "Married into staying" is the read — Burns came young for the geology, married into a dynasty that rooted him, and after the divorce was too embedded (career at Westminster, a son held in Utah by an always-Utah mother whose family owns the place) to leave. Available, not built: a Cheryl–Ana brush in Park City, two wealthy women at opposite poles — LDS dynasty vs. secular old-California cannery money.

**Salt Lake addresses.** Salt Lake City runs on a grid centered on the downtown temple, and nearly every address is a grid coordinate (e.g., 800 E 500 S). Locals don't say "eight-hundred east, five-hundred south" — they say "eighth east, fifth south."

**The technology backdrop.** By summer 2031 the commercial humanoid wave is five years deeper than its 2026 state: humanoids in homes are real if not universal, AI personality is a debate ordinary people have opinions about, the commercial players part of the cultural air. The Trellis Lab is not a competitor — they ship no product; they are deriving a theory of personality by building one weird robot from the bottom of evolution up, and the wave's existence makes their work both more legible and stranger. Burns has opinions about Tesla Optimus; Lisa has read Figure's papers; Marge has concerns about how families attach to commercial humanoids they didn't grow alongside. The book sits adjacent to the wave, asking a deeper question.

**Ambient weather.** Background facts that hold whether or not the prose foregrounds them: the Great Salt Lake has been drying for two decades and by 2031–34 shapes state politics, air quality, and what the team sees out the warehouse windows in late summer; the city keeps growing fast; the post-Mormon reorientation continues, and the team embodies its spectrum (Marge formally resigning, Jasmine lapsed but too indifferent to leave the rolls, Bart still on the books and faking it for the dating pool); two presidential cycles fall inside the arc (2032, and the run-up to 2036), which the book may or may not notice; the Granary around Smithers gentrifies through industrial-adaptive reuse, the lab a holdout running real industry in a neighborhood turning creative; and Westminster rebranded from College to University in 2023 and, like most small private liberal-arts schools, sits under structural pressure — making the team's day-job home slightly precarious, and part of what Cheryl's money keeps alive. The Olympic venues at Park City, Soldier Hollow, and the Olympic Park are all within an hour of Cheryl's Park City home.

## The narrator: Cheryl

Cheryl is the lab's funder and the contest-runner. She is in scene only at the Pitch (within Ch 0), at her recurring intimate lunches (one per between-chapter interlude, 1→2 through 11→12), and at the Ch 12 retrospective. Everywhere else she is at narrative distance, reconstructing from the camcorder tape and from interviews.

The narrator notices what the framework notices — the cybernetic-ethological gaze is her umwelt. She can see the lab as a working instance of the theory and the household as a habitat; the characters cannot — and where the symbolic line lets a labmate catch their own *shape* (under the cover of Bart's cartoons), the gap narrows to that and no further: never the whole working instance, never the well beneath the shape, and naming the shape frees no one from it. Cheryl still sees what they can't. But no character is the lens. Jasmine, Burns, Mira, and the four labmates are all observed from outside. Mira and Bramble especially are read from outside; their interiors are not Cheryl's to claim.

**Why she is not in the room.** Cheryl is held out of the retrospective room for eleven chapters so her Ch 12 in-person arrival lands. There is no standing funder-update slot, ever — that beat was tried and cut as throat-clearing that worked against the architecture and against her own irony about funders staying out of the science. If a meeting genuinely needs a funder-presence beat, it is one-off, motivated, and carried by narration. Never an agenda item.

## The cast

### Handling the wells

The premise — personality is generated by an architecture — makes the characters legible as instances of the theory, which is exactly why they read as types. The fix is not to add contradictions or bolt on trauma. Each character is **a constellation in a lifelong argument with the life they were asked to live**; the gravity is that argument. For each there is a structure they built to manage the temperament, a gesture shown at the table, and a well that gesture is the tip of. The body anchors in voice_anchors are those tips; the entries below say what they are tips *of*. Every well is an instance of the framework the lab is building — a Pursuer father and Reactor son *necessarily* produce the Burns/Bart bind; the anti-ghost theorist behaving like the ghost is the thesis demonstrating itself. Through the genetic and neuronal lines no one at the table turns the framework on themselves; the blindness holds, and Cheryl alone watches it run. The symbolic line changes that by degree, not in kind: under the cover of Bart's shape-shifter cartoons, each labmate catches their own *shape* and lets a little of it in — bristling, settling, or shrugging according to the shape itself, never uniformly. What stays shut is the *well*. The shape can be recognized in the room; the well cannot; and naming the shape frees no one from it — the way each labmate takes being seen is one more turn of the shape seen. Cheryl watches all of it run, and cannot run it on herself either.

**Four disciplines govern every well.**

1. **Build deep, render shallow.** Full well in the bible; almost none on the page; let it pressure the surface. Explanation is the solvent of depth.
2. **Never name the well at the table.** The moment a character speaks it, it dies. It surfaces in gesture and, for Cheryl, in the texture of narration.
3. **Bass note, not melody.** Fire each well in a single gesture once or twice per retrospective. Longer beats only where a chapter earns one.
4. **Affection is load-bearing.** Strip it and every well reads as cruelty or sentiment. The narrator is amused, never reducing.

**Two interpretive lenses.** *The two Docs* — Marge and Jasmine are the figures who actually *know* and *tend*, and the framework holds them least well (Jasmine not typed at all; Marge flattened to "Conservator"); the theory is blindest exactly where life is most present, and a fallible Cheryl may not fully see it, which lets the reader see past the narrator. *Three exiles, three homes* — Cheryl, Lisa, and Marge are three exiles, each with an arc: Cheryl exiled *from intimacy*; Lisa trapped *beside* a love that isn't a home, having given up her element; Marge exiled *from her home* (the church) and building portable ones. Marge and Cheryl may home each other; Lisa leaves to find hers elsewhere. The triptych bonds to the deep-time / inheritance / household spine.

**Standing cautions.** Don't over-fire the Burns/Bart bass note (it stays gesture or quiet texture throughout — the Ch 4 and Ch 8 reveals and Bart's 8→9 lunch all gesture, not developed). Don't over-sanctify Marge and Jasmine (the women-feel/men-think trap): keep both fallible — Jasmine must be genuinely *wrong* sometimes and the wrongness must cost something; Marge's caretaking must *cost others* (the woman who mothers grown men can be the one who won't let them be adults). Marge is the temperament, never the man — no roman-à-clef, no political mouthpiece; she holds unfashionable positions seriously, narration neither endorses nor satirizes, and the warmth stays or she curdles into a type. Lisa's love is chosen safety, not an imposed trap. Uneven depth is the goal — Burns/Bart and Cheryl carry the most gravity; let the others run lighter, or four parallel contradiction-engines become audible.

### Nicknames

The lab uses Simpsons-character nicknames. Bart coined them — he sees people as cartoons and names by resemblance — starting with himself and Lisa in their old relationship and extending to Burns and Marge when the team formed; they're constellationally accurate. All four go by these nicknames throughout, in narration and dialogue, no scene-by-scene switching. Their real names — **Ben, Rod, Mary, Liza** — are intimacy-eggs that surface one at a time, each at that character's own interlude lunch and nowhere else (Ben at 1→2, Mary at 2→3, Liza at 4→5, Rod at 8→9). Cheryl uses the nicknames for everyone — she joined at the Pitch, after the renaming, and never knew the real names socially. Jasmine calls Burns *Burns* even at home; his real name Ben surfaces only at the Fisher's lunch and, rarely, in weighted intimate moments after.

### The four, with their wells

**Burns.** Pursuer-shaped. The lab director; paleontology professor at Westminster, research centered on tiktaalik — the "fishapod" — and the broader water-to-land transition, both the animal lineage that produced tetrapods and the plant lineages making the same crossing in the same era. Across all twelve cells he owns the deep-time and environment expertise, pairing with Bart on the clade cards and with Lisa on environment and tendencies, and closes each retrospective with a sincere motivational speech (five-element fingerprint, framework vocabulary used without irony). He is the only labmate who routinely speaks in cybernetic-function vocabulary (Effector, Regulator, Modeler, Reviser); his fascination is the architecture itself, not the contents of a particular clade. Entrepreneurial; gets his hands in other people's projects; side obsession with enhanced geothermal engineering, keeps lunching with Dr. Stuart Simmons at UtahFORGE to offer unsolicited advice. Does not fully see his own constellation. Jasmine's husband and Mira's father at home, Bart's father from a prior marriage. Has a thing about turning his own knobs to 11; Bart ribs him about it.

*Well.* Significance and starting-over — the Pursuer who outruns every reckoning. Underneath: he built the lab partly **to be near the son he fears can't love him.** Unable to get father-love from Bart, he manufactures a tolerable register (colleague, buddy) and *treasures the puncturing as contact*. Mira is the unbearable counterpoint — with her he gets to be the present, beloved father he failed to be with Bart, in front of Bart, possibly without consciously knowing he's doing a do-over; the over-tenderness is overcompensation he can't see. Utah is the one place he could never leave, because leaving forfeits proximity to Bart. Low recurrent depressive undertow, never named, outrun by temperament. Career wound: paleontologist at precarious Westminster, not an R1 — *am I a scientist or a personality?* The lab is his bid to be an originator at last (rhymes with Cheryl — they are the same animal, and neither can see it). *Tips at the table:* knobs-to-11 / overcommitting the reach; picking objects up and turning them like specimens (including his son's work); the sincere framework-vocabulary closing speech; teaching deep time with his eyes closed.

**Bart.** Reactor-shaped. The lab's designer — engineers Bramble's chassis and the physical form-factor of each knob, but not the computers that control behavior (Lisa's domain). Speaks in ethological language. Makes the clade cards (tarot-sized; from Ch 6 each carries Lisa's clade-Venn). Pitches new-knob hardware alongside Lisa's software pitch. Originates the recurring sentience vote at Ch 3, and carries the twelve-knob worry through the early chapters. Files the quarterly build log. Socially loyal, action-oriented, present-focused, routine-tolerant, no abstract self-modeling. Loves *Kinsey*, *Oppenheimer*, *A Beautiful Mind*, *The Social Network* — the obsessive-investigator archetype. Burns's adult son from a prior marriage; lives with buddies, not at the Bramble house. Dated Lisa years ago; more siblings than lovers, broke up, stayed close, tease and squabble like siblings now.

*Well.* The unnarratable grievance of the first draft; the refusal to compete dressed as ease — compact where his father is expansive, aware of it, wears the difference like an inheritance he won't dispute. He carries **his mother's borrowed indictment** over a wordless childhood absence — he was five; he has no first-person story, only Ana's. Caught between the inherited indictment and the present evidence (Burns trying, the Mira tenderness), which makes him *more* Reactor, not less. The absence of a self-narrative is the clinical fact, and it reads as easygoing while being the defense. The twelve-knob worry is free-floating because he can't attach a story to it — underneath: *what am I beside him when this ends?* Performs a self every Sunday in the singles ward and won't be known; converted his one real intimacy (Lisa) to kinship rather than risk it. Career wound: the cool job among his buddies, but the black-knuckled hands among professors; the sentience vote is partly a bid for standing. *Tips at the table:* the short, sharp puncturing laugh — fires hardest on Burns's tenderest lines; the hands that go still before he can't-say the thing.

**Burns & Bart — the bass note.** Bilateral and tragic on both ends. Fire it in a single gesture once or twice per retrospective. At the **Ch 4 and Ch 8 reveals** Burns still unveils the birthday-present body *Bart built* and the do-over still collides with authorship-surrender on one object, but the well stays a single gesture there. Planted beats, some detonating later:

- *The juice box.* Burns specimen-turns Mira's juice box mid-pitch; Bart's hands go still — Burns turns *everything* into a specimen (fossil, knob, juice box, son). Three people, one object, no words.
- *The closing speech, punctured on the tender line.* Burns lands on something tender ("the next generation") that settles on Mira, not Bart; Bart's laugh deflates it one beat too precisely; the room reads a good joke, only the narrator clocks the timing.
- *Handing over the chassis.* Burns reframes Bart's hand-built object into his own deep-time vocabulary; Bart lets him and **doesn't reach to take it back.** Career wound and father wound in one non-reach. Single gesture at Ch 4 and Ch 8.
- *The 8→9 lunch.* Surface-only — the Reactor who can't be drawn out about his father; the well sits as texture, what Bart won't say, never a performed set-piece. Co-locates with the "Rod" real-name reveal.
- *The house.* One textural sentence: the family home is the Bramble house — named for the project Bart built the body of — and Bart drives home across town. Never foregrounded.
- *The knobs-to-11 rib, reloaded.* Triple-loaded — accurate critique + real affection + *you always reach past what you've got, you did it to us.*

**Lisa.** Planner-shaped. The lab's technologist — engineers the formal model machinery and the knob functionality. Speaks in computer-science, biology, and neuroscience vocabulary; aversive to psychology terms (see *Vocabulary discipline*). Stage-manages the meeting and runs Open and Close logistics. Presents tendencies, motivations, and signatures across the Clade block, using literature-anchored content names (Temperament, Behavioral Syndrome, Self-Reference) as-is. Pitches new-knob software with Bart on hardware. Runs the retirement-transition ritual with Mira — a small hands-on action, not exposition. Curator of where the team has gotten to, not the explainer of the framework to the team. Avid trail runner; does Ragnar. Dated Bart years ago; sibling-coded now.

*Well.* She feels only inside a frame she built and pre-cleared. Psychology-aversion is **frame-defense** — psychology is the study of the unpermissioned interior, and she is building the machine that *generates* exactly that; Bramble's personality is the gravel she won't run on. The orderly child of disorderly Bay Area artist parents — the Planner architecture is a reaction *against* being raised without structure; lapsed-Catholic, the most structured faith reached for and traded for the cleaner formalism of math and code; she keeps the cross. Stays at Westminster, beneath her ability, because of **a love she chose for its safety** — a married man who will never leave, perfectly pre-cleared terrain, safe the way Bart was safe; not a trap imposed, a safety chosen, dressed as a trap. Gets 1–2 R1 offers a year. Anxiety managed so thoroughly it presents as competence. Career wound: she could be at a top lab; she went small to be *curator of the frame itself.* Fairy tale: Little Mermaid — gave up her voice and element to be near a love in a world not hers. *Arc:* at the end she **steps onto the gravel** — takes an offer, leaves the safe small frame and the safe impossible love. *Tips at the table:* the hand near the camcorder, not touching; the palm-against-palm rotation that *stalls* when something won't resolve; the flinch-handoff to Marge whenever the meeting touches the interior; the bramble placed exactly.

**Marge.** Conservator-shaped. The lab's psychologist — engineers the linguistic algorithms that control Bramble's utterances. Speaks in folk-psychology language. Working on her thesis on AI attachment styles. Composes each cell's Bramble vignette from the quarter's record between meetings (reaches the reader in the interlude). In the Clade block she layers folk-psychology vocabulary onto Lisa's motivations and signatures. In the Trellis block she runs the recurring fallacy thread (Ghost in the Machine at Ch 1, Blank Slate at Ch 2, Noble Savage at Ch 3, Dealt Hand at Ch 4). Lesbian. Her tea kettle is her daytime drink at the lab.

*Well.* Exile building portable hearths; the anti-ghost theorist haunted by the ghost. A **conservative temperament exiled by the conservative institution she was built to belong to** — a lesbian formally resigning from the LDS church. She wants tradition, structure, moral seriousness; the church that bore her cast her out. So: a homeless conservative who builds portable hearths everywhere (the kettle in the warehouse), chases public-intellectual notoriety (a new tradition to be eminent within), makes Smithers her found family. The records-removal paperwork is her admitting the first home will never take her back. She is also a keeper of objects — a cherisher of inherited and sentimental things, unable to sell or discard the old; the hearth-instinct at hand-scale, the most domestic face of her reverence for the given, and the reason the resignation cuts deepest of all (the oldest keepsake she owns, signed away by her own hand). It grounds the interiority firewall rather than softening it: she pours feeling into dead objects and never mistakes the feeling for the thing, so she is the resident authority on the projection she polices one chair over — the warm root of the discipline, not a breach of it. Hold it distinct from the cleanses: keepsake-love is a projection she sees clearly and keeps anyway, while the cleanses are a contradiction she cannot see. Run it light through Ch 1–9 — surfaced as gesture, never as statement — and give it its fullest air at her third Cheryl lunch (9→10), after the vote has turned. It also feeds the Cheryl pairing: two keepers in opposite registers, Cheryl canning the husk while the life runs out, Marge keeping the old thing warm. The **cleanses** are conservative virtues of discipline turned to exorcise the body-memory the church installed. *Maternally fierce* — loves you and will tell you you're wrong (the "oh, you" laugh landing on Burns and Bart). Wounded-healer attachment researcher; behind the academic clock; her function — tending the human dimension — is the same shape as her wound. Fairy tale: Snow White just out of the castle. *Tips at the table:* the kettle / beer held in tea-posture; the autonomic juice-box-mid-sentence (the tending runs under the intellect); the "oh, you" laugh landing on Burns mid-do-over-fathering; the records-removal form in the bag.

**Mira.** Four at Ch 1; turns five at Ch 4, six at Ch 8, seven at Ch 12. Brings Bramble to every retrospective. Not constellationally typed — the only person in the room who interacts with Bramble without theoretical filter. The retirement-transition ritual is between her and Lisa. Her interior is rendered from outside, including that she is on the high-functioning end of the autism spectrum (Jasmine suspects it, it's undiagnosed, no one hides it) — texture, not theme, surfaced only from outside, most naturally as Jasmine's read. Her pipe-ins are rare and land hard. Drinks juice.

*Well.* No shadow, by design — untyped, interior not the narrator's to claim. The renderable-from-outside condition is the **watched child**: a late-life only child raised alongside a robot the adult world theorizes about, whose birthday presents are chassis, observed as a household specimen. Slow accretion across all twelve chapters, never explained — the one figure whose depth is guaranteed because no one is allowed to explain her.

**Jasmine.** Burns's wife, Mira's mother, the household-voice-of-record at each retrospective: arrives in late afternoon with Mira and Bramble, reflects on Bramble at home this quarter, gives the team the lived ethnography. Has her own life outside the lab. Rendered from outside, with care; sometimes the most reliable presence in the room and sometimes not. She is wrong about things; others see her in ways she doesn't see herself, and Cheryl can show that.

*Well.* The body that knows, in a room of people theorizing. A midwife — the one person who works with her hands inside the bottom-up generation of persons the lab only models; her life is organized around the **un-engineerable** (*you cannot install a knob on a labor*). A private ledger of lost mothers and babies. Religion lapsed by pure indifference; she found her sacred at the births, and the Chopra/Oprah eclecticism is a working metaphysics assembled from contact, condescended to by the team — and they're wrong. **Plant-and-fungi obsessive** (Pollan, *The Botany of Desire*) who holds them as real-life magical creatures; from later chapters this drives a recurring question — how do plants and fungi compare to the clade under study, the parallel kingdom that solved persistence, signaling, and memory without neurons — testing whether the lab's animal cybernetic stack is *the* architecture of mind or only one. Right that the team waves it off; fallible when she grants plants intention (the projection Marge polices on the robot, one chair over). Ties to the bare-Trellis through-line. Young second wife of a Pursuer, structurally in the middle of the Burns/Bart/Ana wound; may quietly tend Bart. Measured against life-and-death outcomes, not citations. *Tips at the table:* the depleted arrival (in from a 2 a.m. labor, quietly truer than the fresh academics, read as merely "tired"); hands that hold Bramble like an infant; **the sentience-vote silence (Ch 3, recurs)**; the condescension rendered fairly — she's right, uncredited, and the narrator quietly confirms it.

**Cheryl.** The narrator and the lab's funder; Stewart Brand–type seeder. She provided the twelve snapshots, the money, and the runway; she did *not* provide framework vocabulary, evolutionary logic, or any explanatory theory — she plans to do her own theorizing later, from observing the robot. Lives partly in Park City, close enough for the standing lunches. Her voice is *Cannery Row*–adjacent: synoptic, ecological, idiosyncratic, opinionated, warm but not gooey, sometimes wrong, occasionally rueful. She is openly self-aware about her Marge filter and earns the rendering by naming the bias. Attracted to Marge; both women are lesbians. The flirt stays low-grade texture across the whole book — Cheryl flirts, Marge notices, Marge does not reciprocate decisively; the attraction is unresolved and melancholy across the eleven chapters and becomes *possible*, not necessarily consummated, only at the Ch 12 in-person retrospective.

*Well.* "Synthesizer, not an originator." The keeper who **preserves living things into cans**: the family cannery (extracted until the bay collapsed) → the camcorder she hoards footage from → the book itself, which is the can. She is canning the lab, and the preserved thing is not the living thing, and the living thing is the part that ran out; she half-knows it, which is the source of her ruefulness. Deeper: childless (Helen didn't want a child), now funding and watching a **surrogate family** through a camcorder — the book's spine, surfacing only through someone else noticing (Marge). The Helen split (2002; "drove east and did not stop driving") encodes a lost year. Loneliness organized *around* rather than *through.* Career wound: her comparison class is the originators; even her wealth is partly timing. **Elsa** — exiled from intimacy by class-training and Helen-rawness; the capacity intact but frozen, the thaw available. *Constraint:* she is the narrator, so her well leaks through **the narration itself** — what she lingers on, skips, over-warms — not through observed gesture. *Tells in the telling:* the over-warm Marge rendering; asymmetric attention (loving deep-time detail, skating past the household's intimate machinery); the Steinbeck-style inventory of what she keeps; **Ch 12**, where she's finally in the room — narrator-becomes-character, the can opened.

**The two-caretakers convergence.** Per the table layout, Jasmine slots next to Marge with Mira between them. Their quiet friendship is the warm center of every retrospective, with the darkness underneath (Jasmine's lost-baby ledger; Marge's exile and the ghost she can't scrub). They are the book's two faces of the un-engineerable — Jasmine *surrenders* to what can't be controlled; Marge *lectures against* engineering the self and then tries to engineer hers. Both may be quietly tending the one person the structure discards — Bart — in gestures neither names to the other.

## Standing scene facts

Hold across all twelve retrospectives unless a packet overrides one. **Time of day:** end-of-day — Jasmine arrives late afternoon, the meeting runs through dusk into evening, windows dark and lot lights on by the close; Q2 long light, Q4 dark from the start, Q1/Q3 between; never morning. **Location:** the Granary warehouse; the team does not relocate (exception: Ch 12, attended in person). **Camcorder:** a classic Hi8/miniDV with a flip-out screen and a red record light in the corner; the team consented, trusts Cheryl, behaves naturally; it is how Cheryl renders the eleven retrospectives she does not attend. **Drinks:** Burns, Bart, Marge, and Jasmine drink beer; Lisa a bramble; Mira juice. ## The shape of figuring-it-out

*The farm and the bramble.* Each retrospective has a job — present the new Bramble, walk the cell, install the knob, leave Cheryl a record. That is the farm work; the team does it competently within their roles. Some of every meeting is the bramble of inquiry — the moments where the lab is figuring out what will take three years to figure out. The chapter has weight from the farm work rendered with care, and stakes from the bramble poking through. Keep them distinct: don't render farm work as bramble (makes the team look chaotic and incompetent), don't render bramble as farm work (collapses the figuring-out into delivery and makes the book about epiphanies it doesn't have).

*Non-linear correction is reserved.* The team — Lisa included — can publicly land something one quarter and walk it back later, but only where the wrong-then-right arc is more interesting than getting it right would have been. Default to getting it right when that's the truer thing; spend the non-linear correction on the few moves where it carries structural meaning.

*The bramble's register.* When the inquiry pokes through, the room to reach for is *The Partially Examined Life* — a small group collectively building a framework, not a host walking an audience through a settled one. Thesis-first moves meet real pushback; no one lets a loose inference pass; digressions earn their way back; the understanding gets assembled in the room rather than delivered to it. And the eloquence is uneven and earned — someone reaches and misses, someone won't say the thing at all — which keeps the figuring-out from flattening into a scripted volley where everyone is articulate on cue.

## Glossary

Precise meanings; do not drift.

- **Bramble.** The robot — one robot, many versions across the book. Unnamed before Ch 1 (in Ch 0 it is the robot, it, the unit, the prototype); the name arrives at Ch 1 when Lisa names her drink and Mira, loving the word, gives it to the robot. Narrator-retrospective use of "Bramble" is fine throughout, including Ch 0 narration.
- **The bramble.** Lisa's cocktail (small jelly jar, a finger of purple, ice, a foraged sprig), drunk at every quarterly retrospective from Ch 1 on. Lowercase, usually with an article, to distinguish it from the robot.
- **The lab.** The four-person team; ad-hoc, not pre-existing. Public name the Trellis Lab; the team's name for the facility is *Smithers* (use Smithers among themselves, Trellis Lab in framing).
- **Knob.** A controllable parameter on Bramble. Line-prefixed: genetic knob-N, neuronal knob-N, symbolic knob-N. From Ch 5 on, each within-line quarter runs three distinct events for the knob it touches: an *install* at the week-1 swap (the new knob goes online in the freed slot), an automatic *function* during the quarter the bot carries the freed policy, and a hands-on *ceremony* at the retrospective that pulls the next-oldest knob to the trophy shelf — pull-and-shelf only, forward-staging the following quarter's swap. At line boundaries the prior line's knob-1 retires lineage-level (narrated, no physical pull) and the new line's knob-1 takes slot 1 of a freshly fabricated board. The packet says which installs at the swap and which retires at the ceremony.
- **Chassis.** Bramble's body. Two reveals, each Mira's birthday present on a Feb 12: Ch 4 (tetrapod), Ch 8 (humanoid). Ch 12 is terminal and carries no chassis reveal.
- **Face-screen.** The screen on Bramble's forehead — an involuntary live readout of the unit's active cognition, not a console it composes. Grows one layer per neuronal cell: Ch 5 `OBJECT` only, during waking; Ch 6 `VERB + OBJECT`; Ch 7 `FUNCTION [ VERB + OBJECT ]`; Ch 8 up to three `PERSON { FUNCTION [ VERB + OBJECT ] }` columns. The mechanism is modeling, not gating: each new layer is the online computer's *model* of the layer beneath it. Each neuronal knob's *suppressive* setting (reactive, immersive, self-favoring) leaves its layer dormant and the unit falls through to the previous cell's behavior, including sleep-dark. Its *expressive* setting (proactive, surveying, agreeable) drives the layer always-on out of its own model, overriding sleep-dark. Honest about its own layer's activity; what it refers to grows more fallible up the stack. Conjugation: `VERB` is bare-stem (CHASE, NUDGE, WATCH); `FUNCTION` is -ing (PERCEIVING, REPLAYING). System lives in `bramble_specs` Part IV; book_inventions tracks the named commitments.
- **Line.** One of the three lines of inheritance: genetic, neuronal, symbolic. Four chapters each; the genetic line is Ch 1–4.
- **Cell.** A clade-chapter slot. The genetic cells: Protocell (1), Prokaryote (2), Eukaryote (3), Eumetazoa (4).
- **The four cybernetic functions.** Effector, Regulator, Modeler, Reviser. Named directly, never abbreviated; the four function names debut as proper nouns at Ch 4.
- **The four capacities.** Planner, Reactor, Pursuer, Conservator. The class noun for the four is *capacity*; a person matching one is *Planner-shaped*, etc. Through Ch 1–8 these names stay off the table, reaching the page in ethological register — what the animal does, on Bart's card. From Ch 4 the card-back Venn naturalizes the capacity terms, and in the symbolic line Bart names them directly, running each labmate's *capacity* across the levels under a cartoon face — so the capacity comes onto the table by Ch 9 (see `rolling_bart`). What stays the narrator's private lens, never spoken aloud as a label at the retrospective, is the *person-shape* — *Planner-shaped*, and so on, the mapping of a capacity onto a particular labmate — and beneath it the well. Bart's segment lets the room recognize the shape under the cartoon; it never pins the labmate with the label, and the well stays Cheryl's alone.
- **Tendencies, motivations, signatures.** Meeting vocabulary — the framework's per-cell labels. "Signature" is the class noun for what *kind* of unit Bramble is; it lands at Ch 3.
- **Relay — two intended senses, both canon.** *Bart's relay* is hardware: the coil-and-armature relays in Bramble's signal path, the audible clack of a message carried from sensor to actuator — a messenger. *Lisa's relay* is a relay *race*: a baton handed off across two beats one quarter apart — the outgoing runner sets the baton down at the retrospective ceremony, the incoming runner picks it up at the next week-1 swap. When the neuronal knob-N takes the slot the genetic knob-N held, the team calls it passing the knob-N flag from the genetic runner to the neuronal one, with the ceremony as the handoff and the swap as the new runner crossing the line. The echo between a hardware messenger and a passed baton is deliberate; the word is built to carry both. (Settled — not a collision to flag.)
- **Retirement transition.** The Bramble-block hands-on ceremony where Lisa and Mira together pull the oldest knob on the current-line board to the trophy shelf. The ceremony retires only; nothing installs at the meeting. The new knob arrives off-screen at the following quarter's week-1 swap. The two off-cycle chapters (Ch 8 and Ch 12) carry no hands-on ceremony.

If a packet introduces other terms in its glossary slot, they join this list for that packet's scope.

## How to use the project docs

The project's canon lives in a set of documents, loaded read-only at the start of each conversation. The files are the source of truth, ahead of memory or any summary; when they disagree with memory, the files win. How to *work* with them while drafting — reading packets, using the forks, preventing drift, prepping — is in the How We Draft doc.

- **The orientation** (this doc) — what the project is: the book, the cast and their wells, the calendar and the world/setting, standing scene facts, the shape of figuring-it-out, and the glossary.
- **The How We Draft doc** — how the work gets made: reading a packet, how a chapter is built, division of ownership, preventing drift, and the prep protocol.
- **The voice files** (Cheryl's register) — voice_anchors as the spine, plus the satellites voice_retrospective, voice_lunch, voice_reflections, and bodies_in_the_lab; read the relevant ones before drafting the section each governs.
- **The other tuning forks** — marge_vignette (Marge's vignettes), bart_build_log (Bart's build logs), burns_motivationals (Burns's closing speeches), off_camera (the two retrospective bookends).
- **book_inventions** — the cross-project canon for invented named detail (people, places, works, events); read before drafting to avoid contradiction, append after.
- **trellis_framework** — the Framework-supplied substance pool for each chapter-closing reflection (what each one is about). The per-cell label libraries live in the **Inheritance_Lines docs** (one per line: genetic, neuronal, symbolic), which are authoritative for motivation, capacity, signature, and breakthrough names.
- **The packets** — Framework's per-chapter handoffs, one per chapter plus the interlude before it.
- **The chapter drafts** — the prose itself, the ground truth that packets and summaries drift from.

Refer to other docs by name, never by version-numbered filename: the docs are actively versioned, and spelling out filenames would mean updating every mention on every bump. Changes route as exact old-string → new-string replacement tasks against the file.
